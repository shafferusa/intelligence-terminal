Warm-up · from earlier lessons

One from Day 20 and one from Day 8, so they stick. First try counts.

From Day 20. A company’s fast-growth years end with D4 = 3.00, paid at the end of year 4. After that, dividends grow 5% a year forever, and investors require 10%. What is the terminal value V4? To the nearest cent.

per share
Hint The terminal value’s numerator is the first slow-growth dividend, D5, not the last fast one.
Solution

D5 = 3.00 × 1.05 = 3.15. V4 = D5/(r − gL) = 3.15/(0.10 − 0.05) = 3.15/0.05 = 63.00.

BA II Plus: 3 × 1.05 ÷ ( .1 − .05 ) = → 63.0000.

TI-84 Plus: 3 × 1.05 ÷ ( .1 − .05 ) ENTER → 63.0000.

D4 on top gives 60.00, the classic slip. And remember where V4 sits: at the end of year 4, so it is discounted 4 periods.

From Day 8. A perpetuity pays 4 a year forever. The first payment comes at the end of year 3, and the discount rate is 5%. Its value today is closest to:

  • Right. 4/0.05 = 80.00 is a value at the end of year 2, one period before the first payment. Then 80/1.052 = 72.56.

  • That discounts 3 periods: 80/1.053. But A/r already sits one period before the first payment, at t = 2, so only 2 periods are left.

  • That ignores the wait. 80 is what the perpetuity is worth at the end of year 2, not today.

Why

A/r always gives a value one period before the first payment. First payment at t = 3, so A/r is dated t = 2: discount 2 periods. BA II Plus: 4 ÷ .05 = → 80.0000, ÷ 1.05 yˣ 2 = → 72.5624. TI-84 Plus: 4 ÷ .05 ÷ 1.05 ^ 2 ENTER → 72.5624.

This week’s Gordon model is the same perpetuity with growth: set g = 0 in D1/(r − g) and you are back to A/r.

What this week was about

One idea ran through all six days: a price is the present value of the cash its owner will receive, discounted at the market’s rate. A bond is an annuity of coupons plus one lump at the end (Day 15). Its yield to maturity is that rate, worked backwards from the price: the bond’s IRR (Day 16). Between coupon dates the value grows at the yield, and the quoted price leaves out the interest owed to the seller (Day 17). Money-market paper is quoted in simple interest on 360 or 365 days, so convert before you compare (Day 18). And a share is the present value of its dividends: one growing perpetuity (Day 19), or a few dividends listed one by one plus a perpetuity that starts later (Day 20).

Memorize · the week’s formulas
  • Day 15. Bond price: PV = PMT/(1 + r)1 + PMT/(1 + r)2 + … + (PMT + FV)/(1 + r)N
  • Day 15. Semiannual-pay bond (P/Y = 1): PMT = annual coupon/2, rate = annual yield/2, N = 2 × years
  • Day 15. Coupon rate > yield → premium; coupon rate < yield → discount; equal → par
  • Day 16. Current yield = annual coupon / flat price
  • Day 16. Yield to call: N = periods to the call date, FV = call price; solve for r. Yield-to-worst = the lowest of the YTM and the yields to every call date
  • Day 16. Periodicity: (1 + APRm/m)m = (1 + APRn/n)n; semiannual-bond basis ↔ annual-pay: (1 + rs/2)2 = 1 + rannual
  • Day 17. PVfull = PVlast coupon date × (1 + r)t/T; AI = (t/T) × PMT; PVflat = PVfull − AI (government bonds actual/actual, corporates 30/360)
  • Day 18. Discount basis: PV = FV × [1 − (Days/Year) × DR]. Add-on basis: PV = FV / [1 + (Days/Year) × AOR]
  • Day 18. Bond-equivalent yield, a 365-day add-on rate: BEY = (365/Days) × (FV − PV)/PV. Repo interest = principal × repo rate × (days/360)
  • Day 19. V0 = D1/(r − g), with D1 = D0 × (1 + g); implied r = D1/P0 + g; implied g = r − D1/P0
  • Day 20. V0 = Σt=1..n Dt/(1 + r)t + [Dn+1/(r − gL)]/(1 + r)n: the terminal value is discounted n periods
Exam traps
  • Semiannual inputs. Halve the coupon and the yield and double the years going in; double the computed I/Y coming out. That doubled rate is the semiannual-bond-basis yield, not (1 + r)2 − 1.
  • Full vs flat. The buyer pays the full price; quotes (and the BA II Plus BOND worksheet’s PRI) are flat. Grow the last-coupon PV with the periodic yield.
  • D0 vs D1, and the terminal value’s date. “Just paid” is D0; Gordon’s numerator is the next dividend. In two stages the terminal value uses Dn+1 and sits at time n.
  • Day counts. T-bill discount rates and repo interest use 360 days; the bond-equivalent yield uses 365, on the price, not the face value.

On your calculator: a thirty-second check

Problem. Most of today’s questions run through TVM, and a wrong setting gives a confident wrong price. So test the settings on a bond whose answer you already know: a 2-year bond with a 10% coupon paid semiannually, at a 10% yield. Coupon equal to yield means it must price at par, exactly 100.

  1. END mode: coupons arrive at the end of each period

    BA II Plus If BGN shows in the display, press 2ND BGN 2ND SET 2ND QUIT. No BGN means END.
    TI-84 Plus APPS 1:Finance 1:TVM Solver; on the bottom row, PMT: highlight END and press ENTER

  2. One period a year: P/Y = 1. You do the halving and doubling yourself

    BA II Plus 2ND P/Y. If the display does not read P/Y = 1.0000, type 1 ENTER. Then 2ND QUIT.
    TI-84 Plus in the same screen, P/Y=1 and C/Y=1

  3. Price the test bond: halve the coupon and the yield, double the years

    BA II Plus 2ND CLR TVM 4 N 5 I/Y 5 PMT 100 FV CPT PV
    TI-84 Plus N=4, I%=5, PMT=5, FV=100, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, PMT: END; cursor on PV=, then ALPHA ENTER

    Display: PV = −100.0000 (BA II Plus) / PV=−100.0000 (TI-84). If you see −118.1439, P/Y is still 12: go back to step 2. If you see −100.8865, BGN is on: go back to step 1.

Ready: END, P/Y = 1, and the week’s first rule already in your fingers. Clear TVM before each new bond; on the TI-84, set every line.

1. Day 15 · Bond price, on the calculator. A 12-year bond pays a 4.5% coupon semiannually. The market yield is 5.2%. What is its price per 100 of par? Two decimals.

per 100 of par
Hint Semiannual: halve the coupon (PMT = 2.25) and the yield (I/Y = 2.6), double the years (N = 24). FV = 100, P/Y = 1, then compute PV.
Solution

N = 24, I/Y = 2.6, PMT = 2.25, FV = 100 → PV = −93.8089. The price is 93.81; the minus only says you pay it. Below par, because the coupon (4.5%) is below the yield (5.2%).

BA II Plus: 2ND CLR TVM 24 N 2.6 I/Y 2.25 PMT 100 FV CPT PV → PV = −93.8089.

TI-84 Plus: APPS 1:Finance 1:TVM Solver: N=24, I%=2.6, PMT=2.25, FV=100, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, PMT: END; cursor on PV=, ALPHA ENTER → PV=−93.8089.

The sneaky slip: annual inputs (N = 12, I/Y = 5.2, PMT = 4.5) give 93.87, close enough to look right. Discounting at the coupon rate gives exactly 100.00.

2. Day 15 · Pull to par. A 10-year 6% annual-pay bond yields 5% and is priced at 107.72. If its yield is still 5% one year from now, its price then, just after that year’s coupon is paid, will be closest to:

  • That moves the price the right distance (0.61) in the wrong direction. A premium does not keep building: it must be gone by maturity, when the bond pays exactly 100.

  • Right. With 9 years left: N = 9, I/Y = 5, PMT = 6, FV = 100 gives 107.1078. The premium shrinks from 7.72 to 7.11.

  • That assumes a price moves only when the yield moves. Time moves it too: at an unchanged yield a premium bond is pulled down toward par (and a discount bond up).

Explanation

Re-price it with one year gone. BA II Plus: 2ND CLR TVM 9 N 5 I/Y 6 PMT 100 FV CPT PV → PV = −107.1078. TI-84 Plus: TVM Solver N=9, I%=5, PMT=6, FV=100, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, END; cursor on PV=, ALPHA ENTER → PV=−107.1078.

Why it falls: the owner earns the 5% yield on the price, 107.7217 × 1.05 = 113.1078, and is then paid the 6 coupon, which leaves 107.1078. The coupon (6) is more than the 5% earned (5.3861), so the extra 0.6139 comes out of the premium this year, and a little more each year after, until it reaches zero at maturity.

3. Day 16 · Yield to maturity, on the calculator. An 8-year bond with a 5% coupon paid semiannually is priced at 96.50. What is its yield to maturity, quoted the usual way, on a semiannual-bond basis? As a percent, two decimals.

%
Hint N = 16, PV = −96.50 (you pay it), PMT = 2.5, FV = 100. Computing I/Y gives the rate per half-year: double it.
Solution

The rate per half-year is 2.7738%; doubled, the YTM is 5.55%.

BA II Plus: 2ND CLR TVM 16 N 96.5 +|− PV 2.5 PMT 100 FV CPT I/Y → I/Y = 2.7738, then × 2 = → 5.5477 (it doubles the unrounded rate).

TI-84 Plus: TVM Solver N=16, PV=−96.5 (the minus is the (−) key), PMT=2.5, FV=100, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, END; cursor on I%=, ALPHA ENTER → I%=2.7738. Double it: 2.7738 × 2 = 5.5476. Either way, 5.55%.

The slips: 2.77 is the half-year rate, not doubled. 5.62 is (1.027738)2 − 1, the annual-pay equivalent, not the semiannual-bond-basis quote. A positive PV gives Error 5 (BA II Plus) or ERR:NO SIGN CHNG (TI-84).

4. Day 16 · Yield-to-worst, on the calculator. A 10-year bond with a 6.5% coupon paid semiannually is priced at 104.25. It can be called in 4 years at 101. What is its yield-to-worst, on a semiannual-bond basis? As a percent, two decimals.

%
Hint Two yields, the same keys twice, with PV = −104.25 and PMT = 3.25. YTM: N = 20, FV = 100. Yield to call: N = 8, FV = 101, the call price. Double each; the worst is the lower one.
Solution

YTM = 2.9653% × 2 = 5.93%. Yield to call = 2.7640% × 2 = 5.5280%. The worst is the lower: 5.53%.

BA II Plus: 2ND CLR TVM 20 N 104.25 +|− PV 3.25 PMT 100 FV CPT I/Y → I/Y = 2.9653, × 2 = → 5.9305. Only N and FV change for the call: 8 N 101 FV CPT I/Y → I/Y = 2.7640, × 2 = → 5.5280.

TI-84 Plus: TVM Solver N=20, PV=−104.25, PMT=3.25, FV=100, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, END; cursor on I%=, ALPHA ENTER → I%=2.9653. Then change to N=8 and FV=101; cursor on I%=, ALPHA ENTER → I%=2.7640. Doubled: about 5.93 and 5.5280.

Why the call is worse: the bond trades above both par and the call price. If it is called, you lose 3.25 of premium (104.25 → 101) in 4 years instead of 4.25 spread over 10. The slip: FV = 100 in the call gives 5.31.

5. Day 16 · Periodicity. A bond yields 5.80% on an annual-pay basis. To compare it with Treasuries, which are quoted on a semiannual-bond basis, you restate it. Its semiannual-bond-basis yield is closest to:

  • That treats the two conventions as the same. 5.80% compounded twice a year grows by more than 5.80% in a year.

  • That converts the wrong way: (1.029)2 − 1 treats 5.80% as the semiannual quote and finds its annual-pay equivalent.

  • Right: 2 × (1.0580.5 − 1) = 5.7183%. Compounding twice a year needs a lower quoted rate to reach the same 5.80% a year.

Explanation

Set the year’s growth equal: (1 + rs/2)2 = 1.058, so rs/2 = 1.0580.5 − 1 = 2.8591% and rs = 5.7183%.

BA II Plus: 2ND ICONV ↓ (EFF=) 5.8 ENTER ↓ (C/Y=) 2 ENTER ↑ ↑ (NOM=) CPT → NOM = 5.7183. TI-84 Plus: APPS 1:Finance, ↓ to B:►Nom(, ENTER, 5.8 , 2 ) ENTER → 5.7183.

The rule: more compounding periods, lower quote, same growth.

6. Day 17 · Flat price, on the calculator. A 4% government bond pays coupons semiannually. Six coupons remain, the yield is 4.6%, and the trade settles 62 days into a 182-day coupon period (actual/actual). What is the flat price per 100 of par? To the nearest cent.

per 100 of par
Hint Three steps. PV at the last coupon date: N = 6, I/Y = 2.3, PMT = 2, FV = 100. Grow it to settlement: × 1.02362/182. Then subtract AI = 62/182 × 2.
Solution

PV at the last coupon date = 98.3365. Full price = 98.3365 × 1.02362/182 = 99.1012. AI = 62/182 × 2 = 0.6813. Flat = 99.1012 − 0.6813 = 98.4199: 98.42.

BA II Plus: 2ND CLR TVM 6 N 2.3 I/Y 2 PMT 100 FV CPT PV → PV = −98.3365. Then 98.3365 × 1.023 yˣ ( 62 ÷ 182 ) = → 99.1012, STO 1; 62 ÷ 182 × 2 = → 0.6813, STO 2; RCL 1 − RCL 2 = → 98.4199.

TI-84 Plus: TVM Solver N=6, I%=2.3, PMT=2, FV=100, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, END; cursor on PV=, ALPHA ENTER → PV=−98.3365. Home screen (2nd MODE, QUIT): 98.3365 × 1.023 ^ ( 62 ÷ 182 ) ENTER → 99.1012, STO→ ALPHA F ENTER; 62 ÷ 182 × 2 ENTER → .6813; ALPHA F − 2nd (−) (ANS) ENTER → 98.4199.

Carry every decimal of the PV and it is 98.4198; to the cent, 98.42 either way. The slips: 99.10 is the full price, what the buyer pays, not the quote. Growing at the annual 4.6% instead of the half-year 2.3% gives a flat price of 99.17.

7. Day 18 · Money-market yield. A 120-day T-bill is quoted at a 3.90% discount rate. What is its bond-equivalent yield? As a percent, two decimals.

%
Hint Price first, on a 360-day year: 100 × (1 − 120/360 × 0.039). The BEY is a 365-day add-on rate: (365/120) × (100 − price)/price.
Solution

Price = 100 × (1 − 120/360 × 0.039) = 98.70. You pay 98.70 and get 100, so you earn 1.30 on 98.70. BEY = 365/120 × 1.30/98.70 = 4.0062%: 4.01%.

BA II Plus: 100 × ( 1 − 120 ÷ 360 × .039 ) = → 98.7000, STO 1; ( 100 − RCL 1 ) ÷ RCL 1 × 365 ÷ 120 × 100 = → 4.0062.

TI-84 Plus: 100 × ( 1 − 120 ÷ 360 × .039 ) STO→ ALPHA P ENTER → 98.7000; ( 100 − ALPHA P ) ÷ ALPHA P × 365 ÷ 120 × 100 ENTER → 4.0062.

The slips: 3.95 is the add-on rate on a 360-day year (right base, wrong year). 3.90 is the discount rate itself, figured on face value, not on the money you put in.

8. Day 18 · Repo interest. A dealer borrows $25,000,000 for 30 days in a repo at 4.40%. The interest owed at repurchase is closest to:

  • Right: 25,000,000 × 0.044 × 30/360.

  • That uses a 365-day year. Repo rates are money-market rates on a 360-day year.

  • That is a full year’s interest: the 30 days were left out.

Explanation

Repo interest = principal × repo rate × days/360. BA II Plus: 25000000 × .044 × 30 ÷ 360 = → 91,666.6667. TI-84 Plus: the same keys with ENTER → 91666.6667.

9. Day 19 · Implied required return. A share trades at 52.00. It has just paid a dividend of 1.95, and dividends are expected to grow 4% a year forever. What required return is the market pricing in? As a percent, two decimals.

%
Hint “Has just paid” is D0. Grow it once to D1, then r = D1/P0 + g.
Solution

D1 = 1.95 × 1.04 = 2.028. D1/P0 = 2.028/52 = 3.90%, the dividend yield. r = 3.90% + 4% = 7.90%. Check: 2.028/(0.079 − 0.04) = 52.00.

BA II Plus: 1.95 × 1.04 ÷ 52 × 100 + 4 = → 7.9000. TI-84 Plus: the same keys with ENTER → 7.9000.

The slip: D0/P0 + g = 1.95/52 + 4% = 7.75%.

10. Day 20 · Two-stage DDM, on the calculator. A company has just paid a dividend of 1.50. Dividends are expected to grow 18% a year for the next 3 years and 5% a year after that, forever. The required return is 11%. The value of a share today is closest to:

  • That discounts the terminal value 4 periods. V3 = D4/(r − gL) is already a value at the end of year 3.

  • That puts D3 on top of the terminal value: 2.4645/0.06 = 41.08. The numerator is the first slow-growth dividend, D4 = D3 × 1.05.

  • Right: 36.6278, of which 31.54 (86%) is the terminal value.

Explanation

D1 = 1.77, D2 = 2.0886, D3 = 2.4645. D4 = 2.4645 × 1.05 = 2.5878. V3 = 2.5878/0.06 = 43.1296. Year-3 cash flow = D3 + V3 = 45.5941. Discount at 11%: V0 = 36.6278.

BA II Plus: 2ND QUIT CE/C 1.5 × 1.18 = → 1.7700; × 1.18 = → 2.0886; × 1.18 = → 2.4645, STO 1; × 1.05 = → 2.5878; ÷ ( .11 − .05 ) = → 43.1296; + RCL 1 = → 45.5941. Then CF 2ND CLR WORK ↓ 1.77 ENTER ↓ ↓ 2.0886 ENTER ↓ ↓ 45.5941 ENTER NPV 11 ENTER ↓ CPT → NPV = 36.6278.

TI-84 Plus: 1.5 × 1.18 ENTER, then × 1.18 ENTER twice → 2.4645; STO→ ALPHA D ENTER; × 1.05 ÷ ( .11 − .05 ) ENTER → 43.1296; + ALPHA D ENTER → 45.5941. Then APPS 1:Finance 7:npv( and npv(11,0,{1.77,2.0886,45.5941}) ENTER → 36.6278.
