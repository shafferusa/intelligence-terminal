From Day 55. A bond trades 180 bp over Treasuries, and the expected recovery if it defaults is 35%. Using spread ≈ POD × LGD, what annual probability of default does the spread imply? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint Work backwards: POD ≈ spread / LGD, with LGD as a share of the exposure, 1 − 0.35.
Solution

LGD = 1 − 0.35 = 0.65. POD ≈ 1.80% / 0.65 = 2.77%. Check: 2.7692% × 0.65 = 1.80%.

BA II Plus: 1.8 ÷ ( 1 − .35 ) = → 2.7692. TI-84 Plus: the same keys, ending with ENTER → 2.7692.

Dividing by the recovery rate instead gives 5.14%: the spread pays for what is lost, not what is recovered.

From Day 29. A fund beats its benchmark in any year with probability 0.6, independently of other years. The probability that it beats the benchmark in exactly 4 of the next 5 years is closest to:

  • That is one particular order, 0.64 × 0.4. The missed year can be any of the five, so multiply by 5C4 = 5.

  • That is P(at least 4): it adds P(all 5) = 0.65 = 7.78%. The question says exactly 4.

  • Right: 5C4 × 0.64 × 0.4 = 5 × 0.1296 × 0.4 = 0.2592.

Why

Each order with four beats and one miss has probability 0.64 × 0.4 = 0.05184, and there are 5 such orders. 5 × 0.05184 = 25.92%.

BA II Plus: 5 2ND nCr 4 = → 5.0000; × .6 yˣ 4 × .4 = → 0.2592. TI-84 Plus: 2nd VARS (DISTR) A:binompdf( 5 , .6 , 4 ) ENTER → .2592.

What this week was about

Every lesson this week asked the same thing: how much risk are you really taking, and are you paid for it?

Day 50 tested whether a fitted slope is real. A stock’s beta is the slope of its returns on the market’s, and t = (b̂1 − B1)/sb̂1 with n − 2 df says whether it differs from 0, or from 1. Day 51 put error bars on a forecast and used logs to fit growth with a straight line.

Days 52 and 53 priced risk. CAPM pays only for beta; alpha is how far a return sits above the SML; Sharpe, Treynor, M² and Jensen score a manager per unit of the risk that matters. Day 54 measured bonds whose cash flows move with rates by repricing them. Day 55 turned default into a number: expected loss = POD × LGD, which a spread roughly pays back each year.

The week’s formulas

Memorize · Days 50–55
  • Day 50. Slope test: t = (b̂1 − B1) / sb̂1, df = n − 2, with sb̂1 = SEE / √Σ(Xi − X̄)2. Intercept (alpha): t = (b̂0 − B0) / sb̂0.
  • Day 50. Correlation test: t = r √(n − 2) / √(1 − r2), df = n − 2: the same number as the slope t for b1 = 0, and F = t2. Beta = Cov(Ri, Rm) / Var(Rm) = the slope of Ri regressed on Rm.
  • Day 51. Forecast Ŷf = b̂0 + b̂1Xf; sf2 = SEE2 × [1 + 1/n + (Xf − X̄)2 / ((n − 1) sX2)]; prediction interval Ŷf ± tc × sf, df = n − 2.
  • Day 51. Log-lin ln Y = b0 + b1X: b1 = continuous growth rate per unit of X; forecast Y = e(b0 + b1X). Lin-log Y = b0 + b1 ln X. Log-log: b1 = elasticity.
  • Day 52. βi = Cov(Ri, Rm) / σm2 = ρim σi / σm. CAPM: E(Ri) = Rf + βi[E(Rm) − Rf]. α = forecast (or actual) − CAPM required return; α > 0 → undervalued.
  • Day 52. APT / multi-factor: E(Rp) = RF + λ1βp,1 + … + λKβp,K. CML: x-axis σ (efficient portfolios); SML: x-axis β (any asset).
  • Day 53. Sharpe = (Rp − Rf) / σp; Treynor = (Rp − Rf) / βp; M2 = (Rp − Rf) × σm/σp − (Rm − Rf); Jensen αp = Rp − [Rf + βp(Rm − Rf)].
  • Day 53. Sortino = (Rp − MAR) / downside deviation; Calmar = annualized return / maximum drawdown.
  • Day 54. EffDur = (PV− − PV+) / (2 × ΔCurve × PV0); EffCon = [PV− + PV+ − 2 × PV0] / [(ΔCurve)2 × PV0]; key rate durations add up to effective duration.
  • Day 54. CPR = 1 − (1 − SMM)12. Negative convexity: the price rises less when rates fall than it falls when rates rise (callables, MBS).
  • Day 55. EL = POD × LGD; LGD = exposure × (1 − recovery rate); spread ≈ POD × LGD (as % of exposure per year).
  • Day 55. %ΔPV ≈ −ModDur × ΔSpread + ½ × Convexity × (ΔSpread)2. Leverage: debt/EBITDA, FFO/debt, debt/capital; coverage: EBITDA/interest.

Worked example: one stock, three verdicts

Problem. A stock with β = 1.15 returned 13.0% while the market returned 10.0% and Rf = 3.0%. Find the CAPM required return, Jensen’s alpha and the Treynor ratio, and compare it with the market’s.

By hand
  1. Market risk premium: 10.0 − 3.0 = 7.0%. Required return = 3 + 1.15 × 7 = 3 + 8.05 = 11.05%.
  2. Jensen’s alpha = 13.0 − 11.05 = +1.95%: the stock plots above the SML.
  3. Treynor = (13 − 3) / 1.15 = 8.6957; the market’s = (10 − 3) / 1 = 7.00. More excess return per unit of beta.
  4. The verdicts agree, and not by luck: α = (Rp − Rf) − β(Rm − Rf) = β × (Treynorp − Treynorm) = 1.15 × 1.6957 = 1.95.
  5. Neither looks at total risk σ. If this stock were your whole portfolio, you would need Sharpe or M².

Answer: β = 1.34 and a CAPM required return of 9.70%. Against 0, t = 33.77; against 1, t = 8.56. Both exceed 3.182, so beta is not 0 and not 1 either: the 95% interval, 1.34 ± 3.182 × 0.0397, runs from about 1.21 to 1.47.

Exam traps
  • β = 1 versus β = 0. “Is beta different from 1?” puts 1 in the numerator: t = (b̂ − 1)/sb. Against 0 the test only asks whether the market moves the stock at all. Above: 33.77 against 0, but 8.56 against 1. Both use n − 2 df.
  • M² subtracts the market’s excess return. Scaling the fund to the market’s σ gives a risk-adjusted excess return; M² is that minus (Rm − Rf). Day 53’s fund: 9 × 15/4 = 33.75%, minus 7 = 26.75%. Stop early, or subtract Rm itself (23.75%), and the answer is wrong.
  • Recovery versus LGD. LGD = 1 − recovery. With 40% recovery the LGD is 60%: a 2% POD costs about 1.20% a year, not 0.80%.
  • Log-lin forecasts come out in logs. A forecast ln Y = 3.0 means Y = e3 = 20.09, not 3.0. Exponentiate before you answer.

2. Day 50 · Correlation test. Two return series have a sample correlation of 0.40 over 27 months. At the 5% level (two-tailed critical t = 2.060 with 25 df), the t-statistic for H0: ρ = 0 and the decision are:

  • Right: 0.40 × √25 / √(1 − 0.16) = 2.00 / 0.9165 = 2.18, above 2.060.

  • Left out the √(1 − r2) in the denominator. Dividing by 0.9165 is what lifts t over the line.

  • Left out √(n − 2). That factor is how sample size enters: 0.40 from 27 months is far stronger evidence than 0.40 from 3.

Explanation

t = r √(n − 2) / √(1 − r2) = 0.40 × 5 / 0.9165 = 2.18 > 2.060: reject ρ = 0. In a simple regression of one series on the other, the slope t for b1 = 0 is this same 2.18.

This test is included as insurance: its place in the 2027 curriculum is uncertain.

3. Day 51 · Prediction interval, on the calculator. A regression on n = 20 observations has SEE = 2.0. Its X values have mean 5 and sample variance sX2 = 4. For Xf = 9, what is the half-width of the 95% prediction interval (tc = 2.101, 18 df)? To two decimals.

units of Y
Hint Half-width = tc × sf, with sf = SEE × √[1 + 1/n + (Xf − X̄)2 / ((n − 1) sX2)].
Solution

(9 − 5)2 = 16; (n − 1)sX2 = 19 × 4 = 76. Bracket: 1 + 0.05 + 16/76 = 1 + 0.05 + 0.2105 = 1.2605. sf = 2.0 × √1.2605 = 2.2455. Half-width = 2.101 × 2.2455 = 4.72.

BA II Plus: 1 + 20 1/x + 16 ÷ 76 = → 1.2605; √x × 2 × 2.101 = → 4.7177.

TI-84 Plus: 2.101 × 2 × 2nd x² (√) 1 + 1 ÷ 20 + 16 ÷ 76 ) ENTER → 4.7177.

Using the SEE alone gives 2.101 × 2.0 = 4.20, too narrow. At Xf = X̄ = 5 the half-width would be 4.31: forecasts far from the data are less certain.

$ millions

5. Day 52 · Multi-factor (APT) return. RF = 3%. Two factors are priced: the market, with risk premium λ1 = 5%, and size, with λ2 = 2%. A portfolio’s sensitivities are β1 = 1.1 and β2 = 0.6. What is its expected return? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint Each premium times its sensitivity, added to the risk-free rate.
Solution

E(R) = 3 + 1.1 × 5 + 0.6 × 2 = 3 + 5.5 + 1.2 = 9.70%. Leaving out RF gives 6.70%.

BA II Plus: 3 + 1.1 × 5 + .6 × 2 = → 9.7000 (AOS). TI-84 Plus: the same keys, ending with ENTER → 9.7000.

CAPM is the one-factor case: only the market, with λ = E(Rm) − Rf.

6. Day 52 · CAPM alpha. A stock has β = 1.4. Rf = 3% and the expected market return is 8%. An analyst forecasts a 9.3% return. Based on CAPM, the stock is:

  • Compared the forecast with the market return (9.3 − 8). A stock with β = 1.4 must beat the market just to be fairly priced.

  • Right: required return = 3 + 1.4 × (8 − 3) = 10.0%, and 9.3 − 10.0 = −0.70%. Below the SML.

  • Multiplied beta by the market return, not the premium: 3 + 1.4 × 8 = 14.2%. The verdict happens to match; the alpha does not.

Explanation

Required 10.0%, forecast 9.3%: the stock offers 0.70% less than its beta demands, so its price is too high. Overvalued, α = −0.70%.

7. Day 53 · M², on the calculator. A fund returned 10% with σ = 18%. The market returned 8% with σ = 15%. Rf = 2%. What is the fund’s M²? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint Scale the fund’s excess return to the market’s risk (× σm/σp), then subtract the market’s excess return.
Solution

Excess returns: fund 10 − 2 = 8, market 8 − 2 = 6. M² = 8 × 15/18 − 6 = 6.6667 − 6 = 0.67%. At the market’s risk the fund would have earned 2 + 6.67 = 8.67%, against the market’s 8%.

BA II Plus: 8 × 15 ÷ 18 − 6 = → 0.6667. TI-84 Plus: ( 10 − 2 ) × 15 ÷ 18 − ( 8 − 2 ) ENTER → .6667.

Forgetting to subtract (Rm − Rf) gives 6.67%. Check through Sharpe: (0.4444 − 0.4000) × 15 = 0.67.

8. Day 54 · Effective duration, on the calculator. A mortgage-backed security is priced at 102.00. A prepayment model reprices it at 103.10 if the benchmark curve falls 25 bp and at 100.70 if it rises 25 bp. What is its effective duration? To two decimals.

years
Hint EffDur = (PV− − PV+) / (2 × ΔCurve × PV0), with ΔCurve as a decimal: 25 bp = 0.0025.
Solution

EffDur = (103.10 − 100.70) / (2 × 0.0025 × 102.00) = 2.40 / 0.51 = 4.71. Forgetting the 2 gives 9.41.

BA II Plus: ( 103.1 − 100.7 ) ÷ ( 2 × .0025 × 102 ) = → 4.7059. TI-84 Plus: the same keys, ending with ENTER → 4.7059.

The fall adds 1.10 while the rise takes away 1.30: negative convexity, from prepayments.

9. Day 55 · Expected loss. You hold $20 million of a bond. Its annual probability of default is 1.5% and the expected recovery rate is 25%. What is the expected loss over the next year? In dollars.

dollars
Hint LGD first: exposure × (1 − recovery). Then multiply by the POD.
Solution

LGD = $20,000,000 × 0.75 = $15,000,000. EL = 0.015 × $15,000,000 = $225,000, or 1.125% of the exposure: roughly the spread this bond needs.

BA II Plus: 20000000 × .015 × ( 1 − .25 ) = → 225,000.0000. TI-84 Plus: the same keys, ending with ENTER → 225000.0000.

Using the recovery rate gives $75,000; ignoring recovery gives $300,000.

10. Day 55 · Spread shock. A bond has modified duration 5 and convexity 40. Its credit spread widens by 200 bp while the benchmark yield is unchanged. The estimated percentage change in its price is closest to:

  • Subtracted the convexity term. (ΔSpread)2 is positive whichever way the spread moves, so for this bond the term is added.

  • Duration only: −5 × 0.02. Convexity was ignored.

  • Right: −5 × 0.02 + ½ × 40 × 0.022 = −10.00% + 0.80%.

Explanation

Duration term: −5 × 0.02 = −10.00%. Convexity term: ½ × 40 × 0.0004 = +0.80%. Total −9.20%. Square the decimal (0.02), not the 2.
