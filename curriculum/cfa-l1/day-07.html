From Day 6. At 7% a year, ten payments of $1,000, one at the end of each year, are worth $7,023.58 today. If each payment came at the start of its year instead, the stream would be worth closest to:

  • That adds an eleventh payment: $1,000 today on top of all ten year-end ones. An annuity due still has ten payments; each just comes a year sooner.

  • Right. Every payment moves one year earlier, so each is discounted one year less: 7,023.58 × 1.07 = 7,515.23.

  • That divides by 1.07, as if the payments moved a year later. Money that comes sooner is worth more, not less.

Why

Check it the split way: $1,000 today plus an ordinary annuity of the other 9 payments, 1,000 + 1,000 × 6.515232 = 7,515.23. Same answer, because PVdue = PVordinary × (1 + r).

From Day 5. A $30,000 car loan at a 4.8% stated annual rate, compounded monthly, is repaid in 48 equal payments at the end of each month. What is the monthly payment? To the nearest cent.

dollars a month
Hint Monthly numbers: N = 48 and I/Y = 4.8 ÷ 12 = 0.4. PV = 30,000, FV = 0, END mode, then CPT PMT.
Solution

The loan is the PV of the payments, so solve Day 5’s formula for A: A = PV × r / [1 − (1 + r)−N] = 30,000 × 0.004 / (1 − 0.825623) = 120 / 0.174377 = 688.16.

BA II Plus: 2ND CLR TVM 48 N 0.4 I/Y 30000 PV 0 FV CPT PMT → PMT = −688.1642.

TI-84 Plus: TVM Solver N=48, I%=.4, PV=30000, FV=0, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, END; cursor on PMT=, ALPHA ENTER → PMT=−688.1642.

The minus sign: you receive the $30,000 and pay the installments. The 48 payments total $33,031.68, so $3,031.68 of it is interest.

What this week was about

Six days, one equation. Day 1 grew money forward, FVN = PV(1 + r)N, because interest earns interest. Day 2 ran it backwards to a present value and solved the same equation for r or N. Day 3 asked where r comes from: a real rate plus premiums, with real and nominal rates linked by multiplying, not subtracting. Day 4 split the year into m periods: compounding more often raises the effective rate, up to the continuous ceiling ers − 1. Day 5 priced a row of equal payments as N single sums added up, and Day 6 moved the row one period earlier, which multiplies its value by (1 + r).

Memorize · the week on one card
  • Day 1. FVN = PV × (1 + r)N. Simple interest = PV × r × N (no interest on interest).
  • Day 2. PV = FVN / (1 + r)N; DF = 1 / (1 + r)N; r = (FVN / PV)1/N − 1; N = ln(FVN / PV) / ln(1 + r).
  • Day 3. r = real risk-free rate + inflation premium + default risk premium + liquidity premium + maturity premium.
  • Day 3. (1 + nominal rate) = (1 + real rate) × (1 + inflation rate), so real = (1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) − 1. After-tax return = pre-tax return × (1 − tax rate).
  • Day 4. EAR = (1 + rs/m)m − 1; FVN = PV × (1 + rs/m)m × N; back again: rs = m × [(1 + EAR)1/m − 1].
  • Day 4. Continuous: FVN = PV × ers × N; EAR = ers − 1; rcc = ln(1 + EAR).
  • Day 5. PV = A × [1 − (1 + r)−N] / r; FVN = A × [(1 + r)N − 1] / r. Payments at the END of each period, the first at t = 1.
  • Day 6. PVdue = PVordinary × (1 + r), and FVdue = FVordinary × (1 + r); or PVdue = A + PV of an ordinary annuity of N − 1 payments. BGN mode, then back to END.
  • Calculator. P/Y = C/Y = 1; monthly: N = years × 12, rate = annual stated rate / 12. Paid out negative, received positive; solving for I/Y or N needs a sign change.
Exam traps
  • Mixing periodic and annual rates. Monthly or quarterly cash needs the rate per period and N in periods: 6% stated with monthly payments for 5 years is 0.5% and N = 60. The mixed-up versions will be waiting among the options.
  • Forgetting to switch BGN back to END. 2ND CLR TVM does not reset it. Left on, every ordinary annuity after it comes out (1 + r) times too big. Switch back the moment the annuity-due question is done.
  • Exact vs approximate real rate. Nominal − inflation is the shortcut. The exact real rate, (1 + nominal) / (1 + inflation) − 1, is the shortcut divided by (1 + inflation): a little lower when it is positive, a smaller loss when it is negative (Day 3: −16.23%, not −22.08%). When the question says exact, divide; the shortcut will be one of the options.

1. You invest $8,000 at 5.5% a year, compounded annually, for 15 years. What is it worth at the end? To the nearest cent.

dollars
Hint One sum, growing: N = 15, I/Y = 5.5, PV = −8,000, PMT = 0, CPT FV. Or 8,000 × 1.05515.
Solution · Day 1

8,000 × 1.05515 = 8,000 × 2.232476 = 17,859.81. Simple interest would give only 8,000 × (1 + 0.055 × 15) = 14,600.00; the other $3,259.81 is interest on interest.

TI-84 Plus: TVM Solver N=15, I%=5.5, PV=−8000, PMT=0, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, END; cursor on FV=, ALPHA ENTER → FV=17859.8119.

2. An investment bought for $20,000 is worth $35,000 nine years later. What annual compound return did it earn? As a percent, to two decimals.

% a year
Hint The rate is the unknown, so PV and FV need opposite signs: N = 9, PV = −20,000, PMT = 0, FV = 35,000, CPT I/Y. Or (35,000 / 20,000)1/9 − 1.

r = (35,000 / 20,000)1/9 − 1 = 1.751/9 − 1 = 1.064153 − 1 = 6.42%. Not 75% ÷ 9 = 8.33%: that is simple interest, and it ignores each year’s gain earning a return too.

3. At 6% a year, compounded annually, how many years does it take money to triple? To two decimals.

years
Hint Any starting amount works: PV = −1, FV = 3, I/Y = 6, PMT = 0, CPT N. Or N = ln(3) / ln(1.06).
Solution · Day 2

4. A one-year deposit pays 10%, and inflation over the year is 6%. The deposit’s exact real return is closest to:

  • The subtraction shortcut, 10% − 6%. It is only an approximation, and the question asks for the exact rate.

  • That multiplies, 1.10 × 1.06 − 1, treating 10% as the real rate: it is the nominal rate you would need for a 10% real return at 6% inflation.

  • Right: 1.10 ÷ 1.06 − 1 = 3.77%.

Explanation · Day 3

(1 + nominal) = (1 + real) × (1 + inflation), so real = 1.10 / 1.06 − 1 = 1.037736 − 1 = 3.77%. The shortcut is 0.23 points too high: the exact rate is the 4-point gap divided by 1.06, because inflation erodes the interest as well as the principal.

BA II Plus: 1.10 ÷ 1.06 − 1 = → 0.0377, then × 100 = → 3.7736.

TI-84 Plus: 1.10 ÷ 1.06 − 1 ENTER → .0377, then × 100 ENTER → 3.7736.

5. Expected inflation is the same for every year ahead, yet a 10-year US Treasury note yields more than a 1-year Treasury bill. Which component of the required return best explains the gap?

  • Right. The longer the debt, the more its price moves when interest rates change, and investors want extra yield for carrying that risk.

  • US Treasuries are treated as default-free, so neither carries a default risk premium. It cannot explain a gap between them.

  • Both trade in very deep markets and can be sold quickly near fair value. A liquidity premium is for debt that is hard to sell, like a small company’s bonds.

Explanation · Day 3

Required rate = real risk-free rate + inflation premium + default risk premium + liquidity premium + maturity premium. Here the issuer is the same, the inflation outlook is the same, and neither has a default or liquidity premium. The one term that differs is the maturity premium.

8. An investment pays $3,000 at the end of every quarter for 8 years. The stated annual rate is 8%, compounded quarterly. What is it worth today? To the nearest cent.

dollars

9. An office lease costs $1,500 a month for 3 years, each payment due at the start of the month. At a 6% stated annual rate, compounded monthly, what is the lease worth today? To the nearest cent.

dollars
