Warm-up · from earlier lessons

Two quick ones from Days 27 and 12, so they stick. First try counts.

From Day 27. An analyst puts a 20% prior probability on a bond being downgraded this year. Its credit spread jumps before 75% of downgrades, but also for 15% of bonds that are not downgraded. The spread has just jumped. What is the probability of a downgrade now? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint Bayes: the “downgrade and jump” branch, divided by every way a jump can happen.
Solution

P(jump) = 0.75 × 0.20 + 0.15 × 0.80 = 0.15 + 0.12 = 0.27. P(downgrade | jump) = 0.15 / 0.27 = 55.56%.

In 100 bonds: 20 are downgraded, and 15 of them jump. 12 of the other 80 jump too. So 15 of the 27 jumps are real.

BA II Plus: 0.75 × 0.2 = STO 1 → 0.1500; RCL 1 ÷ ( RCL 1 + 0.15 × 0.8 ) = → 0.5556. TI-84 Plus: 0.75 × 0.2 STO→ ALPHA A ENTER → .1500; ALPHA A ÷ ( ALPHA A + 0.15 × 0.8 ) ENTER → .5556.

75% is P(jump | downgrade): the conditional turned around.

From Day 12. A fund returns −20%, +35% and +8% in three successive years. Its compound annual (geometric mean) return is closest to:

  • Right: 0.80 × 1.35 × 1.08 = 1.1664; its cube root is 1.0526; minus 1 gives 5.26%.

  • That is the arithmetic mean, (−20 + 35 + 8) / 3: a typical single year, not the compound rate. Volatility keeps it above the geometric mean.

  • That is the three-year total, 1.1664 − 1: the cube root was never taken.

Why

$100 becomes 80.00, then 108.00, then 116.64. Three years at the unrounded rate, 5.2646%, end at the same 116.64.

BA II Plus: 0.8 × 1.35 × 1.08 = yˣ ( 1 ÷ 3 ) = − 1 = → 0.0526. TI-84 Plus: ( 0.8 × 1.35 × 1.08 ) ^ ( 1 ÷ 3 ) − 1 ENTER → .0526.

What this week was about

This week turned single returns into whole sets of them. Day 22 built indexes: a price-weighted index divides the sum of prices by a divisor that must be reset after every split, while cap and float weights follow what the companies are worth. Days 23 and 24 described a set of returns by where it sits (the median, percentiles at position (n + 1)y/100, trimmed and winsorized means) and how widely it spreads (sample variance over n − 1, the coefficient of variation, downside deviation below a target). Day 25 measured how two series move together, with covariance and its scaled form, correlation. Days 26 and 27 looked ahead: an expected value weights outcomes by their probabilities, the total probability rule averages the scenarios, and Bayes’ formula updates a probability when evidence arrives.

The week’s formulas

Memorize · Days 22–27
  • Day 22. Price-weighted index = Σ Pi / divisor. After a split, choose Dnew so that Σ (post-split prices) / Dnew = the index value just before the split.
  • Day 22. Market-cap weight wi = (Pi × Qi) / Σ (Pj × Qj); float-adjusted uses float shares (shares outstanding − closely held) for Q. Equal weight = 1/N, rebalanced as prices move. Total return = price return + income return.
  • Day 23. The y-th percentile of n sorted observations sits at Ly = (n + 1) × y / 100. Between two values: P = Xbelow + (Ly − whole part) × (Xabove − Xbelow). IQR = Q3 − Q1.
  • Day 23. Trimmed mean: discard the extremes, average the rest. Winsorized mean: replace them with the nearest retained values, then average. A 5% trimmed mean drops 2.5% at each end; a 95% winsorized mean resets 2.5% at each end.
  • Day 24. s2 = Σ (Xi − X̄)2 / (n − 1); σ2 = Σ (Xi − μ)2 / N; MAD = Σ |Xi − X̄| / n; CV = s / X̄.
  • Day 24. Target downside deviation: sTarget = √[ Σ(Xi ≤ B) (Xi − B)2 / (n − 1) ]: sum only the observations at or below B, but divide by n − 1 using all n.
  • Day 25. sXY = Σ (Xi − X̄)(Yi − Ȳ) / (n − 1); rXY = sXY / (sX × sY), so sXY = r × sX × sY. −1 ≤ r ≤ +1, and 0 means no linear relation.
  • Day 25. Spearman rank correlation (2027 coverage uncertain): rs = 1 − 6 Σ di2 / [n (n2 − 1)], di = the gap between observation i’s two ranks.
  • Day 26. E(X) = Σ P(Xi) Xi; σ2(X) = Σ P(Xi) [Xi − E(X)]2; total probability: E(X) = Σ E(X | Si) P(Si) over mutually exclusive, exhaustive scenarios.
  • Day 26. P(AB) = P(A | B) × P(B); P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(AB). Only if P(A | B) = P(A) (independence) is P(AB) = P(A) × P(B).
  • Day 27. Bayes: P(Event | Information) = [P(Information | Event) / P(Information)] × P(Event), where P(I) = P(I | E)P(E) + P(I | not E)P(not E). Prior before the information, posterior after.
Exam traps
  • n versus n − 1. Sample variance, sample covariance and target downside deviation all divide by n − 1, and the downside deviation’s n counts every observation, not only those below the target.
  • Sx versus σx. Both calculators show both. Take Sx for a sample of returns, and pair r with Sx and Sy. Take σx for a probability distribution entered with weights: there is no sample, so no n − 1.
  • P(A | B) versus P(B | A). The chance of the evidence given the event is not the chance of the event given the evidence. Bayes converts one into the other with the prior and P(evidence).

1. Index divisor (Day 22) · calculator. A price-weighted index holds four stocks priced 40, 60, 25 and 155, and its divisor is 2.8. The 155 stock splits 5-for-1. What new divisor keeps the index level unchanged? To four decimals.

divisor
Hint First the index level just before the split, with the old divisor. Then the divisor that gives that same level from the new prices.
Solution

Before: (40 + 60 + 25 + 155) / 2.8 = 280 / 2.8 = 100.0000. After the split the stock trades at 155 / 5 = 31, so the prices add to 40 + 60 + 25 + 31 = 156. New divisor: 156 / 100 = 1.5600.

BA II Plus: 40 + 60 + 25 + 155 = ÷ 2.8 = → 100.0000 STO 1; then 40 + 60 + 25 + 155 ÷ 5 = → 156.0000 (AOS divides first) ÷ RCL 1 = → 1.5600.

TI-84 Plus: ( 40 + 60 + 25 + 155 ) ÷ 2.8 STO→ ALPHA A ENTER → 100.0000; ( 40 + 60 + 25 + 155 ÷ 5 ) ÷ ALPHA A ENTER → 1.5600.

Keep 2.8 by mistake and the index reads 156 / 2.8 = 55.7143: a 44.29% “crash” on a day nothing lost value. BA II Plus: 2ND Δ% 2ND CLR WORK 100 ENTER ↓ 55.7143 ENTER ↓ CPT → %CH = −44.2857. TI-84 Plus: 156 ÷ 2.8 − 100 ENTER → −44.2857 (from a base of 100, points are percent).

2. Float weights (Day 22). An index uses float-adjusted market-cap weights. Stock A: price 50, 300 million shares outstanding, 120 million closely held. Stock B: price 20, 1,000 million shares, all free float. Stock C: price 80, 200 million shares, 50 million closely held. A’s weight in the index is closest to:

  • That is A’s price weight, 50 / (50 + 20 + 80): share prices alone, ignoring how many shares there are.

  • Right: float caps are 50 × 180 = 9,000, 20 × 1,000 = 20,000 and 80 × 150 = 12,000 ($ million), total 41,000; 9,000 / 41,000 = 21.95%.

  • Uses shares outstanding, not float: 15,000 / 51,000. Closely held shares do not trade, so a float-adjusted index leaves them out.

Explanation

Float = shares outstanding − closely held: A 180 million, B 1,000 million, C 150 million. Weight = price × float / total of price × float. A: 9,000 / 41,000 = 21.95% (B 48.78%, C 29.27%).

3. Percentile (Day 23) · calculator. Eleven monthly returns (%): 5.2, −1.4, 3.8, 9.1, 0.6, −3.7, 2.9, 7.5, 1.1, 4.4, −0.2. Using the curriculum’s location formula, what is the 60th percentile? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint Sort first. The location is (n + 1) × y / 100; a location of, say, 4.3 means the 4th value plus 0.3 of the gap to the 5th.
Solution

Sorted: −3.7, −1.4, −0.2, 0.6, 1.1, 2.9, 3.8, 4.4, 5.2, 7.5, 9.1. L60 = (11 + 1) × 60 / 100 = 7.2: 0.2 of the way from the 7th value (3.8) to the 8th (4.4). P60 = 3.8 + 0.2 × (4.4 − 3.8) = 3.8 + 0.12 = 3.92%.

BA II Plus (no sort key: sort by hand): 3.8 + 0.2 × ( 4.4 − 3.8 ) = → 3.9200.

TI-84 Plus: type the returns into L1 (STAT 1:Edit…, clearing old numbers first), then let it sort: 2nd MODE (QUIT), STAT 2:SortA( 2nd 1 ) ENTER → Done. Read rows 7 and 8 in STAT 1:Edit…, then 3.8 + 0.2 × ( 4.4 − 3.8 ) ENTER → 3.9200.

The trap: n × y / 100 = 6.6 gives 2.9 + 0.6 × 0.9 = 3.44%.

4. Winsorized mean (Day 23). Ten annual returns (%), sorted: −12.0, −2.0, 0.5, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 3.5, 4.5, 6.0, 18.0. The 80% winsorized mean, which resets the lowest and the highest value to their nearest neighbours, is closest to:

  • The plain arithmetic mean, 25.0 / 10: nothing was reset.

  • The trimmed mean: −12.0 and 18.0 dropped, 19.0 / 8 = 2.375. Trimming discards the extremes; winsorizing replaces them and keeps all ten.

  • Right: −12.0 becomes −2.0 and 18.0 becomes 6.0; the sum falls from 25.0 to 23.0, and 23.0 / 10 = 2.30%.

Explanation

Winsorize = replace; trim = remove. One value of ten at each end is 10% per end, so this is an 80% winsorized mean (dropping them would be a 20% trimmed mean).

New list: −2.0, −2.0, 0.5, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 3.5, 4.5, 6.0, 6.0. Sum 23.0, mean 2.30%. Shortcut: the sum gains 10.0 at the bottom and loses 12.0 at the top, so 25.0 − 2.0 = 23.0.

5. Sample standard deviation (Day 24) · calculator. Six annual returns (%): 9.0, −3.5, 4.0, 12.5, −1.0, 6.0. What is their sample standard deviation? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint One-variable statistics on the six returns. Two standard deviations appear: take the one that divides by n − 1.
Solution

By hand: mean 27.0 / 6 = 4.50. Deviations 4.5, −8.0, −0.5, 8.0, −5.5, 1.5; squared 20.25, 64.00, 0.25, 64.00, 30.25, 2.25; sum 181.00. Divide by n − 1 = 5: 36.20. √36.20 = 6.02%.

BA II Plus: 2ND DATA 2ND CLR WORK, then 9 ENTER ↓ ↓ 3.5 +|− ENTER ↓ ↓ 4 ENTER ↓ ↓ 12.5 ENTER ↓ ↓ 1 +|− ENTER ↓ ↓ 6 ENTER (each ↓ ↓ steps past Y, the frequency, which stays 1). Then 2ND STAT, 2ND SET until 1-V shows, ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ → n = 6.0000, x̄ = 4.5000, Sx = 6.0166, σx = 5.4924.

TI-84 Plus: STAT 1:Edit…, cursor up onto the L1 title, CLEAR ENTER; then 9 ENTER (−) 3.5 ENTER 4 ENTER 12.5 ENTER (−) 1 ENTER 6 ENTER. 2nd MODE (QUIT), STAT ► (CALC) 1:1-Var Stats 2nd 1 ENTER → x̄=4.5000, Sx=6.0166, σx=5.4924.

σx = 5.49% divides by 6, the population formula: that is the trap.

6. Downside deviation (Day 24) · calculator. The same six returns. What is their target downside deviation for a target return B = 2%? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint Only returns at or below 2% count, measured from 2%, not from the mean. Still divide by n − 1 for all six years.
Solution

At or below 2%: −3.5 and −1.0. Shortfalls from the target: −3.5 − 2 = −5.5 and −1.0 − 2 = −3.0. Squares 30.25 + 9.00 = 39.25. Divide by n − 1 = 5: 7.85. √7.85 = 2.80%.

BA II Plus: ( 5.5 x² + 3 x² ) ÷ 5 = → 7.8500, then √x → 2.8018. TI-84 Plus: 2nd x² (√) ( 5.5 x² + 3 x² ) ÷ 5 ) ENTER → 2.8018.

Type the shortfalls without their minus signs: squaring removes it anyway, and on the TI-84 (−) 5.5 x² gives −30.25. Dividing by the two below-target years instead gives 4.43%; by 6, 2.56%.

7. Covariance (Day 25) · calculator. Five years of returns (%), year by year. Fund X: 15, −8, 4, 22, 9. Fund Y: 3, 5, −1, 6, 2. What is the sample covariance of the two funds’ returns? To two decimals, in %2.

%²
Hint Two-variable statistics give r, Sx and Sy; the covariance is r × Sx × Sy. Store the three instead of retyping them.
Solution

BA II Plus: 2ND DATA 2ND CLR WORK, then 15 ENTER ↓ 3 ENTER ↓ 8 +|− ENTER ↓ 5 ENTER ↓ 4 ENTER ↓ 1 +|− ENTER ↓ 22 ENTER ↓ 6 ENTER ↓ 9 ENTER ↓ 2 ENTER (X01, Y01 … Y05). 2ND STAT, 2ND SET until LIN shows; ↓ ↓ ↓ to Sx = 11.3710, STO 2; ↓ ↓ ↓ to Sy = 2.7386, STO 3; ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ to r = 0.2007, STO 1. Then 2ND QUIT RCL 1 × RCL 2 × RCL 3 = → 6.2500.

TI-84 Plus: in STAT 1:Edit… clear L1 and L2 (cursor onto each title, CLEAR ENTER), then L1 = 15, −8, 4, 22, 9 and L2 = 3, 5, −1, 6, 2. 2nd MODE (QUIT), STAT ► (CALC) 2:2-Var Stats 2nd 1 , 2nd 2 ENTER → Sx=11.3710, Sy=2.7386. Then STAT ► 8:LinReg(a+bx) 2nd 1 , 2nd 2 ENTER → a=2.5940, b=.0483, r²=.0403, r=.2007 (DiagnosticOn, set on Day 25, shows r). Finally VARS 5:Statistics… ► ► (EQ) 7:r × VARS 5:Statistics… 3:Sx × VARS 5:Statistics… 6:Sy ENTER → 6.2500.

By hand, as a check: X̄ = 8.4, Ȳ = 3.0; the products of deviations are 0.0, −32.8, 17.6, 40.8 and −0.6, adding to 25.0; 25.0 / (5 − 1) = 6.25. Retyping the rounded displays gives 6.2499; pairing r with σx and σy gives 5.00, the population covariance (÷ 5).

8. Rank correlation (Day 25). Two analysts rank the same six stocks from 1 (best) to 6. Analyst 1: A 1, B 2, C 3, D 4, E 5, F 6. Analyst 2: A 1, B 3, C 2, D 6, E 4, F 5. The Spearman rank correlation between the two rankings is closest to:

  • The 6 in the numerator was left out: 1 − 8 / 210.

  • Uses n(n − 1) = 30 instead of n(n2 − 1) = 210: 1 − 48 / 30. A negative value for two rankings that mostly agree is the giveaway.

  • Right: Σd2 = 8, and 1 − 6 × 8 / [6 × (36 − 1)] = 1 − 48 / 210 = 0.7714.

Explanation

Rank gaps d (Analyst 1 minus Analyst 2): 0, −1, +1, −2, +1, +1. Squares 0, 1, 1, 4, 1, 1, so Σd2 = 8. n(n2 − 1) = 6 × 35 = 210. rs = 1 − 48 / 210 = 0.7714.

Check: enter the two rank lists as X and Y (BA II Plus LIN, or LinReg on the TI-84) and r is also 0.7714. Spearman is just r on the ranks.

9. Probability-weighted SD (Day 26) · calculator. Next year the market is strong (probability 0.55) or weak (0.45). If strong, a fund returns 18% with probability 0.6 or 8% with probability 0.4. If weak, it returns 2% or −10%, each with probability 0.5. What is the standard deviation of the fund’s return? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint Multiply along each path for four probabilities that add to 1. Enter them as whole-number weights (× 200 makes every one whole) and read σx, not Sx.
Solution

Paths: 0.55 × 0.6 = 0.33 (18%); 0.55 × 0.4 = 0.22 (8%); 0.45 × 0.5 = 0.225 (2%); 0.225 (−10%).

Mean, by total probability: E(R | strong) = 0.6 × 18 + 0.4 × 8 = 14; E(R | weak) = 0.5 × 2 + 0.5 × (−10) = −4; E(R) = 0.55 × 14 + 0.45 × (−4) = 7.70 − 1.80 = 5.90%.

Variance: 0.33 × 12.12 + 0.22 × 2.12 + 0.225 × (−3.9)2 + 0.225 × (−15.9)2 = 48.3153 + 0.9702 + 3.42225 + 56.88225 = 109.59 (%2). σ = √109.59 = 10.47%.

On the calculator, × 100 would give 22.5, not a whole number, so use × 200: weights 66, 44, 45, 45.

BA II Plus: 2ND DATA 2ND CLR WORK, then 18 ENTER ↓ 66 ENTER ↓ 8 ENTER ↓ 44 ENTER ↓ 2 ENTER ↓ 45 ENTER ↓ 10 +|− ENTER ↓ 45 ENTER. 2ND STAT; question 7 left it on LIN, so 2ND SET until 1-V shows; ↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ → n = 200.0000, x̄ = 5.9000, Sx = 10.4948, σx = 10.4685.

TI-84 Plus: clear L1 and L2; L1 = 18, 8, 2, −10 and L2 = 66, 44, 45, 45. 2nd MODE (QUIT), STAT ► (CALC) 1:1-Var Stats 2nd 1 , 2nd 2 ENTER → x̄=5.9000, Sx=10.4948, σx=10.4685.

x̄ = 5.90 confirms the mean. Take σx: the probabilities are the whole distribution, so there is no n − 1.

10. Bayes (Day 27). Only 10% of fund managers are skilled. A skilled manager beats the benchmark three years running with probability 0.60. For an unskilled one each year is a coin flip, so the chance is 0.53 = 0.125. A manager has just beaten the benchmark three years running. The probability that this manager is skilled is closest to:

  • Right: P(three beats) = 0.60 × 0.10 + 0.125 × 0.90 = 0.06 + 0.1125 = 0.1725, and 0.06 / 0.1725 = 34.78%.

  • That is P(three beats | skilled): the conditional turned around.

  • Ignores the 10% prior: 0.60 / (0.60 + 0.125) treats skilled and unskilled managers as equally common.

Explanation

Picture 1,000 managers. 100 are skilled, and 60 of them beat the benchmark three times. 900 are not, and 112.5 of them do it by luck. Of the 172.5 three-time winners, 60 are skilled: 60 / 172.5 = 34.78%. Nearly two streaks in three (65.22%) are luck.

BA II Plus: 0.6 × 0.1 = STO 1; RCL 1 ÷ ( RCL 1 + 0.125 × 0.9 ) = → 0.3478. TI-84 Plus: 0.6 × 0.1 STO→ ALPHA A ENTER; ALPHA A ÷ ( ALPHA A + 0.125 × 0.9 ) ENTER → .3478.
