Warm-up · from earlier lessons

Two quick ones, from Day 41 and Day 15, so they stick. First try counts.

From Day 41. A bond has an annual modified duration of 11.2 and an annual convexity of 160. Its yield falls by 75 basis points. Using duration and convexity, estimate the percentage change in its price. As a percent, to two decimals (a minus sign for a fall).

%
Hint Two terms. A fall makes ΔYield = −0.0075. The convexity term is added whichever way yields move.
Solution

Duration part: −11.2 × (−0.0075) = +0.0840. Convexity part: ½ × 160 × (−0.0075)2 = 0.5 × 160 × 0.00005625 = +0.0045. Total +0.0885 = +8.85%.

BA II Plus: 11.2 × .0075 + .5 × 160 × .0075 x² = → 0.0885. TI-84 Plus: 11.2 × .0075 + .5 × 160 × .0075 x² ENTER → .0885.

The traps: 9.30% forgets the ½; 7.95% subtracts the convexity term; 8.40% is duration alone.

From Day 15. A 15-year bond pays a 5.25% coupon semiannually, and the market yield is 4.6%. Its price per 100 of par is closest to:

  • The yield was not halved: I/Y = 4.6 with N = 30 discounts each half-year at the full annual rate.

  • Right: N = 30, I/Y = 2.3, PMT = 2.625, FV = 100. A premium, because the 5.25% coupon is above the 4.6% yield.

  • The coupon was not halved: PMT = 5.25 every half-year is a 10.5% coupon.

Why

Semiannual: halve the coupon and the yield, double the years. BA II Plus: 2ND CLR TVM 30 N 2.3 I/Y 2.625 PMT 100 FV CPT PV → PV = −106.9873. TI-84 Plus: TVM Solver N=30, I%=2.3, PV=0, PMT=2.625, FV=100, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, PMT: END; cursor on PV=, ALPHA ENTER → PV=−106.9873.

The sneaky one: annual inputs (N = 15, I/Y = 4.6, PMT = 5.25) give 106.93, close enough to look right.

What this week was about

This week traded the single yield for a whole curve, then asked how hard a rate move hits. Days 36 and 37 priced each cash flow at the spot rate for its own date, bootstrapped those spot rates one maturity at a time from par bonds, read forward rates as the links between them, and found the par rate: the coupon that prices a bond at 100 on that curve. Day 38 measured the extra yield a riskier bond pays: over an interpolated government yield (G-spread), over every spot rate (Z-spread), and with the embedded option taken out (OAS). Days 39 to 41 measured rate risk: Macaulay duration is the PV-weighted average time to the cash, modified duration turns it into percent per unit of yield, repricing at ±ΔYield estimates both duration and convexity, money duration turns them into currency, and convexity bends the straight-line estimate back toward the truth.

The week’s formulas

Memorize · Days 36–41
  • Day 36. Price with spot rates: PV = Σ CFt / (1 + Zt)t, each cash flow at its own spot rate. Discount factor: DFt = 1 / (1 + Zt)t.
  • Day 36. Bootstrapping an N-year par bond with coupon c: 100 = c × DF1 + … + c × DFN−1 + (100 + c) × DFN → solve DFN, then ZN = DFN−1/N − 1. The YTM is the single rate that gives the same price: a weighted average of the spot rates.
  • Day 37. Implied forward: (1 + ZA)A × (1 + IFRA,B−A)B−A = (1 + ZB)B; 1y1y = (1 + Z2)2 / (1 + Z1) − 1. Forwards sit above an upward-sloping spot curve, below an inverted one.
  • Day 37. Par rate = (1 − DFN) / (DF1 + DF2 + … + DFN).
  • Day 38. G-spread = YTM − (interpolated) government YTM of the same maturity; I-spread = YTM − swap rate. Interpolation: y = yshort + (T − Tshort) / (Tlong − Tshort) × (ylong − yshort).
  • Day 38. Z-spread: the constant Z with PV = Σ CFt / (1 + zt + Z)t. OAS = Z-spread − option value (callable: OAS < Z-spread). FRN per period: coupon = (MRR + QM)/m × 100, discount rate = (MRR + DM)/m.
  • Day 39. MacDur = Σ t × [CFt / (1 + r)t] / PV; ModDur = MacDur / (1 + r); %ΔPV ≈ −AnnModDur × ΔYield. A zero’s MacDur = its maturity. Semiannual: AnnModDur = MacDur(periods) / (1 + r/2) / 2.
  • Day 39. Duration gap = MacDur − investment horizon. Positive: price risk dominates. Negative: reinvestment risk dominates.
  • Day 40. ApproxModDur = (PV− − PV+) / (2 × ΔYield × PV0); ApproxMacDur = ApproxModDur × (1 + r).
  • Day 40. MoneyDur = AnnModDur × PVfull; ΔPV ≈ −MoneyDur × ΔYield. PVBP = (PV− − PV+) / 2 at ±1 bp. Dp ≈ Σ wi × Di, market-value weights.
  • Day 41. ApproxCon = (PV− + PV+ − 2 × PV0) / [(ΔYield)2 × PV0].
  • Day 41. %ΔPV ≈ −AnnModDur × ΔYield + ½ × AnnConvexity × (ΔYield)2; MoneyCon = AnnConvexity × PVfull. For an option-free bond the convexity term is always positive; longer maturity, lower coupon, lower yield and more dispersed cash flows give more of it.

Worked example: bootstrap, then price

Problem. The 1-year and 2-year par yields are 2.5% and 3.2% (annual coupons). Bootstrap the 2-year spot rate, then price a 2-year bond with a 5% annual coupon at the spot rates, per 100 of par.

By hand (Day 36)
  1. A 1-year par bond is a zero: Z1 = 2.5%, so DF1 = 1/1.025 = 0.975610.
  2. A 2-year par bond pays 3.2 and 103.2 and costs exactly 100: 100 = 3.2 × DF1 + 103.2 × DF2.
  3. 3.2 × 0.975610 = 3.121951, so DF2 = (100 − 3.121951)/103.2 = 96.878049/103.2 = 0.938741.
  4. Z2 = DF2−1/2 − 1 = 0.938741−0.5 − 1 = 3.2113%. A touch above the 3.2% par yield: the first coupon used the lower 1-year rate, so the last payment needs a higher one.
  5. Price = 5 × DF1 + 105 × DF2 = 4.8780 + 98.5678 = 103.4458.
  6. Its YTM at that price is 3.1941%: between the two spot rates, close to the 2-year one, where most of the money sits.

On your calculator: stress-test a long bond

Problem (Days 40–41). A 15-year bond pays a 2% annual coupon and yields 4%. Price it at 4.0%, 3.9% and 4.1%, storing each price. Find its approximate modified duration and approximate convexity (ΔYield = 0.001). Then estimate the percentage price change if yields fall 100 bp, and check it by repricing at 3%.

  1. Price it at 4.0% (PV0) and store the price as a positive number

    BA II Plus 2ND CLR TVM 15 N 4 I/Y 2 PMT 100 FV CPT PV, then +|− STO 1
    TI-84 Plus APPS 1:Finance 1:TVM Solver: N=15, I%=4, PV=0, PMT=2, FV=100, P/Y=1, C/Y=1, PMT: END; cursor on PV=, ALPHA ENTER. Solving there does not set Ans, so store it from the home screen: 2nd MODE (QUIT) CLEAR (−) APPS 1:Finance 4:tvm_PV ( 15 , 4 , 2 , 100 ) STO→ ALPHA A ENTER (the order is N, I%, PMT, FV)

    Display: PV = −77.7632, then 77.7632 (BA II Plus) / PV=−77.7632, then 77.7632 (TI-84)

  2. Yield down 10 bp, to 3.9%: PV−

    BA II Plus 3.9 I/Y CPT PV, then +|− STO 2 (N, PMT and FV stay in memory)
    TI-84 Plus (−) APPS 1:Finance 4:tvm_PV ( 15 , 3.9 , 2 , 100 ) STO→ ALPHA B ENTER (or 2nd ENTER (ENTRY) to edit the last line)

    Display: PV = −78.7266, then 78.7266 (BA II Plus) / 78.7266 (TI-84). Up 0.9634.

  3. Yield up 10 bp, to 4.1%: PV+

    BA II Plus 4.1 I/Y CPT PV, then +|− STO 3
    TI-84 Plus (−) APPS 1:Finance 4:tvm_PV ( 15 , 4.1 , 2 , 100 ) STO→ ALPHA C ENTER

    Display: PV = −76.8138, then 76.8138 (BA II Plus) / 76.8138 (TI-84). Down only 0.9494: the gain beat the loss. That gap is convexity.

  4. ApproxModDur = (PV− − PV+) ÷ (2 × 0.001 × PV0), and store it

    BA II Plus ( RCL 2 − RCL 3 ) ÷ ( 2 × .001 × RCL 1 ) =, then STO 4
    TI-84 Plus ( ALPHA B − ALPHA C ) ÷ ( 2 × .001 × ALPHA A ) ENTER, then STO→ ALPHA D ENTER (D is above x⁻¹)

    Display: 12.2987 (both calculators). The 2 is there because the two prices sit two steps apart.

  5. ApproxCon = (PV− + PV+ − 2 × PV0) ÷ (0.0012 × PV0), and store it

    BA II Plus ( RCL 2 + RCL 3 − 2 × RCL 1 ) ÷ ( .001 x² × RCL 1 ) =, then STO 5
    TI-84 Plus ( ALPHA B + ALPHA C − 2 × ALPHA A ) ÷ ( .001 x² × ALPHA A ) ENTER, then STO→ ALPHA E ENTER (E is above SIN)

    Display: 179.2005 (both calculators). Retype the rounded prices instead and you get 180.0337: the top is a tiny difference of big numbers, so recall, don’t retype.

  6. Estimate for a 100 bp fall, ΔYield = −0.01: −ApproxModDur × (−0.01) + ½ × ApproxCon × (−0.01)2. Minus times minus is plus, and a square is never negative, so both terms are added

    BA II Plus RCL 4 × .01 + .5 × RCL 5 × .01 x² =, then × 100 =
    TI-84 Plus ALPHA D × .01 + .5 × ALPHA E × .01 x² ENTER, then × 100 ENTER

    Display: 0.1319, then 13.1947 (BA II Plus) / .1319, then 13.1947 (TI-84). Duration gives 0.1230 of it (+12.30%), convexity the other 0.0090 (+0.90%).

  7. Check: reprice at 3% and find the true change

    BA II Plus 3 I/Y CPT PV, then +|− ÷ RCL 1 − 1 =, then × 100 =
    TI-84 Plus (−) APPS 1:Finance 4:tvm_PV ( 15 , 3 , 2 , 100 ) ÷ ALPHA A − 1 ENTER, then × 100 ENTER (the subtraction key, not (−), before the 1)

    Display: PV = −88.0621, 0.1324, then 13.2438 (BA II Plus) / .1324, then 13.2438 (TI-84)

Answer: ApproxModDur 12.2987, ApproxCon 179.2005. Duration alone says +12.30%; with convexity, +13.19%; the true change is +13.24%. Duration missed by 0.95 of a point, duration plus convexity by 0.05.

Exam traps
  • The 2 in 2 × ΔYield, with ΔYield as a decimal. PV− and PV+ are two steps apart. Drop the 2 and the walkthrough’s 12.2987 becomes 24.5974; type 10 bp as 0.1 instead of 0.001 and it becomes 0.1230.
  • The ½ in the convexity term, and its sign. It is always +½ × Convexity × (ΔYield)2, whichever way yields move. In the walkthrough, no ½ gives +14.09%; subtracting it “because yields fell” gives +11.40%. The right answer is +13.19%.
  • 2y1y versus 1y2y. The first number is when it starts, the second how long it lasts. 2y1y = (1 + Z3)3 / (1 + Z2)2 − 1. 1y2y = [(1 + Z3)3 / (1 + Z1)]1/2 − 1: a two-year rate needs the square root. On Day 37’s curve (2.5%, 3.0%, 3.4%) they are 4.2047% and 3.8530%.
  • A par yield is not a spot rate. On a rising curve the N-year spot rate sits above the N-year par yield (3.2113% against 3.2% in the worked example). And when spot rates are given, discount each cash flow at its own one, not everything at one rate.

Ten questions, all with new numbers, in the week’s order, graded here. Five are marked for the calculator (1, 2, 4, 7 and 9), so keep it in hand. Each names its day, so a miss tells you which lesson to reopen. Work each before peeking; the first try is what counts.

1. Bootstrapping (Day 36) · calculator. The 1-year par yield is 3.0% and the 2-year par yield is 3.5% (annual coupons). What is the 2-year spot rate? As a percent, to three decimals.

%
Hint The 1-year spot rate is the 1-year par yield. A 2-year par bond pays 3.5 and 103.5 and is worth exactly 100: solve 100 = 3.5 × DF1 + 103.5 × DF2 for DF2, then Z2 = DF2−1/2 − 1.
Solution

DF1 = 1/1.03 = 0.970874. DF2 = (100 − 3.5 × 0.970874)/103.5 = 96.6019/103.5 = 0.933352. Z2 = 0.933352−0.5 − 1 = 3.5088%: 3.509%.

BA II Plus: 1.03 1/x STO 1 → 0.9709; 100 − 3.5 × RCL 1 = → 96.6019; ÷ 103.5 = → 0.9334, STO 2; yˣ .5 +|− = → 1.0351; − 1 = × 100 = → 3.5088.

TI-84 Plus: 1 ÷ 1.03 STO→ ALPHA A ENTER → .9709; ( 100 − 3.5 × ALPHA A ) ÷ 103.5 STO→ ALPHA B ENTER → .9334; ( ALPHA B ^ ( (−) .5 ) − 1 ) × 100 ENTER → 3.5088.

Check: 3.5/1.03 + 103.5/1.0350882 = 100.0000. The trap is 3.5, the par yield itself: the spot rate sits a little above it, because the first coupon was discounted at the lower 1-year rate.

2. Pricing with spot rates (Day 36) · calculator. The 1-, 2- and 3-year spot rates are 3.0%, 3.6% and 4.1%. What is the price per 100 of par of a 3-year bond paying a 4% annual coupon? To the nearest cent.

per 100 of par
Hint Each payment at its own spot rate: 4 at 3.0% for one year, 4 at 3.6% for two years, 104 at 4.1% for three.
Solution

4/1.03 = 3.8835; 4/1.0362 = 3.7268; 104/1.0413 = 92.1894. Total 99.7998: 99.80.

BA II Plus: 4 ÷ 1.03 + 4 ÷ 1.036 x² + 104 ÷ 1.041 yˣ 3 = → 99.7998 (AOS does the powers and divisions before adding).

TI-84 Plus: 4 ÷ 1.03 + 4 ÷ 1.036 x² + 104 ÷ 1.041 ^ 3 ENTER → 99.7998.

The trap: all three payments at the 3-year rate, 4.1%, gives 99.7230. The bond’s YTM at 99.7998 is 4.0723%: a blend of the three spot rates, pulled toward 4.1% because most of the money comes in year 3.

3. Forward rates (Day 37). The same spot curve: 3.0%, 3.6% and 4.1% for 1, 2 and 3 years. The 2y1y implied forward rate is closest to:

  • Right: 1.0413 / 1.0362 − 1 = 1.128112 / 1.073296 − 1 = 5.1073%.

  • That is the 1y2y, two years starting in one: (1.0413 / 1.03)1/2 − 1. The 2y1y is one year starting in two.

  • The plain average of the 2- and 3-year spot rates. A forward links two spot rates; it is not their average, and on a rising curve it sits above both.

Explanation

Two roads to year 3 must end at the same place: 1.0362 × (1 + 2y1y) = 1.0413. So 1 + 2y1y = 1.128112 / 1.073296 = 1.051073, and 2y1y = 5.1073%.

BA II Plus: 1.041 yˣ 3 ÷ 1.036 x² − 1 = → 0.0511. TI-84 Plus: 1.041 ^ 3 ÷ 1.036 x² − 1 ENTER → .0511.

A quick sanity check: 3 × 4.1 − 2 × 3.6 = 5.1. Three years at 4.1 minus two years at 3.6 leaves about 5.1 for the third year.

4. Par rate (Day 37) · calculator. The same spot curve. What coupon rate would price a 3-year annual-coupon bond at exactly par? As a percent, to three decimals.

%
Hint Par rate = (1 − DF3) / (DF1 + DF2 + DF3), with DFt = 1/(1 + Zt)t. Store the three discount factors.
Solution

DF1 = 1/1.03 = 0.970874; DF2 = 1/1.0362 = 0.931709; DF3 = 1/1.0413 = 0.886437. Sum 2.789020. Par rate = (1 − 0.886437)/2.789020 = 0.113563/2.789020 = 4.0718%: 4.072%.

BA II Plus: 1.03 1/x STO 1 → 0.9709; 1.036 x² 1/x STO 2 → 0.9317; 1.041 yˣ 3 = 1/x STO 3 → 0.8864; ( 1 − RCL 3 ) ÷ ( RCL 1 + RCL 2 + RCL 3 ) × 100 = → 4.0718.

TI-84 Plus: 1 ÷ 1.03 STO→ ALPHA A ENTER → .9709; 1 ÷ 1.036 x² STO→ ALPHA B ENTER → .9317; 1 ÷ 1.041 ^ 3 STO→ ALPHA C ENTER → .8864; ( 1 − ALPHA C ) ÷ ( ALPHA A + ALPHA B + ALPHA C ) × 100 ENTER → 4.0718.

Check: a 3-year bond with a 4.0718% coupon, priced with these discount factors, is worth 100.0000. It sits below the 4.1% 3-year spot rate because its early coupons are discounted at lower rates.

5. G-spread (Day 38). A 7-year corporate bond yields 5.40%. Government bonds yield 3.80% at 5 years and 4.30% at 10 years. What is the corporate bond’s G-spread? In basis points, to the nearest basis point.

bp
Hint There is no 7-year government bond, so interpolate: 7 years is 2/5 of the way from 5 to 10.
Solution

Interpolated 7-year government yield: 3.80 + (7 − 5)/(10 − 5) × (4.30 − 3.80) = 3.80 + 0.4 × 0.50 = 4.00%. G-spread = 5.40 − 4.00 = 1.40 percentage points = 140 bp.

BA II Plus: 5.4 − ( 3.8 + 2 ÷ 5 × ( 4.3 − 3.8 ) ) = → 1.4000. TI-84 Plus: the same keys, ENTER instead of = → 1.4000. Times 100 for basis points.

The traps: 160 bp against the 5-year (an unmatched maturity), 110 bp against the 10-year, 135 bp against the plain average of the two.

6. Option-adjusted spread (Day 38). A callable corporate bond has a G-spread of 140 bp and a Z-spread of 150 bp. Its embedded call option is worth 35 bp. Its OAS is closest to:

  • The option value was added. A call belongs to the issuer and works against the holder, so it comes out of the spread; adding is the putable-bond direction.

  • Right: OAS = Z-spread − option value = 150 − 35 = 115 bp.

  • Subtracted from the G-spread. The OAS is built on the spot curve, so it starts from the Z-spread.

Explanation

The 150 bp Z-spread pays the holder for credit risk, liquidity and the call he has sold to the issuer. Take out the call’s 35 bp and 115 bp is left for credit and liquidity. For a callable bond, OAS < Z-spread.

7. Macaulay duration (Day 39) · calculator. A 4-year bond pays a 3% annual coupon and yields 4.5%. What is its Macaulay duration? In years, to three decimals.

years
Hint Two NPVs at 4.5%: of the cash flows 3, 3, 3, 103 (the price) and of each cash flow times its year, 3, 6, 9, 412 (Σ t × PV). MacDur is the second over the first.
Solution

Price = 94.6187. Σ t × PV = 361.7391. MacDur = 361.7391 / 94.6187 = 3.8231: 3.823 years. The last payment carries 91% of the value, so the average sits close to 4.

BA II Plus: CF 2ND CLR WORK ↓ 3 ENTER ↓ ↓ 3 ENTER ↓ ↓ 3 ENTER ↓ ↓ 103 ENTER NPV 4.5 ENTER ↓ CPT → NPV = 94.6187, STO 1; CF ↓ 3 ENTER ↓ ↓ 6 ENTER ↓ ↓ 9 ENTER ↓ ↓ 412 ENTER NPV ↓ CPT → NPV = 361.7391, STO 2; 2ND QUIT RCL 2 ÷ RCL 1 = → 3.8231.

TI-84 Plus: APPS 1:Finance 7:npv( 4.5 , 0 , 2nd ( 3 , 3 , 3 , 103 2nd ) ) ENTER → 94.6187, STO→ ALPHA A ENTER; APPS 1:Finance 7:npv( 4.5 , 0 , 2nd ( 3 , 6 , 9 , 412 2nd ) ) ENTER → 361.7391, STO→ ALPHA B ENTER; ALPHA B ÷ ALPHA A ENTER → 3.8231.

The traps: 3.658 is the modified duration (÷ 1.045), not what was asked. 3.839 weights the years by the cash flows instead of their present values.

8. Duration gap (Day 39). An investor with a 4-year horizon buys a bond whose Macaulay duration is 6.4 years. Right after she buys it, yields rise and stay higher. Which statement is correct?

  • That is what a negative gap (duration shorter than the horizon) would mean. Here the duration is longer, so the sign is reversed.

  • She holds it for 4 years, not to maturity: at year 4 she must sell, at whatever price higher yields leave.

  • Right: gap = 6.4 − 4 = +2.4 years. The fall in the price she sells at outweighs the extra she earns reinvesting coupons.

Explanation

Higher yields do two things: her coupons are reinvested at more (good), and the bond she sells at year 4 is worth less (bad). Macaulay duration is the horizon at which the two roughly cancel. Her horizon is 2.4 years shorter, so the price effect wins.

9. Duration plus convexity (Days 40–41) · calculator. An 8-year bond pays a 3.5% annual coupon and yields 4.2%. Reprice it at 4.15% and 4.25%, then use approximate duration and convexity to estimate the percentage price change if yields rise 100 bp. As a percent, to two decimals (a minus sign for a fall).

%
Hint The walkthrough’s keys with N = 8, PMT = 3.5, yields 4.20, 4.15, 4.25 and ΔYield = 0.0005 (5 bp). Store each price. This time yields rise, so the duration term is negative; the convexity term is still added.

10. Portfolio duration (Day 40). A portfolio holds £6 million (market value) of a bond with modified duration 4.0 and £4 million of a bond with modified duration 9.5. If all yields rise by 50 basis points, about how much does the portfolio lose? In pounds, to the nearest thousand.

pounds
Hint Market-value weights, 6/10 and 4/10. Then money duration × ΔYield, with 50 bp = 0.005.
Solution

Dp = 0.6 × 4.0 + 0.4 × 9.5 = 2.4 + 3.8 = 6.2. Money duration = 6.2 × £10,000,000 = £62,000,000. Loss ≈ £62,000,000 × 0.005 = £310,000.

Bond by bond, as a check: 4.0 × £6,000,000 × 0.005 = £120,000 and 9.5 × £4,000,000 × 0.005 = £190,000; together £310,000.

BA II Plus: ( .6 × 4 + .4 × 9.5 ) × 10000000 × .005 = → 310,000.0000. TI-84 Plus: the same keys with ENTER → 310000.0000.

The traps: equal weights give a duration of 6.75 and a loss of £337,500; typing 50 bp as 0.05 gives £3,100,000.
