From Day 48. A regression on 27 monthly returns has a total sum of squares SST = 0.0900 and a regression sum of squares SSR = 0.0360. What is the standard error of the estimate? To four decimals.

Hint The SEE is built from the part the line misses, SSE = SST − SSR. Divide it by n − 2, then take the square root.
Solution

SSE = 0.0900 − 0.0360 = 0.0540. MSE = 0.0540 / (27 − 2) = 0.0540 / 25 = 0.00216. SEE = √0.00216 = 0.0465. (R2 = 0.0360 / 0.0900 = 0.40.)

BA II Plus: ( .09 − .036 ) ÷ 25 = → 0.0022, then √x → 0.0465. TI-84 Plus: 2nd x² (√) ( .09 − .036 ) ÷ 25 ) ENTER → .0465.

Dividing by n − 1 = 26 gives 0.0456; using SSR instead of SSE gives 0.0379.

From Day 26. Next year the economy expands (probability 0.60) or contracts (0.40). In an expansion a company earns EPS of 3.20 with probability 0.75, or 2.40 with probability 0.25. In a contraction it earns 1.50. Its expected EPS is closest to:

  • That is 0.75 × 3.20 + 0.25 × 2.40 + 0.40 × 1.50: the 0.75 and 0.25 were never multiplied by 0.60, so the weights add to 1.40, not 1.

  • Right: E(EPS | expansion) = 0.75 × 3.20 + 0.25 × 2.40 = 3.00; then 0.60 × 3.00 + 0.40 × 1.50 = 1.80 + 0.60 = 2.40.

  • That averages 3.20 and 2.40 equally (2.80) inside the expansion. The 0.75 and 0.25 matter: 0.60 × 2.80 + 0.40 × 1.50 = 2.28.

Why

Total probability rule: E(X) = E(X | S1)P(S1) + E(X | S2)P(S2). Or multiply along each branch: the joint probabilities 0.45, 0.15 and 0.40 add to 1, and 0.45 × 3.20 + 0.15 × 2.40 + 0.40 × 1.50 = 2.40.

What this week was about

One question ran through all six days: is what the sample shows real, or could chance alone have produced it? Day 43 measured how far a sample mean wanders from sample to sample, its standard error s/√n, and built a confidence interval a few standard errors either side. Day 44 turned the same arithmetic into a test: count the standard errors between the sample and the null, compare with a critical value or read the p-value, and accept a known risk α of a false alarm. Day 45 compared two means: pooled for independent samples, as differences for matched pairs. Day 46 tested spreads: χ2 for one variance against a stated value, F for two. Days 47 and 48 fitted a line by least squares and asked how much it explains: R2, the standard error of the estimate and the F-test of the slope.

Memorize · Days 43–48
  • Day 43. Standard error of the mean: sX̄ = s / √n (σ / √n if σ is known). Central limit theorem: for n ≥ 30, X̄ is about normal with mean μ and variance σ2/n, whatever the population’s shape.
  • Day 43. Confidence interval: X̄ ± reliability factor × standard error. z factors: 90% 1.645; 95% 1.96; 99% 2.58. σ unknown: t with df = n − 1.
  • Day 44. t = (X̄ − μ0) / (s / √n), df = n − 1. Reject H0 if |t| > the critical value, or if p-value < α. Otherwise “fail to reject”, never “accept”.
  • Day 44. Type I: reject a true H0 (probability α). Type II: fail to reject a false H0 (probability β). Power = 1 − β.
  • Day 45. Pooled (independent samples, equal variances): sp2 = [(n1 − 1)s12 + (n2 − 1)s22] / (n1 + n2 − 2); t = (X̄1 − X̄2) / √(sp2/n1 + sp2/n2), df = n1 + n2 − 2.
  • Day 45. Paired (same months, same firms): t = d̄ / (sd / √n), df = n − 1, where n counts pairs.
  • Day 46. One variance: χ2 = (n − 1)s2 / σ02, df = n − 1. Two variances: F = s12 / s22, larger variance on top, df = (n1 − 1, n2 − 1).
  • Day 46. Non-parametric tests: for ranks, or when normality fails (small samples, outliers). Contingency table (2027 coverage uncertain): Eij = row total × column total / overall total; χ2 = Σ(O − E)2 / E, df = (r − 1)(c − 1).
  • Day 47. b̂1 = Cov(X, Y) / Var(X) = r × sY / sX; b̂0 = Ȳ − b̂1X̄; residual e = Y − Ŷ. BA II Plus LIN: a = intercept, b = slope. Assumptions: linearity, homoskedasticity, independent errors, normal errors.
  • Day 48. SST = SSR + SSE; R2 = SSR / SST (= r2); MSE = SSE / (n − 2); SEE = √MSE; F = (SSR / 1) / [SSE / (n − 2)], df = 1 and n − 2.

Worked example: one interval, one test

Problem. 36 monthly returns have a mean of 0.7% and s = 1.8%. Build the 95% confidence interval (t0.025,35 = 2.030) and use it to test H0: μ = 0 at 5%.

By hand
  1. Standard error: 1.8 / √36 = 1.8 / 6 = 0.30%.
  2. Half-width: 2.030 × 0.30 = 0.609. Interval: 0.7 ± 0.609 = 0.091% to 1.309%.
  3. 0 lies outside the interval, so a two-tailed 5% test rejects H0: μ = 0.
  4. The Day 44 way: t = (0.7 − 0) / 0.30 = 2.3333 > 2.030. Same verdict.
  5. They always agree: the interval is exactly the set of μ0 values whose t would land inside ±2.030.
Exam traps
  • n − 1 versus n − 2. Each estimate fed back into the formula costs one degree of freedom. A mean, a paired test and χ2: n − 1. Pooled: n1 + n2 − 2. Regression residuals: n − 2 (two coefficients). In question 10, n − 1 gives F = 21.00 instead of 20.57.
  • One tail or two. “Different from” is two-tailed: α/2 in each tail. “Greater than” puts all of α in one. With 7 df at 5%, that is 2.365 versus 1.895: the wrong table column moves the verdict.
  • F: the larger variance on top, and variances, not SDs. Then F ≥ 1 and only the upper-tail table is needed, with the top sample’s df first. In question 7 the SDs give 1.50 instead of 2.25.

1. Confidence interval (Day 43) · calculator. A fund has 20 monthly returns with a mean of 1.1% and a sample standard deviation of 3.2%. The t-table gives t0.025,19 = 2.093. What is the upper limit of the 95% confidence interval for the mean monthly return? As a percent, to two decimals.

%
Hint Standard error first, s / √n. Then add 2.093 standard errors to the mean.
Solution

SE = 3.2 / √20 = 3.2 / 4.4721 = 0.7155. Half-width: 2.093 × 0.7155 = 1.4976. Upper limit: 1.1 + 1.4976 = 2.60% (lower limit −0.40%).

BA II Plus: 3.2 ÷ 20 √x = STO 1 → 0.7155; 1.1 + 2.093 × RCL 1 = → 2.5976.

TI-84 Plus: 1.1 + 2.093 × 3.2 ÷ 2nd x² (√) 20 ) ENTER → 2.5976. Check: STAT ► ► (TESTS) 8:TInterval…, Inpt: Stats, x̄: 1.1, Sx: 3.2, n: 20, C-Level: .95, Calculate ENTER → (−.3976,2.5976).

The z value 1.96 gives 2.50%; dividing s by n instead of √n gives 1.43%.

2. Reliability factor (Day 43). An analyst builds a 90% confidence interval for a population mean from 12 observations. The population variance is unknown. The reliability factor is closest to:

  • Right: σ unknown, so t; df = 12 − 1 = 11; 90% leaves 5% in each tail. t0.05,11 = 1.796.

  • That is the z value for 90%. With σ unknown and only 12 observations, s is itself uncertain, and t’s fatter tails pay for that.

  • That is t with 12 df. Estimating the mean used one degree of freedom: df = n − 1 = 11.

Explanation

Three choices, three rules: t or z (is σ known?), the df (n − 1), the tail area ((100% − 90%) / 2 = 5%). TI-84 Plus: 2nd VARS 4:invT( .95 , 11 ) ENTER → 1.7959. On the BA II Plus it comes from the t-table.

3. Testing a mean (Day 44). Over 64 months a manager’s mean monthly excess return is 0.45%, with a sample standard deviation of 1.6%. What is the t-statistic for H0: μ = 0? To two decimals.

Hint t = (X̄ − μ0) / (s / √n), and √64 = 8.
Solution

SE = 1.6 / 8 = 0.20. t = (0.45 − 0) / 0.20 = 2.25, with 63 df.

Verdict: at 5% two-tailed the critical value is about 2.00 (invT gives 1.9983), so reject. At 1% it is about 2.66 (2.6561), so fail to reject. The TI-84 two-tailed p-value, 2 × tcdf(2.25,1E99,63), is 0.0280: between 1% and 5%, as the two verdicts say.

BA II Plus: .45 ÷ ( 1.6 ÷ 64 √x ) = → 2.2500. TI-84 Plus: .45 ÷ ( 1.6 ÷ 2nd x² (√) 64 ) ) ENTER → 2.2500.

4. Type I and Type II (Day 44). The null hypothesis is “the manager has no skill.” Concluding that she has skill when in fact she has none is:

  • A Type II error is the opposite miss: failing to reject H0 when it is false, here overlooking a manager who really is skilled.

  • Right: H0 (no skill) is true and you rejected it. That false alarm is exactly the risk α you choose.

  • Power, 1 − β, is the chance of detecting a real effect. Low power causes Type II errors, missed skill, not false claims of it.

Explanation

Ask two things: what is really true, and what did you decide? True H0 + rejected = Type I (probability α). False H0 + not rejected = Type II (probability β). A smaller α cuts false alarms but, for a given sample, raises β; only more data lowers both.

5. Paired test (Day 45) · calculator. Two portfolios are tracked over the same six months. Their monthly return differences (A − B, %) are 0.8, −0.2, 1.1, 0.5, 0.9, 0.3. What is the t-statistic for H0: μd = 0? To two decimals.

Hint Same months, so paired: one-variable statistics on the six differences, then d̄ / (sd / √6). It is the walkthrough with new data.
Solution

d̄ = 3.4 / 6 = 0.5667; sd = 0.4719; SE = 0.4719 / √6 = 0.1926; t = 0.5667 / 0.1926 = 2.94, with 6 − 1 = 5 df. 2.94 > 2.571, the 5% critical value: reject. A’s edge over B is more than chance (TI-84 p = 0.0322).

BA II Plus: 2ND DATA 2ND CLR WORK, then .8 ENTER ↓ ↓ .2 +|− ENTER ↓ ↓ 1.1 ENTER ↓ ↓ .5 ENTER ↓ ↓ .9 ENTER ↓ ↓ .3 ENTER; 2ND STAT, 2ND SET until 1-V shows; ↓ ↓ (x̄ = 0.5667) STO 1 ↓ (Sx = 0.4719) STO 2; 2ND QUIT RCL 1 ÷ ( RCL 2 ÷ 6 √x ) = → 2.9415.

TI-84 Plus: clear L1, then .8 ENTER (−) .2 ENTER 1.1 ENTER .5 ENTER .9 ENTER .3 ENTER; STAT ► ► (TESTS) 2:T-Test…, Inpt: Data, μ0: 0, List: L1, Freq: 1, μ: ≠μ0, Calculate ENTER → t=2.9415, p=.0322.

σx (0.4308) in place of Sx gives 3.22: the divide-by-n trap.

6. Pooled test (Day 45) · calculator. Fund X: 15 months, mean 1.4%, s = 2.2%. Fund Y: 12 other months, mean 0.6%, s = 1.8%. The samples are independent and the population variances are assumed equal. What is the t-statistic for H0: μX = μY? To two decimals.

Hint Pool first: weights n − 1 = 14 and 11, divided by 15 + 12 − 2 = 25. Then divide the 0.8 gap by √(sp2/15 + sp2/12).
Solution

sp2 = (14 × 2.22 + 11 × 1.82) / 25 = (67.76 + 35.64) / 25 = 4.1360. SE = √(4.1360/15 + 4.1360/12) = √0.6204 = 0.7877. t = (1.4 − 0.6) / 0.7877 = 1.02, with 25 df. Below 2.060: fail to reject (TI-84 p = 0.3195).

BA II Plus: ( 14 × 2.2 x² + 11 × 1.8 x² ) ÷ 25 = STO 1 → 4.1360; ( 1.4 − .6 ) ÷ ( RCL 1 ÷ 15 + RCL 1 ÷ 12 ) √x = → 0.6204, 0.7877 on the way, then 1.0157.

TI-84 Plus: STAT ► ► (TESTS) 4:2-SampTTest…, Inpt: Stats, x̄1: 1.4, Sx1: 2.2, n1: 15, x̄2: .6, Sx2: 1.8, n2: 12, μ1: ≠μ2, Pooled: Yes, Calculate ENTER → t=1.0157, p=.3195, Sxp=2.0337 (the root of 4.1360).

7. F-test (Day 46). Fund 1: s = 3.0% from 21 months. Fund 2: s = 2.0% from 16 months. At 5%, one-tailed, you test whether fund 1’s variance is larger. The F-table gives F(20, 15) = 2.33 and F(15, 20) = 2.20. Which is correct?

  • 1.50 is 3.0 / 2.0, a ratio of standard deviations. F compares variances: square both.

  • Right statistic, wrong table cell: 2.20 is F(15, 20), with the df reversed. The numerator df belongs to the top sample, n1 − 1 = 20.

  • Right: 3.02 / 2.02 = 9 / 4 = 2.25, with df (20, 15). 2.25 < 2.33.

Explanation

Larger variance on top, its df first: F(20, 15) = 2.33. 2.25 falls just short, so the evidence is not strong enough at 5%. TI-84: Fcdf(2.25,1E99,20,15) = 0.0570, just above 0.05.

8. Chi-square test (Day 46). 31 monthly returns have a sample standard deviation of 2.4%. Test H0: σ2 ≤ (2.0%)2 against Ha: σ2 > (2.0%)2. What is the χ2 statistic? To two decimals.

Hint (n − 1)s2 / σ02: square both standard deviations.
Solution

χ2 = 30 × 2.42 / 2.02 = 30 × 5.76 / 4 = 43.20, with 30 df. The 5% upper critical value is 43.773, so fail to reject, narrowly (TI-84 p = 0.0563).

BA II Plus: 30 × 2.4 x² ÷ 2 x² = → 43.2000. TI-84 Plus: the same keys, ENTER for = → 43.2000; then 2nd VARS 8:χ²cdf( 2nd (−) (ANS) , 1 2nd , (EE) 99 , 30 ) ENTER → .0563.

Standard deviations instead of variances give 36.00; n instead of n − 1 gives 44.64, which would wrongly reject.

9. Least-squares slope (Day 47) · calculator. Five paired observations: X = 1.0, 2.5, −0.5, 3.0, 1.5 and Y = 2.0, 3.1, 0.2, 4.4, 2.3. What is the slope of the least-squares line of Y on X? To four decimals.

Hint Two-variable statistics: LIN on the BA II Plus, LinReg(a+bx) on the TI-84. On the BA, b is the slope and a the intercept.
Solution

BA II Plus: 2ND DATA 2ND CLR WORK, then 1 ENTER ↓ 2 ENTER ↓ 2.5 ENTER ↓ 3.1 ENTER ↓ .5 +|− ENTER ↓ .2 ENTER ↓ 3 ENTER ↓ 4.4 ENTER ↓ 1.5 ENTER ↓ 2.3 ENTER (X01, Y01 … Y05). 2ND STAT, 2ND SET until LIN shows; ↓ eight times to a = 0.7400, once more to b = 1.1067, once more to r = 0.9833.

TI-84 Plus: clear L1 and L2; L1 = 1, 2.5, −.5, 3, 1.5 and L2 = 2, 3.1, .2, 4.4, 2.3. 2nd MODE (QUIT), STAT ► (CALC) 8:LinReg(a+bx) 2nd 1 , 2nd 2 ENTER → a=.7400, b=1.1067, r²=.9669, r=.9833.

By hand: X̄ = 1.5, Ȳ = 2.4. The products of deviations, 0.20, 0.70, 4.40, 3.00 and 0.00, add to 8.30; the squared X deviations, 0.25, 1.00, 4.00, 2.25 and 0.00, add to 7.50. Slope = 8.30 / 7.50 = 1.1067; intercept = 2.4 − 1.1067 × 1.5 = 0.74. Entering X and Y swapped gives 0.8737.

10. Regression F (Day 48). A simple linear regression on 50 observations has a total sum of squares of 0.0800 and a sum of squared errors of 0.0560. What is the F-statistic for the slope? To two decimals.

Hint SSR = SST − SSE. Then MSR = SSR / 1 and MSE = SSE / (n − 2). Store the MSE rather than retyping a rounded display.
Solution

SSR = 0.0800 − 0.0560 = 0.0240. MSE = 0.0560 / 48 = 0.001167. F = 0.0240 / 0.001167 = 20.57, df (1, 48). Shortcut: (SSR / SSE) × (n − 2) = (0.024 / 0.056) × 48. The 5% critical F(1, 48) is about 4.04, so reject: the slope explains something (p below 0.001). Also R2 = 0.30 and SEE = 0.0342.

BA II Plus: .056 ÷ 48 = STO 1 (shows 0.0012); ( .08 − .056 ) ÷ RCL 1 = → 20.5714.

TI-84 Plus: .056 ÷ 48 STO→ ALPHA A ENTER (shows .0012); ( .08 − .056 ) ÷ ALPHA A ENTER → 20.5714.

Dividing SSE by n − 1 = 49 gives 21.00; putting SST on top gives 68.57.
