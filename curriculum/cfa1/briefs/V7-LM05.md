## Learning outcomes
1. Explain how a forward contract's price and value are set at initiation, during its life and at expiry. Taught in Pricing versus Valuation, pp.106–117 (PDF 114–125).
2. Explain how forward rates are determined for interest rate forward contracts and what they are used for. Taught in Interest Rate Forward Contracts, pp.117–128 (PDF 125–136).

## Core principles
- **Price is fixed, value moves.** The forward price is a number agreed at inception; the value is what the contract is worth if closed out now. The price is set so the contract is worth zero to both sides, which is why no cash changes hands at the start (p.106). Afterwards only value moves, zero-sum. Futures settle daily through a clearinghouse (p.106).
- **At expiry, value is the settlement.** Long gains spot minus forward price; short gets the opposite (p.108).
- **During life, long value = spot minus PV of the forward price** (discounted at the current rate for the remaining time) (p.110). A spot rise helps the long one-for-one; a rate rise lowers the PV of the forward price and hurts the long. With spot unchanged, time pushes the PV of the forward price upward toward the forward price, so the long's MTM drifts down.
- **Carry adjusts the spot first.** Income on the asset (dividends, coupons) lowers the forward price and the long's value; costs (storage) raise them. Only the income and costs still to come are present-valued at time t (pp.112–113).
- **FX forwards follow the rate differential.** The currency with the lower interest rate trades at a forward premium; continuous compounding is used (p.114). A higher price-currency rate or lower base-currency rate raises the forward and cuts the PV of an existing contract's forward price, so a long of the base currency gains in MTM.
- **Term structure needs zero rates.** One flat r no longer works when rates differ by maturity. Coupon-bond yields are converted to zero (spot) rates by bootstrapping, then to discount factors (pp.118–119, PDF 126–127).
- **Implied forward rate (IFR) is the breakeven reinvestment rate.** Rolling a short zero and then reinvesting at the IFR must match holding the longer zero (pp.120–122, PDF 128–130). If the actual future rate ends up above the IFR, the roll beats the long zero; below, it loses.
- **An FRA locks in a rate for one future period.** Its fixed rate is the IFR, so its value at inception is zero. The fixed-rate payer is long and gains if the reference rate (MRR) sets higher. Only the net interest is paid, on a notional never exchanged, and the payment is discounted to the start of the period because the rate is known then (pp.124–126). A borrower exposed to a rising MRR hedges as fixed-rate payer. The FRA is a one-period swap and a building block for swaps (p.126).

## Vocabulary
- Forward price F0(T) — price fixed at t=0 for exchange at T (p.106, PDF 114).
- Value / mark-to-market (MTM), V_t(T) — what the contract is worth if settled now (p.106).
- Cost of carry — net cost of holding the asset: costs minus benefits (p.112, PDF 120).
- Forward premium / discount (FX) — the lower-rate currency trades at a premium, the higher-rate one at a discount (p.114, PDF 122).
- Price currency and base currency — in a quote f/d, f is the price currency and d the base (p.114).
- Term structure — the pattern of rates across maturities (p.117, PDF 125).
- Zero (spot) rate — yield on a bond with one cash flow (p.118, PDF 126).
- Bootstrapping — solving zero rates one maturity at a time from coupon-bond prices (p.119, PDF 127).
- Discount factor DF_i — PV of one currency unit received at period i (p.119).
- Implied forward rate (IFR) — breakeven reinvestment rate between two zero rates (pp.120–121, PDF 128–129).
- Notation "2y3y" (F_2,3) — a 3-year rate starting in 2 years (p.120, PDF 128).
- Market reference rate (MRR) — short-term benchmark rate such as a 3-month rate (first used p.120, PDF 128; worked p.123, PDF 131).
- Forward rate agreement (FRA) — OTC contract fixing a rate for a future period; fixed-rate payer is long (p.124, PDF 132).

## Formulas
Notation: S_t spot at t; F0(T) forward price fixed at 0 for delivery at T; r annual risk-free rate, constant; T, t in years; I, C known income and carrying costs; values per unit of the underlying in the price currency.
1. V0(T) = 0 — initiation, ignoring costs and credit (p.107, PDF 115).
2. V_T(T) = S_T − F0(T) for the long; short = F0(T) − S_T — expiry (p.108, PDF 116).
3. F0(T) = S0(1+r)^T — no income or cost, discrete compounding. (p.110, PDF 118).
4. PV_t of F0(T) = F0(T)(1+r)^−(T−t) (p.110).
5. V_t(T) = S_t − F0(T)(1+r)^−(T−t) — long, no carry; short is the negative; constant r (p.110).
6. F0(T) = (S0 − PV0(I) + PV0(C))(1+r)^T — known income and costs; PV the carry first (p.112, PDF 120).
7. V_t(T) = (S_t − PV_t(I) + PV_t(C)) − F0(T)(1+r)^−(T−t) — long; only remaining cash flows (p.113, PDF 121).
8. F0,f/d(T) = S0,f/d · e^((r_f − r_d)T) — f price currency, d base currency; continuous rates (p.114, PDF 122).
9. V_t(T) = S_t,f/d − F0,f/d(T) · e^−((r_f − r_d)(T−t)) — long the base currency (p.114).
10. DF_i = 1/(1+z_i)^i — z_i is the i-period zero rate (p.119, PDF 127).
11. (1+z_A)^A × (1+IFR_A,B−A)^(B−A) = (1+z_B)^B — same compounding for z and IFR; A<B. (p.122, PDF 130).
12. (1+APR_m/m)^m = (1+APR_n/n)^n — convert compounding frequency before using Eq 11 (p.123, PDF 131).
13. FRA, fixed-rate payer, paid at period end: (MRR − IFR) × Notional × Period; value at the period's start = that payment/(1+MRR×Period). Fixed-rate receiver: (IFR − MRR) × Notional × Period, discounted the same way (pp.125, 128; PDF 133, 136). Units: currency.

## Traps
- Confusing price with value: F0 never changes; V_t does (p.106).
- Wrong sign by side: the standard MTM is the long's; the short's is PV(F) − S_t.
- Thinking a higher rate helps the long. It lowers the PV of F and therefore the long's MTM.
- Carry errors: income is subtracted from spot, costs added; discount only flows not yet paid (p.113).
- FX mix-ups: r_f belongs to the price currency, r_d to the base; MTM is for a long of the base currency; the lower-rate currency is at a forward premium (p.114).
- Treating a coupon yield as a zero rate, or the IFR as an average of spot rates (pp.105, 119, 132).
- Reading "2y3y" backwards, forgetting to convert compounding frequency, or forgetting to discount the FRA payment from period end to period start (pp.105, 123–126).

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical; annual compounding unless stated.
1. Equity forward, no dividends. Spot 100, r = 5%, T = 1: F0 = 105. Six months later spot is 102. PV of F = 105/1.05^0.5 = 102.47, so long V = −0.47, short +0.47. If r fell to 3%, PV of F = 103.46 and long V = −1.46.
2. Dividend forward. Spot 50, r = 4%, T = 1, one dividend of 1.00 at t = 0.5 (PV = 1/1.04^0.5 = 0.9806). F0 = (50 − 0.9806)(1.04) = 50.98, and V0 = 0 by construction.
3. Implied forward and FRA. Zero rates z1 = 3%, z2 = 4%. IFR_1,1 = 1.04²/1.03 − 1 = 5.01%. A fixed-rate payer on notional 10 million for one year, if MRR sets at 6%, receives about 99,000 at period end (using 0.99%), and its value at the period start is about 93,400 (99,000/1.06).
