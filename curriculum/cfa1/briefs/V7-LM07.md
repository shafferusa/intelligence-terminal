## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how a swap resembles, and how it differs from, a strip of forward contracts (Swaps vs. Forwards, pp.156–162 / PDF 164–170).
2. Distinguish a swap's value from its price (Swap Values and Prices, pp.163–169 / PDF 171–177).

## Core principles

- **A swap is a bundle of mini-FRAs, but with one fixed rate.** Each period the fixed-rate payer and floating-rate payer net the gap between a fixed rate (set at inception) and the market reference rate (MRR, set later), times notional times period length. Like FRAs: symmetric payoffs, zero value at the start, no upfront cash, counterparty credit exposure. Unlike FRAs: a standard swap settles at the end of each period over many dates; an FRA settles once, at the start of its period (discounted). pp.156–157 / PDF 164–165.
- **The swap rate is a breakeven on the forwards.** A strip of FRAs would carry a different fixed rate each period (the implied forward rates). The swap uses one constant rate, so with a rising forward curve the fixed payer pays more than it receives early and the reverse later. The PVs of all net amounts add to zero at inception. What breaks this: transaction costs, credit risk, a wrong curve. pp.158–160 / PDF 166–168.
- **How to find the rate.** Treat expected floating payments as the implied forward rates, discount everything with zero rates, and solve for the constant fixed rate that equates the two PVs. Same idea as a par bond coupon, without principal exchange. pp.158–159 / PDF 166–167.
- **Why end users like swaps.** A borrower whose loan resets off the MRR can pay fixed and receive the MRR, so its all-in cost is the swap fixed rate plus the loan spread whatever the MRR does. Investors change rate exposure without trading bonds. Remaining risks: credit, mark-to-market swings, uncertain net flows. pp.160–162 / PDF 168–170.
- **Swaps map to bond positions.** Pay-fixed is like long a floating-rate note and short a fixed-rate bond (principals cancel, leaving the coupon difference); receive-fixed is the reverse. Pay-fixed gains when rates rise, receive-fixed when they fall. The FRN stays near par. p.161 / PDF 169 (Exhibit 4); p.166 / PDF 174.
- **Price is a rate; value is money.** The price is the constant fixed rate. The value is the PV of what each side is owed, from one party's view; zero at inception. Later, value on a settlement date is the settlement just determined plus the PV of all remaining expected settlements. pp.163–164 / PDF 171–172.
- **Two things move value.** (1) Time passing with forwards unchanged: direction depends on the curve's shape. With an upward-sloping curve the payer's early net payments are negative; once paid, the remaining stream has positive PV, so the payer's MTM rises. (2) A shift in expected forwards: higher forwards raise PV(floating), so the payer gains and the receiver loses; lower forwards reverse it. pp.164–166 / PDF 172–174.
- **Credit exposure follows value.** You are exposed when the counterparty owes you, i.e. when your MTM is positive. p.156 / PDF 164; Solutions pp.173–174.

## Vocabulary

- Swap — contract to exchange payments over several dates (pp.153–154).
- FRA — one exchange of fixed vs MRR-based payment, settled at the start of the period (p.156).
- Market reference rate (MRR) — floating benchmark setting each floating payment (pp.154, 156).
- Implied forward rate (IFR) — future-period rate leaving no arbitrage between two zero rates (p.157).
- Par swap rate / swap price — constant fixed rate equating PV(fixed) and PV(floating) (pp.154, 158–159).
- Fixed-rate payer (floating receiver) and fixed-rate receiver — the two sides (p.156).
- Periodic settlement — net amount at period end, payer's view (p.164).
- Mark-to-market (MTM) value — current settlement plus PV of remaining settlements (pp.154, 164).
- FRN at MRR — stays near par; the building block of the bond analogy (p.166).

## Formulas

1. **Implied forward rate (Eq. 1):** (1 + z_A)^A × (1 + IFR_A,B−A)^(B−A) = (1 + z_B)^B. z_A, z_B are zero rates for A and B years (annual compounding); IFR_A,B−A starts at A for B−A years. Assumes no arbitrage. p.157 / PDF 165.
2. **Par bond rate (Eq. 2):** 100 = PMT/(1+z_1)^1 + … + (PMT+100)/(1+z_N)^N. Background for the swap rate; note the final principal a swap lacks. p.158 / PDF 166.
3. **Par swap rate (Eq. 3):** Σ IFR_i/(1+z_i)^i = Σ s/(1+z_i)^i, so s_N = [Σ IFR_i/(1+z_i)^i] / [Σ 1/(1+z_i)^i]. Result in percent per period; rates and periods must be consistent. Assumes floating payments equal the IFRs, discounting on zeros, no costs or credit risk. p.159 / PDF 167.
4. **Periodic settlement, fixed payer (Eq. 4):** (MRR − s_N) × Notional × Period. Decimal rates per year; period in years (0.25, 0.5). Positive means the payer receives; the receiver sees the opposite sign. p.164 / PDF 172.
5. **Swap value (verbal):** V = current settlement + PV(remaining expected settlements); V = 0 at inception. Fixed payer gains if PV(floating received) > PV(fixed paid). pp.164, 166 / PDF 172, 174.
6. **Forward analogy:** V_T(T) = S_T − F_0(T) for a long forward at maturity; for a swap the MRR plays the spot and s_N the forward price. p.164 / PDF 172.

## Traps

- Calling the swap rate the value. The rate is the price, fixed for life; value is money and moves, and is zero at inception.
- Thinking zero value means zero cash flows. Individual periods are positive or negative; only the PV sum is zero.
- Wrong sign. Eq. 4 is the payer's view; flip it for the receiver.
- Mixing up rates: IFRs are the expected floating payment rates, zeros discount.
- Assuming time passing always helps the payer. It follows from an upward-sloping forward curve; with no curve information, value cannot be determined.
- Reversing the bond analogy: pay-fixed is long FRN, short fixed bond.
- Confusing timing: FRA settles at the start of its period, a swap at the end.
- Treating a known first settlement as making future flows known.

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

**A. Par swap rate, two annual periods.** Zeros: z1 = 2.00%, z2 = 3.00%. IFR(1,1) = 1.03² / 1.02 − 1 = 4.0098%. PV of floating = 2/1.02 + 4.0098/1.0609 = 1.9608 + 3.7796 = 5.7404. Annuity factor = 1/1.02 + 1/1.0609 = 1.9230. s_2 = 5.7404 / 1.9230 = 2.985%. Check against the par bond rate: (1 − 1/1.0609) / 1.9230 = 2.985%.

**B. Settlement and a rate change.** USD 50m notional, pay fixed 3.00%, semiannual. MRR sets at 3.40%: (3.40% − 3.00%) × 50m × 0.5 = USD 100,000 received by the payer at period end. Now take only the final settlement, paid in 1 year, with expected forward 3.20% and a 1-year zero rate of 3.50%: PV = (0.20% × 50m × 0.5) / 1.035 = USD 48,309. If the forward rises to 3.60%: 150,000 / 1.035 = USD 144,928. The payer gains USD 96,618 and the receiver loses the same, before costs. Same price, different value.

**C. Locking in a loan cost.** A firm borrows USD 100m at MRR + 1.00%, quarterly, and swaps to pay 2.50% fixed and receive MRR. All-in = 2.50% + 1.00% = 3.50%, or 3.50% × 100m × 0.25 = USD 875,000 per quarter. Check at MRR 4.00%: loan 5.00%, swap receives 1.50%, net 3.50%.
