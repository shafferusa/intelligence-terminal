## Core principles
- **MR below price with market power.** A price taker sells each extra unit at market price, so P = MR = AR. A firm facing a falling demand curve must cut price to sell more, so MR < P; revenue rises while demand is elastic, falls once inelastic. Profit peaks where MR = MC with MC rising. Price comes from demand at that output, not from MC (pp.6–8, PDF 14–16).
- **Economic profit charges for capital.** Economic cost adds the owners' forgone return to accounting cost. Breakeven (P = ATC) therefore means normal profit. Free entry drives profit there; barriers let excess profit last (p.9, PDF 17).
- **Operate vs shut.** Committed fixed costs are sunk and ignored. If revenue covers variable cost, operating loses less than closing, since the excess reduces the fixed bill. Short run: shut if P < AVC. Long run, all costs avoidable: exit if TR < TC (pp.10–13, PDF 18–21).
- **Scale.** Each plant size has its own short-run curve; LRAC is their lower envelope. Falling LRAC is economies of scale (specialisation, technology, bulk buying); rising is diseconomies (coordination strain). Firms off minimum efficient scale carry a cost disadvantage (pp.14–19, PDF 22–27).
- **Monopolistic competition.** Differentiation gives some pricing power and profit; entrants then shift each firm's demand left until profit is zero. Output stays below minimum-ATC output and price is higher than under perfect competition, partly funding advertising. Offset: innovation, variety (pp.25–28, PDF 33–36).
- **Oligopoly is interdependence.** Kinked demand: rivals match cuts, ignore rises, so demand is elastic above the prevailing price and inelastic below; MR has a gap, so MC can shift without moving price. It explains stability, not price level. Cournot: simultaneous outputs, each taking the rival's as fixed; result between competition and monopoly. Nash: no one gains by deviating alone, yet joint profit is not maximised, hence cartel pull. Collusion is likelier with few sellers, similar products and costs, and credible retaliation. A dominant leader's share tends to erode as entrants arrive (pp.29–39, PDF 37–47).
- **Concentration is a proxy.** CR and HHI ignore entry barriers and elasticity. A merger among leaders barely moves CR but lifts HHI sharply (squared shares). Elasticity is the better test but estimation is hard, as price and quantity are jointly determined (pp.40–44, PDF 48–52).

## Vocabulary
- Economic profit — revenue minus all costs including opportunity cost (p.9).
- Accounting profit — revenue minus explicit costs only (p.4).
- Normal profit — return equal to capital's alternative at similar risk (p.9).
- Breakeven point — TR = TC; minimum ATC for a price taker (p.9, p.11).
- Shutdown point — minimum AVC (p.11).
- Sunk cost — unavoidable whatever is chosen; ignore it (p.10).
- Long run — all inputs variable, entry and exit possible (p.14).
- LRAC — lower envelope of short-run average cost curves (p.15).
- Economies / diseconomies of scale — unit cost falls / rises with long-run output (pp.15–17).
- Minimum efficient scale — lowest point of LRAC (p.17).
- Barriers to entry — capital needs, patents, costly exit (p.23).
- Kinked demand curve — two slopes at the prevailing price (p.31).
- Cournot assumption — rivals' output treated as fixed (p.32).
- Nash equilibrium — no firm gains by changing strategy alone (p.34).
- Dominant firm / price leader — large, often low-cost firm others follow (p.36).
- HHI — sum of squared market shares (p.43).

## Formulas
No numbered equations; these relationships are used.
- TR = P × Q (price per unit, quantity per period). Price taker: P fixed (p.6, PDF 14).
- Profit = TR − TC = (P − ATC) × Q (second form is my rearrangement); economic if TC includes opportunity cost (p.9, PDF 17; p.27, PDF 35).
- MR = MC with MC rising; price taker P = MR = SMC. Add output while the extra unit earns more than it costs (p.8, PDF 16).
- Breakeven: TR = TC, P = ATC (pp.9, 11).
- Shutdown: short run if TR < TVC (P < AVC), loss then = TFC; long run exit if TR < TC. Assumes fixed cost sunk (pp.10–13).
- Cournot duopoly, demand Q = a − P, identical constant MC = c: each q = (a − c)/3, Q = 2(a − c)/3. Competition Q = a − c; monopoly Q = (a − c)/2 (pp.32–33, PDF 40–41; rechecked).
- CR(N) = sum of top-N shares, 0–100% (p.42, PDF 50).
- HHI = sum of squared decimal shares; monopoly 1; M equal firms 1/M (p.43, PDF 51). Book uses decimals; regulators often use 0–10,000.

## Traps
- Zero economic profit is not zero accounting profit.
- Shutdown = minimum AVC, breakeven = minimum ATC; between them, keep operating short run.
- MR = MC needs MC rising; minimum ATC is not the profit maximum.
- With market power, read price off demand, not MC; only price takers have supply curves.
- Economies of scale are long-run; constant returns means flat LRAC.
- Nash is not joint-profit maximum; Cournot simultaneous, Stackelberg sequential.
- Easy entry makes even a few firms behave competitively; high CR is not proof of pricing power.
- HHI is a decimal (0.25 for four equal firms, not 25).
