## Core principles
- **Law of one price.** Close substitutes should sell for similar prices per unit of fundamental, so a gap to peers hints at mispricing. But the gap may be earned by lower growth or higher risk, and then the "cheap" verdict fails (pp.213, 217–218).
- **A multiple is a compressed DCF.** Divide the constant-growth value by a fundamental to get a justified multiple. Higher required return lowers it, higher growth raises it, and r must exceed g. A firm cannot raise payout for free, because less retention means less growth (pp.218–219, 232–233).
- **Match claimants.** Equity prices pair with equity measures (earnings, FCFE, book equity, dividends). EV, which adds debt and preferred and nets cash, pairs with all-capital measures (EBITDA, EBIT, FCFF). Price-to-sales mixes them, so it distorts when leverage differs (pp.214–215, 227, 236).
- **EV/EBITDA is cash-flow driven.** Value is FCFF over (WACC - g), so the multiple depends on tax, the depreciation shield, capex and working capital. Heavy reinvestment lowers it, which suits capital-intensive, differently levered firms (p.238).
- **PVGO splits price** into no-growth value plus future investment value. Negative PVGO means reinvestment below the cost of capital, which destroys value (pp.230–231).
- **Peer choice drives the answer.** Industry peers share customers and competition; factor peers share systematic risk but can differ widely in multiples. Remedies: median, normalize outliers, revenue-weighted multiples for conglomerates, DCF if peers are few (pp.251–256).
- **Relative is not absolute.** A stock can be cheap against an overpriced group (p.258).
- **Terminal value is a multiple bet.** Use exit peers that resemble the mature, slower-growth firm; a rich exit multiple inflates value (pp.223, 261, 264).

## Vocabulary
- Method of comparables — compare a multiple with peers' (p.213).
- Method of forecasted fundamentals — derive the justified multiple from growth, profitability, risk (p.218).
- Trailing / forward — denominator from the last or next 12 months (p.218).
- PEG — P/E over expected growth in percentage points (p.220).
- Adjustment vs regression approach — rescale for one driver, or fit a cross-sectional model (pp.220–221).
- Enterprise value — claim of all capital providers, net of cash (p.227).
- PVGO — price portion from future growth investment (p.230).
- Normalized EPS — mid-cycle earnings for cyclicals (p.234).
- EBITDA, EBITA, EBIT — operating denominators for EV multiples (pp.238–239).
- Price to tangible book — P/B excluding intangibles, used for banks (p.241).
- Value-to-book — all-capital book multiple for differing leverage (p.241).
- ROC — after-tax operating income over book capital (p.242).
- Trailing vs leading dividend yield — last or next dividend over price (p.244).
- Factor-based peers — peers sharing exposure to rewarded risk factors (p.254).
- Terminal value — end-of-forecast value, often multiple x forecast fundamental (p.261).

## Formulas
Book equation numbers; annual decimals; multiples unitless; constant growth with r (or WACC) above g.
- **Eq 4 (p.219):** g = (1 - payout) x ROE.
- **Eq 5 (p.219):** justified P/B = (ROE - g)/(re - g).
- **Eq 6 (p.220):** PEG = (P/E)/(growth in percentage points). Assumes linear P/E-growth link.
- **Eq 7 (p.221):** (P/B)_i = a + b1 ROE_i + b2 g_i + b3 re_i + e_i. Fitted is justified; predicted above actual means undervalued.
- **Eq 8 (pp.223, 261):** IV0 = sum E(CFt)/(1+r)^t + E(P_n)/(1+r)^n, terminal price from a multiple.
- **Eq 9 (p.227):** EV = MV debt + MV equity + preferred - cash and short-term investments.
- **Eq 13, 15 (p.230):** P = EPS1/re + PVGO; PVGO/P = 1 - (EPS1/P)/re.
- **Eq 16 (p.232):** justified trailing P/E = payout x (1+g)/(re - g); forward drops (1+g) (p.250).
- **Eq 17 (p.236):** justified P/S = margin x payout x (1+g)/(re - g) = justified P/E x margin.
- **Eq 18-21 (p.238):** EV = FCFF1/(WACC - g); FCFF = EBITDA(1-t) + Dep x t - CapEx - dWC; so EV/EBITDA = [(1-t) + Dep x t/EBITDA - CapEx/EBITDA - dWC/EBITDA]/(WACC - g).
- **Eq 22-23 (p.241):** value to book = (MV equity + MV debt)/(BV equity + BV debt); EV to book nets cash from both.
- **Eq 24-26 (p.242):** EV/book capital = ROC x (1 - reinvestment rate)/(WACC - g).
- **Eq 28 (p.245):** trailing yield = (re - g)/(1+g); leading = re - g.
- **Eq 29-30 (p.246):** V/FCFF1 = 1/(WACC - g); P/FCFE1 = 1/(re - g).
- **Normalized EPS (p.234):** average EPS, or average ROE x current book value per share.
- **Eq 31 CAPM (p.254):** E(R_i) = Rf + beta[E(R_m) - Rf].
- **Weighted multiple (p.254):** sum of weight x segment peer multiple.

## Traps
- Low multiple is not cheap; check risk, growth, earnings quality (p.217).
- Trailing forms carry (1+g); forward forms do not (pp.232, 245, 250).
- Higher payout alone does not lift P/E, since g falls (p.237).
- PEG growth is in whole points: 12, not 0.12 (p.221).
- No equity numerator over all-capital denominator (pp.215, 237).
- Rebuild EV after buybacks or asset purchases (pp.227–228).
- Positive EPS can be junk, such as interest on cash; normalize cyclicals (p.234).
- Use the median for skewed peers or outliers (pp.216, 252).
- Exit multiple from mature-firm peers, not today's growth peers (p.264).

## Original practice seeds

2. **Hypothetical (millions).** 100 shares at 20, debt 600, cash 150: EV 2,450; EBITDA 350 gives 7.0x. Peer median 8.0x implies EV 2,800, equity 2,350, 23.50 per share, 17.5% above price; valid only if peers truly match.
3. **Hypothetical.** Year-5 EPS 4.00, exit P/E 12: 48.00; at 10% for 5 years, 48/1.61051 = 29.80. Exit at 9x: 36/1.61051 = 22.35.
