## Core principles
- **Covered bond = bank debt with a ring-fenced pool.** Loans stay on the bank's balance sheet. Dual recourse: pool first, then the bank's other assets. Usually one bond class, so no tranching. A monitor checks the pool and the issuer replaces prepaid or weak loans. Lower credit risk means lower yield than comparable ABS. pp.468–470 (PDF 478–480).
- **Redemption regimes answer "what if the sponsor fails?"** Hard bullet: a missed payment is an immediate default. Soft bullet: default is postponed to an extended maturity (up to roughly a year). Conditional pass-through: after the original maturity the bond passes pool cash flows through. p.470 (PDF 480).
- **Credit enhancement is built-in loss absorption.** Overcollateralization is an asset cushion, excess spread an income cushion, subordination a priority cushion. Each fails once losses exceed it. pp.472–474 (PDF 482–484).
- **Tranching reallocates, not removes, risk.** Losses hit the most junior (equity/residual) tranche first, then upward. Junior tranches earn higher spreads over a reference rate for first loss. Interest equals holding times (reference rate plus spread) on surviving principal. A tranche can be rated above its sponsor. pp.474–478 (PDF 484–488).
- **Amortizing vs non-amortizing pools.** Auto loans and mortgages pay scheduled principal and face prepayment. Credit card pools have none: in the lockout/revolving period collected principal buys new receivables and investors get only finance charges and fees; then the amortization period pays principal out. Rapid amortization returns principal early if the pool is not replenished or defaults change the economics. pp.479–481 (PDF 489–492).
- **Solar ABS credit logic.** Collateral is loans (or leases) for rooftop systems. Support: prime borrowers whose payment displaces an electricity bill, plus overcollateralization, subordination, excess spread and reserves. Pre-funding lets the issuer add eligible assets after closing. Legal collateral varies. No full credit cycle observed yet. pp.482–484 (PDF 492–494).
- **CDO/CLO economics are leveraged spread capture.** A CDO is backed by a managed debt pool (CBO, CLO, structured finance CDO, synthetic CDO; CLOs dominate). Return is collateral yield minus funding cost; equity and the manager get the residual. The pool is traded, so manager skill matters. Recourse is to the pool only. pp.485–487 (PDF 495–497).
- **CLO tests protect seniors by diverting cash.** After ramp-up come a reinvestment period and then repayment in order. Coverage tests and diversification limits exist; on a breach, cash moves from equity and junior debt to repay senior debt or buy collateral. No guarantee. pp.487–491 (PDF 497–501).

## Formulas
The module has no formal equations; these arithmetic relationships are verified against the pages.
- Overcollateralization amount = collateral value − face value of bonds. p.473 (PDF 483).
- Excess spread = collateral coupon − security coupon. % per year, same basis. p.473 (PDF 483).
- Tranche loss = min(tranche size, max(0, total loss − sum of sizes of more junior tranches)). Implied by the waterfall text, not printed. Ignores recoveries, timing, and excess spread/OC absorbing losses first. p.476 (PDF 486).
- Tranche coupon = reference rate (MRR) + spread; interest = holding × coupon on surviving principal. p.476 (PDF 486).
- Loss threshold before a tranche is hit = OC amount + sum of junior tranches; as % of issue = threshold / note issue. p.482 (PDF 492).
- CLO OC ratio = collateral (loan) principal / CLO debt principal. Each debt tranche has its own target. p.488 (PDF 498).

## Traps
- Covered bonds stay on the balance sheet and have dual recourse; ABS and CDOs are off balance sheet with recourse to the pool only (p.469; table p.485).
- Overcollateralization is an asset cushion; excess spread is an income margin (p.473).
- Subordination protects the senior tranche, not the equity tranche; loss goes by rank, not pro rata (pp.474–476).
- During the revolving period holders receive only finance charges and fees. That is not prepayment risk, and rapid amortization is not normal scheduled amortization (pp.479–481).
- Ratings reflect credit risk, not market risk (pp.478–479).
- CLO equity is unrated with no fixed coupon; it claims residual cash. Failed tests divert cash to seniors but do not guarantee them (p.488).
- Term sheets show face values, not market values (p.466).
- Tranche-loss problems: stack junior tranches from the bottom, run the loss up, check totals sum to the loss.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical; amounts in millions, single point in time, no recoveries.
1. Pool EUR1,000 with tranches A 600, B 250, C 100, D 50. A loss of 120: D takes 50, C takes 70, A and B unharmed. A loss of 420: D 50 + C 100 + B 250 = 400, so A absorbs the remaining 20 and is worth 580.
2. Collateral EUR400 yields 9% (36 a year); notes EUR400 pay 6.5% (26). Excess spread is 2.5%, or 10 a year. If collateral yield falls to 8% (32), excess spread is 6 (1.5%): a 40% smaller cushion.
3. CLO debt USD450, loans USD540: OC ratio 540/450 = 1.20. Assume a trigger of 1.15. If loans fall to 518, the ratio is about 1.151 and passes. If they fall to 510, it is about 1.133 and fails, so cash is diverted from equity to repay senior debt.
