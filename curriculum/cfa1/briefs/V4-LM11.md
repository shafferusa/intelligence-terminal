## Learning outcomes

2. Calculate and interpret activity, liquidity, solvency and profitability ratios (pp.405–424; categories introduced pp.403–404).
3. Describe how ratios relate to each other and judge a company using several together (pp.424–426).
4. Apply DuPont analysis of ROE and interpret component changes (pp.426–431).
5. Describe the use of industry-specific ratios (pp.432–433).
6. Describe how ratios and other tools feed models and forecasts of earnings (pp.433–434).

## Core principles
- **Computation is only input.** A ratio says what happened, not why; analysis links causes to strategy and asks what decision the work serves. The same headline trend can hide different drivers. pp.383–387, 391.
- **Benchmarks make numbers meaningful.** Compare across firms (cross-sectional) or one firm over time (trend). Ratios and common-size statements remove size; currencies are translated or ratios compared; year ends are aligned by trailing 12 months. Differing accounting policies undermine them. pp.387–390, 393–394, 397–399.
- **Match flows to stock averages.** Divide an income or cash-flow item by an average balance; ending balances understate returns when assets grow, beginning ones overstate. Liquidity ratios use ending balances. pp.392–393, 405–406, 412.
- **Look at base and cause.** A big percent change on a tiny base may not matter. Receivables outgrowing sales hints at looser credit or aggressive revenue recognition; profit rising while operating cash flow falls is an earnings-quality warning. pp.396–400.
- **Working-capital cycle.** Stock and customer credit tie up cash while suppliers finance part. A negative cycle suits a cash-rich firm but signals distress if it reflects inability to pay; efficiency ratios can flatter for bad reasons. pp.405–415.
- **Solvency is leverage plus coverage.** More debt and weaker coverage raise default risk; steady cash flow supports more debt. pp.416–419.
- **ROE has drivers.** DuPont splits ROE into margin, asset use and leverage (plus tax and interest burden); leverage helps only when assets earn more than debt costs. pp.426–431.
- **Forecasts need ranges.** Use sensitivity, scenario and simulation, not one point. pp.433–434.

## Vocabulary
- Cross-sectional analysis — firms at one time (p.388, 397); trend (time-series) analysis — one firm across periods (p.388, 398).
- Common-size statement — every line as a percent of a base such as assets or revenue (p.395).
- Vertical vs horizontal common-size — within one period vs against a base period (p.395, 398).
- Trailing 12 months (TTM) — last four quarters whatever the fiscal year (p.388, 394).
- Combined ratio — insurer's losses plus expenses over premiums earned (p.392).
- Cash conversion cycle — days that working capital must be financed (p.412).
- Defensive interval — days of cash spending covered by quick assets (p.412).
- Tax burden, interest burden — NI/EBT, EBT/EBIT (p.429).
- Sensitivity, scenario analysis, simulation — one-input what-if, discrete events, computer-run models (p.434).

## Formulas
Avg = (beginning + ending)/2.
- Percent change = current/prior − 1; index = item/base item (base = 1.00); vertical common-size = item / total assets (balance sheet) or revenue (income statement); effective tax rate = tax/EBT; TTM = last fiscal year + current YTD − prior-year YTD (pp.394–399).
- Net profit margin = net income/revenue (p.391); operating income / avg total assets (p.391); ROE = net income / avg equity (p.393).
- Inventory turnover = COGS/avg inventory; DOH = days/turnover. Receivables turnover = revenue/avg receivables; DSO = days/turnover. Payables turnover = COGS/avg payables; days payable = days/turnover (p.405). Working capital, fixed asset and total asset turnover = revenue / avg of that base (p.406). Annualize a quarterly turnover by ×4 or ×365/90 (p.406).
- Current = CA/CL; quick = (cash + ST investments + receivables)/CL; cash ratio = (cash + ST investments)/CL; defensive interval = (cash + ST investments + receivables) / daily cash spending, where daily spending = (total expenses − non-cash expenses)/days; cash conversion cycle = DOH + DSO − days payable (p.412). Ending balances.
- Debt-to-assets; debt-to-capital = debt/(debt + equity); debt-to-equity; total debt = interest-bearing short plus long-term debt; D/E of 1.0 equals 50% debt-to-capital; financial leverage = avg assets/avg equity; debt-to-EBITDA; interest coverage = EBIT/interest; fixed charge coverage = (EBIT + lease)/(interest + lease) (pp.416–418).
- Margins (gross, operating, pretax, net) over revenue; ROA = NI/avg assets, or (NI + interest×(1−t))/avg assets; ROIC = EBIT×(1−t)/avg (debt + equity); return on common equity = (NI − preferred dividends)/avg common equity (pp.420–422).
- DuPont: ROE = ROA × leverage; = margin × turnover × leverage; five-way = (NI/EBT)×(EBT/EBIT)×(EBIT/revenue)×(revenue/avg assets)×(avg assets/avg equity) (pp.427–429). Tax burden = 1 − tax rate.
- Coefficient of variation = standard deviation/mean (p.432); industry examples include net interest margin, occupancy, ADR, ARPU (pp.432–433).

## Traps
- Taking bigger profit as more profitable; ratios control for size (p.391).
- Using ending assets for a flow/stock ratio; average (pp.392–393).
- Comparing a quarterly turnover with an annual one; annualize first (p.406).
- Treating tax/revenue as the tax rate; use tax/EBT (p.396).
- Confusing index (against base) with percent change (against prior period) (pp.399–400).
- Assuming high turnover, low days payable or a negative cash cycle is always good; ask why (pp.407–416).
- Calling D/E a liquidity ratio (it is solvency), or D/E of 1.0 equal to 100% debt-to-capital (pp.416–417).
- Misreading tax burden: a higher tax burden means lower taxes; leverage helps ROE only when assets earn more than the debt costs (pp.427, 429).
- Reading regression as causation, or stable ratios as proof of health; smoothing is possible (pp.403–405).

2. 365-day year, COGS EUR1,200 thousand, inventory 90 then 110: average 100, turnover 12.0, DOH 30.4. If ending inventory were 150: average 120, turnover 10.0, DOH 36.5.
3. Revenue 1,000, EBIT 100, EBT 80, net income 60, avg assets 800, avg equity 400. Separately, DOH 30.4, DSO 40, days payable 35 give a cash conversion cycle of 35.4 days; with 50 days payable it is 20.4.
