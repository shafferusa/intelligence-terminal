## Learning outcomes
1. Compute an annual yield when the number of compounding periods per year varies, and convert between periodicities (Periodicity section, p.160–165, PDF 170–175).
2. Compare, compute and interpret yield measures and yield spread measures for fixed-rate bonds (p.165–182, PDF 175–192: other yield measures, embedded options, benchmark-rate spreads, curve spreads).

## Core principles
- **Periodicity changes the quote, not the return.** A stated yield is a per-period rate times periods per year. More frequent compounding means a lower stated rate for the same economic return (a more negative rate if yields are negative). Put yields on the same basis before subtracting (p.160–162, PDF 170–172).
- **A zero-coupon bond has no natural periodicity.** Solve annually and convert, or solve semiannually and double; both agree (p.162–163, PDF 172–173).
- **Simple yield measures have blind spots.** Current yield ignores redemption gain or loss, reinvestment and accrued interest. Street yield assumes scheduled dates; true yield uses actual dates and is never above street yield. A corporate 30/360 yield is restated to actual/actual (about 365/360 times it) before comparing with a government yield. Simple yield uses straight-line amortization of discount or premium (p.165–167, PDF 175–177).
- **A call option favors the issuer.** The investor bears call risk and pays less than for an identical non-callable bond. Compute a yield to each call date (call price as final payment, periods to that date); yield to worst is the lowest of those and the YTM. Priced above the call prices, an early call is the worst case; priced below, maturity usually is (p.168–171, PDF 178–181).
- **Split yield into benchmark and spread.** The benchmark is the base rate (real rate plus expected inflation); the spread is the issuer-specific add-on (credit, liquidity, tax). G-spread is bond YTM minus a government YTM, interpolated linearly if no maturity matches. I-spread is YTM minus the same-currency, same-tenor swap rate. An interpolated benchmark moves when other curve points move, so the spread can change with the bond unchanged (p.173–176, PDF 183–186).
- **Z-spread discounts each cash flow on the spot curve.** G- and I-spreads use one yield for all cash flows. Z-spread is a constant added to every benchmark spot rate so discounted cash flows equal the market price, found by trial and error or solver. OAS is Z-spread minus option value in bp per year, and needs an option model and a volatility assumption (p.177–178, PDF 187–188).
- **Option value sign.** Call value is option-free price minus callable price, so option-free-equivalent price = callable price + call value. The book is inconsistent (see slips); reason from the definition (p.171, PDF 181).

## Vocabulary
- Periodicity — compounding periods per year assumed by a stated annual rate (p.160, PDF 170).
- Effective annual rate (EAR) — annual rate on a periodicity of 1 (p.161, PDF 171).
- Semiannual bond basis yield — half-year yield times 2 (p.161, PDF 171).
- Strip bond — zero-coupon security made from a coupon bond's separated payments (p.163, PDF 173).
- Current yield — annual coupon over flat price (p.165, PDF 175).
- Street convention / true yield — scheduled dates / actual dates (p.166, PDF 176).
- Government equivalent yield — corporate yield restated to actual/actual (p.166, PDF 176).
- Simple yield — coupon plus straight-line discount gain or premium loss, over flat price; used for JGBs (p.166, PDF 176).
- Yield to call / yield to worst (p.168–171, PDF 178–181).
- Option-adjusted price and yield (p.171, PDF 181).
- Benchmark and spread; top-down versus bottom-up (p.173–174, PDF 183–184).
- On-the-run / off-the-run — newest, most liquid government issue / older issues; on-the-run yields slightly lower (p.173–174, PDF 183–184).
- G-spread (p.174, PDF 184); I-spread (p.176, PDF 186).
- Asset swap / MRR — converts a fixed coupon into market reference rate plus or minus a spread (p.176, PDF 186).
- Z-spread (static spread) (p.177, PDF 187); OAS (p.178, PDF 188).

## Formulas
1. **Periodicity conversion (Eq. 1, p.162, PDF 172):** (1 + APR_m/m)^m = (1 + APR_n/n)^n. Decimals; n = 1 gives EAR. Assumes same cash flows and reinvestment at the periodic rate.
2. **Price/YTM (p.168, PDF 178):** PV = PMT/(1+r) + ... + (PMT + FV)/(1+r)^N. r per period; annual yield = r times periods per year; valid on a coupon date.
3. **Current yield (Eq. 2, p.165, PDF 175):** CY = annual coupon / flat price.
4. **Government equivalent yield (approximation, p.166, PDF 176):** Yield_ACT/ACT ≈ (365/360) x Yield_30/360.
5. **Yield to call (Eq. 3, p.169, PDF 179):** PV = PMT/(1+r) + ... + (PMT + Call price)/(1+r)^N, N = periods to call. YTW = min(all YTCs, YTM) (p.171, PDF 181).
6. **Interpolated benchmark and G-spread (p.176, PDF 186):** r_target = w_short x r_short + w_long x r_long; w_short = (T_long − T_target)/(T_long − T_short); w_long = 1 − w_short; G-spread = YTM − r_target.
7. **I-spread (text definition, p.176, PDF 186):** YTM − same-tenor, same-currency swap rate, on a common periodicity.
8. **Z-spread (Eq. 4, p.177, PDF 187):** PV = PMT/(1+z_1+Z)^1 + PMT/(1+z_2+Z)^2 + ... + (PMT+FV)/(1+z_N+Z)^N. z_t benchmark spot rates, Z constant; valid on a coupon date.
9. **OAS (Eq. 5, p.178, PDF 188):** OAS = Z-spread − option value (bp per year).
10. **Call value (p.171, PDF 181):** call value = option-free price − callable price; the book prints option-adjusted price = flat price + call value.
11. **Spreadsheet (p.161, PDF 171):** =FV(rate, nper, pmt, pv, type); EFFECT, YIELD and IRR also named.

## Traps
- Subtracting yields quoted on different periodicities; convert first.
- Confusing the per-period yield with the annual semiannual-basis yield (half versus double).
- Thinking more frequent compounding gives a higher stated rate.
- Using par and maturity in a yield to call; use call price and call date.
- Taking the first call as the worst; YTW is the minimum of all, including maturity.
- Applying the government equivalent restatement to the government bond instead of the corporate.
- Mixing up the G-spread (government) and I-spread (swap) benchmarks.
- Applying the Z-spread to the YTM instead of each spot rate; forgetting OAS is below Z-spread for a callable.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
- **A. Periodicity.** A 6.00% semiannual-basis yield: 1.03^2 = 1.0609, so EAR 6.09%. Quarterly rate = 1.0609^(1/4) − 1 = 1.489%, stated annual 5.956%. Order: 5.956% < 6.00% < 6.09%.
- **B. Yield to worst.** A 2-year, 4% bond, coupons semiannually (2.00 per 100), callable after year 1 at 101. At price 102: YTC 2.95% (r = 1.474% per half year), YTM 2.96%, so YTW is the call at 2.95%. At price 98: YTC 7.09%, YTM 5.06%, so YTW is maturity at 5.06%.
- **C. Spreads.** A 7-year bond yields 4.10%; Treasuries 5-year 3.00% and 10-year 3.50%. Weights 0.6 and 0.4 give benchmark 3.20%, G-spread 90 bp. If the 10-year rises to 3.80%, benchmark 3.32% and G-spread 78 bp, bond unchanged. Z-spread check: 2-year 5% annual-pay bond, spot rates 3% and 4%, Z = 1%: PV = 5/1.04 + 105/1.05^2 = 100.046.
