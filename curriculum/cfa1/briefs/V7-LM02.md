## Core principles
- **Firm commitment vs. contingent claim.** In a forward, future or swap both sides must perform at terms fixed today, so one side's gain is exactly the other's loss and the payoff is a straight line in the underlying price. In an option only the holder chooses, so the holder's payoff is kinked and cannot be negative; the price is an upfront premium (p.23, 38).
- **Forward payoff.** Nothing is paid at t = 0. At T the buyer receives the spot price minus the agreed price; the seller gets the mirror image. Gain depends on ST relative to the agreed price, not on today's spot. Each side bears the other's default risk (OTC) (p.26–28).
- **Futures are forwards with daily settlement.** An exchange and clearinghouse stand in the middle; both sides post initial margin; each day losers pay winners at the settlement price. If a balance drops below maintenance margin, the holder must top up to the initial level (not just to maintenance). Total gains match a forward but arrive daily, so with interest they are not strictly identical. Price limits and circuit breakers cap daily moves (p.29–34).
- **Swaps are strips of forwards.** The fixed-rate payer is the floating-rate receiver. Only the net difference moves; notional is never exchanged. Value is about zero at inception, then one side gains as rates move and the other loses (p.35–37).
- **Options: right for a premium.** Buyer's worst case is the premium; the writer collects the premium but is exposed to the exercise payoff. Credit risk runs one way: the writer cannot lose from the buyer defaulting once paid, but the buyer depends on the writer (p.38–42).
- **Short put resembles a long exposure.** The writer keeps the premium if price stays above X and loses as price falls; the loss is large but bounded (p.42).
- **CDS is a contingent claim.** The protection buyer pays a periodic spread; the seller pays a loss-based amount only if a credit event occurs, then the contract ends. The buyer is short credit risk (gains when spreads widen); the seller is long credit risk, like owning the bond (p.43–45).
- **Forward vs. option comparison.** With the strike equal to the forward price, a long call is a long forward with the loss floored, paid for by the premium: the call wins in the low-price region, the forward wins in the high-price region, and the crossover depends on the premium (p.46–48).

## Vocabulary
- Contingent claim — one side decides whether settlement happens (p.23, 38).
- Forward price F0(T) — price fixed at t = 0 for exchange at T (p.27).
- Mark to market — end-of-day repricing to the settlement price, cash moved (p.30).
- Initial margin — deposit to open a futures position (p.30).
- Maintenance margin — minimum balance before a call is triggered (p.30–31).
- Margin call / variation margin — demand to restore the balance to initial margin / the top-up amount (p.31).
- Swap rate / market reference rate (MRR) — fixed vs. floating rate (p.35).
- Exercise price (strike) X; premium c0 / p0 (p.39).
- European / American — exercise only at maturity / any time (p.39).
- Intrinsic value, time value, in/at/out-of-the-money (p.39, 41).
- Credit default swap, credit event, loss given default (p.43–45).

## Formulas
1. **Forward/futures payoff:** long = ST − F0(T) (futures: ST − f0(T)); short = F0(T) − ST. Per unit, times contract size, same currency. No upfront payment, so payoff = profit. p.27, 32 (PDF 35, 40).
2. **Futures daily gain, long:** (ft(T) − ft−1(T)) × contract size; short is the negative. Margin call = initial margin − balance, when balance < maintenance margin. p.31 (PDF 39).
3. **Net swap payment to fixed receiver:** notional × (swap rate − MRR) × period fraction (the notes' own compact restatement, not a printed equation). Rates annual; payments netted. p.36 (PDF 44).
4. **Long call:** cT = max(0, ST − X); profit = max(0, ST − X) − c0 (ignores time value of money); breakeven X + c0. p.40 (PDF 48).
5. **Long put:** pT = max(0, X − ST); profit = max(0, X − ST) − p0; breakeven X − p0; maximum payoff X. p.42 (PDF 50).
6. **Short put:** payoff = −max(0, X − ST); profit = −max(0, X − ST) + p0; max gain p0, max loss X − p0 (p.42; the loss is derived with ST = 0). **Short call:** payoff −max(0, ST − X), profit that plus c0; max gain c0, loss unlimited (mirror image, p.41).
7. **Comparisons with X = F0(T):** call profit = max[0, ST − F0(T)] − c0, forward is better if ST − F0(T) > −c0. Short put profit = −max[0, F0(T) − ST] + p0, forward is better if ST − F0(T) > p0. p.47–48 (PDF 55, 56).
8. **CDS approximations:** MTM change ≈ ΔCDS spread × notional × effective duration; credit-event payment ≈ LGD(%) × notional. Approximations; buyer gains when spread widens. p.45 (PDF 53).

## Traps
- Payoff is not profit: option profit subtracts the premium, so a positive payoff can still be a loss; forward payoff equals profit (p.27, 40).
- Forward gain uses ST − F0(T), never today's spot.
- "No payment at start" for futures still involves margin; a margin call restores to initial margin, not maintenance (p.30–31).
- Futures and forward totals match but timing differs (p.32–33).
- Swap notional is not exchanged; only the net moves; the fixed payer is the floating receiver (p.36).
- Short put: loss is big but bounded; short call: loss is unlimited (p.42).
- Credit risk in options sits with the buyer, who depends on the writer, not the reverse (p.41).
- CDS buyer is short credit risk and gains when spreads widen; time value is never negative before expiry and is zero at maturity (p.41, 44).

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical; computed here.
1. **Forward.** A buyer agrees to purchase 5,000 bushels at USD 8.00, settled in 3 months. If ST = 8.60: long +0.60 × 5,000 = +3,000; short −3,000. If ST = 7.50: long −0.50 × 5,000 = −2,500. Breakeven ST = 8.00.
2. **Options.** European call X = 50, c0 = 3. ST = 57: payoff 7, profit 4. ST = 52: payoff 2, profit −1. ST = 48: payoff 0, profit −3. Breakeven 53 (54 if premium were 4). Put X = 50, p0 = 2.50, ST = 40: long payoff 10, profit 7.50; short payoff −10, profit −7.50; breakeven 47.50.
3. **Swap.** Notional EUR 50m, fixed 3.00%, annual. MRR 3.40%: fixed payer owes 1.5m, receives 1.7m, nets +EUR 200,000. MRR 2.60%: nets −EUR 200,000.
