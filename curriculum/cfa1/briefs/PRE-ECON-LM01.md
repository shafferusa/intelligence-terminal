## Core principles

- **Elasticity is unit-free.** Raw coefficients depend on units, so use percentage changes. On a straight line the slope is fixed but P/Q is not, so |E| is large at high prices and small at low prices; never quote one elasticity for the whole line. pp.7–8 (PDF 15–16).
- **Elasticity and spending.** Elastic demand: a price cut raises buyers' total spending; inelastic: it lowers it; unit elastic: unchanged. Stated in the Summary, p.28 (PDF 36), not in the elasticity section.
- **Determinants.** Close substitutes, a large budget share, discretionary purchases, narrow definitions and longer adjustment time raise elasticity; durables can break the time pattern. pp.9–10 (PDF 17–18).
- **Signs classify.** Income elasticity: positive normal, negative inferior. Cross-price: positive substitutes, negative complements. Income or related-price changes shift the curve; own-price changes move along it. pp.11–13 (PDF 19–21).
- **Two channels.** A price fall triggers a substitution effect (toward the cheaper good) and an income effect (real income rises). For normal goods both raise quantity; for inferior goods the income effect opposes and may offset the substitution effect. "Inferior" describes income response, not quality. pp.14–16 (PDF 22–24).
- **Marginal product eventually falls.** Cost per extra unit is the wage divided by marginal product, so rising MP lowers marginal cost and falling MP raises it. This underlies U-shaped cost curves (short run, one variable input, fixed wage). pp.17–21, 24 (PDF 25–29, 32).
- **Stop where the last unit stops paying.** Expand while MR exceeds MC; the optimum is MR = MC with MC not falling. A price taker has MR = P; a firm facing a downward-sloping demand curve has MR < P. pp.22–24 (PDF 30–32).
- **Averages follow marginals.** MC below an average pulls it down, above pulls it up, so MC crosses AVC and ATC at their minimums. Minimum ATC is not the profit-maximizing output. pp.24–27 (PDF 32–35).

## Formulas

1. **Demand function:** Q_x = f(P_x, I, P_y). p.4 (PDF 12).
2. **Own-price elasticity:** E = %ΔQ/%ΔP = (ΔQ/ΔP)(P/Q). p.7 (PDF 15).
3. **Income elasticity:** E_I = %ΔQ/%ΔI = (ΔQ/ΔI)(I/Q). p.11 (PDF 19).
4. **Cross-price elasticity:** E = %ΔQ_x/%ΔP_y = (ΔQ/ΔP_y)(P_y/Q). p.11 (PDF 19); also p.13 (PDF 21).
5. **Market demand:** sum of individual demands; n identical households means multiply by n. p.12 (PDF 20).
6. **Total input cost:** TC = wL + rK. p.18 (PDF 26).
7. **Productivity:** AP_L = Q/L; MP_L = ΔQ/ΔL, other inputs fixed. pp.20–21 (PDF 28–29).
8. **Marginal revenue:** MR = ΔTR/ΔQ = P + Q(ΔP/ΔQ). Perfect competition: ΔP/ΔQ = 0 so MR = P; otherwise MR < P. p.23 (PDF 31).
9. **Marginal and variable cost:** MC = ΔTC/ΔQ; SMC = w/MP_L; AVC = TVC/Q = w/AP_L (labor the only variable input, fixed wage). pp.23–24 (PDF 31–32).
10. **Profit rule:** Profit = TR − TC; maximum where MR = MC with MC not falling. pp.22, 24 (PDF 30, 32).
11. **Cost identities:** TC = TFC + TVC; ATC = TC/Q = AFC + AVC; AFC = TFC/Q. pp.24–27 (PDF 32–35).
12. **Expenditure rule:** |E| > 1, price cut raises spending; |E| < 1, lowers it; |E| = 1, unchanged. p.28 (PDF 36).
13. **Breakeven:** TR = TC, equivalently price = ATC. p.30 (PDF 38).

## Traps

- Slope is not elasticity; elasticity varies along a straight line (pp.7–8, PDF 15–16).
- "Inferior" does not mean poor quality; a good can be normal for one group and inferior for another (p.15).
- For an inferior good the income effect opposes the substitution effect (p.16).
- MP can fall while total product still rises; a high average can hide a small latest contribution (p.21).
- Minimum ATC is not the profit-maximizing output; fixed cost persists at zero output (pp.26–27).
- AVC reaches its minimum at lower output than ATC; MC cuts both at their minimums (PDF 33).

## Original practice seeds

1. **(Hypothetical) Elasticity along a line.** Snack-bar demand Q = 100 − 4P. At P = $10, Q = 60, E = −4(10/60) = −0.667 (inelastic); revenue $600, and at $11 it is 56 × 11 = $616, higher. At P = $20, Q = 20, E = −4 (elastic); revenue $400, and at $21 it is 16 × 21 = $336, lower.
2. **(Hypothetical) Three elasticities.** Q_x = 50 − 2P_x + 0.5P_y + 0.01I, with P_x = 5, P_y = 8, I = 1,000. Q = 54. Own E = −2(5/54) = −0.185; cross E = 0.5(8/54) = +0.074 (substitutes); income E = 0.01(1,000/54) = +0.185 (normal). If P_y rises to 12, Q = 56 and cross E = 0.5(12/56) = 0.107.
3. **(Hypothetical) Profit-maximizing output.** A price taker has MC of 4, 3, 5, 7, 9, 12 for units 1–6. At MR = £6, unit 3 (MC 5) is worth making, unit 4 (MC 7) is not, so output is 3. At £8, unit 4 (7 < 8) is worth making, unit 5 (9 > 8) is not, so output is 4.
