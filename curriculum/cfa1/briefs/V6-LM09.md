## Learning outcomes
1. Define spot rates and the spot curve, and price a bond by discounting each cash flow at the spot rate for its own date. Printed pp.214–220 (PDF 224–230).
2. Define par and forward rates; derive par rates from spot rates, forwards from spots, and spots from forwards; price a bond using forwards. Printed pp.221–227 (PDF 231–237).
3. Compare the shapes of the spot, par and forward curves (upward, flat, inverted). Printed pp.228–233 (PDF 238–243).

## Core principles
- **A bond is a bundle of zeros.** Each coupon and the principal is a separate claim at its own date, and the market pays a different rate for each date. Discount each at its own spot rate; that gives the no-arbitrage price, since a dealer could strip and rebuild the bond and pocket any gap, costs aside (pp.211–212, 216).
- **YTM is a blended average, not a spot rate.** One YTM reproduces the total price but not the value of individual cash flows. It sits near the spot rate of the largest, last cash flow. Only a zero's YTM equals its spot rate. Credit risk adds a spread to the spots (pp.216–219).
- **Isolating maturity needs like-for-like bonds.** Credit, liquidity, tax, currency and periodicity also move yields, so curves are built from recent, liquid government issues with straight-line interpolation. (pp.214–215).
- **Par rate = the coupon that makes a hypothetical bond worth 100 on the spot curve.** The coupon exactly repays the discounted claims. One-year par equals one-year spot, since only one cash flow exists. On-the-run yields are close to, not equal to, par rates (p.221).
- **A forward rate is a breakeven reinvestment rate.** Buying a long zero locks in today's long spot; buying a short zero means reinvesting at an unknown future rate. The forward is the rate that makes the two equal. If you expect the future rate to exceed it, the short bond plus reinvestment wins; if below, buy the long bond. (pp.222–224).
- **Spot rates are geometric averages of one-year forwards.** Multiply the growth factors, take the root; never add rates. Pricing with chained forwards gives the same price as spots, or an arbitrage exists. Multi-year forwards can be chained if the periods are consecutive (pp.224–227).
- **Curve shapes follow the spot curve.** Upward spot: par below spot, forward above (the forward is the marginal return from extending a year). Inverted: forward below spot, par above. Flat: all equal. Negative rates do not change the algebra (pp.228–233).

## Vocabulary
- Term (maturity) structure — how yield varies with time to maturity, other factors held equal (p.214).
- Spot rate (zero rate) — yield on a default-free zero from today to a date (pp.211–212, 214).
- Spot (zero, strip) curve — spot rates across maturities; the base for the other two curves (p.214).
- Inverted curve — short yields above long yields (p.215).
- Par rate — coupon, equal to YTM, pricing a hypothetical bond at 100 on the spot curve (p.221).
- On-the-run security — most recently issued government bond; yield near its par rate (p.221).
- Implied forward rate — breakeven reinvestment rate linking a shorter and a longer zero (p.222).
- Forward curve — forward rates for a fixed tenor across start dates (p.228 onward).
- "3y1y" notation — starts in 3 years, lasts 1 year (p.223).

## Formulas
1. **Price from spot rates (Eq. 1, p.217, PDF 227):** PV = PMT/(1+Z1)^1 + PMT/(1+Z2)^2 + ... + (PMT+FV)/(1+ZN)^N. PMT = coupon per period, FV = principal, Z_t = spot rate for t periods, effective per period. Assumes risk matches the spot curve (else add a spread).
2. **Par rate (Eq. 2, p.221, PDF 231):** 100 = PMT/(1+z1)^1 + ... + (PMT+100)/(1+zN)^N; par rate = PMT/100 per period. Closed form (derived in the notes, not in the book): PMT = 100(1 − DF_N)/sum DF_t, with DF_t = 1/(1+z_t)^t. Valid on a coupon date; between dates the flat price is 100.
3. **Implied forward rate (Eq. 3, p.223, PDF 233):** (1+Z_A)^A (1+IFR)^(B−A) = (1+Z_B)^B, so IFR = [(1+Z_B)^B/(1+Z_A)^A]^(1/(B−A)) − 1. A = start (years), B = end, B−A = tenor; annual effective rates.
4. **Spot from one-year forwards (p.225, PDF 235):** (1+Z_N)^N = (1+f_0y1y)(1+f_1y1y)...(1+f_(N−1)y1y). The spot is the geometric mean of the forwards.
5. **Price with forwards (p.225):** PV = sum over t of CF_t / [(1+f_0y1y)...(1+f_(t−1)y1y)]. Equals the spot-based price.
6. **Curve relationships (Exhibit 9, p.231, PDF 241):** upward: par < spot < forward; flat: all equal; inverted: forward < spot < par. A general rule; par is only "near" spot.

## Traps
- Treating YTM as the spot rate, or discounting every cash flow at the YTM and calling it the no-arbitrage price (pp.217–219).
- Par rate versus YTM: a par rate assumes price 100 on the spot curve; YTM fits whatever the actual price is (p.226).
- Reading "3y1y" as maturity 3 or a 3-year tenor. 2y5y and 5y2y differ (p.223).
- Adding forwards to get a spot rate instead of multiplying (1+f) factors and taking the root (p.225).
- Treating the forward as a forecast; it is a breakeven against your own view (p.223).
- Negative rates: the algebra is unchanged, but the usual positive-rate ordering of par versus spot can reverse; test it with the par formula rather than recalling the rule (pp.232, 238).
- Mixed forward chains must cover consecutive periods (p.237).

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical. Annual spots: 1y 2.0%, 2y 2.5%, 3y 3.0%.
- **A. Price and par rate.** Discount factors 0.98039, 0.95181, 0.91514 (sum 2.84734). A 4% annual 3-year bond: 4 × 2.84734 + 100 × 0.91514 = 102.90. Par rate = (1 − 0.91514)/2.84734 = 2.980%, below the 3% three-year spot, as an upward curve implies.
- **B. Forward and a changed input.** 2y1y = 1.03^3/1.025^2 − 1 = 4.007%. Raise only the 3-year spot to 3.5%: 2y1y = 1.108718/1.050625 − 1 = 5.529%, because the longer maturity's extra return must be earned in one added year. Par rises to 3.460% and the 4% bond falls to 101.53.
- **C. Pricing with forwards.** One-year forwards 1%, 2%, 3%: cumulative factors 1.01, 1.0302, 1.061106. A 3-year 1% bond = 1/1.01 + 1/1.0302 + 101/1.061106 = 97.14. Implied 2y spot 1.499%; 3y spot 1.997%.
