3. Describe matrix pricing. Taught in Matrix Pricing, printed pp.148–152 (PDF 158–162).

## Core principles

- **Price is a present value.** Coupons and principal are fixed at issue, so only the discount rate (and time) moves the price. Required rate above the coupon rate gives a discount; below gives a premium; equal gives par. Assumes no default. p.132 (PDF 142).
- **YTM is the price run backwards.** The single rate equating PV of promised flows to the observed price (an IRR). It is earned only if the bond is held to maturity, every payment arrives, and coupons are reinvested at that yield. No closed form for a coupon bond: use a calculator, IRR or spreadsheet. pp.134–135 (PDF 144–145).
- **Buyer pays the full price; screens show the flat price.** The buyer compensates the seller through accrued interest on top of the flat price. Accrued interest depends only on days elapsed, period length and coupon, not on yield, so yield changes move the flat price, not the accrued interest. pp.136–137 (PDF 146–147).
- **Between coupons, discount from the last coupon date, then roll forward** at the periodic yield by the fraction of the period elapsed. p.138 (PDF 148).
- **Sensitivity rules.** Price and yield move oppositely. For the same yield change, a lower coupon or longer maturity usually gives a larger percentage price move. The rare exception is a long-dated, low-coupon bond at a discount; zero-coupon, par and premium bonds always obey the maturity rule. pp.144–145 (PDF 154–155).
- **Pull to par and convexity.** At unchanged yield, a discount bond rises and a premium bond falls toward par with time (no default). A yield fall raises price by more than an equal rise cuts it. pp.145–147 (PDF 155–157).
- **Matrix pricing fills in missing prices.** For thinly traded or new bonds: pick actively traded comparables of similar maturity, coupon and credit quality, compute and average their YTMs by maturity, interpolate linearly, discount the target's flows at that yield. For a new issue it estimates a spread over a benchmark. Screen quotes are often estimates, not last trades. pp.149–152 (PDF 159–162).

## Vocabulary

- Market discount rate / required yield — return demanded for the bond's risk; the discounting rate (p.132).
- Par, discount, premium bond — price equal to, below, above face value (p.134).
- Yield-to-maturity — the one rate equating PV of promised flows to price (p.134).
- Flat (clean, quoted) price — excludes accrued interest (p.136).
- Accrued interest — seller's earned but unpaid share of the next coupon (p.137).
- Full (dirty, invoice) price — flat price plus accrued interest (p.136).
- Settlement date — date of payment and delivery (p.136).
- Day count (30/360, actual/actual) — day-counting convention (p.137).
- Constant-yield price trajectory / pull to par — drift toward face value at unchanged yield (pp.145–146).
- Matrix pricing — estimating price or yield from comparables (p.149).
- Required yield spread — YTM minus a benchmark government yield of similar maturity (p.151).
- Negative yield — YTM below zero (p.135).

## Formulas

Rates per period unless stated; prices per 100 of par.

1. Eq.1: PV = PMT_t / (1+r)^t. PMT_t = cash flow in period t; r = periodic discount rate. p.132 (PDF 142).
2. Eq.2: PV = PMT_1/(1+r)^1 + PMT_2/(1+r)^2 + ... + (PMT_N + FV_N)/(1+r)^N. FV = face value; N = periods to maturity. Valid on a coupon date. p.132 (PDF 142).
3. Spreadsheet: =PV(rate, nper, pmt, FV, type) (minus sign for a positive price; type 0 = end-of-period); =YIELD(settlement, maturity, rate, pr, redemption, frequency, [basis]) with rate = coupon rate, pr = price per 100; IRR also gives YTM. YIELD fails for zero-coupon bonds. pp.133–135 (PDF 143–145).
4. Zero-coupon yield: r = (FV/PV)^(1/t) − 1, t in years. p.136 (PDF 146).
5. Eq.3: PV_Full = PV_Flat + AI. p.136 (PDF 146).
6. Eq.4: AI = (t/T) × PMT. t = days from previous coupon to settlement; T = days in the coupon period (same day-count rule); PMT = annual coupon / frequency. p.137 (PDF 147).
7. Eq.5: PV_Full = PMT/(1+r)^(1−t/T) + PMT/(1+r)^(2−t/T) + ... + (PMT+FV)/(1+r)^(N−t/T). N = coupons remaining at the start of the current period. p.138 (PDF 148).
8. Eq.6: PV_Full = PV × (1+r)^(t/T), PV = Eq.2 value at the previous coupon date (not the flat price). p.138 (PDF 148).
9. Matrix pricing: interpolated yield = y_short + (m − m_short)/(m_long − m_short) × (y_long − y_short); then Eq.2 at that yield (periodic rate = annual yield / frequency). Required spread = bond's YTM − interpolated benchmark yield. pp.150–151 (PDF 160–161).
10. Annualisation: stated annual rate = periodic rate × periods per year (simple). p.134 (PDF 144).

## Traps

- Quoted price is the flat price; the buyer pays the full price (pp.136–137).
- Accrued interest ignores YTM (p.137).
- "Discount" for a bond price is unrelated to "discount rate" (p.132).
- Price with periodic coupon and periodic rate; double a semiannual rate to quote the annual one (p.134).
- In Eq.6, PV is the value at the previous coupon date, not the flat price (p.138).
- YTM is not a guaranteed return (p.134).
- "Longer maturity always means bigger moves" fails only for long, low-coupon discount bonds (p.145).
- Pull to par is time, not a yield effect. Matrix pricing uses comparable cash bonds, not derivatives (pp.145–146, 149).

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

1. A 2-year bond, par 100, pays 4 at the end of year 1 and 104 at the end of year 2, priced on the issue date. At 5%: 4/1.05 + 104/1.1025 = 98.141 (discount). At 3%: 4/1.03 + 104/1.0609 = 101.913 (premium).
2. A 5-year, 3% semiannual bond (1.50 per period, 10 periods), YTM 4% (2% per period), settled 60 days into a 180-day period, 30/360. PV at last coupon date = 95.509. Full = 95.509 × 1.02^(60/180) = 96.141. AI = (60/180) × 1.50 = 0.500. Flat = 95.641. If yield rose to 5%, AI would stay 0.500.
3. A 3-year zero at 4% is 100/1.04^3 = 88.900; at 5% it is 86.384 (−2.83%). A 3-year 4% annual coupon bond falls from 100.000 to 97.277 (−2.72%).
4. Comparable average YTMs are 2.00% at 2 years and 3.00% at 5 years; for a 3-year target, yield = 2.00 + (1/3) × 1.00 = 2.333%.
