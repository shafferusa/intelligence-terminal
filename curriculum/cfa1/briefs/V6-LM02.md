## Core principles
- **Bullet is the baseline.** Level coupons, principal at the end (p.26). Ask what moves earlier, later or up and down, and who bears that risk.
- **Amortizing trades credit risk for reinvestment risk.** Less principal outstanding cuts the lender's exposure, but larger early cash flows must be reinvested at possibly lower rates. Stronger for full than partial amortization (pp.27–32). Sinking funds and waterfalls (senior tranche paid first) share this flavor (p.32).
- **Floaters remove most rate risk, not credit risk.** Coupon = reference rate plus spread, reset each period; price stays near par, yet worse credit still lowers it (pp.32–33). Step-ups shift the coupon on dates or events; PIK grows principal instead of paying cash, so it demands a higher rate (pp.33–34).
- **Index-linked bonds pass inflation to the holder.** Capital-indexed: principal adjusts and the fixed coupon rate applies to it; maturity payoff is usually at least par. Interest-indexed: principal fixed, coupon carries the adjustment (pp.35–36).
- **Zero and deferred coupons concentrate return late.** A zero has nothing to reinvest, suiting liability matching. Deferred coupons suit issuers short of early cash; zero is the extreme (p.36).
- **Embedded options shift price and yield by holder.** A call helps the issuer, so the investor wants more yield; price is capped near the call price when yield is below the coupon, and the call matters little above it. A put is the mirror: a price floor when yield is above the coupon, higher price and lower yield than an option-free bond. A convertible behaves like a straight bond when the stock is far below the conversion price and like the shares when far above; its low coupon pays for the option, not for lower risk (pp.39–41).
- **CoCos and warrants differ from ordinary conversion.** A CoCo converts automatically on a downside trigger, protecting the issuer, so it yields more. A warrant is a separately traded option attached to a bond (p.42).
- **Currency matters more than venue; tax follows timing.** Price depends on the rates of the denomination currency more than the place of issue (p.47). OID bonds pay nothing before maturity yet may be taxed on yearly accrual, or only at maturity as a gain, by jurisdiction (pp.47–48).

## Vocabulary
- Bullet bond: all principal on the final date (p.26).
- Fully / partially amortizing: principal repaid over the life; partial leaves a balloon (pp.27–28).
- Balloon payment: lump sum of remaining principal at maturity (p.28).
- Sinking fund: scheduled early retirement of part of an issue (p.32). Waterfall: ranked tranches paid principal in sequence (p.32).
- Market reference rate (MRR): benchmark a floating coupon resets to (p.32).
- Step-up coupon: coupon rises on dates or events (p.33). Credit-linked note: coupon tied to covenants or ratings (p.33).
- Payment-in-kind (PIK): interest added to principal (p.34).
- Capital-indexed / interest-indexed: inflation adjusts principal / only the coupon (pp.35–36). Real rate is roughly nominal minus inflation (p.35).
- Zero-coupon bond: no coupons; return is the discount; can be created by stripping (p.36). Deferred coupon bond: little or no early interest (p.36).
- Contingency provision (embedded option): a right, not separately tradable (p.39).
- Call protection period / make-whole call: no calls allowed / call at a price tied to a sovereign yield plus spread (p.40).
- Conversion ratio / price / value (pp.40–41). Warrant, CoCo (p.42).
- Domestic / foreign / Eurobond / global bond: see Traps (pp.44–46). Sukuk: Shari'a-compliant, returns from assets not interest (pp.44–45).
- OID: par minus issue price (p.47).

## Formulas
1. **Level payment:** A = r × Principal / (1 − (1 + r)^(−N)). A = payment per period; r = annual rate / periods per year; N = number of payments; level payments in arrears. Partial amortization: use Principal minus PV of balloon, PV = balloon / (1 + r)^N. pp.29–30 (PDF 39–40).
2. **Period split:** interest = r × opening balance; principal repaid = A − interest; new balance = old balance − principal repaid. p.30 (PDF 40).
3. **FRN:** coupon = MRR + credit spread; period interest = coupon × principal / periods per year. pp.32–33 (PDF 42–43).
4. **Capital-indexed:** adjustment = (CPI_new − CPI_old) / CPI_old; adjusted principal = principal × (1 + adjustment); semiannual coupon = coupon rate × adjusted principal / 2. Deflation lowers principal. pp.35–36 (PDF 45–46).
5. **Conversion ratio** = par value / conversion price. Eq. 2, p.41 (PDF 51).
6. **Conversion value** = conversion ratio × current share price. Eq. 3, p.41 (PDF 51). **Conversion premium** = (conversion price − share price) / share price (same page; inline, unnumbered).
7. **OID** = par − issue price. p.47 (PDF 57); applied p.48 (PDF 58).

## Traps
- Amortizing lowers credit risk but raises reinvestment risk; never both lowered (pp.27, 32).
- A partial amortizer's last payment is the final level payment plus the balloon (p.31). Use per-period rates and count periods, not years (pp.30–31).
- A floater still carries credit risk (p.33).
- A step-up coupon is a cash-flow feature, not an embedded option (pp.33, 39).
- Call: yield below coupon caps upside; above, it barely matters. Put is the reverse. Put and conversion raise price and lower yield; a call lowers price and raises yield (pp.40, 43).
- A CoCo converts on a capital-ratio trigger, not a share-price trigger (p.42).
- "Domestic" refers to the issuer's incorporation, not the investor's residence (pp.44–46). "Eurobond" means outside any one country's jurisdiction, any currency, not "in euros" (p.45). The introduction's looser wording (p.24) differs; trust the section.
- Capital-indexed adjusts principal; interest-indexed only the coupon (p.36). OID tax is on accrual, not cash (pp.47–48).

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
1. **Level payment.** EUR100,000 loan, 4% a year, 3 annual year-end payments. A = 4,000 / (1 − 1.04^−3) = 4,000 / 0.111004 ≈ 36,034.9. Year 1: interest 4,000, principal 32,034.9, balance 67,965.1. Year 2: interest 2,718.6, principal 33,316.3, balance 34,648.8. Year 3: interest 1,386.0, principal about 34,648.9, balance about 0. At 6%, A = 6,000 / 0.160381 ≈ 37,411.0, about 1,376 higher.
2. **Capital-indexed coupon.** Principal 1,000,000, 2% coupon semiannual. CPI 200 to 202: +1.0%, adjusted principal 1,010,000, coupon = 0.02 × 1,010,000 / 2 = 10,100. If CPI falls to 198: principal 990,000, coupon 9,900.
3. **Convertible.** Par 1,000, conversion price 50, ratio 20. Share price 60: conversion value 1,200. Share price 40: value 800, so straight-bond value dominates. Premium at 40 = (50 − 40)/40 = 25%.
