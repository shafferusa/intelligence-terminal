## Learning outcomes

1. Compute and interpret present value of bonds and stocks from expected cash flows (printed 127–148, PDF 137–158).
2. Compute and interpret implied return of bonds, and required return and implied growth of stocks (printed 149–161, PDF 159–171).
3. Explain cash flow additivity, no-arbitrage, and their use for forward rates, forward FX rates and option values (printed 162–178, PDF 172–188).

## Core principles

- **Value is discounted cash flow.** A higher required rate lowers price because the same payments are worth less today. Bond payments are contractual (issuer pays investor); dividends are discretionary, so equity value fails if forecasts fail. Rate and periods must share a unit (pp.127, 135, 138).
- **Zeros converge on par.** Investor pays now and receives face later. At a negative yield the investor pays above face (safety, regulation, policy) and the premium amortizes down to par (pp.130–132).
- **Coupon vs required return sets par, premium or discount.** Floaters reset to reference rate plus margin; perpetuals have no principal; level-payment loans start mostly interest and shift to principal (pp.133–139).
- **Equity value rides on r − g.** Gordon needs r > g and the next dividend; a thin spread makes price fragile. Two-stage adds a terminal value, discounted back n periods (pp.141–145).
- **Implied return reverses pricing.** Price plus promised flows reveal the rate. YTM assumes hold to maturity, no default, reinvestment at YTM; selling early after a rate rise earns less (pp.149–155). For stocks, r = next dividend yield + g, so price pins only r − g (pp.156–158).
- **Additivity and no-arbitrage.** Equivalent strategies have zero-PV difference, given one constant rate. Identical payoffs must cost the same, else borrow cheap, lend dear, lock a free profit (pp.162–164).
- **Forwards from two riskless routes.** Lend two years vs one year then a rate fixed today; or stay home vs convert, earn foreign rate, convert back at a forward. Rates and spot pin the forward; it is no forecast (pp.164–169).
- **Options by replication.** Combine asset and option so payoff is equal in both states; that portfolio is riskless. Option value needs no probabilities; it fails if payoffs cannot be replicated (pp.171–174).

## Vocabulary

- Required return / discount rate — return demanded for risk and timing (p.127).
- Continuous compounding — limit of ever more frequent compounding (p.128).
- Annualized return — holding-period return rescaled to a year (pp.128–129).
- Yield-to-maturity — one rate discounting promised bond flows to price (p.129).
- Zero-coupon bond — single payment at maturity (p.129).
- Stripped bond — coupon or principal flows traded as zeros (p.130).
- Accretion / amortization — price drifting to par from below or above (pp.131–132).
- Floating-rate bond — coupon is reference rate plus fixed margin (p.136).
- Perpetual bond — no maturity (pp.136–137).
- Level-payment loan — equal payments of interest plus principal (p.129).
- Terminal value — value at end of explicit forecast of later dividends (p.144).
- Implied return — rate equating discounted flows to price (p.149).
- Forward P/E — price over next-period earnings (p.158).
- No-arbitrage principle — identical payoffs, identical prices (p.162).
- Forward rate F(1,2) — rate fixed today for a one-year loan starting in a year (p.165).
- Hedge ratio / replicating portfolio — asset units equalizing payoffs / assets mimicking a payoff (p.171).
- Real option — right to take a later business action (p.175).

## Formulas

- Eq.1/3: FV = PV(1+r)^t; PV = FV(1+r)^(−t) (pp.127–128). r per period.
- Eq.2/4: FV = PV e^(rt); PV = FV e^(−rt) (p.128). r annual, t years.
- Annualized = (1 + R_period)^(periods per year) − 1 (p.128). Assumes same reinvestment.
- Eq.5 zero: PV = FV/(1+r)^t (p.130).
- Eq.6 coupon bond: PV = PMT/r × [1 − 1/(1+r)^T] + FV/(1+r)^T (p.133). Per-period r and PMT; semiannual: halve both, double periods.
- Eq.7: PV = PMT/r (p.137).
- Eq.8: PMT = r × PV_0 / [1 − (1+r)^(−t)] (p.138). Interest = prior balance × r.
- Eq.9/10: PV = Div_(t+1)/r (p.142).
- Eq.13/14: PV_t = Div_t(1+g)/(r−g) = Div_(t+1)/(r−g) (p.143). Needs r > g, constant g forever.
- Eq.15–18: PV_0 = Σ(i=1..n) Div_0(1+g_s)^i/(1+r)^i + E[S_n]/(1+r)^n; E[S_n] = Div_(n+1)/(r − g_l); Div_(n+1) = Div_0(1+g_s)^n(1+g_l) (p.144).
- Eq.19: r = (FV_t/PV_0)^(1/t) − 1 (p.150). Lump sum.
- Coupon YTM: price = Σ PMT/(1+r)^k + FV/(1+r)^n, solved by RATE or IRR (p.154).
- Realized return: r = (FV_n/PV_0)^(1/n) − 1, FV_n = coupons grown at reinvestment rate + ending price (p.154).
- Eq.20–22: r − g = Div_(t+1)/PV_t; r = Div_(t+1)/PV_t + g; g = r − Div_(t+1)/PV_t (p.156).
- Eq.23/24: trailing P/E = (Div_t/E_t)(1+g)/(r−g); forward P/E = (Div_(t+1)/E_(t+1))/(r−g) (p.158).
- Additivity: PV(A+B) = PV(A) + PV(B) (pp.162–164). One constant rate.
- Eq.25/26: F_(1,2) = (1+r2)^2/(1+r1) − 1 (p.165). Annual, risk-free, no costs. Two-year forward in a year: (1+S_3)^3 = (1+S_1)(1+F)^2 (p.184).
- FX: F(P/B) = S(P/B) × exp[(r_P − r_B)T]; P per 1 B; T years (pp.167–169).
- Hedge ratio: h = (c_up − c_down)/(S_up − S_down); V_0 = hS_0 − c_0 = V_1/(1+r) for a short call; put: h = (p_down − p_up)/(S_up − S_down), V_0 = hS_0 + p_0 (pp.171–174).
- Real option = (NPV with − NPV without) − PV(option cost) (p.175); see slips.

## Traps

- Semiannual or monthly flows with annual rate or periods (pp.135, 138).
- Next-year bond price uses shortened maturity; total return includes coupon (pp.122–123).
- Gordon with Div_0 instead of Div_1 (p.143).
- Terminal value undiscounted or discounted the wrong years (pp.124, 183).
- Negative yield: price above par, falling to par; par/premium/discount depends on coupon vs yield, not face (pp.132, 134).
- YTM equals realized return only under its three assumptions (p.153).
- Forward rate is not r2 − r1 or an average; FX forward is no forecast; check quote direction (pp.168, 185).
- Option value is not a probability-weighted payoff (p.171).
- Required return is yield plus growth, not their product (p.183).

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.
1. Price 50, next dividend 2.00, g = 3%: r = 2/50 + 3% = 7.0%. At required 9%, implied g = 9% − 4% = 5.0%. With payout 50%, forward P/E = 0.5/0.04 = 12.5; at r = 8%, 0.5/0.05 = 10.
2. Spot rates 3% (1 year), 4% (2 years): F(1,2) = 1.0816/1.03 − 1 = 5.01%. Check: 100 × 1.03 × 1.0501 = 108.16.
3. Asset 100 goes to 120 or 90; call strike 100 pays 20 or 0; r = 5%. h = 20/30 = 2/3. Long 2/3 asset, short call: 60 either way; 60/1.05 = 57.14; c_0 = 66.67 − 57.14 = 9.52.
