## Core principles
- **Risk management is choosing risk, not minimising it.** Decide the wanted level, measure the actual level, close the gap, to raise value or utility. The work is done before the event: it cannot stop a loss but removes surprise about its size (stress tests, an earlier board discussion). Not prediction, not pure caution. pp.241–243.
- **Driver, position, exposure differ.** Driver is the underlying uncertainty, position the amount held, exposure (simply) their product. Whoever holds the position bears the loss; no position, no exposure. p.242.
- **A framework is tailored machinery.** Seven parts: governance; identification and measurement; infrastructure; policies and processes; monitoring, mitigation, management; communication; strategic analysis. A loop: exposure out of line with tolerance triggers modification and re-measurement. Systems add error risk, so test them. pp.243–248.
- **Governance runs top-down and sees the whole balance sheet.** The board sets goals, appetite and authority; management picks activities. A pension fund ignoring liabilities, or an individual ignoring human capital, misjudges risk. A dedicated CRO (not the CEO) and an operating-level committee. pp.249–252.
- **Tolerance, then budget, then exposures.** Tolerance (set in calm times, from inside failure points and outside drivers) says what loss can be borne; budgeting converts it into limits on chosen metrics (standard deviation, beta, VaR, scenario loss), allocated by risk characteristics. Budgeting neither requires nor forbids hedging. pp.252–256.
- **Risks compound non-linearly.** A market move creates credit exposure, then settlement and legal issues. Wrong-way risk: the counterparty is likeliest to fail when it owes you. Leverage, illiquidity and funding stress together cost far more than separately; hence scenarios. pp.263–265.
- **Four modification methods, none dominant.** Avoid (forgo the reward); accept (self-insure within tolerance, diversify); transfer (insurer pools uncorrelated risks, insured pays premium); shift (derivatives reshape outcomes; forward locks the result with no upfront cash, option keeps upside for a premium). Choose by cost against benefit relative to tolerance. pp.272–281.
- **VaR is a floor on the bad tail.** Three elements: amount, period, probability; silent on how bad the tail gets. pp.269–270.

## Vocabulary
- Risk driver / position / exposure — uncertainty source / amount at stake / value change (p.242).
- Risk management framework — infrastructure, process, analytics (p.243).
- Risk appetite / tolerance — losses and shortfalls accepted (p.252).
- Risk budgeting — tolerable risk spread across activities in numbers (p.254).
- Liquidity risk — selling price shock, beyond the known spread (p.258).
- Settlement risk — paying without receiving; a timing issue (p.259).
- Solvency risk — cash runs out though assets exceed debts (p.262).
- Mortality / longevity risk — dying young / outliving assets (p.263).
- Wrong-way risk — exposure rises as the counterparty weakens (p.264).
- Delta, gamma, vega, rho — sensitivity to price, delta, volatility, rates (p.269).
- VaR / CVaR — minimum tail loss / average loss beyond it (pp.269–270).
- Scenario analysis / stress testing — themed package / extreme rare moves (p.270).
- Self-insurance — retain the risk, perhaps with a reserve (p.273).
- Risk shifting — reshaping outcomes, mainly with derivatives (p.276).

## Formulas
1. Risk exposure = risk position x risk driver (p.242). Currency units x percent. Stylised case only.
2. R_P = w1 x R1 + (1 − w1) x [(1 + R_lc) x (1 + R_FX) − 1] (p.277, PDF 287). w1 domestic weight, R_lc foreign local-currency return, R_FX currency return. Expected values.
3. σ_p = sqrt(w1²σ1² + w2²σ2² + w3²σ3² + 2ρ12w1w2σ1σ2 + 2ρ13w1w3σ1σ3 + 2ρ23w2w3σ2σ3) (pp.277–278, PDF 287–288). Weights may sum above 1 when currency is an overlay.
4. After a full currency hedge: σ_p = sqrt(w1²σ1² + w2²σ2² + 2w1w2ρ12σ1σ2) (p.278, PDF 288). Currency term drops out.
5. Hedge cost: forward-versus-spot gap times hedged weight lowers expected return (p.278).

## Traps
- "Minimise risk" or "predict events" as the aim (p.242).
- Calling the known spread the liquidity risk; the risk is the cost ballooning (p.258).
- Reading VaR as a maximum loss (pp.269–270).
- Solvency risk is not insolvency; settlement risk is timing, not credit quality (pp.259, 262).
- Blurring roles: the CRO leads risk and the committee works at operating level beneath the board (pp.251–252).
- Thinking risk budgeting requires, or forbids, hedging (pp.254–256).
- Insurance is transfer, derivatives are shifting, reserves are acceptance (pp.273–276).
- A full hedge is imperfect: the hedged value drifts (pp.278–279).

## Original practice seeds
1. Hypothetical: 5,000,000 euro receivable, driver 2% move: exposure 5,000,000 x 2% = 100,000. Hedge 3,000,000: 2,000,000 x 2% = 40,000, a 60% cut.
2. Hypothetical: one-day 5% VaR of $2.0m. Over 250 days expect 250 x 0.05 = 12.5 days with loss of at least $2.0m; no cap on how much worse.
3. Hypothetical wrong-way put: pays 500 in a crash state (10% probability), expected 50. Seller default 2% independent: 50 x 0.98 = 49. Seller default 30% in the crash: 0.10 x 500 x 0.70 = 35. Correlation costs 14 more.
