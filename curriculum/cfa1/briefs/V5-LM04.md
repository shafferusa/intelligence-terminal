## Learning outcomes

1. Describe dividends, share repurchases, stock splits and reverse splits and why companies use them. Taught in Dividends, Share Repurchases, and Stock Splits, pp.78–84 (PDF 88–94).
2. Describe the dividend date sequence and what happens to the share price around it. Taught in Dividend Payment Chronology, pp.85–88 (PDF 95–98).
3. Calculate price return and total return, and relate today's price to expected future cash flows. Taught in Equity Total Return, pp.89–98 (PDF 99–108).

## Core principles

- **No contractual payout.** Owners get price change plus whatever the board distributes, with no maturity. Dividends are discretionary, never legally required, even when profits are high. pp.75–76.
- **Dividends signal.** Steady or rising payouts signal confidence; a cut usually signals weak prospects, unless it is really a reinvestment choice. Young growth firms retain cash; mature firms pay. pp.78–79.
- **Buyback is roughly a dividend in aggregate, not for each holder.** Only voluntary sellers get cash; non-sellers own a larger percentage and the share count and EPS denominator shrink. Reasons: signal undervaluation, flexibility, tax efficiency, offsetting option-driven share creation, lifting EPS. Equivalence fails with taxes and signalling. pp.80–81.
- **Splits and own-share stock dividends move units, not value.** Price scales inversely; market cap and ownership percentages stay put. Motive: a practical trading range (low price means large percentage bid-ask cost; high price excludes small investors). A reverse split can meet an exchange minimum price. A spin-off differs: holders receive shares of another entity. pp.82–84.
- **Ex-date is where the price drops.** A buyer on or after the ex-date misses the dividend, so, other things equal, the price falls by about the dividend that day, not on declaration or payment. The record date follows by about the settlement lag. Observed change = dividend effect + market move + company-specific residual (beta of 1 assumed, taxes ignored). pp.85–88.
- **Total return counts cash and price.** Return = price change plus distributions, over the starting price. A reinvested mid-period dividend buys fractional shares at that day's price, so later price risk applies to them: reinvesting helps if the price then rises and hurts if it falls. Splits require restating price and dividend consistently. pp.89–93.
- **Price is the PV of expected receipts.** Price = PV of expected dividends plus expected end-of-horizon price at r_e. Higher r_e lowers price; a newly announced dividend with an unchanged price target raises today's fair price by its PV. For typical horizons the terminal price dominates; for a constant dividend held forever, dividends dominate. pp.94–96.

## Vocabulary

- Regular cash dividend: cash at predictable intervals (p.78, PDF 88).
- Special (extraordinary) dividend: one-off, alone or on top of the regular one (p.80, PDF 90).
- Liquidating dividend: return of capital on winding up or selling a business; can exceed retained earnings (p.80, PDF 90).
- Share repurchase (buyback): firm buys its own shares from willing sellers (p.80, PDF 90).
- Stock dividend (bonus issue): extra shares instead of cash (p.82, PDF 92).
- Stock split (forward split): more shares at a proportionally lower price (p.82, PDF 92).
- Reverse stock split: fewer shares at a proportionally higher price (p.83, PDF 93).
- Declaration, ex-dividend, record and payment dates: the four dates, in that order (p.85, PDF 95).
- Settlement: the days after a trade when shares and cash change hands (p.85, PDF 95).
- Reinvested dividends: dividends used to buy more shares of the same issuer (p.91, PDF 101).
- Fractional share: part of one share, from reinvestment or reverse splits (p.93, PDF 103).
- Required return on equity (r_e): discount rate shareholders demand (p.94, PDF 104).

## Formulas

1. **Eq.1 (p.86, PDF 96):** closing price change = −DPS + overall market change + company-specific change. Work in percentages of the prior close; dividend term = −D / prior close. Assumes one-for-one movement with the index; ignores taxes.
2. **Eq.2 (p.89, PDF 99):** r_E = (P_{t+1} − P_t) / P_t. No dividends or restructuring; split-adjusted prices.
3. **Eq.3 (p.90, PDF 100):** r_E = (P_{t+1} − P_t + D_{t+1}) / P_t. Dividend at period end; price and dividend in the same split-adjusted units; taxes ignored.
4. **Eq.4 (p.92, PDF 102):** r_E = (P_{t+1} × (1 + D/P_PD) − P_t) / P_t. D received mid-period at date PD and reinvested at once at P_PD; later dividends ignored; fractional shares allowed. Text on p.93 writes S_PD for the same price. If the dividend is instead invested at a risk-free rate, replace D with its future value in the Eq.3 form.
5. **Eq.5 (p.94, PDF 104):** P_t = E(P_{t+1}) / (1 + r_e). No-dividend stock, one period.
6. **Eq.6 (p.95, PDF 105):** P_t = [E(P_{t+1}) + E(D_{t+1})] / (1 + r_e). Dividend at period end.
7. **Eq.7 (p.95, PDF 105):** P_t = Σ_{i=1..n} D / (1 + r_e)^i + E(P_{t+n}) / (1 + r_e)^n. Constant D at each period end; r_e must match period length. Growing dividends: D_i = D_0(1+g)^i.
8. Supporting: shares repurchased = cash / price (p.81, PDF 91); price after an X:1 split = old price / X (p.84, PDF 94); multi-period return = (1+r1)(1+r2) − 1 (p.97, PDF 107); price-only share of total return = price index / total-return index (p.94, PDF 104).

## Traps

- "Profit means a dividend": it is discretionary (p.76, PDF 86).
- "A split creates value": market cap and ownership share are unchanged (pp.82–83, p.99).
- Placing the drop on declaration or payment day: it is the ex-date (pp.85–86).
- Confusing ex-date and record date: record follows by the settlement lag (p.85).
- Attributing the whole ex-date move to the dividend: subtract market and company parts (p.86).
- Adjusting only price or only dividend for a split; scale both (pp.96–97).
- Summing yearly returns instead of compounding (p.97).
- Mismatched periods: r_e and dividend timing must share a period basis.
- Treating a PV-of-terminal-price question as a return: it asks a share of today's price (p.103).
- "Repurchase pays everyone": only sellers get cash (p.80).

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

**A. Ex-date decomposition.** Stock closes at 50.00 before ex-date, dividend 1.00, index falls 0.5%, closes 49.00. Total −2.00%; dividend −1.00/50 = −2.00%; market −0.50%; company-specific = −2.00 − (−2.00 − 0.50) = +0.50%. A flat index would give 0.00%.

**B. Reinvestment.** Buy at 40. Mid-year dividend 2 at price 50, reinvested: 2/50 = 0.04 extra shares. Year-end 55: 55 × 1.04 = 57.20, return 17.20/40 = 43.0%. As cash: (55 − 40 + 2)/40 = 42.5%. If year-end were 45: reinvested (46.80 − 40)/40 = 17.0% vs cash 17.5%.

**C. Price from PV.** Target 60 in 2 years, dividend 3 at each year-end, r_e = 10%: 3/1.10 + 3/1.21 + 60/1.21 = 2.727 + 2.479 + 49.587 = 54.79. At 12%: 2.679 + 2.392 + 47.832 = 52.90.
