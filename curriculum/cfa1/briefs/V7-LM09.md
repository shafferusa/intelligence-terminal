## Learning outcomes
1. Explain put–call parity for European options (why call plus zero-coupon bond must be priced like put plus underlying). Taught in Put–Call Parity (pp.203–206), Option Strategies (pp.207–210) and Firm Value (pp.213–217).
2. Explain put–call forward parity for European options (a forward plus a bond stands in for the cash underlying). Taught in Put–Call Forward Parity (pp.211–213) and applied in Firm Value.

## Core principles
- **Equal payoffs, equal prices.** Combine instruments so that the final payoff is the same in every state of the world; with no arbitrage, today's cost must then also be the same. Scope: European options, no income, no frictions, borrowing and lending at r. If prices differ, sell the dear package, buy the cheap one, and keep the gap (pp.203–207).
- **Fiduciary call versus protective put.** Investor A holds a call plus a zero-coupon bond paying the strike X at expiry. Investor B holds the asset plus a put struck at X. Both end with the larger of X and the final asset price, so both cost the same today (pp.204–206).
- **Parity is a four-block identity.** Call, put, underlying and bond: any one equals a combination of the other three. Moving a term across the equals sign flips long and short. Put = long call + long bond + short underlying; underlying = long call + short put + long bond. A covered call (long asset, short call) behaves like a long bond plus a short put: premium kept, upside given up, downside retained (pp.207–210).
- **Forward replaces cash.** A long forward plus a bond whose face value is the forward price replicates owning the asset, because the bond's present value equals today's spot when there is no income. So parity still holds with the discounted forward price in place of spot. Rearranged, the put-minus-call gap depends only on strike versus forward price: put dearer when X is above the forward, cheaper when below (pp.211–212).
- **Equity is a call on the firm.** With zero-coupon debt of face D due at T, equity holds a call struck at D (floor zero, open upside). Debt equals a risk-free bond minus a put the debtholders have effectively sold to shareholders (pp.213–215).
- **The put prices credit risk.** The extra yield compensates debtholders for the short put. Higher leverage or riskier assets raise the put's value: riskier debt, lower debt value. The setup assumes a single zero-coupon debt issue (pp.213–215).

## Vocabulary
- Put–call parity — the no-arbitrage price link between a European call, put, underlying and bond with matching strike and expiry (p.206).
- Fiduciary call — long call plus a bond paying the strike at expiry (p.204).
- Protective put — long underlying plus long put; insurance with a floor at X (p.205).
- Covered call — long underlying plus short call; equals a bond minus a put (p.209).
- Put–call forward parity — parity with the discounted forward price replacing spot (p.211).
- Synthetic underlying — long forward plus a bond with face value equal to the forward price (p.211).
- Synthetic protective put — that synthetic underlying plus a long put (p.211).
- Zero-coupon bond value, X(1+r)^−T — today's value of a single payment of X at T (pp.204–206).
- Solvency / insolvency — firm value above or below the debt's face value at maturity (pp.213–214).
- Risky debt — debt whose full face is paid only if the firm is solvent; equals risk-free debt minus a put (p.214).
- Credit spread — extra yield over the risk-free rate compensating lenders for insolvency risk, linked to the put's value (p.215).

## Formulas
Common assumptions: European options, same underlying, strike X and expiry T (years), no income, no frictions, risk-free rate r (decimal per year).
1. Put–call parity (Eq. 1): S0 + p0 = c0 + X(1+r)^−T (p.206). S0 spot, p0 put, c0 call, X strike.
2. Put (Eq. 2): p0 = c0 + X(1+r)^−T − S0 (p.208).
3. Underlying (Eq. 3): S0 = c0 − p0 + X(1+r)^−T (p.208).
4. Call (rearranged, not numbered): c0 = S0 + p0 − X(1+r)^−T (solution to Q1, p.220).
5. Covered call equivalence: S0 − c0 = X(1+r)^−T − p0 (p.209).
6. Forward parity (Eq. 4): F0(T)(1+r)^−T + p0 = c0 + X(1+r)^−T (p.211). F0(T) is the forward price agreed today for delivery at T; with no income, F0(T) = S0(1+r)^T, so the discounted forward equals spot (used on p.213).
7. Put minus call (Eq. 5): p0 − c0 = [X − F0(T)](1+r)^−T (p.212).
8. Firm value (Eq. 6): V0 = c0 + PV(D) − p0 (p.215). V0 asset value, c0 and p0 options on firm value struck at D, PV(D) risk-free present value of face D.
9. Payoffs at T: shareholders max(0, V_T − D); debtholders min(V_T, D) = D − max(0, D − V_T) (p.214).
10. Fiduciary call and protective put at expiry both pay max(X, S_T) (p.206).

## Traps
- Discounting the strike: the bond is X(1+r)^−T, not X, and T is in years (six months is 0.5) (p.206).
- Signs flip when a term crosses the equals sign; rewrite the equation before reading positions, then check against the equivalence table (p.209).
- A covered call has a short call and equals bond minus put; do not confuse with the fiduciary call or protective put, which are both long options (p.209).
- In forward parity the bond's face value is the forward price F0(T), not X. One in-text solution loosely names the strike as the bond's face; trust F0(T) (p.211).
- Firm value: shareholders hold the call and debtholders are short the put; a higher put value means more credit risk, not less.
- Exhibit 3 (p.205) is captioned as the protective put but its graphs show a covered call and sold put plus bond; use the text and Exhibit 4 (p.206) instead.
- Eq. 5 is often verbalised as a long bond plus a short forward; that omits the borrowing that makes the forward zero-cost, so lean on the algebra, not the phrase (p.212).

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
- **A.** Stock 50, one-year European call struck at 55 costs 4.00, r = 5% per year, no dividends. PV of strike = 55/1.05 = 52.381. Put = 4.00 + 52.381 − 50 = 6.38.
- **B.** Same as A with r = 8%. PV of strike = 55/1.08 = 50.926; put = 4.00 + 50.926 − 50 = 4.93, about 1.45 lower. A higher rate cuts the strike's present value, so the implied put falls.
- **C.** Zero-coupon debt of face 100 due in a year. If V_T = 140: debt 100, equity 40. If V_T = 70: debt 70 (= 100 − 30, the sold put's payout), equity 0.  If asset volatility rises with V0 fixed, call and put both gain, so equity gains and debt loses value.

## Book slips and uncertainties
- Exhibit 3 (p.205, PDF 213): graphic shows a covered call / sold put plus bond instead of a protective put. Text and Exhibit 4 are correct.
- Firm Value setup (p.213) writes V0 = E0 + PV(D), where PV(D) is the debt's market value, while Eq. 6 uses risk-free PV(D) and subtracts the put. They agree when debt value = PV(D) − p0 and E0 = c0. Trust Eq. 6.
- Eq. 5 wording (p.212) omits the implied short-bond leg; see Traps.
- An in-text solution on the synthetic protective put gives the strike as the bond's face; the exhibit and text say F0(T). Use F0(T).
