## Core principles
- A risk-free asset has zero variance and zero covariance, so mixing it with a risky portfolio gives a straight line (the CAL). Lending means holding the fixed-payoff asset; the only risk taken is the risky share. pp.62–65.
- A unique best risky portfolio needs homogeneous expectations; analysts differ on cash flows and required returns, so valuations differ. Prices are set by the best-informed participants, so the market portfolio is the base case. pp.65–67.
- The CML is the CAL through the market portfolio. Slope is extra return per unit of total risk. Left of the market the investor lends; right of it, borrows. A borrowing rate above the lending rate flattens the line right of the market because the investor pays the lender more. Points above are unattainable, below dominated. pp.67–74.
- Only systematic risk is paid. If diversifiable risk paid extra, investors would buy it, diversify it away and bid its price up until the premium vanished. Variances, not standard deviations, add. pp.75–76.
- Beta is covariance over market variance, mixing correlation and relative volatility. A volatile asset uncorrelated with the market has beta 0 and earns Rf; a negative-beta asset can earn below Rf because it hedges. pp.80–84.
- CAPM: expected return depends on beta only. The SML prices any asset; the CML only efficient mixes. It fails if the market proxy is wrong, betas drift, or one period and shared views do not hold. pp.84–93.
- Sharpe and M2 use total risk (whole portfolio); Treynor and Jensen use beta (diversified fund). Jensen's alpha is the gap above the SML; the families can rank managers differently. pp.94–100.
- Construction: hold a broad index, add positive-alpha securities, avoid or short negative alpha, scaling weights by alpha over nonsystematic variance. Positive alpha can reflect low correlation, not mispricing. pp.100–105.

## Vocabulary
- CAL — line for mixes of Rf and any risky portfolio (p.63).
- CML — CAL using the market portfolio; total risk on x-axis (pp.67–68).
- Market portfolio — all risky assets by market value, proxied by an index (pp.66–67).
- Systematic / nonsystematic risk — economy-wide, undiversifiable / firm-specific, diversifiable (p.75).
- Single-index / market model — one market factor; regression form with alpha, beta, error (pp.78–79).
- Beta — sensitivity to the market; market 1, Rf 0 (p.80).
- SML — graph of CAPM against beta (p.87).
- Security characteristic line — regression of excess security return on excess market return (p.101).
- APT / four-factor model — multi-factor pricing; market, size, value, momentum (pp.93–94).

## Formulas
- CML mix: E(Rp) = w1·Rf + (1−w1)·E(Rm); sigma_p = (1−w1)·sigma_m. w1 negative = borrowing. p.69.
- CML: E(Rp) = Rf + [(E(Rm) − Rf)/sigma_m]·sigma_p. Efficient portfolios only. p.69.
- Kinked CML (w1 < 0): E(Rp) = Rb + [(E(Rm) − Rb)/sigma_m]·sigma_p, Rb > Rf. p.73.
- Total variance = systematic + nonsystematic; sigma_i² = beta_i²·sigma_m² + sigma_e². pp.75, 79.
- Single-index: E(Ri) − Rf = beta_i[E(Rm) − Rf]. p.78.
- Market model: Ri = alpha_i + beta_i·Rm + e_i, e uncorrelated with market. pp.79–80.
- Beta = Cov(Ri,Rm)/sigma_m² = rho_im·sigma_i/sigma_m. p.80.
- CAPM/SML: E(Ri) = Rf + beta_i[E(Rm) − Rf]. Single period. pp.84, 87.
- Portfolio beta = sum w_i·beta_i (Rf beta 0). p.88.
- APT: E(Rp) = Rf + sum lambda_k·beta_pk. p.93.
- Four-factor: E(R_it) = alpha_i + b_MKT·MKT + b_SMB·SMB + b_HML·HML + b_UMD·UMD. pp.93–94.
- Sharpe = (E(Rp) − Rf)/sigma_p (CAL slope). p.95.
- Treynor = (E(Rp) − Rf)/beta_p. p.96.
- M2 = (E(Rp) − Rf)(sigma_m/sigma_p) + Rf; M2 alpha = M2 − Rm. p.97.
- Jensen's alpha = Rp − {Rf + beta_p[E(Rm) − Rf]}. p.97.
- SCL: Ri − Rf = alpha_i + beta_i(Rm − Rf). p.101.
- Active weight proportional to alpha_i/sigma_ei²; information ratio = alpha_i/sigma_ei. pp.104–105.

## Traps
- CML vs SML: total risk and efficient mixes vs beta and any asset (p.87).
- Adding standard deviations instead of variances (p.75).
- Treating total risk as priced (p.76).
- Equating beta with volatility (pp.87–88).
- Market risk premium is E(Rm) − Rf, not E(Rm) (p.87).
- Applying the higher borrowing rate on the lending side (pp.72–73).
- Ranking by Sharpe or Treynor with negative excess returns, or Treynor with negative beta (pp.95–96).
- Reading positive alpha as always mispricing (p.104).

## Original practice seeds
Hypothetical A: Rf 2%, market 8% / 15% sd; 120 in the market on 100 wealth, borrowing 20 at 4%. Return = 1.2×8 − 0.2×4 = 8.8%; sigma = 1.2×15 = 18%.

Hypothetical B: sigma_i 30%, correlation 0.8, sigma_m 20%, Rf 2%, market 8%. Beta 1.2; E(R) 9.2%. If correlation drops to 0.5: beta 0.75, E(R) 6.5%.

Hypothetical C: fund 11%, sigma 18%, beta 1.2; market 8%, sigma 12%; Rf 2%. Sharpe 0.50 equals the market's, so M2 alpha is 0, yet Jensen = 11 − 9.2 = +1.8%.
