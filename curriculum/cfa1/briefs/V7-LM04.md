## Learning outcomes

1. Explain how arbitrage and replication price derivatives. Arbitrage (p.80–82) and Replication (p.83–87).
2. Explain how spot and expected future price differ, and what the cost of carry of holding the underlying is. Costs and Benefits (p.88–97). Cost of carry is developed fully; the spot versus expected-future-price contrast is only implicit.

## Core principles

- **A forward is another way to hold the same exposure, so its price is tied to spot.** Otherwise free money exists. Arbitrage is a riskless profit with no net investment (p.80). Two forms: identical cash flows at different prices (law of one price), and an asset with a known future price trading away from that price discounted at the risk-free rate (p.80–82).
- **Cash-and-carry.** If spot is too low relative to the discounted forward, borrow at r, buy the asset, sell it forward; at maturity repay the loan and deliver, keeping the leftover. Buying lifts spot and forward selling lowers the forward until they align (p.82). Mirror case, spot too high: short the asset, lend the proceeds, go long the forward (p.87). It fails with transaction costs, borrowing above the risk-free rate, or storage costs. The repo rate stands in for r in practice (p.82).
- **Replication copies a derivative with the underlying plus borrowing or lending.** Long forward = borrow, buy the asset. Short forward = short the asset, lend. Long asset plus short forward = risk-free bond. Any one of spot, forward, bond can be built from the other two (p.83–86). The book uses replication at fair prices to mirror or offset a derivative; its arbitrage examples use mispricing (p.83).
- **Borrowing to buy the asset is not risk-free.** It costs nothing today but leaves a risky payoff. The short forward is what locks in a return equal to r (p.86).
- **Cost of carry = costs of owning minus benefits.** The risk-free rate is the opportunity cost of holding the asset, borrowed or not (p.88). Storage and insurance raise the forward, since the forward seller who holds the asset must be compensated. Dividends, coupons and convenience yield lower it, since the forward buyer misses them (p.89). Costs above benefits give F above S; below gives F below S.
- **The forward comes from spot, r and carry, not from a forecast of the future spot** (the notes' inference for LO 2; the reading does not say it outright).
- **FX forward = carry from the interest differential.** Borrow one currency, buy the other, lend it; only the rate difference matters, and a positive price-currency minus base-currency difference puts the forward above spot (p.91–93).
- **Convenience yield** is a non-cash benefit of holding physical stock, strongest when inventories are low; a forward well below the no-arbitrage level signals strong demand for the physical good (p.94).

## Vocabulary

- Arbitrage — riskless profit with no net investment (p.80).
- Law of one price — identical assets should trade at one price (p.80).
- Replication — rebuilding a derivative's payoff from the underlying plus borrowing/lending (p.83).
- Forward price F0(T) — price agreed now for delivery at T (p.82–83).
- Repo rate — borrowing rate on loans backed by liquid securities; practical risk-free rate (p.82).
- Cost of carry — net costs minus benefits of owning the underlying over the period (p.88).
- Opportunity cost — risk-free return forgone by holding the asset (p.88).
- Convenience yield — non-cash benefit of holding physical commodity stock (p.94, PDF 102).
- Price currency (f) and base currency (d) — in S0,f/d, f is the numerator, d is the unit priced (p.91, PDF 99).
- Forward premium/discount (FX) — book usage: price currency at premium when rf < rd, discount when rf > rd (p.92–93).
- Discrete versus continuous compounding — (1+r)^T versus e^(rT) (p.81).

## Formulas

Symbols: S0 spot now; ST spot at T; F0(T) forward price; r annual risk-free rate (decimal); T years; I, C known income/cost amounts per unit; i, c continuous income/cost rates; prices in currency per unit.

1. FV_N = PV(1+r)^N; FV_T = PV·e^(rT) (p.81, PDF 89).
2. S0 = ST(1+r)^−T for a known future price with no carry (p.82, PDF 90). S0 below this means cash-and-carry arbitrage.
3. Eq 3: F0(T) = S0(1+r)^T (p.88, PDF 96). Assumes borrowing/lending at r, no transaction costs, no income or cost. If r < 0, F < S.
4. Eq 4: F0(T) = S0·e^(rT) (p.88).
5. Eq 5: F0(T) = [S0 − PV0(I) + PV0(C)](1+r)^T (p.89, PDF 97; same as Eq 8, p.97, PDF 105). Discount each flow from its own date at r; amounts and timing known.
6. Eq 6: F0(T) = S0·e^((r+c−i)T) (p.89).
7. Eq 7: F0,f/d(T) = S0,f/d·e^((rf−rd)T) (p.92, PDF 100). f price currency, d base currency, continuous rates.
8. Payoffs: short forward F0(T) − ST; long forward ST − F0(T); long asset ST − S0; long asset plus short forward F0(T) − S0, a return of r when F0(T) = S0(1+r)^T (p.82–86).
9. Arbitrage profit at T: quote above S0(1+r)^T, sell forward, borrow, buy spot, profit F − S0(1+r)^T; quote below, short spot, lend, buy forward, profit S0(1+r)^T − F (p.87, PDF 95).

Intuition: the forward is spot grown at the net cost of holding the asset until T.

## Traps

- Dividends and coupons lower F; storage and insurance raise it (p.89).
- The interest rate lifts F above S only when r is positive; with negative r, F is below S (p.88).
- Discount each dividend or storage cost from its own date (p.90, PDF 98).
- Quote too high: sell forward, buy spot. Quote too low: reverse. With several contracts, the biggest mispricing is the best trade.
- FX: f sits in the numerator and the exponent is rf − rd; swapping flips the answer (p.92).
- Scale rates by time in years (days/365; six months is 0.5).
- Borrowing and buying alone is not a risk-free return; you need the short forward (p.86).
- Convenience yield is a benefit, so it pushes F down (p.94).

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

1. S0 = EUR100, r = 3%, T = 1, discrete. F = 100 × 1.03 = 103. If quoted at 105: borrow 100, buy the asset, sell forward. At T repay 103, deliver, receive 105; riskless profit EUR2.
2. S0 = EUR80, r = 4%, T = 0.5, discrete; one dividend at T. With EUR1.00: F = 80 × 1.04^0.5 − 1.00 = 81.584 − 1.00 = 80.58. With EUR2.00: 79.58, below spot, as the benefit exceeds the interest cost.
3. S0 = 1.10 USD per EUR (USD is f), rUSD = 2%, rEUR = 0.5%, T = 1, continuous. F = 1.10 × e^0.015 = 1.1166. If rUSD = 0.5%, F = 1.1000, equal to spot.
