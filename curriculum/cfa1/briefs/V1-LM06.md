## Core principles
- **Three descriptions of one random variable.** The CDF gives the chance of being at or below a level and works for every type. A discrete variable has a PMF (probability at each exact value, summing to 1). A continuous one has a PDF, and only the area under it is a probability; any single exact value has zero probability. (pp.253–256).
- **Expectation, variance, covariance with no extra information.** The mean is a probability-weighted average, not a plain average. Variance is the expected squared deviation, quickly found as E[X²] − (E[X])². Covariance gives only the direction of co-movement; its size depends on units. Negative covariance is the diversification logic. The law of large numbers says sample averages settle on the true mean only for large, random, representative samples (pp.257–263).
- **Binomial and Poisson count things.** Binomial: successes in n independent identical trials. Poisson: rare independent events at a constant rate λ. A default-intensity model turns λ into survival odds, so a lender's loss risk grows with λ and the horizon. (pp.265–270).
- **Pick the distribution that respects the variable's limits.** Prices cannot go below zero, so log-normal. Log returns add across periods, which suits multi-period modelling. Returns are often modelled as normal (symmetric, kurtosis 3). Logistic has heavier tails and drives binary-outcome regression. Uniform draws through an inverse CDF drive Monte Carlo (pp.271–278).
- **Conditioning re-weights outcomes.** Once you know a scenario, use the probabilities that apply inside it. The unconditional mean is the scenario-probability-weighted average of the conditional means. News moves the expected value and often changes the variance (pp.285–293).
- **Bayes revises beliefs.** Posterior = likelihood × prior ÷ probability of the evidence, where the evidence probability comes from the total probability rule over all hypotheses. The same signal moves a high prior much further than a low one. (pp.294–301).
- **Moments.** Know each family's mean and variance and the sign and size of skewness and excess kurtosis (p.279, PDF 289).

## Vocabulary
- Random variable — a numerical outcome of an uncertain experiment (p.253).
- CDF — probability that X is at or below x (p.254).
- PDF — density whose area over an interval is a probability (p.254).
- Expectation — probability-weighted average of outcomes (p.258).
- Bernoulli / binomial — one yes/no trial / count of successes in n trials (p.266).
- Poisson, hazard rate (intensity) λ — event counts at a constant rate; the default-risk rate (pp.266–269).
- Geometric Brownian motion — price path with drift and normal shocks, so prices stay positive (p.272).
- Log-normal — the log of the variable is normal (p.276).
- Marginal density — a joint density with the other variable integrated out (p.284).
- Conditional expectation — mean given a known event or value (p.285).
- Total probability rule (for expected value) — weighted average over exclusive, exhaustive scenarios (p.287).
- Prior / posterior probability — belief before / after the evidence; diffuse prior (pp.294–299).

## Formulas
1. CDF F(x)=P(X≤x); P(a≤X≤b)=∫f(x)dx=F(b)−F(a); total area 1; discrete: sum of P(X=x). Non-decreasing from 0 to 1. (pp.254–256)
2. E[X]=Σ xᵢP(xᵢ) or ∫x f(x)dx. (p.258)
3. Var(X)=E[(X−E[X])²]=E[X²]−(E[X])²; SD is its square root. (p.258)
4. Cov(X,Y)=E[(X−E[X])(Y−E[Y])]=E[XY]−E[X]E[Y]. Independent implies zero; zero does not imply independent. (p.258)
5. Discrete uniform: mean (a+b)/2, variance ((b−a+1)²−1)/12. Continuous uniform: mean (a+b)/2, variance (b−a)²/12, excess kurtosis −6/5. (pp.266, 271, 279)
6. Binomial: P(X=k)=C(n,k)p^k(1−p)^(n−k); mean np; variance np(1−p). Normal approximation when np>5 and n(1−p)>5. Independent identical trials. (pp.268, 280)
7. Price-tree node: S(i,j)=S0·u^i·d^j, i ups and j downs. (p.267)
8. Poisson: P(k events in t)=(λt)^k e^(−λt)/k!; mean=variance=λ per unit time; skewness 1/√λ; excess kurtosis 1/λ. Scale λ by t. (pp.268–269)
9. Default: P(no default by t)=e^(−λt); P(default)=1−e^(−λt); expected time to default 1/λ. Assumes constant λ. (pp.268–269)
10. Normal N(μ,σ²): skewness 0, kurtosis 3; about 68% within ±1σ, 95% within ±2σ. (p.274)
11. Log-normal (Y=e^X, X normal): mean e^(μ+σ²/2); variance (e^(σ²)−1)e^(2μ+σ²). Log return = ln(S1/S0). (pp.276–277)
12. GBM: S_t=S0·exp[(μ−σ²/2)t+σW_t], W_t standard normal noise. (p.272)
13. Logistic: mean μ; variance s²π²/3; skewness 0; kurtosis 4.2 (excess 1.2). (pp.278–279)
14. Joint and marginal: F12(x1,x2)=P(X1≤x1, X2≤x2); independence means f12=f1·f2; marginal by integrating the other variable out. (pp.283–284)
15. Conditional: E[X|Y=y]=Σ x·P(X=x|Y=y); Var(X|Y=y)=E[X²|Y=y]−(E[X|Y=y])²; total probability: E[X]=ΣE[X|Sᵢ]P(Sᵢ). (pp.285, 287)
16. Bayes: P(A|B)=P(B|A)P(A)/P(B), with P(B)=ΣP(B|Aᵢ)P(Aᵢ). (pp.294–295)
17. Two-asset portfolio SD: σp=√(w1²σ1²+w2²σ2²+2w1w2ρσ1σ2). Appears only in a solution, not in the reading (p.305, PDF 315).

## Traps
- A PDF height is not a probability; P(X=x)=0 for continuous X.
- Variance is in squared units, not SD.
- A large covariance is not a strong relationship; independence implies zero covariance, not the reverse.
- Poisson needs λt for the interval; default probability is 1−e^(−λt), not λ×t.
- Binomial skewness is (1−2p)/√(np(1−p)), positive when p<0.5. Trust the formula (see slips).
- Log-normal mean is e^(μ+σ²/2); exp of the mean log is the median-type price. Know which the question wants.
- P(A|B) is not P(B|A). Compute P(B) over all hypotheses before dividing.
- Weight by probabilities, not equally.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
- **A. Binomial.** 20 independent daily trades, each winning with p=0.55. Mean 11 winners, variance 20×0.55×0.45=4.95, SD≈2.22. np=11 and n(1−p)=9, both above 5, so the normal approximation is acceptable. At p=0.60: mean 12, variance 4.80.
- **B. Poisson default.** Hazard rate λ=0.04 per year. Three-year default probability 1−e^(−0.12)=1−0.8869=11.3%; expected time to default 25 years. At λ=0.06: 1−e^(−0.18)=16.5%, expected time 16.7 years.
- **C. Bayes.** Prior default probability 0.10 over 12 months. A warning flag appears for 80% of eventual defaulters and 20% of non-defaulters. P(warning)=0.08+0.18=0.26; posterior=0.08/0.26≈0.308. With prior 0.20: P(warning)=0.32, posterior=0.16/0.32=0.50.

## Book slips and uncertainties
- Binomial skew: the text on p.268 gives the direction opposite to Exhibit 18 (p.279); trust the formula.
- Exhibit 18 log-normal "excess kurtosis" entry is actually plain kurtosis; true excess kurtosis is e^(4σ²)+2e^(3σ²)+3e^(2σ²)−6.
- Exhibit 19 writes the logistic scale as β while the text uses s; Exhibit 20's uniform CDF entry is incomplete.
- Eq 8 is typeset loosely; Eq 30 prints P(X|Sᵢ) where E[X|Sᵢ] is meant, so use the expectation form.
- A scenario-return case computes its unconditional variance wrongly, ignoring within-scenario variance; correct is the average conditional variance plus the variance of conditional means. The three-period tree has a garbled intermediate line (p.291); the logic is fine.
- Small rounding mismatches in one case; a path-count clash in the Monte Carlo case; a typo for d in the tree.
- A knowledge check on a log-normal price forecast uses the exp of the mean log; the true mean differs.
