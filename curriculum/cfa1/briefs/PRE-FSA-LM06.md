## Core principles
- **Capitalise or expense.** Costs of getting an asset ready for use (price, freight, installation, testing, asset-specific site work) are capitalised; training and routine upkeep are expensed; later spending that extends life or capacity is capitalised. Capitalising lifts current profit, assets and operating cash flow (the outflow is investing) and the cost returns later as depreciation. Expensing does the reverse now and leaves higher later profit growth. Risk: aggressive policies flatter results; compare across firms (pp.184–187).
- **Borrowing costs.** Interest on funds used to build an asset is added to its cost (PP&E if own use, inventory if for sale); the lender is paid either way. Under IFRS only, income earned on idle loan proceeds reduces the capitalisable amount. Coverage ratios should count capitalised interest, still owed (pp.186–187, 206).
- **Method changes timing, not total.** Straight-line, accelerated and units-of-production expense the same depreciable cost. Accelerated gives lower early profit and assets, higher later margins and ROA, so method alone can make a firm look better run. (pp.188–194).
- **Book-tax link.** If books and tax may differ, accelerated-for-tax with straight-line books creates a deferred tax liability that reverses. If tax must follow books, accelerated depreciation cuts early cash tax and raises operating cash flow, though depreciation is non-cash (p.194).
- **Estimates and components.** Longer life or higher residual means lower expense; estimates are reviewed periodically. IFRS requires depreciating significant parts separately; same total, more estimates (pp.194–196).
- **Amortisation.** Only finite-life intangibles are amortised, following the pattern of benefit; indefinite-life intangibles and land are not, and are impairment-tested instead (pp.197–198).
- **Revaluation (IFRS only).** A class is carried at fair value less later depreciation. First increase goes to OCI surplus; a decrease hits profit unless it unwinds surplus; a later increase hits profit only up to an earlier loss. Revaluing up raises assets and equity, lowering leverage, but later depreciation rises and ROA/ROE fall. Managers may time it near covenants or fund-raising (pp.198–201).
- **Investment property.** Held for rent or gain. IFRS allows cost model or fair value model (no depreciation; all fair value changes through profit). US GAAP has no category; most US firms use cost (pp.202–206).

## Formulas
Currency units and years; no mid-year convention.
1. Straight-line = (Cost − Residual) / Life (p.188). Even spread.
2. Declining balance in year t = Rate × opening carrying amount; double rate = 2 × (1/Life). Residual not in base, but carrying amount never goes below residual (pp.188, 190). Front-loaded.
3. Units-of-production = (Cost − Residual) × Units in period / Total lifetime units (p.188). Follows usage.
4. DDB-to-straight-line switch: expense = (Carrying amount at switch − Residual) / Remaining life (p.190).
5. Component depreciation = sum of (component cost − component residual) / component life (p.195).
6. Asset turnover = Revenue / Average total assets; Operating margin = EBIT / Revenue; Operating ROA = EBIT / Average total assets (p.192).
7. Leverage = Average total assets / Average equity (p.199); adding equal amounts to a ratio above 1 lowers it. Fixed asset turnover = Revenue / Average net fixed assets; interest coverage = EBIT / total interest incl. capitalised (Solutions p.220).
8. Capitalised borrowing cost = Interest on construction borrowing − (IFRS only) investment income on unspent proceeds (pp.186–187).
9. DB carrying amount after n years = Cost × (1 − rate)^n (p.221).
10. Straight-line accumulated amortisation = (Cost − Residual)/Life × years elapsed (p.221).
11. Revaluation carrying amount = Fair value at revaluation − later accumulated depreciation (p.199).

## Traps
- Capitalising only delays the cost; total expense is unchanged. It raises operating cash flow.
- Accelerated methods ignore residual in the rate but stop at residual.
- Depreciation and impairment are non-cash; accelerated depreciation raises CFO only via lower tax.
- Accelerated depreciation lowers early margins then margins rise; not a falling trend.
- Upward revaluation is not income, but reversing an earlier loss is; order matters.
- Revaluation lowers leverage but also ROA and ROE.
- Coverage uses capitalised plus expensed interest; capitalising lowers fixed asset turnover.
- Revaluation model is IFRS only; investment property fair value gains are not sales and carry no depreciation.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
A. Van costs 12,000, residual 2,000, life 5 years. Straight-line = 10,000/5 = 2,000. DDB rate 40%: year 1 = 4,800, year 2 = 7,200 × 0.40 = 2,880. Year-1 profit 2,800 lower under DDB. With 4-year life, straight-line = 2,500; total still 10,000.
B. Machines bought for 5,000. Path 1: value 5,600 (600 to OCI surplus), then 4,900 (600 against surplus, 100 loss). Path 2: value 4,500 (500 loss), then 4,900 (400 gain reversing loss). Both end at 4,900, cumulative profit effect −100; earnings run 0, −100 vs −500, +400.
C. Borrow 2,000,000 at 8% for 1.5 years: interest 240,000; idle-fund income 15,000. IFRS capitalises 225,000; US GAAP 240,000.
