## Learning outcomes
1. Evaluate practices, policies and conduct against the Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct (all sections, pp.237–279; PDF 251–293).
2. Explain why given practices, policies and conduct do or do not breach the Code and Standards (same sections; shown in each case's solution).

## Core principles
- **Method.** No calculation. For each fact pattern: name the Standard, test the conduct, check the usual excuses (disclosure, client consent, no harm, good result, good motive, a superior's order, reliance on others), then state what compliant conduct looks like. Decision framework: Identify, Consider, Decide and act, Reflect (p.238; PDF 252).
- **Disclosure does not waive core duties.** Loyalty, prudence and care, suitability, honesty and fair allocation cannot be disclosed away (pp.252, 258, 275). Exception: conflicts and referral benefits, where full disclosure (plus employer consent where needed) is what the Standard asks (pp.265, 272, 276).
- **Harm and outcome are irrelevant to breach.** Manipulation, deceptive error tricks, front-running and tipping are violations even if clients gained (pp.247, 251, 274-275).
- **Material nonpublic information.** A few analysts hearing it is not public; a public place does not make overheard inside information public (pp.249-250). Mosaic theory is allowed; trading on an inferred material fact is not.
- **Client duties scale with the relationship.** Advisory accounts carry suitability and a total-portfolio view (p.257); a self-directed, limited-scope account under a clear contract carries narrower duties (p.253), but good faith still applies.
- **Employer duties have limits.** Preparing to change firms is allowed; until leaving, do not harm or disparage the employer or promote the new firm to its clients; client lists are employer property (pp.261-264). Whistle-blowing can be justified to protect clients (p.263). Supervisors need reasonable efforts and cannot hide behind missing policies (pp.266-267).
- **Client priority.** Client orders come before personal and employer trades; pooled orders are allocated fairly and promptly (p.275).
- **Fair dealing** means equal opportunity to act, not identical service; a paid tier is fine if open to all, disclosed, and non-payers are not disadvantaged (p.256).

## Vocabulary
- Dissociate: withdraw from a violation; silent involvement can count as assisting (p.239; pp.278-279).
- Mosaic theory: public data plus nonmaterial nonpublic pieces; permissible (p.250).
- Material: would move price or matter to a reasonable investor (p.250). Nonpublic: not yet available to the market at large (p.250).
- Selective disclosure: material information to some parties first (pp.250-251).
- Information-based manipulation: false information creating a misleading market picture (p.251).
- Front-running: trading personally ahead of client orders (p.274).
- Issuer-paid research: funded by the company covered; acceptable with disclosure and safeguards (pp.265, 269).
- Omnibus (block) order: pooled order allocated to accounts afterwards (p.275).
- Suitability analysis: client experience, objectives, constraints, total-portfolio fit (p.257).
- Referral fee: any compensation or benefit for recommending services; disclose (p.276).
- Whistle-blowing: reporting employer misconduct outward (pp.262-263).
- Misrepresentation by omission: leaving out an important fact (pp.244, 259).
- Lapsed membership: unpaid dues end use of the designation (pp.278-279).

## Formulas
None in this module (the only list is the four-step framework, p.238).

## Traps
- "Disclosure fixes it": true for conflicts and referral fees, false for waivers, suitability and trade allocation.
- "No one was hurt": harm is not an element of breach.
- Treating a public setting as public information; answering questions as a defence to selective disclosure.
- Overgeneralising limited-scope accounts to advisory ones, and the reverse.
- Leaving a firm: preparing is fine; disparaging, soliciting before leaving, or taking the client list is not.
- Following a client's wishes instead of doing the suitability work.
- Personal conduct with no dishonesty link is not Misconduct; dishonest conduct is.
- Supervisors blaming missing procedures; late resignation does not replace declining a role.
- The designation is not a permanent degree; silence about a colleague's misuse is assisting.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
1. A manager buys 1,000 shares at $50.00, then places a 400,000-share client order that lifts the price to $50.60, and sells her shares there. Her gain is 1,000 x $0.60 = $600. Clients pay up to 400,000 x $0.60 = $240,000 more than at $50.00. Violation of VI(B). If the client order were tiny and the price did not move, gain is $0 but the breach stands.
2. A CFO tells two of 20 analysts a regulatory approval "went better than hoped"; the stock moves from $25.00 to $27.00 (+8%: 2.00/25.00). Those analysts' clients bought 10,000 shares at $25.20 first, giving a paper gain of 10,000 x $1.80 = $18,000. Selective disclosure of material nonpublic information; a press release first would remove the edge. A pure pleasantry with no price effect would not be material.
3. An adviser rebates 10% of a $20,000 annual fee to a client for a referral: 0.10 x $20,000 = $2,000. Prospects must be told of it. The same rebate given to every client regardless of referral is not a referral fee.
