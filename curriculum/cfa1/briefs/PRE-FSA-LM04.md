## Core principles
- **Accrual income is not cash.** The income statement records revenue when earned; the cash flow statement records cash when it moves. A firm can book credit sales, never collect, and fail. The statement also ties opening cash to closing cash. Failure case: treating net income as a proxy for liquidity (p.96, PDF 104).
- **Three buckets, by purpose of the activity.** Operating = day-to-day revenue-producing activity plus anything not investing or financing (customer receipts, payments to suppliers, staff, taxes). Investing = buying or selling long-term assets and non-trading securities. Financing = raising or returning capital (stock, bonds, borrowings). Ask whether operations fund investment and payouts or the firm leans on new debt and asset sales; only operating cash recurs (pp.96–97, PDF 104–105).
- **Edge cases that decide questions.** Trading/dealing securities are operating even if not the main business. Supplier credit (payables) is operating, not financing. Gains and losses on asset sales or debt retirement are non-cash income items: strip them from operating cash and show actual proceeds or payments in investing or financing (p.97, PDF 105; Exhibits pp.101, 108).
- **Only actual cash counts.** A transaction with no cash in or out (an asset swap, bonds converted into stock) stays out of the statement but must be disclosed in a note or supplementary schedule, because it still changes the capital structure and asset base (p.99, PDF 107).
- **IFRS gives choices, US GAAP does not.** IFRS: interest and dividends received may be operating or investing; interest and dividends paid may be operating or financing (apply consistently, disclose). US GAAP: interest paid and received and dividends received are operating; dividends paid are financing. The choice moves cash between categories and shifts reported CFO without changing total cash change (p.98, PDF 106).
- **Direct and indirect give the same CFO.** Only the presentation differs, and only the operating section may use the indirect format; investing and financing are always gross. Direct lists gross receipts and payments, naming sources and uses of cash, which suits analysts and lenders. Indirect starts from net income and bridges via non-cash charges, non-operating gains/losses and working-capital changes, showing why income and cash diverge; preparers find it cheaper, so most firms use it. Both are permitted under both regimes, with direct encouraged (pp.99–100, PDF 107–108).
- **US GAAP adds disclosures.** A direct-method firm must also supply the net-income-to-CFO reconciliation; an indirect-method firm must still disclose interest paid and taxes paid (pp.107, 109; PDF 115, 117).
- **Reading a statement.** Start from the change in cash, then ask which section supplied and used it: CFO versus capex, then financing mix (borrowing, equity, payouts). Receivables rising cuts CFO; payables rising raises it (pp.101–108).

## Vocabulary
- Operating activities — day-to-day cash activity of earning revenue, plus residual items (p.97).
- Investing activities — cash used or received buying and selling long-term assets and non-trading securities (p.97).
- Financing activities — cash from raising or repaying owner and lender capital (p.97).
- Dealing/trading securities — held for resale; their cash flows are operating (p.97).
- Non-cash transaction — an exchange with no cash movement; disclosed, not reported in the statement (p.99).
- Cash flow from operations (CFO) — net cash provided by operating activities (p.99).
- Direct method — presents gross operating cash receipts and payments (p.99).
- Indirect method — starts at net income and adjusts to operating cash (p.99).
- Reconciliation of net income to CFO — schedule required under US GAAP when the direct method is used (p.107).
- Working-capital adjustment — change in receivables, inventory, payables and similar accruals added or deducted in the indirect method (pp.101, 108).
- Non-operating gain/loss adjustment — removal of gains and losses on disposals or debt retirement from operating cash (pp.101, 108).

## Formulas
None formally numbered in this module. Informal relationships from the notes:
- **Net change in cash = CFO + CFI + CFF (+ effect of exchange rates on cash)**; ending cash = beginning cash + net change. Units: currency. Assumes outflows carry negative signs. Every cash movement lands in exactly one bucket (verified p.101, 104, 106, 108).
- **CFO (indirect) = net income + non-cash charges (e.g. depreciation) ± non-operating losses/gains ± changes in operating working capital.** Signs: receivable or inventory increase is negative, payable increase positive, gains on disposal deducted, losses added back. Undo accrual accounting to reach cash (p.99; signs seen pp.101, 108).

## Traps
- Putting supplier credit (payables) in financing: it is operating.
- Putting trading securities in investing: operating; only non-trading securities are investing.
- Assuming interest is always operating: only under US GAAP; IFRS allows operating or financing for interest paid.
- Assuming the direct method yields a different CFO: same figure, different presentation.
- Placing a non-cash transaction (e.g. bonds converted to stock) in investing or financing: it is a note disclosure.
- Leaving gains or losses on disposals in operating cash: remove them; proceeds go to investing.
- Forgetting US GAAP extras: reconciliation if direct; interest and taxes paid if indirect.
- Claiming the investing or financing section can be indirect: only operating can.

## Original practice seeds
(All hypothetical.)
1. **Indirect method.** Net income 100, depreciation 30, receivables up 20, payables up 10, gain on equipment sale 5. CFO = 100 + 30 − 20 + 10 − 5 = 115. If receivables rose 50 instead, CFO = 100 + 30 − 50 + 10 − 5 = 85: same income, less cash.
2. **Direct method.** All sales on credit, no bad debts; credit sales 500; receivables rose 40. Cash from customers = 500 − 40 = 460. With a rise of 90, cash collected = 410.
3. **IFRS choice.** Operating cash before interest 80, interest paid 12, dividends paid 20. US GAAP: CFO = 68, CFF = −20. IFRS with interest and dividends both in financing: CFO = 80, CFF = −32. The two sections together total 48 either way (68 − 20 = 48; 80 − 32 = 48), but CFO differs by 12.
