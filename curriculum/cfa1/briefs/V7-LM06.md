## Core principles
- **Zero value at the start, price set by no-arbitrage.** Nothing is paid at inception, so value is zero. The arbitrage-free price is spot grown at the risk-free rate; higher r or longer T raises it because financing the asset costs more. Needs known costs and income (p.136, PDF 144).
- **Income lowers, costs raise the price.** Known income is subtracted from spot as a present value; storage and insurance are added. A futures price well below the no-arbitrage level hints at a convenience yield (p.137, PDF 145).
- **Forward value drifts; futures value is reset daily.** A forward's price is fixed, but its value moves with spot and the shrinking discount period, and no cash moves until maturity, so the winner carries credit exposure. A futures contract is marked to market daily, the account is debited or credited, and value returns to zero. A balance below maintenance margin must be topped up to the initial level, so liquidity risk replaces credit risk (p.138–139, PDF 146–147).
- **Same total, different timing.** Cumulative realized futures gains are about the same as a similar forward's payoff; only the cash schedule differs (p.133–135, PDF 141–143).
- **Rate futures run on 100 minus the rate.** Price and rate move opposite ways. Long futures gains when rates fall (like a lender); short gains when rates rise (like a borrower). Short futures matches the pay-fixed (long) FRA; long futures matches the receive-fixed (short) FRA. Each basis point is worth a fixed amount, so payoff is linear (p.140–142, PDF 148–150).
- **Correlation with rates decides which price is higher.** With constant rates, or futures prices unrelated to rates, the two prices coincide. With positive correlation the long prefers futures (gains arrive when rates are high, losses when low), so futures trade higher. With negative correlation the forward trades higher. Rule of thumb: the contract the long prefers carries the higher price. More rate volatility widens the gap (p.145, PDF 153).
- **Convexity bias.** Futures settle linearly in the rate; an FRA settles at a present value discounted at the new rate, so its payoff is slightly curved, and more so with longer discount periods. For short maturities the gap is tiny (p.145–147, PDF 153–155).
- **Central clearing narrows the gap.** Dealers post margin with a central counterparty and ask clients for the same, so OTC forwards act more like futures. The gap shrinks but does not vanish; investors need spare cash or quality collateral and bear financing and operating costs (p.147–149, PDF 155–157).

## Vocabulary
- Value vs price — price is the agreed delivery price; value is what the contract is worth now (p.136, 138).
- Cost of carry — net cost or benefit of holding the underlying (p.133).
- Convenience yield — benefit of holding the physical asset (p.137).
- Daily settlement / MTM — daily cash transfer of the day's gain or loss through margin (p.133–134, 139).
- Initial margin, maintenance margin, margin call — opening deposit; floor that triggers a call; demand to restore the initial level (p.139).
- Variation margin — cash moved to settle the daily change (p.140).
- Realized vs unrealized MTM — forward MTM stays unrealized until maturity (p.135).
- Market reference rate (MRR) — benchmark deposit rate underlying rate contracts (p.141).
- Basis point value (BPV) — currency change per basis point on a futures contract (p.142).
- Implied forward rate (IFR) — FRA fixed-rate payer is the long (p.146).
- Convexity bias — gap between linear futures and discounted, curved FRAs (p.146–147).
- Central counterparty (CCP) — clearing house standing between the parties and collecting margin (p.147).

## Formulas
1. V0(T) = 0, forward and futures at initiation (p.136).
2. f0(T) = S0 (1 + r)^T. S0 spot, r annual risk-free rate (decimal), T years (days/365). No income or cost; discrete compounding (p.136).
3. f0(T) = S0 e^(rT). Continuous compounding; indexes, portfolios, currencies (p.136).
4. f0(T) = [S0 − PV0(I) + PV0(C)] (1 + r)^T. PV of known income and known costs, discounted at r; excludes convenience yield (p.137).
5. Forward value for the buyer: Vt(T) = St − F0(T)(1 + r)^−(T−t). T − t is remaining life in years (p.138).
6. f(A, B−A) = 100 − (100 × MRR(A, B−A)). MRR as a decimal (p.141).
7. Futures BPV = Notional × 0.01% × Period. Three months = 1/4, one month = 1/12; linear, no discounting (p.142).
8. FRA net payment = (MRR(B−A) − IFR(A, B−A)) × Notional × Period; cash settlement = net payment / (1 + MRR × Period). Positive for the fixed-rate payer when MRR exceeds the fixed rate (p.146).
9. Futures daily MTM = (today's futures price − previous price) × quantity, leading minus for a short (p.144).

Day counts vary (days/365 commodities, days/360 rates). No formula gives the forward–futures gap; only its direction is taught.

## Traps
- Price vs value: the forward price stays fixed while its value moves; futures value resets to zero daily though the futures price keeps moving.
- A large forward MTM is unrealized until maturity: credit exposure, no cash.
- Daily settlement changes timing, not the cumulative gain.
- Eq. 4 signs: income subtracted, cost added, both as present values before compounding.
- A price of 97 means a 3% rate. A price above 100 implies a negative rate. A long futures is NOT a pay-fixed FRA; it matches receive-fixed.
- Positive correlation puts futures above forward; negative puts forward higher.
- Convexity bias comes from FRA discounting, not the futures, and grows with longer discount periods.
- Central clearing narrows the gap; it does not remove it.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
**A. Inception price.** Stock 50 euros, no dividend, risk-free 3%, six months: f0 = 50 × 1.03^0.5 = 50.74. At 4%, f0 = 50 × 1.04^0.5 = 50.99, higher because financing costs more. With a 1.00 dividend at maturity, PV = 1/1.014889 = 0.985, so f0 = (50 − 0.985) × 1.014889 = 49.74, equal to 50.74 − 1.00.
**B. Daily margin.** Long one contract on 500 units; initial margin 1,500, maintenance 1,200. Day 1 price falls 0.40: loss 200, balance 1,300, no call. Day 2 falls 0.50: loss 250, balance 1,050, below maintenance, so deposit 450 to return to 1,500. Total cash lost 450 = 0.90 × 500, the same as a forward realizes, paid in two steps.
**C. Rate futures BPV.** Notional 10 million, three-month MRR, price 97.40, so MRR = 2.60%. BPV = 10,000,000 × 0.0001 × 0.25 = 250 per bp. Price falls to 97.15 (+25 bp): a long loses 25 × 250 = 6,250; a short gains 6,250. A receive-fixed FRA loses the same 6,250 before discounting; discounted at the settled rate, 6,250 / (1 + 0.0285/4) = 6,205.79, a little less, which is the convexity effect.
