## Core principles

- Equity pays no contractual coupon and has no maturity, so management decides the payout. Pick a cash measure, forecast it, discount it, add a terminal value. p.148 (PDF 158); five steps pp.169–172 (PDF 179–182).
- Dividends are the observable cash to a non-controlling holder: best for mature, steady, high-payout firms; they ignore buybacks and issuance. FCFE is cash left for shareholders after reinvestment and net borrowing; it can be negative, dividends cannot. pp.149–159 (PDF 159–169).
- FCFF is cash to all capital providers, discounted at WACC; debt is subtracted afterward. It barely moves when leverage changes (FCFE rises with debt-financed investment), so it suits changing capital structure. Numerator and discount rate must match (FCFE with cost of equity, FCFF with WACC); a mismatch is invalid whichever way it moves value. pp.159–160, 169–172, 186–187 (PDF 170, 179–182, 196–197).
- Residual income is not cash: earnings minus a charge on beginning book equity. Profit adds value only if ROE exceeds cost of equity. p.166 (PDF 176).
- Constant growth is a growing perpetuity: next period's cash flow over (required return minus g). Required return must exceed g; g nominal and not above long-run economy growth; value is very sensitive to the gap. pp.174–176 (PDF 184–186).
- Multistage: explicit high-growth years, then a terminal value from the first stable-growth cash flow, discounted over the explicit years only. Terminal value dominates, more so the shorter the explicit forecast; a small rate rise cuts value sharply. pp.177–185, 190–192 (PDF 187–195, 200–202).
- Limits: one metric can mislead; historical growth depends on the start year; distressed firms need probability-weighted scenarios; use long-term average rates matched to the horizon. pp.194–199 (PDF 204–209).
- Preferred pays a fixed dividend, no growth, ranked ahead of common, so its required return is below common's. Callable favours the issuer (higher dividend); putable and convertible favour the investor (lower dividend). pp.200–204 (PDF 210–214).

## Vocabulary

- Clean surplus accounting — book equity changes only via net income and owner transactions (p.167).
- Target debt ratio — assumed debt share of new financing, used to forecast net borrowing (p.157).
- Terminal value — value at the end of the explicit forecast of all later cash flows (pp.171, 180).
- Base effect — distortion of a growth estimate by an unusual start year (pp.147, 182).
- WACC — market-weighted blend of after-tax debt cost and equity cost (p.186).
- Callable / putable / convertible preferred — issuer redemption right / investor sell-back right / right to swap into common (p.202).

## Formulas

1. Eq. 1: IV_t = sum over i=1..n of E(CF_t+i)/(1+r)^i + E(P_t+n)/(1+r)^n; r must suit the cash measure. p.148 (PDF 158).
2. Eq. 2: IV_t = D_t(1+g)/(r_e − g); Eq. 3: IV_t = D_(t+1)/(r_e − g); r_e > g. p.152 (PDF 162).
3. Eq. 4: FCFE = CFO − Capex + Net borrowing; CFO = NI + Depreciation − change in working capital. p.155 (PDF 165).
4. Eq. 5: FCFE = change in Cash + Dividends − change in Equity (issuance − repurchases); Eq. 10 restates it with repurchases and issuance. pp.155, 157.
5. Eq. 6: DR = Total debt / (Total debt + Total equity). Eq. 7: FCFE = NI − Net investment + change in Debt. Eq. 8: change in Debt = DR × Net investment. Eq. 9: FCFE = NI − (1 − DR) × Net investment. p.157 (PDF 167).
6. Eq. 11: FCFF = CFO + Interest(1 − t) − Capex. Eq. 12: FCFF = FCFE + Interest(1 − t) − change in Debt. Eq. 13: FCFF = EBITDA(1 − t) + Depreciation(t) − change in gross LT assets − change in working capital. pp.160, 163 (PDF 170, 173).
7. Eq. 14: RI per share_t = EPS_t − r_e × BVPS_(t−1). p.166 (PDF 176).
8. Eq. 15: IV_t = E(CF_t+1)/(r − g); r = r_e for equity flows, WACC for FCFF. p.174 (PDF 184).
9. Eq. 16: g_implied = r − E(CF_t+1)/P_t. p.176 (PDF 186).
10. Eq. 17–19 two-stage: IV_t = sum over i=1..n of FCFE_t(1+g_s)^i/(1+r_e)^i + IV_(t+n)/(1+r_e)^n; IV_(t+n) = FCFE_(t+n+1)/(r_e − g_l); FCFE_(t+n+1) = FCFE_t(1+g_s)^n(1+g_l). Discount terminal value n periods. pp.179–180 (PDF 189–190).
11. Eq. 20: SGR = (1 − Dividends_t/NI_t) × NI_t/BookEquity_(t−1). Eq. 21: dividends replaced by FCFE. Eq. 22: (1 − FCFF_t/NOPAT_t) × NOPAT_t/BookTotalCapital_(t−1). Eq. 23: NOPAT = operating income × (1 − t). pp.183–184 (PDF 193–194).
12. Eq. 24: WACC = [MV_D/(MV_D+MV_E)] r_d(1−t) + [MV_E/(MV_D+MV_E)] r_e. Eq. 25: IV_equity = [sum of FCFF_(t+i)/(1+WACC)^i + E(P_t+n)/(1+WACC)^n] − MV_Debt. Eq. 27: WACC ≤ r_e. pp.186–187 (PDF 196–197).
13. Eq. 28: IV = sum over i=1..n of D/(1+r_p)^i + FV/(1+r_p)^n. Eq. 29: IV = D/r_p; implied r_p = D/price. p.200 (PDF 210).
14. Probability-weighted value = sum of probability × scenario value (p.197).

## Traps

- D_t instead of D_(t+1) in the numerator; adding g in the denominator; dividing by g.
- Believing issuance raises FCFE or that FCFE cannot be negative.
- Adding before-tax interest for FCFF; believing more debt raises FCFF.
- Pairing WACC with FCFE or r_e with FCFF.
- Treating EBITDA as complete cash flow (it ignores capex and working capital).
- Discounting terminal value n+1 times or not at all; forgetting the extra growth year in the terminal cash flow.
- Residual income on ending book value.
- SGR with payout instead of retention; deducting debt from an equity value (but do deduct it from an FCFF value).
- Reading negative g as a negative price.
- Preferred: using the dividend rate as market yield (use D/price); thinking a call feature lowers the dividend.

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

1. Constant growth. Current dividend 2.00, g 3%, r_e 9%. D1 = 2.06; IV = 2.06/0.06 = 34.33. At r_e = 10%: 2.06/0.07 = 29.43, about 14% lower.
2. Two-stage, USD millions. Base FCFE 10.0, g_s 10% for 2 years, g_l 3%, r_e 8%. FCFE1 = 11.0, FCFE2 = 12.1, FCFE3 = 12.463; TV at end of year 2 = 12.463/0.05 = 249.26. IV = 11.0/1.08 + 12.1/1.1664 + 249.26/1.1664 = 10.185 + 10.374 + 213.70 = 234.26; terminal value is about 91% of it. At r_e 9%: IV about 195.1, roughly 17% lower.
3. FCFE and FCFF, USD millions. NI 100, depreciation 20, working-capital rise 10, capex 50, net borrowing 15, interest 12, t 25%. CFO = 110; FCFE = 75; FCFF = 110 + 9 − 50 = 69 (check: 75 + 9 − 15 = 69). Preferred add-on: par 100, 5% dividend, perpetual at r_p 4% gives 125; three-year at 6% gives 97.33.
