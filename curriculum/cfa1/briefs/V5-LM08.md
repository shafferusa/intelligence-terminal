## Learning outcomes

1. Explain why and how analysts build and use forecast financial statement models (pp.271–304, PDF 281–314).
2. Evaluate whether a model's structure fits company characteristics such as life-cycle stage and control (pp.305–312, PDF 315–322).
3. Estimate and interpret an equity value from model output (pp.313–330, PDF 323–340).

## Core principles

- **Disaggregation makes growth an output.** One growth rate assumes all drivers move together. Separate revenue, cost, investment and funding forecasts let margins and growth emerge from the story. Cost: more lines mean more noise and updating (pp.274–281).
- **Fixed costs and fixed interest create leverage.** Operating lines growing at one rate with flat interest make net income grow faster. A cost with a fixed part falls more slowly than sales, so margins swing. (pp.280–281, 287, 302–303).
- **Three revenue builds.** Historical (mature, stable firms), bottom-up (units times price, capacity times yield, stores times same-store sales), top-down (market size times share). (pp.282–286).
- **A margin assumption is an expense assumption.** Regress changes in costs on changes in revenue, never levels. The intercept is the fixed part, the slope the variable part. Net margin also carries non-operating items (pp.287–291).
- **Financing moves FCFE, hardly FCFF.** Financing need is asset growth not covered by non-debt liabilities and retained earnings. A need is met by cash, debt or equity; a surplus goes to cash, repayment or payouts, and that choice changes FCFE. Dividends raise the need. (pp.295–300).
- **Life stage decides where value sits.** A start-up pays nothing early, so value is a terminal sales multiple divided by the return multiple the investor demands. Growth firms use the same logic with earnings. Mature firms suit constant growth. A firm with real failure risk weights going-concern and distress values by probability. (pp.305–312).
- **Firm value is not equity value.** Discount FCFF and the terminal value at WACC, then subtract the market value of debt. Constant growth needs positive, stable cash flow and WACC above g; a high-growth, cash-negative firm uses a later-year sales or earnings multiple (pp.318–321, 326–330).
- **Scenarios bracket value, not improve accuracy.** Weights are subjective, only one scenario occurs, and margin assumptions drive most of the spread (pp.321–324).

## Vocabulary

- Bottom-up / top-down revenue — units and price or capacity / market size times share (pp.282–285).
- Net investment — change in working capital plus change in net long-term assets (p.280).
- NOPAT — operating profit after tax, before interest (p.280).
- Financing need — asset growth not covered by internal sources (p.296).
- Life-cycle stages — start-up, growth, mature, decline (p.306).
- ROI (return multiple) — proceeds over cash invested, no interim flows (p.307).
- Post-money / pre-money value — value after / before the new equity (p.308).
- Financial distress — likely failure to meet debt obligations (p.310).
- Going-concern vs distress sale value — continued operation vs sale or liquidation (p.311).
- FCFF sustainable growth rate — reinvestment rate times return on capital (p.319).

## Formulas

1. Eq 1 (p.276): IV_t = Σ_(i=1..n) FCFF_(t+i)/(1+WACC)^i + E(P_(t+n))/(1+WACC)^n. E(P) = terminal value.
2. Eq 2 (p.276): IV_t = FCFF_(t+1) × EV Multiple_t; multiple = EV/FCFF from peers.
3. Eq 3 (p.280): NOPAT = [Revenue − COGS − SG&A − D&A](1 − t).
4. Eq 4 (p.280): Net investment = ΔWorking capital + ΔNet long-term assets; FCFF = NOPAT − net investment.
5. Eq 5 (p.287): Change in expense_t = a + b × (Change in revenue)_t. All absolute or all percentage changes; a fixed, b variable.
6. Eq 6 (p.293): % Change in asset item_t = a + b × % Change in revenue_t. a = 0, b = 1 means constant turnover.
7. Eq 7 (p.295): FCFE = Net income − (ΔGross LT assets − Depreciation) − ΔWorking capital + ΔDebt.
8. Eq 8 (p.295): FCFE = ΔCash + Dividends + Stock repurchases − Stock issuance (equivalent to Eq 7).
9. Eq 9 (p.296): Financing needs = ΔOperating assets − ΔNon-debt liabilities − ΔRetained earnings; ΔRetained earnings = net income − dividends.
10. Eq 10 (p.307): IV_t = [Expected revenue × (P/S)] / (1 + required return)^n.
11. Eq 11 (p.307): ROI = (1 + IRR)^n.
12. Eq 12 (p.307): IV_t = [Expected revenue × (P/S)] / ROI = post-money value.
13. Growth variant (p.310, unnumbered): IV_t = [Year-n net income × P/E] / ROI, interim flows about zero.
14. Eq 13 (p.308): Pre-money = Post-money − New equity.
15. Eq 14 (p.308): Fractional ownership = New equity / Post-money.
16. Eq 15 (p.311): IV = Going-concern value × (1 − P(distress)) + Distress sale value × P(distress); distress value in present value.
17. Eq 16 (p.319): Firm value at t+n = FCFF_(t+n) × (1 + g) / (WACC − g), g = FCFF SGR; needs WACC > g.
18. Eq 17 (p.320): FCFF SGR = (1 − FCFF/NOPAT) × (NOPAT / prior-year-end book capital).
19. Eq 18 (p.321): Equity IV = [Σ FCFF_(t+i)/(1+WACC)^i + E(P_(t+n))/(1+WACC)^n] − Market value of debt.


## Traps

- Terminal value left undiscounted, or firm value reported as equity (p.321).
- ROI treated as a percentage or divided by n; annual rate is ROI^(1/n) − 1 (p.307).
- Ownership computed on pre-money value (p.308).
- Distress value not discounted, or distress handled only by a higher discount rate (pp.311–312).
- Negative financing need read as a shortfall; it is a surplus (pp.296–297).
- Regressing levels instead of changes (p.287).
- Next-year earnings multiple called a terminal value, or P/E used on negative earnings (p.329).
- Treating scenario probabilities as objective or more accurate (pp.273, 324).

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

1. Start-up: year-5 sales USD50m, P/S 4x, target ROI 10x. Terminal value 200; post-money 200/10 = USD20m; IRR = 10^(1/5) − 1 = 58.5%. A USD5m stake is 5/20 = 25%; pre-money USD15m. At 8x the value is USD25m and the stake 20%.
2. Terminal value: FCFF 8, NOPAT 10, prior-year capital 80, WACC 9% (EUR m). Reinvestment 0.20, return on capital 12.5%, g = 2.5%. TV = 8 × 1.025 / 0.065 = 126.15. With FCFF 7, g = 3.75%, TV = 138.33; more reinvestment helps because return exceeds WACC.
3. Financing need (USD m): operating assets +60, non-debt liabilities +10, net income 40, dividends 15. Retained earnings +25, need = 60 − 10 − 25 = +25. Dividend cut to 5 gives +15.
