## Core principles
- **Two sets of books.** Financial reporting (IFRS: IAS 12; US GAAP: ASC 740) and the tax return recognise revenue and expense at different times; the gap appears as deferred tax. Analyst question: does reported tax expense predict the cash tax to be paid? (p.224, PDF 232).
- **Tax base vs carrying amount.** An asset's tax base is what the firm can still deduct as it recovers the asset; if the benefit is untaxed, it equals carrying amount. Carrying amount above tax base means more taxable benefit than deductions ahead: a DTL. Below: a DTA. It fails if the difference will not reverse (pp.224–226, PDF 232–234).
- **Liability tax base.** Book value less amounts deductible (or not taxable) when settled. Income received in advance but already taxed has a zero tax base: a DTA. Loan principal has no tax effect. Items never deductible (donations) are permanent and create no deferred tax (pp.226–227, PDF 234–235).
- **Rate changes re-measure.** Deferred balances use the enacted rate expected at reversal, so an enacted change re-measures them through income tax expense in that period. A cut shrinks a DTL (lower expense) and a DTA (higher expense); the net depends on which is larger (pp.228–229, PDF 236–237).
- **Exceptions.** No deferred tax on a gap created at initial recognition outside a business combination that affects neither book nor taxable profit, or on initial goodwill. For subsidiaries, branches, associates and JVs, a DTL is recognised unless the parent controls reversal and it probably will not reverse; a DTA only if it will reverse and taxable profit will exist (pp.229–230, PDF 237–238).
- **Losses and valuation allowance.** IFRS recognises a loss carryforward only to the extent future taxable profit is probable; US GAAP recognises it fully, then cuts it with an allowance if more likely than not some is unusable. Creating the allowance cuts income; releasing it lifts income with no cash received, so judgement is the risk (pp.231–233, PDF 239–241).
- **Measurement.** Current tax uses balance-sheet-date rates; deferred tax uses the rate expected at realisation or settlement. No discounting (p.232, PDF 240).
- **Equity-charged tax.** If the underlying item bypasses the income statement (such as an IFRS asset revaluation), its deferred tax goes to equity too (pp.233–235, PDF 241–243).

## Vocabulary
- Carrying amount — book value of an asset or liability (p.224).
- Tax base — value for tax purposes: future deductions for an asset; book value less future deductions for a liability (pp.224–226).
- Temporary difference — carrying-vs-tax-base gap that reverses, creating deferred tax (p.224).
- Permanent difference — book/tax gap that never reverses; no deferred tax, but it moves the effective tax rate (p.227, p.236).
- Deferred tax asset (DTA) — tax saved in later periods (p.224).
- Deferred tax liability (DTL) — tax expense booked now, payable later (p.224).
- Current tax — tax payable or recoverable for the period at balance-sheet-date rates (p.232).
- Valuation allowance — US GAAP contra account cutting a DTA when use is not more likely than not (pp.231–233).

## Formulas
None formally numbered; working relationships follow.
1. **Deferred tax = temporary difference × tax rate**; asset difference = carrying amount − tax base. Rate is the enacted rate at reversal; no discounting. For assets a positive gap gives a DTL, a negative gap a DTA; for liabilities the signs reverse (p.229 PDF 237; p.232).
2. **Tax base of an asset** = future tax-deductible amount (cost less cumulative tax deductions); equals carrying amount if the benefit is untaxed (pp.224–226).
3. **Tax base of a liability** = book value − future deductions (for income received in advance: less the portion not taxed later). Depends on the jurisdiction (pp.226–227).
4. **Rate-change effect on a DTL = temporary difference × (old rate − new rate)**. The book splits it into (change in the difference this year × rate change) + (opening difference × rate change); the split differs slightly from the direct figure through rounding (p.229, PDF 237).
5. **Equity-charged revaluation** (building revalued up, not recognised for tax): DTL charged to income = rate × (carrying − tax base) − rate × revaluation surplus. Income-statement change = that figure less the prior DTL (p.235, PDF 243).
6. **Annual surplus transfer to retained earnings** = (revaluation amount ÷ remaining life) × (1 − rate) (p.235, PDF 243).

## Traps
- Assuming tax base equals carrying amount (p.224).
- Booking deferred tax on every book-tax gap; permanent differences and the initial-recognition and goodwill exceptions get none (pp.227, 229–230).
- Saying a rate cut always helps; DTAs fall too (p.228).
- Discounting deferred taxes, or using the future rate for current tax (p.232).
- Treating a valuation allowance release as cash; it is non-cash and flatters income (p.233).
- Mixing the sign of the liability-table difference with DTA/DTL direction (pp.225, 227).
- Putting all deferred tax in the income statement; revaluation-linked tax goes to equity (pp.233–235).

## Original practice seeds
A. A machine costs 100,000. Books depreciate 20,000 a year, tax allows 30,000, rate 30%. End of year 1: carrying 80,000, tax base 70,000, difference 10,000, DTL = 3,000. End of year 2: carrying 60,000, tax base 40,000, difference 20,000, DTL = 6,000.
B. A firm collects 50,000 of subscriptions in advance, taxed on receipt, revenue deferred in the books. Carrying 50,000, tax base 0, deductible difference 50,000. At 25% the DTA is 12,500. If the enacted rate falls to 20%, the DTA is 10,000, a write-down of 2,500 that raises tax expense in the year of change.
C. A firm has a DTA of 8,000 and judges 3,000 of it more likely than not unusable (US GAAP). Allowance 3,000, net DTA 5,000, net income falls 3,000 through tax expense. If later released, net DTA returns to 8,000 and net income rises 3,000 with no cash received.
