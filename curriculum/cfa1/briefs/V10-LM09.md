## Learning outcomes

The module states three generic outcomes (p.223, PDF 237), taught throughout.
1. Apply the Code and Standards to situations involving professional integrity (VII(A) and VII(B) examples, pp.225–228 and 232–233).
2. Recommend practices that prevent violations (VII(B) Compliance Practices, p.231; the guidance sections).
3. Distinguish conforming from violating conduct (examples, Exhibit 1 on p.231, practice problems p.234).

## Core principles

- Two duties owed to the institution, with different victims. VII(A) protects the security and reputation of CFA programs (future candidates and the credential's value are harmed by leaks and cheating). VII(B) protects the public from inflated claims about the designation. Ask: does this conduct damage a program, or overstate what the credential means? (p.223–224)
- VII(A) covers every CFA Institute program, not just the exam. Banned conduct: giving or getting exam help, breaking test rules, leaking confidential material, evading security, trading on the CFA Institute name for personal gain, and misleading CFA Institute. (p.224–225)
- Confidentiality is broad and does not expire. Specific question details, and also which topics or formulas were or were not tested, stay secret until CFA Institute publishes them itself, so the end of the sitting releases nothing. The risk is to future candidates' fair footing. (p.224–225)
- Opinion is safe, content is not. You may say the exam was hard, voice worry, or criticize CFA Institute policy, but the opinion cannot carry confidential content. Discussing non-confidential prep material in a study group is fine. (p.225)
- Volunteers (item writers, graders, committee members) face a higher bar: no disclosing questions, deliberations or scoring, and no using the role to win business. Incentive angle: the temptation is personal or client gain. (pp.225, 228)
- VII(B) wants facts, not promises. Saying you hold a status or passed levels is fine if accurate. Implying better performance or superior ability from membership, the designation or candidacy is not. This applies in every medium (cards, signatures, resumes, social profiles, speech). (pp.229–231)
- Status depends on continuing conditions. Membership needs three annual items (Membership Agreement, Professional Conduct Statement, dues); missing any one ends membership and the right to use "CFA" until reinstated. Candidacy requires an accepted application and enrollment in (or results pending for) a specified exam. Failing a condition flips the conclusion. (p.230)
- Prevention: circulate the guidance to legal, compliance, PR and marketing, and give the firm standard templates for referring to CFA affiliation. (p.231)

## Vocabulary

- Confidential exam information — any exam aspect CFA Institute has not published, including topics or formulas tested or not (pp.224–225).
- Volunteer — a member who helps write, administer, grade or oversee programs (p.225).
- Member — regular or affiliate member who meets the annual requirements (p.230).
- Professional Conduct Statement — annual renewal of commitment to the Code and Standards; skipping it lapses membership (p.230).
- Charterholder — person granted the right to the designation who keeps meeting annual membership requirements (p.230).
- Candidate — accepted applicant enrolled for, or awaiting results of, a specified exam (p.230).
- Partial designation — any implied credential from passing some levels; prohibited unless CFA Institute authorizes (p.230).
- Retired status — a formal reclassification, with reduced dues, that preserves the right to the designation (p.232).
- Emergency deferral — free postponement for a genuine emergency; faking one violates (p.226).
- CBT center — computer-based test venue, with rules on screens and written material (p.226).
- Pseudonym rule — an anonymous handle may not carry the designation (p.230).

## Traps

- Thinking only exact questions are confidential. Topic coverage ("lots on X", "nothing on Y") is also a breach (p.224–225).
- Thinking complaints are violations. Difficulty and policy opinions are allowed; adding content is not (p.225).
- Treating a vendor's request as cover. The solicited candidate violates, and member or candidate staff at the vendor violate too (p.228).
- Writing "CFA Level II", or an expected completion date. These are a partial designation or a forecast (pp.230–231).
- Assuming a charterholder keeps the letters forever. Any missed annual requirement ends use until reinstatement; the proper route for someone leaving the field is formal retired status (pp.230, 232).
- Overreacting to consecutive passes. Stating it as fact is allowed; tying it to superior ability or fund performance is not (pp.231–232).
- Believing "candidate until results arrive" always holds. Declining to sit ends candidacy until re-enrollment (p.230).
- Forgetting overlap: a faked deferral also breaches I(C) Misrepresentation (p.226).

## Original practice seeds

Hypothetical 1. Mei passes Level I and adds "CFA Level I" to her email signature. Answer: violation (partial designation). Compliant wording states the fact that she passed Level I, or that she is a Level II candidate while enrolled for Level II.

Hypothetical 2. Dev files his Membership Agreement and Professional Conduct Statement but never pays dues. Answer: he is not a member for this purpose and must remove "CFA" from cards, profiles and reports until reinstated; had all three been met, nothing changes. The notes say the text gives no exact lapse date, so assume none.

Hypothetical 3. After the exam, Lara posts "That was tough; I'm worried about my score" and "lots of pension accounting questions." Answer: the first sentence is an opinion and fine; the second reveals topic coverage and violates VII(A). Deleting it makes the post compliant.
