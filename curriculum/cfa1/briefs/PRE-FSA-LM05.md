## Core principles
- **Asset first, expense later.** Purchase price net of discounts, duties, inbound freight and conversion costs are capitalised; abnormal waste, unneeded storage, selling and admin costs are expensed now. (pp.116–117, PDF 124–125).
- **Cost flow is an accounting split, not a physical flow.** FIFO expenses oldest costs, LIFO (US GAAP only) newest, weighted average a blend. (pp.118–119, PDF 126–127).
- **Rising costs: LIFO lowers profit and tax.** Lower profit and tax, higher operating cash flow, lower inventory and equity, lower current ratio, higher debt-to-equity. Falling costs reverse everything (pp.125–128, PDF 133–136).
- **LIFO reserve bridges the two.** Adding it to inventory, and netting its yearly change against cost of sales, converts to FIFO. The cash effect is only tax, shown as a deferred tax liability (p.128, PDF 136; pp.132–133, PDF 140–141).
- **LIFO liquidation is a one-off, low-quality gain.** After cost rises, selling more units than bought expenses old cheap layers against current-price sales. Signal: a falling reserve (pp.128–136, PDF 136–144).
- **Write-downs shift expense and flatter activity ratios.** They lower profit, liquidity and solvency measures but shrink the turnover denominator. IFRS reversals are capped at the original write-down; US GAAP forbids them. (pp.137–144, PDF 145–152).
- **High turnover is not automatically good.** It may mean efficiency, stock-outs, or heavy write-downs; LIFO turnover looks better only because inventory is lower (pp.127, 133, 146).
- **Class mix signals demand.** Finished goods outgrowing sales hint at excess stock; raw materials rising hint at demand (p.147, PDF 155).

## Formulas
1. Weighted average cost per unit = total cost of goods available / total units available. p.119 (PDF 127).
2. Cost of sales (periodic) = beginning inventory + purchases − ending inventory. p.122 (PDF 130).
3. Gross profit = sales − cost of sales; gross margin = gross profit / sales. p.127.
4. FIFO inventory = LIFO inventory + LIFO reserve. p.128 (PDF 136).
5. FIFO cost of sales = LIFO cost of sales − increase in LIFO reserve (add a decrease). p.128 (PDF 136).
6. FIFO net income = LIFO net income + increase in reserve × (1 − t); t held constant. p.132 (PDF 140); p.180 (PDF 188).
7. FIFO retained earnings = LIFO retained earnings + ending reserve × (1 − t). pp.132–133; p.180.
8. LIFO liquidation profit = units liquidated × (replacement cost − old layer cost); pre-tax, costs rose. pp.135–136 (PDF 143–144).
9. Inventory turnover = cost of sales / average inventory; DOH = 365 / turnover. pp.133, 146.
10. IFRS write-down = carrying cost − NRV; reversal limited to the earlier write-down. US GAAP LIFO and retail users: market = replacement cost, capped at NRV, floored at NRV less normal margin; no reversal. pp.137–138 (PDF 145–146).
11. COGS adjusted = COGS − write-down charges in COGS − change in reserve; NI adjusted = NI + write-down × (1 − t); inventory without allowance = net inventory + allowance. p.142 (PDF 150); p.177 (PDF 185).
12. Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities; ROA = NI / average total assets; ROE = NI / average equity; growth = (this year − last year) / last year. pp.134–135, 148, 150.

## Traps
- Calling LIFO a physical flow; reading higher LIFO turnover as better management.
- Saying conversion to FIFO changes purchase cash; adding the whole reserve to equity (add after-tax, with a deferred tax liability).
- Treating periodic and perpetual as equal for LIFO or weighted average; only specific ID and FIFO match.
- Assuming write-downs hurt every ratio, or that reversals are universal (US GAAP bans them).
- Forgetting liquidation profit needs earlier cost increases.
- Mixing percent with percentage points for margin changes (p.156).

## Original practice seeds
1. (Hypothetical, periodic, rising costs.) Shop buys 100 units at 10, then 100 at 12, and sells 150. FIFO cost of sales = 1,000 + 600 = 1,600; ending inventory 600. LIFO cost of sales = 1,200 + 500 = 1,700; ending inventory 500. At price 20, gross profit is 1,400 vs 1,300.
2. (Hypothetical, 25% tax.) LIFO inventory 500, reserve 80 to 100, LIFO COGS 1,000. FIFO inventory 600; COGS 980; net income +15; tax paid 5 higher.
3. (Hypothetical.) Cost 100, NRV 80: write down 20. Next year NRV 95: IFRS reverses 15, US GAAP stays at 80. If NRV were 120, IFRS caps the reversal at 20.
