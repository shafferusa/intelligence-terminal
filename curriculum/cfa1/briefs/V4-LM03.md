2. Explain reporting and disclosure for goodwill (origin, no amortization, impairment, analyst adjustments). Goodwill, pp.97–99 (PDF 109–111).
3. Explain reporting and disclosure for financial instruments (measurement categories, where fair-value changes go). Financial Instruments, pp.100–103 (PDF 112–115); one case spills onto p.104.
4. Explain reporting and disclosure for non-current liabilities (long-term financial liabilities, deferred tax liabilities). pp.104–106 (PDF 116–118).
5. Calculate and interpret common-size balance sheets and liquidity and solvency ratios. pp.107–116 (PDF 119–128).

## Core principles
- **Recognition follows measurability.** A purchased intangible has an observable price, so it is capitalized. Home-grown brands, training and start-up costs lack reliable cost or provable benefit, so they are expensed. Analyst fix: tangible book value, but check each intangible rather than zeroing all (pp.93–94).
- **IFRS splits R&D; US GAAP mostly does not.** Research is expensed. Development is capitalized once criteria such as technical feasibility and intent and ability to complete and use or sell are met. US GAAP expenses nearly all of it. Cash spent is identical either way (p.94, PDF 106).
- **Finite lives amortize; indefinite lives and goodwill do not.** Both face at least annual impairment testing, and "indefinite" must be re-justified. Revaluation of intangibles is IFRS-only and needs an active market (pp.93–94).
- **Goodwill is a residual.** The buyer paid more than the fair value of identifiable net assets, for unrecognized assets, R&D that failed the criteria, and synergies. Impairment is non-cash but cuts earnings and assets, and can flatter later ROA. Fair values are management judgments. Analysts strip goodwill from ratio inputs and impairments from operating trends (pp.97–99).
- **Measurement category decides where unrealized gains land.** Amortized cost ignores price changes (hold-to-collect, principal-and-interest cash flows). Fair value through OCI sends unrealized gains to AOCI in equity. Fair value through profit or loss sends them to earnings, raising volatility. Total equity is the same under the two fair-value routes. Realized gains always reach the income statement (pp.100–102).
- **Liabilities at amortized cost converge to face value.** A discount bond's carrying amount accretes up to face; a premium amortizes down. Held-for-trading and derivative liabilities use fair value (pp.105–106).
- **A deferred tax liability is tax payable later.** It arises when taxable income is currently below accounting profit, for example accelerated tax depreciation against straight-line book depreciation. Ask how much cash leaves and when (p.106).
- **Common-size removes scale, not judgment.** Divide each item by total assets; differences prompt investigation, not verdicts. Liquidity ratios: higher means lower risk. Solvency ratios: higher means more risk. Limits: point-in-time data, year-end financing moves, mixed businesses (pp.107–116).

## Vocabulary
- Intangible asset — identifiable, non-monetary, no physical form; identifiable means separable or from legal rights (p.93).
- Finite vs indefinite life — amortization versus impairment-only (pp.92–93).
- Cost vs revaluation model — IFRS both; US GAAP cost only (p.93).
- Tangible book value — equity minus intangibles (p.93).
- Goodwill — purchase price over fair value of net identifiable assets (p.97); bargain purchase gain to profit or loss (p.98, PDF 110).
- Accounting vs economic goodwill — recorded in an acquisition vs reflected in market value (p.97).
- Fair value vs amortized cost (p.100); unrealized (holding-period) gain (p.100).
- FVOCI, FVTPL; held-to-maturity, available-for-sale, trading (pp.100–102); AOCI (p.102).
- Deferred tax liability — future tax from taxable temporary differences (p.106).
- Liquidity vs solvency (p.107); vertical vs horizontal common-size (p.107, footnote 9).

## Formulas
Balance sheet data as of one date; no averages; definitions vary by analyst. Exhibit 13, pp.114–115 (PDF 126–127).
1. Current ratio = current assets / current liabilities (p.114).
2. Quick ratio = (cash + marketable securities + receivables) / current liabilities (p.114).
3. Cash ratio = (cash + marketable securities) / current liabilities (p.114).
4. Long-term debt-to-equity = total long-term debt / total equity (p.114).
5. Debt-to-equity = total debt / total equity (p.114). Total debt is current plus non-current financial liabilities (p.116).
6. Total debt ratio = total debt / total assets (p.115).
7. Financial leverage = total assets / total equity (p.115).
8. Vertical common-size item = line item / total assets x 100% (p.107).
9. Goodwill = purchase cost − (fair value of identifiable assets − fair value of liabilities and contingent liabilities); a negative result is a bargain purchase gain (p.98, PDF 110).

## Traps
- Goodwill is amortized: no. It is impairment-tested at least annually; impairment is non-cash but reduces earnings and total assets.
- Capitalizing all R&D: only IFRS development meeting criteria. Internally generated brands never appear; acquired ones do.
- US GAAP available-for-sale covers debt only; equity securities go through profit or loss. Held-to-maturity debt stays at amortized cost.
- FVOCI gains do not vanish: they sit in AOCI, not profit. Total equity is the same under FVOCI and FVTPL.
- A discount bond's carrying amount rises to face value.
- A deferred tax liability does not mean tax is due now.
- Balance sheet common-size divides by total assets; it flags differences, not good or bad.
- For an impairment, trace which of equity and assets fall, then work out each ratio's direction.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
1. **Development costs (IFRS vs US GAAP).** A firm spends 300 on research and 500 on development after feasibility and intent are shown. IFRS capitalizes 500 and expenses 300 (pre-tax effect -300 this year, before later amortization). US GAAP expenses 800. If feasibility is not yet shown, IFRS also expenses all 800.
2. **Goodwill and impairment.** Buyer pays 900; target's identifiable assets at fair value 1,000, liabilities 400, net 600. Goodwill = 900 − 600 = 300. At a price of 550, a bargain purchase gain of 50 goes to profit or loss. If goodwill is later impaired by 120, earnings fall by 120 (non-cash) and goodwill drops from 300 to 180.
3. **Ratios and repayment.** Cash 40, receivables 60, inventory 100, PP&E 300 (total assets 500); current liabilities 150 (all borrowings), long-term debt 200, equity 150. Current ratio 200/150 = 1.33; quick 100/150 = 0.67; cash 40/150 = 0.27; leverage 500/150 = 3.33; debt-to-equity 350/150 = 2.33. Alternative start with cash 90 and PP&E 250: use 50 of cash to repay 50 of long-term debt. Current ratio falls from 1.67 to 1.33, leverage from 3.33 to 3.00, debt-to-equity from 2.33 to 2.00. Liquidity worsened while solvency improved.
