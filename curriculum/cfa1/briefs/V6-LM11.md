## Core principles

- **Yield duration measures price risk to the bond's own yield**, assuming certain cash flows. Curve duration (sensitivity to a benchmark curve, allowing default or options) is a later idea. p.267–268 (PDF 277–278).
- **Modified duration is the price-yield slope scaled by price.** Differentiating the discounted cash flows gives minus Macaulay duration over (1 + periodic yield); drop the sign. The estimate is a straight line, so symmetric for rises and falls, while the true curve bends (convexity): it overstates losses and understates gains. p.272, 274 (PDF 282).
- **Duration applies to the full (dirty) price**, which includes accrued interest. p.272, 277.
- **With no clean Macaulay duration, reprice.** Shift yield down and up equally, take the price gap, divide by twice the shift and the starting price. Works for uncertain cash flows. p.275 (PDF 285).
- **Money duration and PVBP convert percentages to currency.** Money duration is modified duration times full price (or position value); PVBP is the currency price change for 1 bp, from repricing both ways and halving. All assume an instantaneous yield change, no passage of time and certain cash flows. p.281–283 (PDF 291–293).
- **Floating-rate notes carry little duration.** The coupon resets, so exposure lasts only to the next reset; duration is at most one coupon period. A zero's duration equals its maturity; a perpetuity's is finite. p.283–287 (PDF 293–297).
- **Properties: duration rises with maturity, falls with coupon, falls with yield.** A lower coupon or yield puts more present value in the final payment. Par and premium bonds approach (1+r)/r from below as maturity grows; long-maturity discount bonds can overshoot, peak and fall. At constant yield, duration shrinks within a coupon period and jumps at each coupon date (saw-tooth). p.288–292 (PDF 298–302).

## Vocabulary

- Yield duration — price sensitivity to the bond's own YTM, cash flows certain (p.267–268).
- Curve duration — sensitivity to a benchmark curve (p.268).
- Modified duration — percentage price change per unit yield change (p.272).
- Annualised modified duration — periodic modified duration divided by periods per year (p.272).
- Macaulay duration — present-value-weighted average time to cash flows (p.272; prior lesson).
- Full (dirty) price — price including accrued interest (p.272).
- Approximate modified duration — estimated from prices with yield shifted down and up (p.275).
- Money duration (dollar duration) — modified duration times full price or position value (p.281; PDF 291).
- PVBP / PV01 / DV01 — currency price change for a 1 bp yield change (p.282; PDF 292).
- Basis point value (BPV) — money duration times 0.0001 (p.282).
- Convexity — curvature of the price-yield curve (p.274).
- Saw-tooth pattern — duration falls through a coupon period, jumps at each coupon date (p.289; PDF 299).

## Formulas

(r = YTM per coupon period unless stated; c = coupon per period; N = periods; t/T = fraction of current period elapsed.)

1. ModDur = MacDur/(1+r); AnnModDur = ModDur / periods per year. Eq 1–2, p.272 (PDF 282). Fixed-rate, option-free bonds. An already-annualised MacDur divided by (1 + periodic yield) gives AnnModDur directly.
2. %ΔPV_full ≈ –AnnModDur × ΔAnnYield. Eq 3, p.272 (PDF 282). Linear, symmetric, instantaneous, full price.
3. AnnModDur ≈ (PV− – PV+)/(2 × ΔYield × PV_0). PV−, PV+ = full prices with yield down and up by ΔYield; PV_0 = starting full price. Eq 4, p.275 (PDF 285).
4. AnnMacDur ≈ AnnModDur × (1+r). Eq 5, p.276 (PDF 286). r = yield per period.
5. MoneyDur = AnnModDur × PV_full (per 100 par, or position value). Eq 6, p.282 (PDF 292).
6. ΔPV_full ≈ –MoneyDur × ΔYield. Eq 7, p.282 (PDF 292). Yield change in decimals.
7. PVBP = (PV− – PV+)/2, with 1 bp shifts. Eq 8, p.282 (PDF 292). Per 100 par unless scaled by position; ignores convexity.
8. BPV = MoneyDur × 0.0001. p.282 (PDF 292), text only.
9. Perpetuity MacDur = (1+r)/r. Eq 9, p.283 (PDF 293). Annual coupons: r annual, answer in years.
10. FRN MacDur = (T–t)/T. Eq 10, p.283 (PDF 293). T = days in coupon period, t = days since last reset. Result is a fraction of one coupon period, not years.
11. Closed form: MacDur = {(1+r)/r – [1 + r + N(c–r)]/[c((1+r)^N – 1) + r]} – t/T. p.288 (PDF 298). For comparative statics.
12. Zero-coupon: MacDur = time to maturity; ModDur = maturity/(1+y). p.283 (PDF 293), stated in words.

## Traps

- Using flat price instead of full price (p.272, 277).
- Mixing periodic and annual figures: divide by 1 + the periodic yield, then annualise (p.272–273).
- Treating the estimate as exact: real prices gain more for a fall than they lose for an equal rise (p.270, 274).
- Confusing PVBP (per 1 bp, per 100 par) with money duration (per 100% yield move, whole position): factor of 10,000 and position size (p.282).
- Comparing bonds on the wrong yardstick: modified duration, PVBP per 100 par and loss on equal money invested can rank bonds differently, because full prices differ (p.293–296).
- Assuming longer maturity always means higher duration: long discount bonds can peak and fall (p.289).
- Treating an FRN as duration-free, or reading its fractions of a period as years (p.283–287).
- Reading the saw-tooth as an error (p.289–290). With a negative yield, MacDur is below ModDur, reversing the usual ordering (p.277).

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

1. A 4-year zero, annual compounding, 5% yield, face 100. Price = 100/1.05^4 = 82.270. MacDur = 4; ModDur = 4/1.05 = 3.8095. A 100 bp rise gives an estimated change of –3.81%. The actual price at 6% is 100/1.06^4 = 79.209, a change of –3.72%. The estimate overstates the fall because of convexity.
2. Annual-coupon perpetuity. At a 5% yield, MacDur = 1.05/0.05 = 21 years. If the yield falls to 4%, MacDur = 1.04/0.04 = 26 years. Lower yield, longer duration, more risk.
3. A position with AnnModDur 6.0 and full market value USD2,000,000. Money duration = 12,000,000. A 25 bp rise gives –12,000,000 × 0.0025 = –USD30,000. BPV = 12,000,000 × 0.0001 = USD1,200 per bp. FRN twist: 182-day reset period with 60 days elapsed gives (182–60)/182 = 0.670 of a coupon period.

## Book slips and uncertainties

- FRN units are inconsistent: the text gives a maximum in months, yet tabulated figures and one practice item are fractions of a coupon period, while fixed-rate durations are in years. Unresolved. Trust the formula; the exam point is only that FRN duration is small (p.283–287).
- A table label in the first worked example says years to maturity where periods are meant (PDF 283).
- Exhibit 6's axis covers only part of the bond's life (p.290; PDF 300).
- The money-duration example uses unrounded durations, so rounding gives a slightly different total (p.282).
- In Q7's solution, the at-issue duration of one bond is used although the question describes a later yield move (p.296).
- PDF 277–279, 284, 287–290, 295–297, 301–306 were checked from text only; no errors seen.
