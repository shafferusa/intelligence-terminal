1. Describe how economic rights, voting rights and other features differ across jurisdictions (direct vs indirect access, depositary receipts) and equity classes (dual class, differential voting). Taught in Jurisdictions and Equity Classes, p.28–34 (PDF 38–44).
2. Describe the voting process and the roles of issuer management, the board, proxy advisors, asset managers and asset owners. Taught in Equity Voting Process, p.35–40 (PDF 45–50).

## Core principles

- **Foreign access has a cost; DRs shift it.** Buying direct means local accounts, local settlement and tax rules, other time zones and local currency. A depositary receipt is a domestic-market certificate backed by foreign shares held by a bank, so the investor trades like a local. The holder gets a proportional economic interest and trades in the domestic currency. Risk that remains: currency and the home-market share price. DRs remove access friction, not FX risk (p.28–29, PDF 38–39).
- **DR price is derived, and arbitrage keeps it honest.** DR price follows from home share price, DR-to-share ratio and the exchange rate. If the DR drifts away, traders buy the cheap side and sell the other. This fails when the link is cut, for example when conversion into ordinary shares is barred or sanctions halt trading, and then DRs can sit at a lasting discount (p.29–31, PDF 39–41).
- **Sponsored vs unsponsored sets who votes.** In a sponsored DR the issuer takes part, holders have the rights of direct owners (votes, dividends), and reporting is heavier. In an unsponsored DR the depository bank, not the investor, holds the votes, and reporting is lighter (p.32, PDF 42).
- **ADR, EDR, GDR differ by where they trade.** ADR trades in the US, EDR in Europe, GDR outside both the home country and the US. A GDR may avoid home-country limits on foreign ownership and capital flows (p.29–31, PDF 39–41).
- **Dual class separates control from cash flow.** Classes can differ in votes, dividends or both. A founder holding a small slice of the economics can control the vote through multi-vote shares. Outside holders carry the risk that strategy reflects the insider's priorities, so ask who really decides and whether giving up votes is compensated (higher dividend, discount price). The conclusion weakens when the weaker-vote class is illiquid or the extra dividend is only paid if a dividend is declared (p.32–34, PDF 42–44).
- **The voting chain has distinct roles.** The issuer vets proposals and issues the proxy statement. Materials pass through the custodian to the beneficial owner, who instructs through a voting service. A tabulator counts proxy votes and reports to the issuer. Proxy advisors only research and recommend, and do not collect or count votes. Proxy votes are due earlier than in-person votes (p.35–37, PDF 45–47).
- **Record date fixes eligibility.** Holders on the record date (often 30 or more days before the meeting) may vote. Someone who sells after it still votes, and someone who buys after it does not. The custodian may be the holder of record while the beneficial owner has the economic stake (p.36–37, PDF 46–47).
- **Proposals are usually non-binding but can bite.** Management items and shareholder proposals both appear in the proxy statement. Shareholder proposals are generally non-binding and not often passed, yet strong support can push management to respond to avoid reputational harm. The test for backing a proposal that touches management is a clear link to material value or risk (p.35–38, PDF 45–48). Voting aims to help the board protect and grow capital (p.39, PDF 49).

## Vocabulary

- Home asset bias — over-allocating to domestic stocks (p.28).
- Direct investing — buying foreign shares in their home market (p.28).
- Dual listing — same shares trading on two or more exchanges; not a share-class feature (p.29).
- Depositary receipt (DR) — local-market certificate backed by foreign shares held by a depository (p.29).
- ADR / EDR / GDR — receipts traded in the US / Europe / outside both home country and US (p.29–31).
- Sponsored DR — issuer participates; holders have votes and full rights (p.32).
- Unsponsored DR — issued without the issuer; the depository holds the votes (p.32).
- DR ratio — number of DRs per underlying share (p.29).
- Dual class structure — classes with different voting and/or economic rights (p.32).
- Differential voting rights (DVR) shares — lower votes in exchange for higher dividend or lower price (p.34).
- Proxy statement — meeting materials with agenda, proposals and board recommendations (p.36).
- Shareholder proposal — investor-submitted request for action, usually non-binding (p.35, p.38).
- Vote by proxy — casting or delegating a vote in advance (p.36).
- Custodian / beneficial owner / holder of record — who holds title vs who owns the economics (p.36–37).
- Record date — cutoff that sets who may vote (p.37).
- Proxy advisor — research firm that issues vote recommendations (p.36–37).
- Tabulator / registrar — counts proxy votes and reports to the issuer (p.37).
- Shareholder Rights Directive (SRD) — EU rules improving voting infrastructure and intermediary duties (p.38).

## Formulas

No numbered formulas. Two implied-FX relations appear on p.30 (PDF 40):

- 1 ADR = 1 share: implied FX = home share price (local) / ADR price (USD), in local currency per USD.
- n ADRs per share: implied USD per local unit = (ADR price x n) / home share price (local).
- Rearranged for fair value (not stated in this form in the book): ADR price (USD) = home share price (local) x USD per local unit / n.

Assumptions: same-time prices, no costs, arbitrage not blocked. The receipt is a slice of the share, so scale by the ratio and convert currency before comparing. Check the quote direction (local per USD vs USD per local).

## Traps

- Dual listing is not dual class: listing is about venues, class is about rights (p.26).
- DRs do not remove FX risk. They remove access hurdles only (p.29, p.34).
- Unsponsored DR holders do not vote. The depository does (p.32).
- Compare ADR to share only after applying the ratio and FX. Three DRs per share means each DR is one-third of a share, not three shares.
- Record date: sellers after it can vote, buyers after it cannot (p.37).
- Shareholder of record is not necessarily the owner. Custodian vs beneficial owner (p.36–37).
- Proxy advisors recommend; they do not vote, count or take instructions (p.36–37).
- Shareholder proposals are not binding and rarely pass, but do not conclude they have no effect (p.38).
- Do not assume dual class always means different dividends. Some structures differ in votes only, some in both (p.32–34).
- A GDR is not listed on US exchanges, though it may be placed privately with US institutions (p.31).

## Original practice seeds

1. (Hypothetical) 4 ADRs represent 1 share. The share closes at EUR 80, and USD per EUR is 1.10. Fair ADR = 80 x 1.10 / 4 = USD 22.00. If the ADR trades at USD 21.50, the discount is 0.50/22.00 = 2.3%, a possible mispricing before costs.
2. (Hypothetical) Same set-up, but USD per EUR rises to 1.20 with the EUR price unchanged. Fair ADR = 96.00 / 4 = USD 24.00, up 9.1%, the same as the FX move (1.20/1.10 - 1). The ADR carries the currency exposure.
3. (Hypothetical) Class A: 1 vote, 200m shares, public. Class B: 10 votes, 10m shares, all held by the founder, who also holds 10m Class A. Total votes = 300m. Founder votes = 110m = 36.7%. Equal dividends, so economic share = 20m/210m = 9.5%. If B carried 25 votes, the founder has 260m of 450m = 57.8% of votes, a majority, on the same 9.5% economics.
4. (Hypothetical) Record date 1 March, meeting 5 April. An investor who sells on 10 March can still vote. A buyer on 10 March cannot.
