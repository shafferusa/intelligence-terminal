## Learning outcomes
1. Describe the qualitative and quantitative factors used to judge a corporate borrower's creditworthiness (Qualitative Factors incl. Quantitative Factors, p.414–420, PDF 424–430).
2. Compute and interpret the credit ratios: profitability, coverage, leverage, cash-flow based (Financial Ratios, p.421–427, PDF 431–437).
3. Describe seniority rankings, secured versus unsecured debt, priority of claims, recovery, and how these feed issuer and issue ratings (p.428–436, PDF 438–446).

## Core principles
- **A lender is paid from operating cash flow, and its upside is capped.** The bondholder gets coupons and par at most, so the question is not just whether the firm beats its cost of capital but whether the size and timing of cash flows reliably cover fixed payments. Lower business risk and weaker competition mean more debt capacity (p.414–420).
- **Credit risk splits into POD and LGD.** Business and financial strength drive probability of default; seniority and collateral drive loss given default. Investment-grade senior unsecured holders mostly care about POD; secured, subordinated and high-yield holders care about LGD too (p.411, 420).
- **Tenor changes the question.** Short paper is mostly liquidity; long paper depends on how the business model holds up over many years. (p.415–417).
- **Collateral is most valuable when POD is high.** Pledged hard assets (PP&E, inventory, cash) cut LGD, so a risky issuer can still borrow. If collateral is worth less than the secured claim, the shortfall becomes a senior unsecured claim (p.417, 430).
- **Governance works through covenants, not votes.** Debtholders cannot vote, so they depend on covenants and management behaviour. Red flags: debt-funded buybacks, dividends or acquisitions, aggressive accounting, opaque structures. (p.418–420).
- **Four drivers of POD:** profitability, leverage, coverage, liquidity. A solvent firm can still default if it runs short of cash. Forecasts are top-down, bottom-up or hybrid (p.419–420).
- **Ratios are forward-looking and relative.** Project them under scenarios and compare to peers and rating thresholds in the same industry. Definitions are not IFRS-standard, so they vary by provider and may adjust for leases (p.421–427).
- **Same issuer, same POD, different LGD.** Cross-default typically gives all an issuer's debt about the same POD, so issue ratings differ by recovery. The issuer rating maps to senior unsecured; other issues are notched up or down. Notching is small for strong issuers and larger for weak ones. Holdco debt is structurally subordinated: it is paid only from cash upstreamed after subsidiary creditors (p.433–434).
- **Bankruptcy in practice.** Absolute priority is the rule in principle, but negotiated outcomes often give juniors something because delay destroys value. (p.433).

## Vocabulary
- Probability of default (POD) — chance the issuer fails to pay (p.411).
- Loss given default (LGD) — share of exposure lost if default occurs, 1 − recovery (p.411).
- Business risk — possible deviation of demand, revenue or margin from expectations (p.415).
- Hard vs soft collateral — tangible versus intangible assets (p.417).
- Affirmative covenant — a promise to do things (obey laws, insure assets, pay taxes) (p.418).
- Top-down / bottom-up / hybrid forecasting — macro first, company drivers first, or both (p.419–420).
- Solvent but illiquid — assets exceed liabilities yet cash is short (p.420).
- EBIT, EBITDA, EBITDAR — operating profit; plus depreciation and amortisation; plus rent (p.422).
- FCF, FFO, RCF — conservative cash-flow measures (p.422).
- Net debt — debt less cash and marketable securities (p.422–423).
- Priority of claims / seniority ranking — order in which claimants are paid (p.429).
- First lien, first mortgage, second lien — secured debt backed by specific assets, in rank order (p.429).
- Senior unsecured, subordinated, junior subordinated — general-claim debt in descending rank (p.430).
- Pari passu — equal rank, sharing pro rata regardless of maturity (p.431).
- Recovery rate — share of a claim recovered in default (p.431).
- Expected loss (EL) — POD × LGD (p.433).
- Issuer vs issue rating (CFR, CCR, IDR) — obligor-level rating mapped to senior unsecured vs rating on one specific debt (p.433).
- Cross-default — default on one debt triggers default on others (p.433).
- Notching — moving an issue rating up or down from the issuer rating to reflect LGD (p.433).
- Structural subordination — holdco debt ranks behind subsidiary-level claims (p.434).

## Formulas
Notation is the notes' linear form of the printed exhibit (p.422, PDF 432).
1. EBIT margin = EBIT / Revenue. Percent. Higher is better; some providers use EBITDA. (p.422)
2. EBIT coverage = EBIT / Interest expense. Times. Higher is safer. (p.422)
3. Debt / EBITDA. Times. Higher is riskier. Debt may be adjusted for leases. (p.422)
4. RCF / net debt = RCF / (Debt − Cash and marketable securities). Percent. Higher is safer, the opposite orientation to Debt/EBITDA. (p.422–423)
5. RCF = net cash flow from operating activities − dividends paid. (p.422)
6. FFO = net income from continuing operations + D&A + deferred income tax + other non-cash items. (p.422)
7. FCF = net income − working-capital increases − investment in fixed assets − net interest paid, as the module defines it (unusual versus operating cash flow minus capex). (p.422)
8. Debt/capital = Debt / (Debt + Equity). (p.440, PDF 450)
9. Expected loss = POD × LGD, LGD = 1 − recovery rate. (p.433)

## Traps
- Higher coverage means lower risk; higher Debt/EBITDA means higher risk; higher RCF/net debt means lower risk. (p.422–423, 427).
- Maturity does not change rank: all senior unsecured debt is one class, so a long bond and a short bond share the claim and the issue rating (p.431, 436).
- The issuer rating is not every bond's rating; secured and subordinated issues are notched (p.433).
- Different bonds of one issuer differ in LGD, not POD, under cross-default (p.433, 436).
- A collateral shortfall is not lost; it joins the senior unsecured pool (p.430).
- Liquidity weakness is not a sign of strength, and a solvent firm can still default (p.420).
- Agencies differ: Moody's ratings reflect expected loss; S&P and Fitch lean on POD with separate recovery ratings or notching (p.434–435).
- Recovery statistics are averages with wide dispersion; do not apply them blindly (p.432).

## Original practice seeds
**A (hypothetical, USD m).** Debt 600, EBITDA 200, interest 30. Debt/EBITDA = 3.0x; EBITDA/interest = 6.67x. EBITDA falls to 150: 4.0x and 5.0x. A covenant requiring coverage above 5.8x is breached.

**B (hypothetical liquidation, USD m).** Claims: secured loan 200 (collateral worth 150), senior unsecured 300, subordinated 100. Value realised 400: secured gets 150 from collateral; the 50 shortfall joins unsecured, making a pool of 350. Remaining 250/350 = 71.4%. Secured total = 150 + 50 × 0.714 = 185.7; senior unsecured = 214.3; subordinated 0. Check: 185.7 + 214.3 = 400.

**C (hypothetical).** POD 2% for all of an issuer's bonds. Recovery 60% senior unsecured, 20% subordinated. EL senior = 0.02 × 0.40 = 0.8%; EL subordinated = 0.02 × 0.80 = 1.6%. Double the EL, so a lower notched rating.
