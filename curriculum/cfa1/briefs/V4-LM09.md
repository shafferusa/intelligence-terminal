## Learning outcomes

1. Distinguish accounting profit, taxable income, taxes payable and income tax expense; separate temporary from permanent differences. Taught pp.279–283 (PDF 291–295), reinforced in the DTA/DTL section.
2. Explain how deferred tax liabilities and assets arise and how an analyst treats them, including realizability and debt/equity/excluded treatment of DTLs. Taught pp.283–289 (PDF 295–301) and in the case discussion pp.301–303 (PDF 313–315).
3. Calculate, interpret and contrast the effective, statutory and cash tax rates. Taught pp.290–296 (PDF 302–308).
4. Analyse tax disclosures (deferred tax items, rate reconciliation) and explain the effect on statements and ratios. Taught pp.297–305 (PDF 309–317).

## Core principles

- **Two rulebooks, two numbers.** Accounting standards measure profit for investors; tax law measures taxable income, which alone sets tax payable. Ask whether the gap reverses (timing) or never does (permanent). (pp.278–279)
- **Timing gaps create balance-sheet items.** Accelerated tax depreciation against straight-line book depreciation gives the same total deduction, only earlier: less cash tax now, more later, hence a DTL. (pp.279–280, 283)
- **Direction rule (Exhibit 1).** Asset: carrying amount above tax base gives a DTL; below gives a DTA. Liability: carrying amount above tax base gives a DTA; below gives a DTL. (p.280)
- **Expense is current plus deferred; cash is only the current part.** Deferred tax is the balance (year-end difference times enacted rate); only the year's change hits the income statement. A rate cut shrinks both DTLs and DTAs. (pp.283, 284)
- **A DTA is a claim on future profit, not a saleable asset.** If profit may fall short, IFRS writes the asset down; US GAAP keeps it gross and subtracts a valuation allowance. Changing the allowance moves expense and earnings with no cash change; it is management judgment. (p.284)
- **DTLs: debt, equity or excluded.** Expected to reverse with cash payment: debt. Never expected to reverse: equity. Amount and timing both uncertain: exclude. (pp.302–303, PDF 314–315)
- **Effective rate for earnings forecasts, cash rate for cash flow forecasts.** The blended effective rate is profit-weighted across countries. The wedge between the two rates is the deferred tax change. Normalise one-offs first. (pp.290–291)
- **Notes show what recurs.** Rate reconciliation, current/deferred split, valuation allowance and carryforward expiry show whether a low rate is sustainable. Carryforwards are worth the present value of the tax they shield. (pp.297–302)

## Vocabulary

- Accounting profit (pretax income) — income before tax under accounting standards (p.279).
- Taxable income — income under tax law; drives tax payable (p.279).
- Income tax payable — amount owed for the period (p.279).
- Income tax expense (provision) — current tax plus change in deferred tax (p.283).
- Tax base / carrying amount — value under tax rules / in the statements (p.279).
- Temporary / permanent difference — gap that reverses / never reverses (pp.279, 282).
- Taxable temporary difference — higher future taxable income; DTL (p.280).
- Deductible temporary difference — lower future taxable income; DTA (p.280).
- Deferred tax liability / asset — tax expensed now but payable later / future saving needing future profit (p.284).
- Valuation allowance — US GAAP reduction of a DTA to the more-likely-than-not amount (p.284).
- Tax loss carryforward (NOL) — past loss usable against future taxable income (p.279).
- Statutory, effective, cash tax rate — headline rate; total expense over pretax income; current taxes over pretax income (p.290).
- Rate reconciliation — note bridging statutory-rate tax to reported tax (p.291).
- Matching principle — tax cost reported with the activity that caused it (p.283).

## Formulas

The module has no numbered formulas; these relationships are stated in words and arithmetic.

1. Temporary difference = carrying amount − tax base for an asset (sign flips for a liability). Exhibit 1, p.280 (PDF 292).
2. Deferred tax balance = temporary difference × enacted tax rate; DTL if taxable, DTA if deductible. Assumes one rate, expected reversal, and (for DTAs) realizability. p.286 (PDF 298).
3. Income tax expense = income tax payable (current) + change in DTL − change in DTA. pp.283, 287 (PDF 295, 299).
4. Effective tax rate = income tax expense (total provision) / pretax income. Use for earnings forecasts. p.290 (PDF 302).
5. Cash tax rate = cash taxes paid / pretax income. The module's problems use the current provision as a proxy. Use for cash flow forecasts. pp.290, 296 (PDF 302, 308).
6. Blended effective rate = Σ(profit_i × rate_i) / Σ profit_i, not a simple average. pp.290, 297 (PDF 302, 309).
7. Tax at the statutory rate (reconciliation start) = pretax income × statutory rate. p.297 (PDF 309).
8. Pretax income backed out of a reconciliation = tax at statutory rate ÷ statutory rate. p.305 (read from text only).
9. Jurisdiction effective rate = (current + deferred tax of that jurisdiction) / its pretax income. pp.304, 310 (PDF 316, 322).

## Traps

- Treating tax expense as tax paid (p.283).
- Reversing the direction: asset carrying amount above tax base is a DTL, but liability carrying amount above tax base is a DTA (p.280).
- Giving permanent differences a deferred tax. Fines, tax-exempt income, non-deductible donations and direct tax credits move only the effective rate (p.282).
- Averaging country rates simply instead of weighting by profit (p.290).
- Swapping the rates' uses: effective for earnings, cash for cash flow (p.291).
- Summing deferred tax across years instead of computing it on the year-end balance (p.286).
- Thinking a valuation allowance changes cash taxes. Raising it lifts expense, lowering it cuts expense (p.284).
- Assuming a rate cut is bad for everyone. It shrinks a DTL (helpful) and a DTA (harmful) (p.284).

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

**A. Deferred tax build.** A firm buys a machine for 1,000 at the start of Year 1 and earns pretax income of 400. Books use straight-line over 5 years (200/yr); tax uses 2 years (500/yr). Rate 25%. End Year 1: carrying amount 800, tax base 500, difference 300, DTL = 75. Taxable income = 400 − 300 = 100, tax payable = 25, expense = 25 + 75 = 100 (ETR 25%), cash tax rate = 25/400 = 6.25%. End Year 2: difference 600, DTL 150, change another 75.

**B. Rate cut.** Same firm; DTL is 150 at the end of Year 2 (difference 600). A cut to 20% is enacted, so the DTL becomes 120. The 30 remeasurement lowers that year's deferred tax expense, a one-off earnings boost with no cash effect.

**C. Blended rate.** Profit 600 in a 30% country and 200 in a 10% country: tax = 180 + 20 = 200, ETR = 200/800 = 25.0% (simple average of rates would say 20%). If profits become 400 and 400: tax = 160, ETR = 20.0%.

## Book slips and uncertainties

- The advance-payment paragraph near p.284 (PDF 296) is internally inconsistent: advances taxed immediately give a DTA, yet the text speaks of a DTL. Trust the standard logic (DTA).
- A solution on p.304 (PDF 316) words a current-tax figure as partly deferred; the correct arithmetic subtracts the deferred benefit from current tax.
- The book's stated sign for a valuation allowance change on the case company's provision does not reconcile with its own exhibit; do not rely on the sign.
