## Learning outcomes
1. Explain why financial reporting exists and why standards matter for analysis and valuation (Objective, pp.4–5; Framework, p.7).
2. Explain what standard setters do versus regulators in creating and enforcing standards (Standards Boards, pp.5–7).
3. Explain the IASB Conceptual Framework: qualitative characteristics, constraints, elements (pp.7–11).
4. Explain the general requirements for IFRS financial statements (pp.10–12, plus the list in the Summary, p.25).
5. Explain what the balance sheet, comprehensive income statement, equity statement and cash flow statement each reveal (pp.12–24).

## Core principles
- **Information gap.** Managers know far more than outside capital providers, so reports bridge the gap. The objective is decision usefulness for those deciding whether to supply equity, debt or credit, and for judging management's stewardship (p.4). Standards narrow judgment but never remove it. (pp.4–5)
- **Setters write, regulators enforce.** Private bodies (IASB, FASB) write standards; legal authorities (such as the SEC) recognize and enforce them and can overrule the setters. A standard has force only where a regulator adopts it.  (pp.5–7)
- **Everything flows from decision usefulness.** Two fundamental characteristics: relevance (changes decisions; predictive and/or confirmatory value; materiality is a facet) and faithful representation (complete, neutral, free from error). Four enhancing: comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability. Constraints: cost versus benefit, and trade-offs among the enhancing ones (no fixed ranking). An estimate can raise relevance and faithfulness yet lower verifiability. (pp.7–9)
- **Elements and the two assumptions.** Assets are controlled resources from past events; liabilities are present obligations to transfer a resource; equity is the residual. Income and expenses drive performance, excluding owner contributions and distributions. Accrual records events when they occur, not when cash moves, so profit and cash differ. Going concern assumes continuation; if liquidation were expected, values would differ sharply (inventory sold at once), so the conclusion would fail. (pp.10–11)
- **Measurement basis drives the balance sheet.** Historical cost, amortised cost, current cost, realizable or settlement value, present value and fair value (an exit price at the measurement date, not the price paid). Book equity is a residual at these bases, not market value. (pp.11–12)
- **Four statements, four lenses.** Balance sheet: a point-in-time stock of resources and claims (liquidity, solvency). Income statement: a period flow of performance. Equity statement: roll-forward of owners' claims. Cash flow statement: cash sources and uses in operating, investing and financing, harder to manage than accruals. Consolidation includes 100% of a controlled subsidiary's line items (control presumed above 50% of votes) and then shows the minority owners' share. (pp.12–24)
- **Comprehensive income.** Net income plus OCI equals every equity change except owner dealings. OCI items (pension remeasurements, currency translation) skip profit or loss, so net income can hide equity swings. (pp.20–21)

## Vocabulary
- Standard-setting body — private body that writes standards (p.5).
- Regulator — legal body that enforces standards and may override setters (p.5).
- Conceptual Framework — IASB's concepts behind standards; not authoritative under US GAAP (p.7, note p.31).
- Relevance / materiality — information that affects decisions; omission or misstatement could change them (p.8).
- Faithful representation — complete, neutral, free from error (p.8).
- Asset, liability, equity — resource controlled, obligation, residual (p.10).
- Income, expense — income includes revenue and gains, expenses include losses; owner transactions excluded (p.10).
- Fair value — exit price (pp.11–12).
- Noncontrolling interest — share of a consolidated subsidiary owned by others (p.18).
- OCI — equity changes that bypass profit or loss (p.20).
- Indirect vs direct method (pp.22–24).

## Formulas
- Assets = Liabilities + Owners' equity (equivalently equity = assets − liabilities). (p.13)
- Revenue + Other income − Expenses = Net income (basic form Revenue − Expenses). A negative result is a net loss. (pp.17–18)
- Total comprehensive income = Net income + OCI. (p.20)
- Ending equity = opening equity + total comprehensive income + capital increases − dividends (and buybacks) ± other changes. (pp.21–22)
- Net change in cash = CFO + CFI + CFF + effect of exchange rates; ending cash = beginning cash + net change. (pp.23–24)
- Basic EPS = profit attributable to ordinary shareholders / weighted-average ordinary shares outstanding. Diluted EPS uses diluted shares (options and convertibles assumed exercised) and an adjusted profit figure. (p.19)

## Traps
- Standard setters enforce rules: no, regulators do and can overrule setters.
- "Accuracy" or "reliability" as a quality: the framework term is faithful representation; the book says it is sometimes called reliability.
- Understandability is enhancing, not a constraint.
- Profit is not cash: accrual accounting can show profit with weak or negative operating cash flow.
- The balance sheet is a stock at a date; income, equity and cash statements cover periods.
- Book value is not market value, and fair value is an exit price, not the price paid.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
1. A firm has assets of 500 and liabilities of 320, so equity is 180. It borrows 50 and holds the cash: assets 550, liabilities 370, equity still 180. It then records a 30 loss (assets fall by 30): assets 520, equity 150, liabilities 370, and 520 = 370 + 150.
2. A firm pays 60 for goods in November, sells them in December for 100, and is paid in January. December profit = 100 − 60 = 40; December operating cash = 0.
3. Net income is 80 and an OCI translation loss is 25, so comprehensive income is 55. Dividends of 20 and opening equity of 400 give closing equity 400 + 55 − 20 = 435. If the loss were 5 instead, comprehensive income is 75 and closing equity 455, while net income is unchanged.
