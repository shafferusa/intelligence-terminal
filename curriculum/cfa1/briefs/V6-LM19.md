3. Describe RMBS types (pass-throughs, CMOs) and their cash flows and risks. Printed pp.512–519 (PDF 522–529), plus agency/non-agency p.508.
4. Describe CMBS characteristics and risks. Printed pp.520–526 (PDF 530–536).

## Core principles
- **Prepayment is a borrower call option.** Rates fall: refinancing, early principal (contraction), reinvestment at lower yields, capped price upside. Rates rise: slower repayment (extension), longer life at higher discount rates, lower value. Use weighted average life, not contractual maturity. Pp.498, 502 (PDF 508, 512).
- **Tranching redistributes prepayment risk; it never removes it.** Time tranching pays one class at a time. Credit tranching (junior absorbs losses first) is a different axis and often coexists. Pp.503–504, 514 (PDF 513–514, 524).
- **Collateral quality drives default risk.** Low LTV means more borrower equity; low DTI means affordability. The lender holds a first lien and can foreclose. Pp.506–508 (PDF 516–518).
- **Recourse changes incentives.** With recourse, the lender can pursue the borrower for a shortfall. With non-recourse, recovery stops at the property, so negative equity makes strategic default more likely. Pp.509–510 (PDF 519–520).
- **Who stands behind the pool.** Agency RMBS: federal agency (full faith and credit) or GSE (GSE guarantee only). Non-agency (private-label): credit enhancement such as subordination, insurance or guarantees. P.508 (PDF 518).
- **Pass-through cash flow.** Interest, scheduled principal and prepayments flow to holders net of servicing/guarantee fees, so the pass-through rate is below WAC. Pool averages weight by current balance. Pp.512–514 (PDF 522–524).
- **Sequential-pay CMO.** All principal goes to the first tranche until retired, then the next. The first tranche is shielded from extension by later ones; later tranches are shielded from contraction by it. Special tranches: Z (accrues interest, no cash coupon until its turn), PO (gains when prepayments speed up), IO (loses when they speed up), floaters and inverse floaters, residual, PAC (steady schedule inside a prepayment range), support (absorbs the variability). Pp.515–519 (PDF 525–529).
- **CMBS are loans repaid from property income.** Pools are small and concentrated. Loans are usually non-recourse. Call protection (structural: sequential pay and loss order; loan-level: lockout, penalty points, defeasance) keeps contraction risk low, but balloon loans create refinancing risk, which is extension risk. Pp.520–526 (PDF 530–536).

## Vocabulary
- Prepayment, contraction, extension risk — principal repaid faster or slower than scheduled; early return when rates fall; slower repayment when rates rise (p.502).
- Time tranching — classes with different expected maturities paid in sequence (p.503).
- Credit tranching — classes ranked by who absorbs losses first (p.504).
- First lien / foreclosure — senior claim; right to seize and sell (p.506).
- LTV, DTI — loan relative to property value; monthly debt payment relative to monthly gross income (p.507).
- Prime / subprime; agency / non-agency — borrower quality; guaranteed vs private-label (p.508).
- Prepayment option / penalty; recourse / non-recourse (p.509).
- Strategic default — walking away from a loan worth more than the property (pp.509–510).
- Pass-through rate — pool coupon net of fees (pp.512–513). WAC / WAM — balance-weighted rate / remaining months (p.513).
- CMO — pool cash flows sliced into tranches (p.514). Z, PO, IO, residual, PAC, support (p.517).
- Call protection, lockout, penalty points, defeasance — CMBS prepayment deterrents; 1 point = 1% of balance (p.521).
- Balloon risk / workout period (p.522). DSC and NOI (p.522).
- WAMP — average of the mortgage rates in a CMBS pool, weighted by mortgage amount (p.520).

## Formulas
1. LTV = loan amount / property value. Percent. CMBS pool: total loans / total property values. Pp.507, 534 (PDF 517, 544).
2. DTI = monthly debt payment / monthly pre-tax gross income, monthly income = annual income / 12. Trap: using annual income. P.508 (PDF 518).
3. WAC = sum of (mortgage rate_k x CB_k / sum of CB), CB = current balance. Pp.513–514 (PDF 523–524).
4. WAM = sum of (months to maturity_k x CB_k / sum of CB). Remaining term, current-balance weights. P.514 (PDF 524).
5. WAMP = sum of (mortgage rate_i x mortgage amount MA_i / sum of MA). Pp.520, 534–535 (PDF 530, 544–545).
6. Weighted average tranche coupon = sum of (coupon_j x tranche principal_j / sum of principal). P.534 (PDF 544).
7. DSC = NOI / debt service; NOI = rental income minus cash operating expenses minus replacement reserve; debt service = annual interest plus principal. Times (x). Excludes depreciation and capex. Above 1.0x means income covers debt service while keeping the property in its initial state of repair. P.522 (PDF 532).
8. Level mortgage payment = P x r / (1 - (1+r)^-n), monthly r, n months. Not stated as a formula in the book; added by the notes as a check.

## Traps
- Believing tranching eliminates prepayment risk (p.514).
- Confusing time tranching (maturity ladder) with credit tranching (loss order).
- Calling "reinvest at lower rates" extension risk. That is contraction.
- DTI with annual income; WAC/WAM with beginning balances or simple averages.
- Pass-through rate equals WAC. It is lower by fees.
- "Agency means fully government backed." Only federal-agency securities carry full faith and credit.
- Balloon risk is extension risk, not contraction. Lockout, penalty points and defeasance protect; sequential pay is structural protection.
- IO value falls and PO value rises when prepayments speed up (p.517).

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
1. A buyer purchases a USD400,000 home with a USD320,000 loan; monthly payment USD1,900; annual income USD96,000. LTV = 320/400 = 80%. Monthly income = 8,000, so DTI = 1,900/8,000 = 23.75%. If the home falls to USD350,000 with the balance still about USD320,000, LTV = 91.4%.
2. Pool today: Loan 1 USD60,000 at 4.0%, 100 months left; Loan 2 USD40,000 at 6.0%, 200 months left. WAC = 0.6x4.0 + 0.4x6.0 = 4.8%. WAM = 0.6x100 + 0.4x200 = 140 months. If Loan 2 prepays fully, WAC = 4.0% and WAM = 100 months.
3. Rent USD500,000, cash operating expenses USD180,000, replacement reserve USD20,000: NOI = 300,000; debt service USD250,000 gives DSC 1.20x. If rent falls to USD420,000, NOI = 220,000 and DSC = 0.88x, so balloon refinancing is doubtful.
