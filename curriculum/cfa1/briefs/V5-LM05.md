## Learning outcomes

1. Contrast price and value for an equity security (Equity Price and Value, pp.108–114; framed p.105).
2. Contrast book value of equity, market capitalization and enterprise value as indicators of value (Observable Measures of Company Value, pp.115–125).
3. Describe the main categories of equity valuation models (present value, asset-based, relative) and the strengths and weaknesses of each (Equity Valuation Models, pp.126–139; recapped p.140).

## Core principles

- **Price is observed, value is estimated.** Price blends everyone's expectations of the size, timing and risk of owner cash flows. Intrinsic value is never seen; each investor holds a private perceived value (pp.105, 108).
- **Three ways to form a view.** Story-based, relative (peer multiples), absolute (discounted cash flows or asset values). (pp.109–111).
- **The gap drives action.** Price below value is undervalued, above is overvalued. Issuers repurchase when undervalued and issue when overvalued. Trades fail if the model is biased or bad news arrives. Analysts use ranges and probability-weighted scenarios (pp.111–114).
- **Book, market cap and EV answer different questions.** Book equity is backward-looking (historical cost, depreciation, intangibles, goodwill) and weak for asset-light firms. Market cap is forward-looking. A buyback cuts book equity by the cash paid; price effects come from the signal. Negative book equity can come from payouts above earnings and need not mean distress (pp.115–121).
- **EV prices the whole business.** Only net debt counts. EV spans all capital providers, so it compares firms with different leverage better than equity value. It is in reporting currency, so compare EV multiples across currencies. More debt at equal asset value means lower equity value, EV roughly unchanged (pp.118–126).
- **Match the model to the firm.** Present value models discount forecast cash flows plus a terminal value. Dividends fit stable payers; FCFE (at r_e) fits non-payers with stable leverage; FCFF (at WACC) fits changing leverage; residual income fits clean book values and irregular dividends, with less weight on terminal value. Asset-based models suit divisions, single assets and liquidation but ignore going-concern earning power. Relative models are fast, but peer choice drives the answer and multiples differ for real reasons (pp.126–135).
- **Reconcile, do not average blindly.** Combine methods through ranges (football-field chart). Wide dispersion is inconclusive; recheck assumptions and reweight. A conclusion can flip when weights change (pp.136–139).

## Vocabulary

- Market price — the price at which a share trades now (p.108).
- Intrinsic value — unobserved worth, the discounted value of expected cash flows (p.108).
- Perceived value — one investor's own estimate (pp.105, 108).
- Relative / absolute valuation — against peers / directly from cash flows or assets (p.109).
- Scenario analysis — probability-weighted bullish, base, bearish outcomes (p.112).
- Book value of equity — carrying value of assets minus liabilities (p.115).
- Goodwill — acquisition price above fair value of identifiable net assets (p.116).
- Market capitalization — price times shares outstanding (p.118).
- Price-to-book — price over book equity per share (p.121).
- Net debt — debt less cash and short-term investments (p.122).
- Enterprise value — equity, preferred and debt at market, less cash and short-term investments (pp.122–123).
- Residual income — earnings after a charge for equity capital (p.129).
- Asset-based valuation — fair value of assets minus liabilities (p.130).
- Justified multiple — fair-value multiple supported by comparables or fundamentals (p.135).

## Formulas

- **Intrinsic value (Eq. 1, p.109 / PDF 119):** IV_t = sum_{i=1..n} E(CF_{t+i}) / (1+r_e)^i + E(P_{t+n}) / (1+r_e)^n. Per share; r_e is cost of equity; E(P_{t+n}) is an estimate. Eq. 10 (p.127) restates it with the sum from i = t+1, the same series.
- **Valuation test (p.111):** P < IV undervalued; P > IV overvalued; P about IV fair.
- **Gap-closing return (p.112, unnumbered):** (IV − P)/P for a non-dividend stock, assuming convergence.
- **Scenario value (Eq. 2, p.112):** IV = sum_i Probability(i) × P_i; probabilities sum to 1 and are judgmental.
- **Book value of equity (Eq. 3, p.115):** book assets − book liabilities. **Per share (Eq. 4, p.116 / PDF 126):** shareholders' equity / shares outstanding. **Eq. 5 (p.117):** share capital + APIC + retained earnings − treasury stock.
- **Market cap (Eq. 6, p.118):** P × shares outstanding.
- **P/B (Eq. 7, p.121):** P / (BVE per share). Meaningless if BVE is negative.
- **EV (Eq. 8, p.123):** market value of debt + equity + preferred − cash and short-term investments. Par or book debt is often used.
- **EV multiple (Eq. 9, p.124):** EV / (revenue or EBITDA). Unitless.
- **FCFF (Eq. 11, p.127):** CFO + interest expense × (1 − tax rate) − capex; discount at WACC.
- **FCFE (Eq. 12, p.127):** CFO − capex + net debt issuance (borrowing − repayment); discount at r_e. FCFE − FCFF = net borrowing − after-tax interest.
- **Residual income (Eq. 13, p.129 / PDF 139):** IV = BVE_t + sum_{i=1..n} RI_{t+i}/(1+r_e)^i + [E(P_{t+n}) − BVE_{t+n}]/(1+r_e)^n. RI = net income − r_e × book equity (beginning-of-period book equity is the standard convention; the reading does not spell it out).
- **P/E (Eq. 14, p.132):** P / EPS, trailing or forward; positive, normalized EPS only.
- **EV/EBITDA rule (p.133):** firm above the peer composite suggests relatively overvalued; below, undervalued, all else equal.
- **Derived (not printed):** net debt = EV − market cap when no preferred; equity value = EV − debt − preferred + cash.

## Traps

- Treating price as value, or "undervalued" as a guaranteed return (pp.108, 111–112).
- Reading book value as true worth, or P/B as meaningful when book equity is negative (pp.115–118, 121).
- Reading a low P/B as cheap without checking leverage, asset quality or distress (pp.120–121).
- Sign error on cash in EV: subtract going to EV, add back going from EV to equity value (pp.122–123).
- Comparing EVs across currencies instead of EV multiples (pp.123–124).
- Mismatching cash flow and discount rate: FCFF with WACC; FCFE, dividends, residual income with r_e (p.130).
- Assuming present value models need dividends; FCFE or residual income handle non-payers (pp.127–129).
- Using P/E with negative EPS or unadjusted one-off gains (pp.132–134).
- Calling a firm overvalued from a higher multiple alone; growth and risk may justify it (p.135).

## Original practice seeds

All examples below are hypothetical.

1. **EV and cash (USD).** 20m shares at 40, debt 300m, preferred 50m, cash 100m, EBITDA 150m. Market cap = 800m; EV = 800 + 300 + 50 − 100 = 1,050m; EV/EBITDA = 7.0x. With cash of 200m, EV = 950m and the multiple is 6.33x.
2. **Scenario value (one year, no dividend).** Price 50; year-end base 60, bull 70, bear 45. Weights 0.4/0.25/0.35 give 24 + 17.5 + 15.75 = 57.25, return 14.5%. Weights 0.2/0.2/0.6 give 12 + 14 + 27 = 53, return 6.0%.
3. **FCFF vs FCFE (USD m).** CFO 500, interest 40, tax 25%, capex 200, borrowing 100, repayment 30. FCFF = 500 + 30 − 200 = 330. FCFE = 500 − 200 + 70 = 370. Difference 40 = net borrowing 70 − after-tax interest 30. With repayment 80, net borrowing is 20 and FCFE = 320, below FCFF.
