## Core principles
- **A lease is a financed right to use an asset.** A contract is a lease only if it names a specific asset, gives the customer nearly all its economic benefits, and lets the customer direct its use (p.243). The analyst asks how much hidden financing a firm carries (p.244–245).
- **Classification sets expense pattern, not total cash.** Finance lease if any one of five tests is met: ownership passes, a purchase option is reasonably certain, the term covers most of the asset's life, PV of payments is close to or above fair value, or the asset has no alternative use to the lessor (p.245). Otherwise operating (p.250).
- **Lessee, IFRS: one model.** Liability and ROU asset at the PV of payments, discounted at the implicit rate or the lessee's secured borrowing rate. Each payment splits into interest (rate x opening liability) and principal; the ROU asset is amortised straight-line, so total expense is front-loaded (p.247). Exemptions: terms of 12 months or less, and low-value assets (IFRS only); expensed straight-line (p.246).
- **Lessee, US GAAP: two models.** Finance matches IFRS. Operating has the same liability schedule, but ROU amortisation is a plug so total expense equals the payment, one operating expense line (p.248–250). Versus finance, all else equal: lower EBITDA margin, lower asset turnover, lower operating cash flow (p.251).
- **Lessor.** Finance lease: derecognise the asset, book a receivable at PV, recognise any selling gain at inception, then interest income. Operating lease: keep and depreciate the asset, straight-line revenue (p.251–252).
- **DC vs DB is risk allocation.** DC: employer pays an agreed amount, expense equals contribution, employee bears the risk (p.253–254). DB: employer promises a payout and bears the risk; the balance sheet shows PV of obligation minus fair value of plan assets (p.254).
- **DB expense lands in different places.** IFRS: service cost and net interest in profit or loss; remeasurements in OCI, never recycled (p.254–255). US GAAP: service cost, interest and expected asset return (reducing expense) in profit or loss; past service cost and actuarial gains/losses first in OCI, then amortised; immediate recognition allowed (p.255–256).
- **Share pay costs the firm without cash.** Equity-settled awards are measured at grant-date fair value and expensed over the service period; later price moves do not change it (p.257–259, 261–263). Retained earnings fall and paid-in capital rises, so total equity is unchanged. Cash-settled forms (SARs, phantom stock) create a liability (p.263).

## Vocabulary
- ROU asset — lessee's asset, initially the PV of payments (p.247).
- Implicit rate — rate equating PV of payments to fair value (p.247, 251).
- Net interest — discount rate x net DB position (IFRS) (p.255).
- Remeasurements — actuarial gains/losses plus asset return versus the interest-implied amount (p.255).
- SAR / phantom stock — cash-settled pay tied to share value (p.263).

## Formulas
Only item 9 is a displayed formula; the rest are working relationships.
1. Lease liability = sum over t of payment_t / (1 + r)^t; r = implicit or secured borrowing rate, annual decimal; payments at period end. p.247 (PDF 259).
2. Interest = r x opening liability (receivable); principal = payment − interest. Lessee p.247, lessor p.251 (PDF 263).
3. IFRS ROU amortisation = initial ROU / lease term (usually straight-line). p.247.
4. US GAAP operating lessee: ROU amortisation = payment − interest, so total expense = payment. p.248–250.
5. Net pension liability = PV of DB obligation − fair value of plan assets (positive is a deficit). p.254 (PDF 266).
6. Net interest (IFRS) = discount rate x net DB position at start of period. p.255 (PDF 267).
7. Annual share expense = grant-date fair value x awards / vesting years (straight-line). p.259, 261, 263.
8. Expected next-year expense ≈ unrecognised cost / weighted average remaining period (rough). p.262 (PDF 274).
9. EBITDA margin = EBITDA / total revenues; asset turnover = total revenues / total assets; cash flow per share = CFO / shares outstanding. p.251 (PDF 263).

## Traps
- "Operating lease means off balance sheet." Wrong: ROU asset and liability are recognised for terms over 12 months (p.247–248).
- A US GAAP operating lessee shows one lease expense, and the whole payment sits in CFO (p.249).
- Lessor accounting is nearly the same under both standards; the lessee side differs (p.251).
- IFRS actuarial items go to OCI and are never amortised; US GAAP usually amortises them (p.255).
- Option expense uses grant-date fair value. Higher volatility, longer life and higher risk-free rate raise option value; higher dividend yield lowers it (p.260, 263).
- Pension assets and obligation are netted on the balance sheet (p.254).

## Original practice seeds
**A (hypothetical, EUR).** Lease of EUR10,000 at each year end for 3 years, rate 8%. PV = 10,000 x 2.57710 = 25,771. Year 1: interest 2,062, principal 7,938, liability 17,833. Year 2: interest 1,427, principal 8,573, liability 9,260. Year 3: interest 741, principal about 9,259, liability about 0. IFRS year-1 expense = 2,062 + 25,771/3 (8,590) = 10,652; US GAAP operating expense = 10,000 flat. At 12% the PV falls to 24,018.

**B (hypothetical, USD).** 6,000 RSUs, grant-date value USD30, 3-year vesting. Total cost 180,000; expense 60,000 a year, offset in paid-in capital. If the price later reaches USD50, expense is unchanged. Unrecognised cost after year 1 is 120,000.

**C (hypothetical).** One pension payment of 1,000 due in 10 years. At 5%, PV = 613.91; at 4%, PV = 675.56 (up 61.65). With plan assets of 600, the net liability moves from 13.91 to 75.56. A lower rate worsens funded status; under IFRS the change is a remeasurement (OCI).
