## Learning outcomes

1. Describe credit risk and its two components, probability of default and loss given default. Sources of Credit Risk, printed pp.348–358 (PDF 358–368).
2. Describe how agency ratings are used and where they fall short. Credit Rating Agencies and Credit Ratings, printed pp.359–364 (PDF 369–374).
3. Describe the macroeconomic, market and issuer-specific drivers of the level and volatility of yield spreads, and the price effect of spread changes. Factors Impacting Yield Spreads, printed pp.365–378 (PDF 375–388).

## Core principles

- **Credit risk is the chance of not being paid in full and on time.** The lender is paid a spread for that risk, measured as expected loss = POD x LGD (pp.348, 354).
- **Two levers: POD and LGD.** POD is how likely default is; LGD is how much is lost if it happens. Security and seniority change LGD, not POD. Investment-grade unsecured holders mainly fear a rise in POD; high-yield holders mainly try to shrink LGD through covenants and collateral (pp.354–358).
- **Repayment sources.** Corporates pay from operating, investing and financing cash flows, with asset sales or new capital as backup. Sovereigns pay from taxes and revenues, with new borrowing or privatisation as backup (pp.350–353).
- **Fair compensation test.** A credit spread over the government curve should roughly equal POD x LGD. It ignores liquidity, risk premium, option features and tax, so it is a first screen, not a price (p.354).
- **Ratings are slow summaries, not prices.** Issuer-paid, forward-looking letter opinions used for comparison, covenants and regulation. They lag prices, can miss fraud or sudden shifts, and can split between agencies. Outlooks are closer to the market (pp.359–364).
- **Spreads follow the cycle and the issuer.** They tighten in strong economies and widen in weak ones; high-yield spreads move more. In stress investors flee to quality and liquidity dries up, especially for small, low-rated issuers. Compare with peers to separate issuer-specific from macro moves (pp.365–370).
- **Spread changes hit price through duration and convexity.** For option-free bonds the same measures that capture benchmark-yield sensitivity apply. Wider spread lowers price; longer duration means bigger moves; add convexity for larger moves. Benchmark yields and spreads often move together, so independence can fail (pp.371–378).

## Vocabulary

- Credit risk; default — loss from non-payment as promised; a missed interest or principal payment (p.348).
- Cs of credit — borrower-specific: capacity, capital, collateral, covenants, character; top-down: conditions, country, currency (p.349).
- Illiquid vs insolvent — cannot raise cash now vs liabilities exceed assets (p.350).
- Pari passu; cross-default — equal ranking; default on one debt triggers default on others (p.351).
- POD — annual chance of default, conditional on no earlier default (p.354).
- Expected exposure; recovery rate; loss severity; LGD; EL (p.354).
- G-spread / credit spread — yield over the government curve (p.354).
- Investment grade vs high yield — Baa3/BBB– and above vs Ba1/BB+ and below (p.360).
- Credit migration risk (p.359); outlook (p.360); split rating (p.362).
- Credit spread risk (p.365); flight to quality (p.366); market liquidity risk (p.368); debt coverage and leverage (p.369).

## Formulas

1. **Expected loss (p.354, PDF 364).** EL = POD x LGD, where LGD = EE x (1 – RR). POD annual, decimal; EE net of collateral; RR decimal. Single period; ignores timing and correlation.
2. **Credit spread approximation (p.354, PDF 364).** Credit spread ≈ POD x LGD. Annual, decimal or bp.
3. **Spread price impact, duration only (p.373, PDF 383).** %ΔPV_full = –AnnModDur x ΔSpread. ΔSpread in decimal (1% = 0.01). Small instantaneous changes, option-free bond.
4. **With convexity (p.373, PDF 383).** %ΔPV_full = –(AnnModDur x ΔSpread) + 0.5 x AnnConvexity x (ΔSpread)^2. Convexity must be on the duration-squared scale (a reported figure often needs multiplying by 100); result is a decimal fraction.
5. **Approximate modified duration (p.374, PDF 384).** ApproxModDur = (PV_– – PV_+) / (2 x ΔYield x PV_0). PV_– after a yield fall, PV_+ after a rise.
6. **Approximate convexity (p.374, PDF 384).** ApproxCon = (PV_– + PV_+ – 2 x PV_0) / (ΔYield^2 x PV_0).
7. **Yield spread decomposition (p.372, PDF 382).** Yield spread = bond yield – benchmark yield. Liquidity spread = bid-price yield – offer-price yield. Credit spread = yield spread – liquidity spread. Bond yield uses the mid price.

## Traps

- Secured status lowers LGD, not POD (p.354).
- Exposure is net of collateral in the expected-loss calculation (p.354).
- Spread equals expected loss is only an approximation (p.354).
- Illiquid is not insolvent (p.350).
- Ratings lag prices and are issuer-paid; Moody's C is not the same as S&P/Fitch D (pp.360–362).
- Sign and units: widening hurts price, the convexity term is added, convexity must be rescaled, spreads go in as decimals (p.373).
- Solving backward, check the direction: a price rise implies a spread fall (p.373).
- High-yield returns are steadier than equity because income is a larger share of return (p.365).
- Investment-grade bonds still carry spread risk; duration-only error grows with maturity and move size (pp.373, 376).

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

1. **EL and compensation.** Face USD1,000,000, collateral USD200,000, so EE = 800,000. RR on the uncovered amount is 40%, POD 2%. LGD = 800,000 x 0.60 = 480,000 (48% of face). EL = 0.02 x 480,000 = USD9,600, or 0.96% of face. A 110 bp spread exceeds EL by 14 bp. If POD rises to 3%, EL = 14,400 (1.44%) and the same spread is 34 bp short.
2. **Spread impact.** Option-free bond, modified duration 6, convexity 50 (duration-squared scale), spread widens 80 bp, benchmark unchanged. %ΔP = –6 x 0.008 + 0.5 x 50 x 0.008^2 = –0.048 + 0.0016 = –4.64%. Duration only gives –4.8%. With duration 3 and the same convexity: –0.024 + 0.0016 = –2.24%.
3. **Spread decomposition.** Mid-price yield 5.40%, benchmark 4.10%, bid-price yield 5.45%, offer-price yield 5.35%. Yield spread = 130 bp. Liquidity spread = 10 bp. Credit spread = 120 bp.
