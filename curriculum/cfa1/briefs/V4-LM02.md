## Core principles

- **Revenue follows control, not cash.** Credit sale: receivable. Cash first: contract liability, released as delivery occurs. Booked but conditional: contract asset. Risk: customer never pays or returns goods (pp.39–41).
- **Five steps:** contract, performance obligations, price, allocation, recognition. Steps 3–4 fix the amount, step 5 the timing. Collectability "probable" is a lower bar under IFRS than "likely" under US GAAP (pp.40–41).
- **Business model moves margins.** An agent reports only its fee, so margin percentage rises while profit dollars stay the same. Long-term contracts use progress (cost incurred over estimated total cost), so a revised cost estimate shifts profit between periods with no cash change (pp.41–44).
- **Capitalizing delays expense; totals do not change.** The cash is spent either way. Capitalizing puts the outlay in investing cash flow and spreads non-cash depreciation. Early on: higher income, ROE, margin, CFO, assets and equity; later: lower income. The boost fades once depreciation catches up with steady spending. Managers may capitalize to hit targets. Tax effects assume tax follows book (pp.47–54).
- **Capitalized interest hides interest.** It sits on the balance sheet, returns as depreciation or COGS, and the outflow is investing. Use total interest (expensed plus capitalized) in coverage; CFO is flattered (pp.55–57).
- **Development cost.** Expensing lowers income while current spending exceeds past amortization. To compare, restate the capitalizer: add cost to expense, remove amortization and the asset, cut CFO and investing outflow equally. P/E, P/CFO and EV/EBITDA then rise because denominators fall at an unchanged price (pp.58–61).
- **Separate non-recurring items, but judge recurrence.** Discontinued operations (separable component being disposed of) appear net of tax after continuing operations. Unusual or infrequent items stay in continuing operations. Policy changes are retrospective; estimate changes are prospective; error corrections restate prior periods (pp.63–68).
- **Diluted EPS is the conservative view.** Common holders are residual claimants. Assume potential shares exist; exclude any instrument that would raise EPS (pp.69–77).

## Vocabulary

- Contract liability (unearned revenue) — cash received before delivery (p.39).
- Contract asset — revenue booked, payment still conditional (p.41).
- Performance obligation — a distinct promised good or service (p.40).
- Principal vs agent — controls the product (gross) vs arranges a sale (net fee) (p.41).
- Bill and hold — customer owns goods, delivery delayed (p.44).
- Matching — expense in the period of related revenue (p.45).
- Period cost — expensed as incurred (p.47).
- Capitalization — recording spending as an asset (p.47).
- Technological feasibility — point after which software cost is capitalized (p.58).
- Conservative policy — expenses sooner or revenue later (p.62).
- Discontinued operation — separable component being disposed of (p.65).
- Retrospective application — restate all periods shown (p.66).
- Simple vs complex capital structure — no vs some dilutive securities (p.69).
- Warrant — company-issued call option on new shares (p.69).
- Antidilutive — would raise EPS if included (p.77).
- Common-size statement — every line as a percentage of revenue (p.78).

## Formulas

1. Basic EPS = (Net income − Preferred dividends) / Weighted average shares. Currency per share. Time-weight issues and buybacks; splits and stock dividends apply retroactively; common dividends are ignored. P.70 (PDF 82).
2. Diluted EPS, convertible preferred = Net income / (Weighted average shares + New shares at conversion). Conversion assumed at period start, so no preferred dividend deducted. P.72 (PDF 84).
3. Diluted EPS, convertible debt = (Net income + After-tax interest − Preferred dividends) / (Weighted average shares + Additional shares at conversion); after-tax interest = interest × (1 − tax rate). The book does not explain the dividend term. P.73 (PDF 85).
4. Treasury stock method = (Net income − Preferred dividends) / [Weighted average shares + (New shares at exercise − Shares repurchased) × Proportion of year outstanding]; repurchased = options × exercise price / average market price. Options at or above the average price add nothing. Intuition: exercise cash buys back shares. P.75 (PDF 87).
5. Net profit margin = Net income / Revenue; gross margin = (Revenue − COGS) / Revenue. P.81 (PDF 93). Operating and pretax margins likewise, p.82 (PDF 94).
6. Common-size item = Line item / Revenue (taxes against pretax income). Pp.78–80.
7. Percentage of completion: progress = cost to date / estimated total cost; period revenue = progress × price − prior cumulative revenue. P.44 (PDF 56).
8. Adjusted coverage = (EBIT + depreciation of earlier capitalized interest) / (interest expense + capitalized interest). Pp.56–57.
9. Remaining life = Net plant and equipment / annual depreciation. P.55 (PDF 67).

## Traps

- Treating revenue as cash received.
- Thinking capitalizing creates income; the expenser's later income jump is timing, not performance.
- Comparing CFO under both methods without the tax difference.
- Leaving capitalized interest out of coverage.
- Calling a fire loss or restructuring a discontinued operation.
- Restating for a change in estimate (it is prospective).
- Deducting common dividends, or time-weighting a split.
- Pre-tax interest in the convertible-debt numerator; exercise price instead of average market price in the buyback; including antidilutive securities (diluted never exceeds basic).
- Reading a higher margin as better without checking strategy.

## Original practice seeds

All hypothetical.

**A. Percentage of completion.** Price USD900,000, estimated cost 600,000, year-1 cost 240,000: progress 40%, revenue 360,000, profit 120,000. Estimate revised to 800,000: progress 30%, revenue 270,000, profit 30,000.

**B. Capitalize vs expense.** Machine USD60,000, three years straight line; pretax income before it 100,000 a year; tax 25% for book and tax. Capitalized: net income 60,000 each year. Expensed: 30,000, 75,000, 75,000. Both total 180,000. Year-1 CFO: 80,000 vs 30,000, a gap of 50,000 (outlay 60,000 less 10,000 extra tax).

**C. Treasury stock method.** Net income USD500,000; 200,000 weighted shares; basic EPS 2.50. Options 12,000 at strike 20, average price 30: repurchase 8,000, net 4,000, diluted EPS = 500,000/204,000 = 2.451. At average price 18 the options are excluded, so diluted = 2.50.

## Book slips and uncertainties

- Example 3: the text says the capitalizer's CFO gap equals the full outlay, but its exhibit shows a smaller gap because of extra tax. Trust the exhibit (pp.48–49).
- Several small typos exist (text versus exhibit mismatches, a mistyped figure on p.52, a wrong exhibit label); trust exhibits over text, including the p.81 margin.
