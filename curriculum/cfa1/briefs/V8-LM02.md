## Core principles
- **Timing is part of the skill being judged.** The GP controls when capital is called and returned, so a return measure must respect timing. IRR does (it assumes financing and reinvestment rates); MOIC does not. Fees come before earnings, hence the J-curve. Do not judge a young fund by early returns. (pp.37–39, PDF 45–47)
- **MOIC is a quick multiple, not a return.** Invested capital is paid-in capital less fees and expenses, not committed capital. (p.39, PDF 47)
- **Leverage magnifies both directions.** Each borrowed unit earns the asset return minus the borrowing cost; the lender is paid first. An unmet margin call forces sales at depressed prices. (pp.40–41, PDF 48–49)
- **Valuation is a model, and the manager has a stake.** Level 1 uses quoted prices for identical assets, Level 2 other observable inputs, Level 3 unobservable inputs (typical for private equity and real estate). Smoothed or stale marks make returns look steady and volatility and correlation look low; fees tied to value create a conflict. Calm numbers are not low risk. (pp.41–42, PDF 49–50)
- **Fee terms decide who keeps the profit.** GP pay is a management fee (here on year-end AUM) plus a performance fee. A hard hurdle pays only on the excess; a soft hurdle with full catch-up pays the GP its share of all profit once cleared. A high-water mark prevents paying twice for recovering a loss; it is after fees and depends on entry date. A clawback returns carry if later losses mean the GP took too much. (pp.45–51, PDF 53–59)
- **Waterfall design changes GP cash timing.** European (whole fund): LPs get capital and preferred return first. American (deal by deal): carry as deals profit, so LPs rely on clawback. (pp.52–57, PDF 60–65; practice pp.59–60, 63)
- **Index and structure effects flatter results.** Hedge fund indexes overstate returns via survivorship and backfill bias. Compare PE and real estate with same-vintage peers. A fund of funds adds a fee layer but may buy diligence, diversification and access. Lockups, notice periods and gates protect remaining investors; redemptions cluster when performance is poor. (pp.44, 52–53, PDF 52, 60–61)

## Formulas
Notation: P0 beginning assets, P1 end assets (before fees), r_m management fee rate on year-end AUM, p performance fee rate, r_h hurdle rate, r_i investor net return, R_GP GP compensation (currency). The fee base (beginning, end or net) must be read from the question; hurdles are simple, not compounded, unless told (p.35, PDF 43).
- **IRR** (p.38, PDF 46): sum over t=0..n of CF_t/(1+r)^t = 0. Outflows negative.
- **MOIC, Eq 1** (p.39, PDF 47): (Realized + Unrealized value)/Total invested capital.
- **Eq 2** (p.40, PDF 48): r_L = [r(V_c + V_b) − V_b r_b]/V_c. V_c own capital, V_b borrowed.
- **Eq 3** (p.40, PDF 48): r_L = r + (V_b/V_c)(r − r_b). Breakeven r_b = r (p.41, PDF 49). Divide by own capital.
- **Eq 4** (p.46, PDF 54): R_GP = P1 r_m + max[0, (P1 − P0)p]. Fees independent.
- **Eq 5** (p.46, PDF 54): r_i = (P1 − P0 − R_GP)/P0.
- **Eq 6** (p.47, PDF 55): R_GP = P1 r_m + max{0, [P1(1 − r_m) − P0]p}. Net basis, so smaller.
- **Eq 7** (p.47, PDF 55): R_GP = P1 r_m + max{0, [P1(1 − r_m) − P0(1 + r_h)]p}. Hard hurdle; the fee falls by P0 × r_h × p (see slips).
- **Eq 8** (p.48, PDF 56): R_GP = P_t r_m + max[0, (P_t(1 − r_m) − P_HWM)p]; P_HWM is the highest prior year-end after-fee value. **Eq 9**: r_i = (P_t − P_(t−1) − R_GP)/P_(t−1), using the investor's own after-fee starting capital.
- **Unlevered from leveraged** (p.61, PDF 69): r = (V_c r_L + V_b r_b)/(V_c + V_b).
- **Combined hurdle and HWM test** (p.63, PDF 71; not numbered): fee = p × max(0, P_end(1 − r_m) − max(HWM, P_begin(1 + r_h))). Treats the hurdle as hard.
- **Waterfall arithmetic** (pp.35, 54, PDF 43, 62): LPs get capital plus simple preferred return; full catch-up = preferred return/0.8 × 0.2 (for 20% carry); the remainder is split 80/20.

## Traps
- Higher MOIC is not a better return. The denominator is paid-in capital less fees, not commitments (p.39).
- Soft hurdle with full catch-up pays on all profit once cleared; hard only on the excess (pp.53–54).
- Read whether fees are independent or net; net gives a smaller performance fee (p.47).
- HWM is after fees and per entry date. When the hurdle level is below the HWM, use the larger of the two (pp.48–49, 63).
- Either/or fee: the larger, not the sum (p.56). Convert currency fees to a percent of P0 before stating a return.
- Index biases push reported returns up; a fund of funds is not automatically inferior (pp.52–53).

## Original practice seeds
**A (hypothetical). MOIC vs IRR.** Invest 100 now; Fund X returns 200 at year 2, Fund Y at year 10, nothing between. Both MOIC 2.0x. IRR X = 2^(1/2) − 1 = 41.4%; IRR Y = 2^(1/10) − 1 = 7.2%.

**B (hypothetical). Leverage.** Own capital 50, borrow 50 at 4%, one year. Assets +6%: r_L = 0.06 + 1(0.06 − 0.04) = 8%. Assets −3%: r_L = −0.03 + 1(−0.03 − 0.04) = −10%. Check: −3 − 2 = −5, over 50 = −10%.

**C (hypothetical). Hurdle effect.** P0 = 200, P1 = 230, r_m = 1.5%, p = 15%, net basis, one year. Management fee = 3.45. 4% hard hurdle: (226.55 − 208) × 0.15 = 2.7825; total 6.2325; r_i = 23.7675/200 = 11.88%. 8% hurdle: (226.55 − 216) × 0.15 = 1.5825; total 5.0325; r_i = 12.48%. A higher hurdle helps the investor while profit stays above it.
