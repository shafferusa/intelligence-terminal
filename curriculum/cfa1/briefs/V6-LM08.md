## Core principles
- **A floater stays near par because its coupon resets.** Coupon = MRR set at period start plus a fixed quoted margin, paid at period end (in arrears). Rate risk is small; credit risk is carried by the margin. Fails if reset periods are long (the note then acts like a short fixed-rate note). pp.190–191 (PDF 200–201), p.195 (PDF 205).
- **Quoted margin vs required margin sets premium or discount.** Quoted margin (QM) is fixed at issue. Required margin, also called discount margin (DM), is the spread the market demands now. If DM > QM the price is below par by the PV of the shortfall; if DM < QM, premium; equal, par. pp.191 (PDF 201).
- **Required margin reflects issuer-specific risk (credit, liquidity, tax).** Macro moves show up in the MRR, not the margin. p.188 (PDF 198), p.208 (PDF 218).
- **Between resets, price drifts then is pulled to par.** The text says a premium results if MRR has fallen and a discount if it has risen; the price converges to par at the next reset if DM equals QM. p.191 (PDF 201).
- **The FRN model is a simplification.** One MRR sets and discounts coupons, valuation is on a reset date (no accrued interest), 30/360. So DM from a price is a model-dependent estimate, solved by calculator. pp.192–194 (PDF 202–204).
- **Discount rate quotes divide by face value; add-on quotes divide by money invested.** Discount instruments (CP, T-bills, bankers' acceptances): pay less than face, receive face. Add-on instruments (CDs, repos, MRRs): pay principal, receive principal plus interest. Face exceeds price, so DR understates return and cost. pp.197–199 (PDF 207–209).
- **Compare money market instruments on one basis.** Day-count and quote type differ, so convert to BEY, then compare or measure spreads over T-bills. Rates are simple interest with periodicity Year/Days; convert periodicity to compare with bond yields. pp.199–202 (PDF 209–212).
- **Selling a CD early:** value the maturity amount at the current add-on rate for the days remaining; a higher market rate means a lower price. pp.198–199 (PDF 208–209).

## Vocabulary
- Floating-rate note (FRN) — debt whose coupon resets to a reference rate plus a spread (pp.187–190, PDF 197–200).
- Market reference rate (MRR) — short-term benchmark rate that sets the floating part of the coupon (p.191).
- Quoted margin (QM) — fixed spread over MRR written into the terms (p.191).
- Required margin / discount margin (DM) — spread the market demands now; the spread that would price the FRN at par on a reset date (pp.188, 191).
- In arrears — rate fixed at period start, interest paid at period end (p.191).
- Pulled to par — flat price converging to par as reset nears when margins match (p.191).
- Money market instrument — debt with original maturity of one year or less (p.197, PDF 207).
- Discount rate (DR) — annualized interest divided by face value (pp.197–198).
- Add-on rate (AOR) — annualized interest divided by amount invested (pp.198–199).
- Bond equivalent yield (BEY) — money market rate stated as an add-on rate on a 365-day year (p.200, PDF 210).
- Periodicity — periods per year implied by a rate; Year/Days for money market rates (pp.198, 201).

## Formulas
1. **Simplified FRN price (Eq. 1), p.192 (PDF 202):** PV = sum over t=1..N of [(MRR + QM) x FV / m] / (1 + (MRR + DM)/m)^t, plus FV / (1 + (MRR + DM)/m)^N. PV per 100 par; MRR, QM, DM annual decimals; m payments per year; N periods. Valid on a reset date, 30/360, same MRR for coupons and discounting, no accrued interest. Intuition: coupon uses QM, discounting uses DM, so the gap prices the note.
2. **Solving DM, p.194 (PDF 204; repeated PDF 218):** PMT = (MRR + QM) x FV / m; r = (MRR + DM)/m; so DM = m x r − MRR. Solve r from PV (negative), FV, PMT, N on a calculator. Intuition: back out the periodic discount rate, then strip off MRR.
3. **Price, discount basis (Eq. 2), p.197 (PDF 207):** PV = FV x (1 − (Days/Year) x DR). DR annual; Days settlement to maturity; Year 360 or 365.
4. **Discount rate (Eq. 3), p.198 (PDF 208):** DR = (Year/Days) x (FV − PV)/FV. Denominator FV, so it understates return.
5. **Price, add-on basis (Eq. 4), p.198 (PDF 208):** PV = FV / (1 + (Days/Year) x AOR). FV includes interest; PV is principal.
6. **Add-on rate (Eq. 5), p.199 (PDF 209):** AOR = (Year/Days) x (FV − PV)/PV. With Year = 365 this is the BEY.
7. **BEY from a discount quote, p.201 (PDF 211):** (1) PV = FV(1 − Days/Year x DR); (2) AOR = (365/Days)(FV − PV)/PV; (3) read as BEY.
8. **Periodicity conversion, p.202 (PDF 212):** (1 + APR_m/m)^m = (1 + APR_n/n)^n; for a BEY, m = 365/Days. Gap shrinks at low rates.

## Traps
- Calling a floater riskless: little rate risk, but credit (DM) risk and between-reset price moves remain.
- Thinking QM is today's required return; QM is fixed, DM is current.
- Reading macro rate or inflation moves as DM changes; they belong to MRR.
- Using DR as the investor's yield; it divides by FV and understates return.
- Comparing quoted rates across DR/AOR or 360/365 without converting to BEY.
- Treating money market rates as compounded; they are simple interest.
- Assuming BEY means a semiannual bond yield; it is a 365-day add-on, needing another conversion for semiannual comparison.
- Calculator sign errors: PV negative, FV and PMT positive in the rate solve. Also, for a sold CD use days remaining, not days held.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical.
1. One-year quarterly FRN (m = 4, N = 4), par 100, MRR 3.00%, QM 100 bp. At DM = 100 bp the coupon is 1.00 per quarter, discount rate 1.00% per quarter, price 100.00. If DM rises to 140 bp, per-quarter rate is 1.10% and price is 99.611 (the 0.10 per-quarter shortfall has PV about 0.39). If DM falls to 60 bp, price is 100.391.
2. 182-day bill, face 100, DR 2.00%, 360-day year. Price = 100 x (1 − 182/360 x 0.02) = 98.9889. Interest 1.0111. BEY = (365/182) x 1.0111/98.9889 = 2.048%, above the quote because the base is price and the year is 365.
3. CD of 1,000,000 at AOR 3.00%, 60 days, 365 basis. Redemption = 1,000,000 x (1 + 60/365 x 0.03) = 1,004,931.51. Sold with 30 days left when 30-day CDs yield 2.00%: price = 1,004,931.51 / (1 + 30/365 x 0.02) = about 1,003,282.
