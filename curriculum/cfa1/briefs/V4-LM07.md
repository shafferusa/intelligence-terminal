## Learning outcomes
1. Compare reporting of intangibles bought separately, built internally and obtained in a business combination (Acquisition of Intangible Assets, pp.210–214).
2. Explain and evaluate how impairment and derecognition of PP&E and intangibles affect statements and ratios (Impairment and Derecognition, pp.215–219).
3. Analyze and interpret disclosures about PP&E and intangibles (Presentation and Disclosure pp.220–226; Using Disclosures in Analysis pp.227–230).

## Core principles
- **Only some things become assets.** The balance sheet recognizes a narrow set (no reputation, no trained workforce). Once recognized, assets sit at cost (US GAAP) or cost or revaluation (IFRS) (p.209).
- **Build vs buy changes the statements, not the economics.** Bought intangibles are capitalized, so assets rise and the cash outflow lands in investing. Internally built ones are generally expensed, so profit and assets are lower now and the cash outflow lands in operating cash flow (pp.211–212). The earlier IFRS development criteria are judged met, the more is capitalized and the higher current profit.
- **R&D rules differ.** IFRS expenses research and capitalizes development once criteria (technical feasibility, intent, ability to use or sell) are shown. US GAAP expenses R&D except certain software: for-sale software after technological feasibility, internal-use software once completion is probable (pp.211–212).
- **Goodwill is a leftover.** In an acquisition the buyer books the target's identifiable assets and liabilities at fair value; whatever price exceeds that net amount is goodwill, which cannot be separated from the business (pp.213–214).
- **Impairment is a one-off write-down, not depreciation.** Depreciation spreads cost on a plan; impairment records an unplanned fall in recoverable value. It is non-cash: assets and net income fall, operating cash flow does not (p.215). Later depreciation falls on the reduced base.
- **IFRS and US GAAP measure impairment differently.** IFRS compares carrying amount with recoverable amount (higher of fair value less costs to sell and discounted value in use). US GAAP first tests recoverability on undiscounted cash flows of the asset group, and only on failure measures the loss against fair value (p.215). IFRS can book a loss where US GAAP does not; a US GAAP loss can be larger. Reversals: IFRS permits them, but not above the previous carrying amount; US GAAP bars them for held-for-use assets (p.218).
- **Disposals move cash to investing.** Gain or loss = proceeds less carrying amount; proceeds are an investing inflow and the gain is subtracted from operating cash flow under the indirect method. Abandonment is a non-cash loss; spin-offs record no gain or loss; assets slated for abandonment, exchange or spin-off stay held for use until disposed (pp.218–219).
- **Disclosures reveal reinvestment pressure.** Age, remaining life and capex versus depreciation estimate how worn the asset base is and whether capacity is being replenished. Mixed methods, land and fully depreciated assets make these rough (pp.227–228).

## Vocabulary
- Intangible asset — non-monetary asset with no physical form (p.210).
- Identifiable intangible — separable, or arising from legal or contractual rights; goodwill is not (p.210).
- Goodwill — price paid above fair value of net identifiable assets (pp.210, 213).
- Research vs development — seeking new knowledge vs applying it before commercial production (p.211).
- Impairment — write-down when carrying amount is not recoverable (p.215).
- Recoverable amount — higher of net sale proceeds after selling costs and value in use (p.215).
- Value in use — present value of cash flows the asset is expected to generate (p.215).
- Held for sale / held for distribution — disposal highly probable and immediate; depreciation stops (pp.217–218).
- Spin-off — distribution of a unit to existing shareholders, no gain or loss (p.219).
- Nature vs function of expense — income statement showing depreciation separately vs embedding it in cost of sales and SG&A (p.221).
- Cash-generating unit (CGU) — level at which goodwill is tested (p.224).
- Fixed asset turnover; asset age ratios (p.227).

## Formulas
1. **IFRS impairment loss** = carrying amount − recoverable amount; recoverable = max(fair value − costs to sell, value in use). Currency; only if carrying exceeds recoverable. (p.215).
2. **US GAAP:** not recoverable if carrying amount > undiscounted expected cash flows of the asset group; then loss = carrying amount − fair value (p.215).
3. **Held-for-sale write-down** = carrying amount − (fair value − costs to sell), if positive (pp.217–218).
4. **Gain/loss on sale** = proceeds − carrying amount (cost − accumulated depreciation, adjusted for impairment or revaluation) (p.218).
5. **Fixed asset turnover** = revenue / average net PP&E (times). Higher is usually read as more efficient (p.227).
6. **Age** = accumulated depreciation / depreciation expense; **remaining life** = net PP&E / depreciation expense; **total life** = historical cost / depreciation expense (= age + remaining). Assumes cost model, straight line, zero salvage (otherwise use cost − salvage), one asset pool; rough estimate (p.228).
7. **Capex / depreciation expense** — above 1 suggests capacity is being replenished or grown (p.228).

## Traps
- Impairment is not depreciation, and it does not cut operating cash flow (p.215).
- US GAAP tests on undiscounted flows but measures the loss to fair value; IFRS discounts (value in use) (p.215).
- IFRS reversals are capped; US GAAP forbids reversal for held-for-use assets but allows it for held-for-sale (p.218).
- Indefinite life means no amortization and a test at least annually; finite-life assets are tested on triggering events (p.217).
- Gain on sale is not operating cash flow; strip it out under the indirect method; proceeds go to investing (p.219).
- Age and remaining-life formulas are easy to swap: age uses accumulated depreciation, remaining life uses net PP&E. Check any stem that states them.
- Capitalized interest sits in the asset rather than expense: decide which interest figure a coverage ratio uses, and what happens to the asset base in turnover.

## Original practice seeds
All hypothetical, currency units (CU).
- **A. Impairment, year-end.** Machine carrying amount 100; undiscounted cash flows 105; value in use 90; fair value 85; costs to sell 5. IFRS: recoverable = max(80, 90) = 90, loss 10. US GAAP: 100 < 105, no loss. If undiscounted flows were 95, US GAAP fails the test and loss = 100 − 85 = 15, larger than IFRS's 10.
- **B. Age ratios.** Cost 500, accumulated depreciation 300, net PP&E 200, depreciation 50 a year: total life 10, age 6, remaining 4. With depreciation of 40 on the same balances: 12.5, 7.5, 5.0. The answer rests on the depreciation estimate.
- **C. Sale.** Cost 80, accumulated depreciation 50, sold for 38. Carrying 30, gain 8, investing inflow 38; the indirect operating section subtracts 8.
