# SIE question style — the house model for quizzes and practice exams

Logan supplied a 75-question third-party practice exam on 2026-09-25 as the model for what his
quizzes should feel like. It is summarised here as patterns (not copied: this repo is public and
the source is a commercial prep product). Every SIE Program quiz, drill and practice exam follows
these patterns, and every graded answer carries an explanation in the style described below.

## Stem patterns to use, in roughly this mix

1. **Direct definition / identification** — "Which of the following is most likely to earn a
   commission?" · "What is interpositioning?" · "Treasury bills are associated with which term?"
2. **Calculation from a quote or term sheet** — call protection from a call schedule (first call
   date); call premium from a call price; corporate price from eighths (86 5/8 → $866.25);
   Treasury price from 32nds at the ASK (106-12 – 106-16 → $1,065.00); split share counts and
   prices; combined option + stock max gain/loss (short stock at 52, long 55 call at 3 → max gain
   $4,900).
3. **Ordering** — yields for a premium or discount bond, asked "highest to lowest" in one question
   and "lowest to highest" in another; bonds ranked by interest-rate risk (80-day T-bill vs
   25-year AAA corporate).
4. **Roman-numeral combinations** — "I. par typically $100 · II. par typically $1,000 ·
   III. suits capital appreciation · IV. suits income" → pick the true pair.
5. **EXCEPT / NOT / false** — "All of the following apply to a real estate program EXCEPT"
   (depletion); "Which statement is false?" about U5 filing and license lapse.
6. **Named-customer scenarios** — a customer withdrawing from an IRA at 32 for a disability
   (income tax, no penalty); a rep offering to buy back shares below $90 (performance guarantee);
   a resigning rep (U5 within 30 days, 2-year lapse, 2-year FINRA jurisdiction).
7. **Which document / which party** — offering memorandum → Reg D; who sets a mutual fund's
   ex-date (the fund's board); who holds fund assets (custodian); who can buy an IPO (a
   grandparent can; a rep's sibling cannot).
8. **Best-fit recommendation** — one security for growth AND income across asset classes
   (balanced fund); who suits Class B shares; which note a municipality issues ahead of a bond
   sale (BAN).
9. **Time and date traps** — tax event for a dividend (payable date); cash-settlement ex-date
   (the day after record); option expiration expressed in a different time zone.

Keep stems short and plain, as the real exam does. Distractors are the neighbouring concept (RAN
vs BAN vs GAN vs TRAN), the reversed relationship, the adjacent number, or the right rule for the
wrong account.

## Explanation style (every graded question)

One short paragraph that (1) states the rule, (2) walks the arithmetic when there is any, step by
step, and (3) says why each tempting distractor is wrong. Where the sample used a memory aid
("the bond see-saw", "fraction → boot → scoot" for price quotes), a good mnemonic is welcome — but
always alongside the reason, never instead of it.

## Where the sample was wrong or loose — FINRA wins (teach these as traps)

The program was told to prefer FINRA/SEC rules over third-party conventions. The sample contains
several statements that are wrong or incomplete; never reproduce them:

- **"The Uniform Securities Act is enforced by NASAA."** No. NASAA writes model rules; each
  state's securities administrator enforces that state's version of the Act. (The question's
  answer — the SEC does not enforce it — is still right.)
- **"Immediate family for IPO purposes = parents, siblings, children."** Incomplete. FINRA Rule
  5130 also covers spouses, in-laws and anyone materially supported. Grandparents and cousins are
  indeed outside it.
- **"Stock splits of any form must be approved by shareholders."** Not a FINRA or SEC rule.
  Splits are governed by state corporate law and the charter; forward splits are often
  board-approved, reverse splits usually need a vote. Do not build a graded question on it.
- **"Interest rates and inflation are negatively correlated."** Loose. The Fed raises rates *in
  response to* inflation, so in the data they often rise together; the lever the exam wants is
  "higher rates are used to bring inflation down". Teach the causal chain, not a correlation sign.
- **Licence lapse "after 2 years"** is right, but the Maintaining Qualifications Program (up to 5
  years with annual CE) exists and should be mentioned.
- **Changing Regulation T** listed as a Fed monetary-policy tool is an exam convention (Reg T has
  been 50% since 1974). Accept it on the exam; explain that it is a credit control in practice.
- **Dividend taxable on the payable date** is right for ordinary corporate dividends; mutual fund
  and REIT dividends declared in Oct–Dec and paid in January are taxed as if received Dec 31.
