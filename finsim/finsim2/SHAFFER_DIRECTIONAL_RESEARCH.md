# Shaffer Alpha vs Shaffer Directional — research

Run 2026-09-25 17:03:52 · score version 2.1 · 815,950 point-in-time records · 1686.9 s · generated by `python -m finsim2 lab --directional`. Research only: the production Shaffer Score is unchanged.

The production Shaffer Score is a zero-centred evidence score (is this setup better or worse than normal?). Earlier reports judged its sign against the sign of the *absolute* future return. This research keeps two questions apart:

* **A. Shaffer Alpha** — the production score (and signal-level challengers fitted to the same target), judged as a ranking of relative opportunity: target = the vol-scaled return in excess of a point-in-time base expectation. It does not need to beat "always bullish"; the question is whether higher scores outperformed lower ones.
* **B. Shaffer Directional** — p_up = P(R_h > 0 | point-in-time information), DirectionalScore = 100·(2·p_up − 1), from a point-in-time base-return prior plus Shaffer evidence. Judged on absolute direction against the best naive baseline (always bullish, the asset's PIT positive-return frequency, previous direction, 12-1 momentum, 1-month mean reversion) and as a probability (Brier score against the climatology forecaster = the hierarchical PIT positive-return frequency).

Base priors (all point-in-time; outcomes enter only once matured): **zero** — always-zero drift; **class** — expanding asset-class drift; **hier** — class → sector → asset drift (shrunk); **beta_market** — β × PIT market drift; **carry** — observable carry (bond yield, FX interest parity); **hedge_prior** — Shaffer Hedge market prior (PIT); **product** — product-specific prior; **frequency** — hierarchical PIT positive-return frequency (the climatology forecaster itself). Shrinkage: K = 20 independent outcomes (crypto 200). The main prior for every challenger is **product**, fixed in advance. Eras: → 2008 test 2009–12, → 2012 test 2013–16, → 2016 test 2017–20, → 2020 test 2021–24, → 2024 test 2025–now, plus the 2018 split; weights and calibration frozen before each test period.

Gates, fixed before any result — Directional: **G1** walk-forward Brier better than climatology (paired date-clustered t ≥ 2) *and* accuracy above the best naive baseline; **G2** Brier better than climatology in ≥ 3 of 4 complete eras and the 2018 split's t ≥ 1. Alpha: **G1** paired Δ IC vs production (on the excess-return target) t ≥ 2 and Δ rank IC ≥ 0; **G2** Δ IC > 0 in ≥ 3 of 4 eras and the split's t ≥ 1. **G3** live shadow, then a manual promotion.

*Accuracy* below always means absolute direction: sign(p_up − ½) = sign(R_h) (p = ½ abstains). *Excess accuracy* = accuracy minus the best naive baseline's accuracy on the same records.

## Reading these results (written for this run, 2026-09-25)

**Bottom line.** Judged against the right targets, the Shaffer Score is a weak *ranking* signal at short horizons and
carries almost no information about *absolute direction* beyond a point-in-time base rate. Production stays unchanged;
nothing is eligible for promotion.

**A. Can Shaffer rank assets by relative opportunity? — Yes at 1D and 1W, not beyond.** Against the excess-return target,
production's weekly cross-sectional rank IC is 0.041 (t 7.7) at 1D and 0.038 (t 6.1) at 1W, with a top-minus-bottom
quintile spread of +0.10% per day (t 4.5) and +0.22% per week (t 4.2). At 1M it fades (0.019, t 1.5) and from 3M on it is
zero. No alpha challenger (signal weights fitted to the excess target, any hierarchy depth, or the interactions)
improves production with t ≥ 2; the global depth is best at almost every horizon and ticker-level weights are worst.

**B. Can Shaffer evidence plus a PIT base-return prior estimate the probability of a positive return? — Barely, and the
little skill there is comes from the prior, not from Shaffer.**

1. The production score *read literally* as a direction is right 50–51% of the time because it is centred on zero while
   52.8% (1D) to 70.9% (12M) of outcomes are positive; its bearish calls are right only 48% (1D) down to 28% (12M).
   Its Brier score is worse than the climatology forecaster at every horizon (−0.7% at 1D to −18% at 12M).
2. Adding a point-in-time base prior fixes the bias: the best directional models are right 52.9 / 55.1 / 59.1 / 63.1 /
   65.9 / 71.8% — essentially the naive baseline (52.8 / 54.8 / 58.7 / 62.7 / 66.0 / 72.1%). Excess accuracy is +0.2 to
   +0.4 points at 1D–3M and slightly negative at 6M–12M. Balanced accuracy is 50.8–51.7%: the models almost always
   say "up".
3. The **product-specific prior** is the best prior at every horizon, and it beats an always-zero drift on the same
   records at 1D, 1W and 1M (paired Brier t 2.2, 4.8, 2.7) — not at 3M+. The hierarchical positive-frequency prior is
   close behind at 1D–1M and fails at 12M.
4. **Shaffer evidence adds nothing beyond the prior** except at 1D, and there only marginally: alpha + prior (global)
   vs prior alone, paired Brier gain t 2.6 at 1D, 1.2 at 1W, 0.2 at 1M, ≈ 0 or negative at 3M–12M. Signal-level weights
   fitted to the directional target never add to the prior, and they over-fit as the hierarchy deepens (Brier t −4.6
   at 1D asset level).
5. **Bearish precision is structural, not Shaffer.** The high P(R < 0 | score < −20) at 1M–6M (70–82% against a 34–42%
   unconditional rate) comes almost entirely from inverse and volatility products (SQQQ, SDS, SH, PSQ, VXX) whose base
   prior is negative by construction. The few bearish calls on ordinary equities (e.g. BAC, JPM at 6M) were wrong.
6. **Score strength and realized p_up.** On the bullish side, stronger directional scores do correspond to higher
   realized p_up (e.g. 1W: 53% → 59% → 66% → 94% → 97% across the bands from −10…10 to +75…100; 3M: 61% → 70% → 72% →
   92%), and the strongest bands are small (a few hundred records, few independent weeks). Given point 4 — Shaffer adds
   almost nothing to the prior — that strength comes mainly from the base prior. Monotonicity holds at 1D, 1W and 3M,
   not at 1M, 6M or 12M.

**Gates and what they mean.** By the gates fixed in advance, four 1D directional challengers pass G1 and G2 (alpha +
prior at global / class / sector, signal + prior at global) and enter the live shadow. But at 1D even a *calibrated
constant* beats the climatology forecaster (Brier t 4.0) — asset-level positive frequencies are noisy when the base rate
is close to one half — so passing that gate is not by itself evidence of Shaffer skill. The paired test says only
alpha + prior (global) adds to the prior at 1D, and only just. I did not change the gates after seeing the results; the
statuses above are what the pre-registered rules produce. **Recommendation for the next version (a new, pre-registered
gate, not applied retroactively):** a directional challenger must beat the prior-only model on the same records (paired
t ≥ 2) and the best naive baseline, not only climatology; the live-shadow G3 should use the same comparison.

**The signals flagged by the weight research, re-examined.** With the corrected targets none of them is stable across
eras: idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol_252`) still takes a negative weight for both targets at every horizon, but it
is not kept in every era fit (it drops to zero in some); trend (`ma_cross`, `macd`) and raw 3M / 6M / 12M momentum move
between eras. The weights that are kept in every era are short-term reversal (`ret_1d`) at 1D, 1W and 6M, and
relative value (`rel_value`) at 1D–1W, for both targets — consistent with where the ranking signal actually lives.

**Should Alpha and Directional stay separate?** Yes. The hypothesis holds on the evidence: the alpha score is the
better ranker (1D–1W rank IC 0.041 / 0.038 vs 0.040 / 0.023 for the directional p_up), and the directional model is the
better absolute forecaster (accuracy 52.9–71.8% vs 50.2–51.1% for production read literally, and better Brier scores).
But the directional model's edge over a naive base rate is small and mostly comes from the prior — so a future
"63% UP" display should present the base rate honestly and show Shaffer's contribution separately.
