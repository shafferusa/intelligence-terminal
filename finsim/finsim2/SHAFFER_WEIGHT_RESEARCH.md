# Shaffer Score — signal-level weight research

Run 2026-09-25 16:10:37 · score version 2.1 · 819,392 point-in-time records over 155 assets · 763.4 s · generated by `python -m finsim2 lab --weights`.

Question: which weights *inside the Shaffer equation* would have made the point-in-time score more accurate at each horizon, and do they hold on periods they never saw? Every challenger is the Shaffer equation `raw = 100·tanh(Σ_f g_f Σ_i ω_i δ_i x_i c_i r_i d_i)` with some parameters learned per horizon — signal weights ω, family weights W·A·H (learned jointly as one β per signal), the confidence / regime / decay strengths, five regime interactions — each hierarchy node's ridge shrunk toward its parent (global → class → sector → industry → asset), weights bounded (|β| ≤ 0.2 on standardised inputs), shrinkage λ = 200 effective observations. The research records reproduce production's raw score from the stored pieces (test `SignalRecords`).

Validation: weights frozen before each test era, trained only on outcomes that had matured before it: → 2008 test 2009–12, → 2012 test 2013–16, → 2016 test 2017–20, → 2020 test 2021–24, → 2024 test 2025–now; plus the earlier split (→ 2017, test 2018–). Gates fixed before any result: **G1** pooled walk-forward paired Δ IC date-clustered t ≥ 2 and Δ accuracy ≥ 0; **G2** Δ IC > 0 and Δ accuracy ≥ 0 in ≥ 3 of the 4 complete eras, the 2018 split's Δ IC t ≥ 1, and walk-forward accuracy above the best naive baseline; **G3** ≥ 60 graded live-shadow forecasts, not worse than production on the same ones. Promotion is manual.

Accuracy = sign(score) = sign(return) (a score of exactly 0 abstains), averaged per week then across weeks, 95% interval with n_eff = weeks × 5 / max(5, h). IC = per-asset standardised score × outcome, pooled per week (date-clustered t). Rank IC = cross-sectional Spearman per week. Naive baselines on the same records: always bullish, the asset's PIT positive-return frequency, previous-period direction, 12-1 momentum, 1-month mean reversion.

## Reading these results (written for this run, 2026-09-25)

**Bottom line: keep production. No re-weighting of the Shaffer equation passed the historical gates at any horizon, so
nothing enters the live shadow and nothing is eligible for promotion.** The research did cover the signal-level
parameter space the family-level study could not: 74 signal weights (family weights folded in), the confidence / regime /
decay strengths, five regime interactions, free signed weights, and five hierarchy depths — 9 challengers × 6 horizons,
each validated on four unseen eras, 2025–now and the 2018 split.

What the evidence says:

1. **Directional accuracy is ~50–51% at every horizon, far below the naive baselines at long horizons** (always bullish is
   right 66% at 6M and 72% at 12M). The Shaffer Score is a relative-evidence score centred on zero with no drift term, so
   on direction alone it cannot beat "always bullish" where markets mostly rise. Re-weighting cannot fix this; only an
   explicit drift / base-rate term could, and that is a design decision, not a weight.
2. **The score is asymmetric.** Bullish scores work, and more strongly with size: at 1D the direction is right 55% for
   +10…25, 57% for +25…50, 64% for +50…75 and 71% for +75 and above; at 1M 59% → 64% → 82% → 88% (base rate 58%). Bearish
   scores are right *less* than half the time at 1M–6M (e.g. 45% for −50…−10 at 1M) — prices kept rising. Most records
   (83–92% depending on the horizon) sit in −10…+10, a coin flip, which drags the average to 50%. So **|Shaffer Score| does carry information on the
   bullish side**; the bearish side needs work (the challenger's lower thresholds did not fix it).
3. **Production ranks assets.** Its weekly cross-sectional rank IC is small but clearly significant at 1D (0.038, t 7.2),
   1W (0.034, t 5.5) and 1M (0.028, t 2.3), not at 3M+. The best challengers raise rank IC at 3M (t 1.9) and 6M (0.082,
   t 2.3) — encouraging, but not enough to pass G1, which is judged on the per-asset time-series IC.
4. **Re-weighting gains shrink by half or more out of sample.** Best challengers gain +0.4 (1D) to +7.0 (12M) accuracy
   points on unseen eras against +0.9 to +11.5 in training; the IC gains are near zero and none reaches t ≥ 2. Free signed
   weights (most parameters) show the biggest in-sample gains (+22.6 points at 12M) and the worst out-of-sample behaviour
   (−2.3 points) — the textbook over-fitting pattern the gates exist to catch.
5. **Specialisation does not help direction.** Class-level weights are the best depth for accuracy at every horizon;
   sector, industry and asset levels are equal or worse, even with shrinkage to the parent.
6. **One weight change is stable everywhere:** idiosyncratic volatility (`idio_vol_252`) should lean *against* the asset —
   a consistently negative weight in every era fit at every horizon (the low-volatility anomaly); production gives it
   almost none. Trend signals (`ma_cross`, `macd`, the 52-week percentile) gain weight at 1M–12M; raw 3M / 6M / 12M
   return momentum, Sharpe / Sortino and risk-adjusted momentum lose it; at 1D–1W short-term reversal (`ret_1d`) is kept.
   These are hypotheses for the next formula version, not changes: none produced a validated improvement on its own.

Method note (disclosed): during this run the pooled walk-forward IC was briefly standardised per asset *within* each era;
with 4-year eras and overlapping 3M–12M outcomes that biased long-horizon ICs strongly negative (production's 12M IC read
−0.11 against +0.002 over its full history). The reported pooled IC standardises each asset over its whole walk-forward
period, as the ML Lab did before; era-level ICs remain within-era and carry some of that bias at 3M–12M (it affects
production and challenger alike, and the gates use paired differences). The pooled 3M–12M ICs below are still slightly
negative for production over 2009–2026. Rank IC uses the volatility-scaled outcome so assets are comparable.

What would move this forward: (a) a drift / base-rate term, or scoring direction against the asset's point-in-time drift
instead of against zero; (b) research on why bearish scores fail (the regime and valuation families); (c) a formula
version that adds the idiosyncratic-volatility lean and more trend weight, built as a hypothesis and put through the same
eras and the live shadow.
