1 · Refresher: market structure and pricing power

Five features sort every market: the number and size of sellers, how different the products are, power over price, barriers to entry, and competition on things other than price (pp.38–39). Easy entry with identical products leaves no pricing power; barriers with a unique product allow lasting profit.

Every firm stops at MR = MC; only a perfect competitor also has P = MR. Each extra unit it sells adds exactly the market price to revenue. Profit draws in new firms until the price falls to the bottom of average cost: P = MC = minimum average cost, and zero economic profit (pp.50–51). That still pays the owners a normal return, because economic cost includes what their time and money could earn elsewhere (p.46).

A monopolist picks a point on the demand curve. To sell one more unit it must cut the price on every unit, so marginal revenue falls below price. It sets MR = MC, then charges what buyers will pay for that quantity. Marginal cost is positive, so marginal revenue is too, which puts the price where demand is elastic (p.56). Charging different buyers different prices raises revenue further, if it can tell them apart and stop resale (pp.58–59).

Memorize
  • Profit peaks at MR = MC in every market; P = MR only under perfect competition
  • Perfect competition, long run: P = MC = minimum AC, zero economic profit
  • Monopoly: P = MC ÷ (1 − 1/ε), with ε > 1 (p.57)
  • Straight-line demand: MR has the same intercept and twice the slope (p.54)
