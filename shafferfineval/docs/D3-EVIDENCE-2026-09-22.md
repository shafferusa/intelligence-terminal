### 1(f) Economic consequence (FACT where cited; the rest is reasoning, labelled)

Facts the consequence rests on:
- The transform is `100·tanh(x/g)` with g = cohort IQR (`pit_normalization.py:689, 544`); output clamped to ±100; no input winsorisation (`winsorize_cohort` false in the frozen body, `pit_frozen_spec.py:477`).
- The engine's other rate factor refuses non-positive bases: `pit_replay.py:798-799` `if base <= 0.0: out.why["real_revenue_growth"] = R_NON_POSITIVE_BASE` (**d3-18**); the EPS policy floors the base at `EPS_GROWTH_MIN_POSITIVE_BASE_V1 = 0.01` (`pit_price_basis.py:237`) with the rationale "$0.005 -> $0.50 prints +9,900%" (l.244-245). Neither uses `abs()`.
- The project already flags EBITDA/Assets as an intensity measure: `pit_factor_blocks.py:225-228` "an EBITDA-based restatement (EBITDA per dollar of assets) ... is close to what `ebitda_efficiency` already does per dollar of SALES -- raising the question of whether both are wanted".
- Weight: `ebitda_growth` is 0.25 of E (`pit_factor_blocks.py:435`), 0.0875 of the company score; v1 weight was 0.20 × 0.25 = 0.05.

Reasoning (not measured anywhere in the repo):
- **Under A** the sign is economically right for negative bases (a shrinking loss scores positive, as both `pit_derive.py:484` and `company_scoring.py:595-597` argue), but the magnitude is unbounded as |E_{t-1}| → 0; with tanh saturating near 3g, near-zero-base companies pin at ±100 regardless of dollar size, a zero base is undefined (v1 returns None), and if a quarter or more of a cohort sits in the blow-up region the cohort IQR g inflates and compresses everyone else. A is scale-free across company size and asset intensity.
- **Under B** every sign case is a bounded dollar-per-asset change and no company saturates from a near-zero base, but the quantity is ΔEBITDA/Assets, not growth: the same 10% EBITDA growth reads 15× larger at EBITDA/Assets = 0.30 than at 0.02, so asset-light/high-margin firms are structurally favoured and the factor correlates with the EBITDA/Assets level that sits next to `ebitda_efficiency` and `ebitda_benchmark` in the same block (redundancy risk). It adds an Assets-at-lag-1 dependency the frozen eligibility rule does not charge for (d3-04).
- **Comparability with v1**: v1 ranks the rate within industry; v2 tanh-scales either quantity within a SIC cohort. Rate → amount is a change of quantity (multiplication by |E_{t-1}|/Assets), not a monotone transform, so B reorders companies relative to v1 whenever asset intensity differs within a cohort; A preserves v1's raw semantics but not its score.

### 2(f) Economic consequence (FACT where cited; the rest is reasoning, labelled)

Facts: EBITDA = operating_income + D&A (`pit_derive.py:469`), so `EBITDA/interest − OI/interest = D&A/interest` identically. PERCENTILE_RANK is invariant to cohort-wide constants and monotone transforms (`pit_normalization.py:592-593`), refuses below `MIN_RANK_PEERS = 4` (l.318), and has no domain-validity rule of its own. v1's leverage ratio refuses `EBITDA <= 0` because a non-positive denominator "inverts the meaning of the ratio" (`company_scoring.py:746-753`). `net_debt_ebitda` and `fcf_conversion` already consume EBITDA (`pit_derive.py:546-547`; `pit_coverage.py:577`).

Reasoning (not measured): the two numerators differ by D&A/interest, which is largest in capital-intensive, debt-financed sectors; a uniform shift is invisible to a rank, but D&A share varies within a SIC cohort, so the choice moves ranks. The EBITDA form labels companies with OI < 0 < EBITDA as positively covered; the OI form does not. The EBITDA form inherits the scarcer D&A leaf and would need a matched-period rule the current eligibility rule lacks; it also makes three of Q's four factors share one EBITDA input, which the block rationale (l.476-478) guards against. Whichever numerator wins, `interest_expense == 0` is a division by zero and a negative resolved value inverts the ratio, and the promised refusal (d3-19) is unimplemented; how zero-interest firms are treated changes every peer's percentile.
