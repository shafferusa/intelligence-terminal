Top Stories

Central bank signals slower balance-sheet runoff

A policymaker said the committee is “close to the point” where runoff should slow — the clearest signal yet, and the first to name a condition.

In prepared remarks published on the central bank’s site at 2:15 AM ET, the policymaker said the committee is “close to the point” at which the pace of balance-sheet runoff should slow. Runoff has proceeded at the current cap for fourteen months, and reserves have declined toward the range staff have described as ample. (Sample text.)

The remark is the individual’s, not the committee’s: no vote has occurred, and the next scheduled decision is on the calendar below. Two wire services carried the speech within the hour; the text itself is the primary source.

Why it matters The pace of runoff drives bank reserves and, through them, money-market conditions and how easily the market absorbs Treasury supply. The front end is the most sensitive part of the curve to it.
What’s next Minutes in two weeks, which will show whether the view is shared. Watch SHY, IEF, TLT, SGOV and the regional banks for the transmission — exposure, not direction.

Central bank speech text (primary) · two wire reports · desk commentary, labelled as analysis.
