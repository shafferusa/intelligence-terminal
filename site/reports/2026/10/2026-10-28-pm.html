Top Stories

The Fed holds at 3.75–4.00%, but two officials vote to hike and Warsh refuses to rule out December

The statement upgraded growth to “strongly” and said more information will guide the next move; markets moved odds of a December hike up to about 85%.

The Federal Open Market Committee left the federal funds target range at 3.75–4.00% on Wednesday, as almost every forecast expected. The vote was 10–2. Governor Lisa D. Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth M. Hammack dissented, both preferring to raise the range by a quarter point, according to the statement published on the Fed’s website. Fortune reported that it is the first time since October 1992 that a governor and a regional bank president have dissented in the same direction at one meeting. It was Cook’s first dissent; it comes ahead of a White House hearing on whether she can be removed over mortgage-fraud allegations she disputes, Axios reported.

The statement described economic activity as expanding “strongly,” up from “solid” in September, called inflation “still elevated,” and added that the “accumulation of more information” will help the committee decide its stance, according to CNN, CNBC and Bloomberg. The range has been unchanged since September’s quarter-point hike.

At his press conference, Chair Kevin Warsh said “the overwhelming consensus is that what we’ve done in this monetary medicine is starting to work some, but there is no firm conclusions,” and that he does not “believe in forward guidance, absent exigent circumstances,” as quoted by Fortune, CNN and CNBC. He said politics played no role in the decision ahead of next Tuesday’s midterms. Readings of the press conference differ: CNN said he subtly hinted at a December hike, while Bloomberg said he gave no signal. He also announced that one of the eight yearly meetings will become a strategy session with no rate decision; outlets differ on whether that starts in 2027 or 2028.

Markets leaned hawkish anyway. CME FedWatch odds of a December hike were reported at 85–86% during the afternoon, up from about 78% on Tuesday (CoinDesk, Yahoo Finance); no closing figure was available. Futures price the funds rate near 4.7% by September 2027, about three more quarter-point hikes, AP reported. The 2-year Treasury yield touched about 4.77% before the decision and fell back to 4.67% at the close, while the 10-year rose to 5.05%.

Why it matters. Two hike dissents show the hawkish wing is larger than the vote alone suggests. The steeper curve says investors expect the Fed to keep short rates high but are demanding more to hold long bonds, which raises borrowing costs for mortgages and companies whatever the Fed does in December.
What’s next. Thursday’s September PCE report, forecast at about 3.6% headline, is the first inflation reading after the decision. The next meeting is Dec. 8–9. Our call: a December hike at 70–85%, in line with futures pricing, discounted slightly because Warsh would not commit.

Federal Reserve FOMC statement, Oct 28; CNN; CNBC; Bloomberg; Fortune; Axios; AP via Yahoo Finance; CoinDesk; US Treasury par-yield curve.

Alphabet falls about 9% after hours as an $8.7 billion investment loss and $51 billion of capital spending overshadow Cloud’s doubling

Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7 billion, but Search and YouTube missed estimates and free cash flow was negative for a second quarter.

Alphabet’s third-quarter revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion, slightly below the roughly $128 billion analysts expected, according to its 8-K filed at 4:01 PM. Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to $30.7 billion and its operating income tripled to $11.1 billion; the company cited sales of its TPU chip systems as a meaningful contributor. Search revenue rose 12% to $63.4 billion and YouTube ads 9% to $11.2 billion, both below estimates of about $64.9 billion and $11.4 billion cited by investingLive. Chief executive Sundar Pichai said search query growth was the fastest in five years and the Gemini app had reached one billion users.

Earnings per share were $2.12, down from $2.87 a year earlier and well short of consensus near $3.00. The filing shows why: other income swung to a loss of $8.2 billion from a gain of $12.8 billion, mostly an $8.7 billion loss on equity securities. Interest expense rose to $967 million from $143 million. Operating income rose 30% to $40.6 billion, below the roughly $43.2 billion expected.

Cash is the bigger story. Purchases of property and equipment were $51.4 billion in the quarter, against $24.0 billion a year earlier, and free cash flow was −$9.1 billion after −$5.9 billion in the second quarter. To fund that, Alphabet sold $12.4 billion of new shares through an at-the-market program and $28.7 billion of senior notes during the quarter, and now pays dividends on mandatory convertible preferred stock. Shares were about $294.50 at 4:40 PM, about 9% below the regular-session close of $324.89 (Yahoo Finance).

Why it matters. Alphabet is now funding the AI build-out partly by issuing stock and debt rather than from cash flow. Investors punished Meta last week for the same pattern, and the reaction suggests strong cloud growth alone no longer protects a stock when spending outruns cash.

Alphabet 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (SEC EDGAR); investingLive; Reuters via Global Banking & Finance; Benzinga; Yahoo Finance (after-hours price).

Microsoft rises about 4% after hours as Azure grows 48% and revenue beats by $1.7 billion

Microsoft Cloud revenue rose 30% to $63.9 billion; quarterly capital spending of $39.4 billion came in below what analysts had pencilled in.

Microsoft’s fiscal first-quarter revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion, against consensus of about $90.6 billion and the company’s own guidance of $89.85–90.95 billion, according to its 8-K. Operating income rose 21% to $45.9 billion. GAAP earnings were $4.92 a share; excluding its OpenAI investment, $4.89, against about $4.72 expected (Benzinga). Azure revenue grew 48%, against guidance of about 44–45%, under a new, narrower Azure definition introduced this quarter. Commercial remaining performance obligations rose 73% to $677 billion, about flat from the prior quarter. The company now reports two segments, “Agents and Infra” ($76.8 billion, +25%) and “Devices and Consumer” ($15.5 billion, −3%), and filed a second 8-K with recast financial statements.

Additions to property and equipment were $39.4 billion, double a year earlier. One report put analysts’ estimate for spending including finance leases at about $46–51 billion; the difference is probably one of definition, and the call at 5:30 PM is where Microsoft gives the like-for-like figure and its December-quarter guidance. Shares were about $553.90 at 4:40 PM, up 4.3% from a flat regular-session close of $531.01 and near their 52-week high of $551.15 (Yahoo Finance). On Monday Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees.

Why it matters. Side by side, the two reports set the terms for the AI trade: investors rewarded the company whose cloud revenue is growing faster than its spending and punished the one whose spending is outrunning its cash.

Microsoft 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (SEC EDGAR); Benzinga; Seeking Alpha; CNBC; Yahoo Finance (after-hours price).

The Treasury curve steepens: the 10-year closes at 5.05% and the 30-year at 5.37%

Short yields fell after the Fed while long yields rose, widening the 2s10s spread by 9 basis points in a day.

On the Treasury’s end-of-day par curve, the 2-year yield fell 3 basis points to 4.67%, the 5-year rose 3 to 4.85%, the 10-year 6 to 5.05% and the 30-year 7 to 5.37%. The 20-year, at 5.41%, still yields more than the 30-year. Bloomberg reported that the 2-year fell as much as 4 basis points after the press conference as traders read the meeting-schedule change as slightly dovish; DoubleLine’s Jeffrey Gundlach said on CNBC that the Fed should be hiking. The iShares 20+ year Treasury fund fell 0.8% and investment-grade corporate bonds 0.2%.

Supply comes next: $44 billion of seven-year notes sells Thursday, and the quarterly refunding announcement is reported for Nov. 4. Results of Wednesday’s two-year floating-rate note and 17-week bill auctions were not yet published when we checked.

Why it matters. A bear steepener led by long yields usually points to worries about inflation, deficits or bond supply rather than the Fed’s next move, and it is the long end that sets mortgage rates and the discount rate on equity valuations.

US Treasury daily par-yield curve; Bloomberg; CNBC; Treasury fiscal data (auction schedule); Yahoo Finance.

The Bank of Canada raises rates for the first time since 2023, to 2.50%

Governor Tiff Macklem said the hike aims to stop high energy prices spreading into broader inflation, and more may follow.

The Bank of Canada raised its policy rate a quarter point to 2.50% even though Canada lost 68,000 jobs in September. Macklem said further increases may be needed but the bank would proceed carefully, and the bank raised its 2027 inflation forecast to 3.1% from 2.0% in July, according to The Globe and Mail and The Canadian Press. Markets priced about a 43% chance of another hike on Dec. 9 (LSEG via CP). Economists split: TD expects one more hike early next year, while David Rosenberg called the move “totally unnecessary and premature.” We could not reach the bank’s own statement.

The Globe and Mail; The Canadian Press via CP24 and BNN Bloomberg; Yahoo Finance Canada.

Oil rises as US crude stocks fall and reports point to renewed strikes on Iran after the midterms

Brent settled about $115 and WTI about $103; Saudi Arabia still has not confirmed damage at Ras Tanura.

Brent settled at $115.32 a barrel, up 1.3%, and WTI at $102.78, up 2.9%, according to CNBC, which linked the rise to reports that the United States told Gulf states to prepare for renewed strikes on Iran after the Nov. 3 midterms. Another price service showed lower figures, probably for a different contract, so these settlements rest on one source. The Energy Information Administration reported US crude stocks fell 1.9 million barrels in the week to Oct. 23, against analysts’ forecasts of no change; Gulf Coast stocks are the lowest since December 2024. Gasoline stocks rose 3.9 million barrels and distillates fell 1.5 million (Reuters via Kitco; Newsquawk).

The Houthis claimed a ballistic-missile strike on Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery on Tuesday. Saudi Arabia said it intercepted missiles and drones and has not confirmed damage; a source told Bloomberg a fire at the terminal was contained. Aramco has not commented.

Why it matters. Oil above $100 is the main reason inflation is running near 3.7% and the Fed and the Bank of Canada are hiking. Any confirmed damage to Saudi export infrastructure would push that pressure higher.

CNBC; Reuters via Kitco and BOE Report; Newsquawk; Bloomberg; The Maritime Executive.

Israel’s count leaves Netanyahu’s bloc at about 53 seats, short of a majority, with a record Arab vote

Neither bloc can reach 61 without Arab parties, and two key opposition leaders have ruled them out.

With most votes counted, Haaretz and Ynet put Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc at 53 seats, down from about 54 to 56 earlier in the count, and the bloc around former army chief Gadi Eisenkot at about 48. Arab-led parties are projected at 16 to 19 seats, a historic high (Haaretz, Ynet, AP via PBS). Official results are due by Nov. 4. Avigdor Lieberman and Naftali Bennett have ruled out governing with Arab parties, without which the opposition cannot reach 61; Ra’am leader Mansour Abbas signalled he would join an Eisenkot coalition, and Eisenkot set conditions for Arab partners. The Times of Israel notes that a deadlock could lead to fresh elections or a unity government, with Netanyahu staying on as caretaker meanwhile.

Why it matters. A long coalition process would leave a caretaker government in Israel while Washington weighs renewed strikes on Iran, the risk now priced into oil.

Haaretz; Ynet; AP via PBS; The Times of Israel; Israel Hayom; NPR.

A judge signals he will not let the Justice Department set aside 20,000 California mail ballots before the election

Judge David Carter said he would rule Wednesday and plans a special master to examine how the government built its list; no written order had appeared at press time.

At a three-hour hearing in Los Angeles on Tuesday, US District Judge David O. Carter called the department’s request for a temporary restraining order a “monumental change” to an election under way and said “some of these may be very valid voters,” according to ABC News, NBC and the AP. He told the government to “go out and indict” if it believes crimes were committed, and ordered that threats to state and county election workers stop. He said he would appoint a special master to review the list and California’s claim that the ballots cannot be separated, with the review finishing before the state’s Dec. 11 certification deadline. Politico’s reporter at the hearing said Carter signalled he would reject the emergency request; his order had not been published when we checked. On Monday a Ninth Circuit panel dismissed the department’s appeal 2–1 because Carter had not yet ruled.

ABC News; NBC Bay Area; AP; Politico; Bloomberg; CBS News; Democracy Docket.

What Changed Today

The Fed’s December decision. This morning: a hold was about 91% priced, a December hike about 75%, and one dissent was possible. Now: two officials voted to hike, the statement upgraded growth, and December odds are about 85%. Why it matters: the bar for not hiking in December has risen. The AI spending test. This morning: Microsoft and Alphabet were the next test of whether AI revenue keeps pace with spending. Long rates. This morning: the 10-year was just under 5%. Now: 5.05% with a steeper curve. Why it matters: higher long rates weigh on housing and on richly valued stocks. Confidence: high (Treasury data).

Canada. This morning: previews conflicted. Now: a hike to 2.50%, as most economists and our Tuesday forecast expected. Confidence: high on the decision.

The Economy

Today’s data. The advance September goods-trade deficit widened to $134.9 billion from $132.1 billion, against a forecast of about $130 billion, as imports hit a record; capital-goods imports rose 7.5% on the month, which economists tie to AI equipment (Newsquawk; Reuters via Yahoo Finance). These are nominal dollar figures. Some economists estimate trade could subtract several points from third-quarter GDP growth, though that view is not universal. Advance wholesale inventories rose 0.6% (forecast 0.5%) and retail inventories excluding autos 0.4%. Mortgage-application figures were not available.

The Fed and the Treasury. The effective funds rate is 3.88% and SOFR 3.95% (FRED, Tuesday). The end-of-day par curve: 3-month bill 4.18% (−4 bp), 2-year 4.67% (−3), 5-year 4.85% (+3), 10-year 5.05% (+6), 30-year 5.37% (+7). 2s10s is 38 basis points, 3-month to 10-year 87 and 5s30s 52. Real 10-year yields were 2.64% and 10-year breakevens 2.35% on Tuesday (FRED), so the rise in nominal yields has mostly been in real rates, not inflation expectations. Thursday’s $44 billion seven-year note follows a September sale at 5.085% with 2.42 bids per dollar. We found no remarks from Secretary Bessent today.

Credit. The high-yield spread narrowed to 3.18 percentage points on Tuesday from 3.30 on Monday and 3.40 last Wednesday (ICE BofA via FRED), its tightest in about two weeks. The high-yield bond fund (HYG) was flat today and investment-grade (LQD) fell 0.2% with Treasuries.

Central banks abroad. Besides Canada’s hike, the ECB decides Thursday at 8:15 AM ET and is expected to hold its deposit rate at 2.50%, with euro-area inflation at 3.8% and most economists in one survey expecting a December hike. The Bank of Japan decides Friday and is expected to hold at 1.25% and publish its quarterly outlook; Japan’s finance minister said on Tuesday Tokyo is ready to act on the yen, which is near 158 per dollar.

The World

Yemen and Saudi Arabia. A Houthi missile reportedly landed inside the perimeter of Aden airport after the group claimed a Saudi cargo plane was carrying weapons; a police source said the plane was not hit (AP, AFP). Russia and Ukraine. A local humanitarian ceasefire in Russian-occupied Oleshky, in Kherson region, brokered by Melania Trump, appeared to hold, allowing evacuations and aid (NBC, Al Jazeera). Russia said it struck transport infrastructure in Kyiv and a logistics hub in Kharkiv region. Kyiv and Moscow have agreed to US-mediated technical talks in Abu Dhabi in early November.

Europe. British Prime Minister Andy Burnham urged closer ties with the European Union and floated eventually rejoining after the next election, the AP reported, reopening a debate both main parties had avoided.

AI & Technology

Chipmakers buy into Meta. Nvidia is investing $3 billion in Meta and expanding a partnership on its agentic AI platform, and AMD said on Tuesday it will invest $1 billion, with its Helios server racks entering testing at Meta (Yahoo Finance; Digitimes). The investments are part of Meta’s roughly $35 billion stock offering announced Monday, with a further at-the-market program planned for 2027. Critics call the arrangement circular: suppliers fund a customer that buys their chips. Meta fell 1.2% today. With Alphabet’s share sales disclosed tonight, two of the largest AI spenders are now issuing equity to pay for the build-out.

Apple’s touchscreen Mac. Apple unveiled its first touchscreen MacBook Pro, with a tandem OLED display and M5 Pro and M5 Max chips, from $2,899; it ships Nov. 6 (TechCrunch, Macworld). Apple shares fell 0.9%.

A contested proof. OpenAI and Anthropic each said this week that an internal AI model found a refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a central conjecture in computer science, with machine-checked proofs. Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi and Ryan Williams will lead an independent review. Until then it is a claim; even if it holds, it would not settle whether P equals NP or give practical speed-ups (R&D World; Technology.org).

Also. Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, a free, open-weight speech-to-text model for up to 40 languages (TechCrunch). Game studio Warhorse confirmed a cyberattack in which company data was accessed.

Science

Rubin finds its first dwarf-planet candidate. The Minor Planet Center announced 2026 ED138, a body beyond Pluto estimated at 600 to 1,500 kilometres across, the first dwarf-planet candidate from the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. Matthew Holman of the Harvard & Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics found it, and Mario Jurić of the University of Washington traced 20 earlier detections back to March. It is a candidate: its size, and so its status, depends on how reflective it is, which is not yet measured (Rubin Observatory; CNN; GeekWire).

Earth’s water. A University of New Mexico team reports in Nature Geoscience that the early Earth may have made much of its own water through reactions between hydrogen and molten silicate rock at 1,200–1,400°C, based on laboratory experiments. It is one result supporting a long-debated alternative to water delivered by asteroids, not a settled answer.

Climate. The UN Environment Programme’s 2026 Emissions Gap Report says current policies point to about 2.8°C of warming this century and that holding warming to 1.5°C is no longer possible; global emissions were about flat in 2025 at 58.4 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent (NBC; The Globe and Mail).

Space

SpaceX. Falcon 9 launched 27 Starlink satellites from Vandenberg early Tuesday; booster B1082 flew for a 26th time and landed on its droneship. SpaceX shares fell 2.3% to $145.23 after a lawsuit challenging tax incentives for its planned $100 billion Louisiana spaceport (Investing.com); the stock is 36% below its 52-week high.

Artemis. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said last week the agency remains on track to land astronauts on the Moon in 2028. Artemis III is now an Earth-orbit docking test of Starship and Blue Moon prototypes, no earlier than summer 2027, with landings on Artemis IV and V; Kennedy Space Center crews are stacking booster segments for a tanking test (Bloomberg; NASASpaceflight).

Rocket Lab. The debut of its Neutron rocket has slipped into 2027 after voids were found in a first-stage part on Oct. 14. United Launch Alliance’s next Vulcan flight, carrying Amazon Leo satellites, is listed for November.

Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania voters have returned 934,000 mail ballots, 71% of those requested

More than 1.3 million absentee and mail ballots were requested by Tuesday’s deadline, and 933,707 had been returned by 8 AM Wednesday, more than 72% of them from registered Democrats, according to the Department of State as reported by abc27. Completed ballots must reach county election offices by 8 PM on Nov. 3; postmarks do not count. Spotlight PA reported that Democratic mail-ballot requests were down 14% from 2022 and Republican requests up 11%, as of Oct. 19. Gov. Shapiro, who leads Republican Stacy Garrity by more than 20 points in recent polls, said in Pittsburgh this week that the state is “incredibly prepared” for any federal challenge to the count (WESA).
