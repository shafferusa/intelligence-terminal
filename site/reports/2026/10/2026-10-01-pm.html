Top Stories

The 10-year touches its highest since 2002, then reverses, and the 2-year falls 10 basis points

The global long-end selloff peaked before New York opened. By the close the whole Treasury curve was lower and steeper, and stocks had recovered their early losses.

Overnight the 10-year Treasury yield traded as high as 5.34%, its highest since 2002, according to Reuters and CNBC citing LSEG data. The 30-year briefly touched about 5.67%. Britain’s 30-year gilt yield peaked at about 6.03%, the highest since 1998, and Japan’s 30-year yield rose to about 4.20%. Reuters’ summary was that a global benchmark of government debt had its biggest quarterly rise this century in the third quarter.

Then the market turned. By late morning CNBC had the 10-year and 30-year 5bp and 3bp lower on the day, and Treasury’s official close confirms it. The rally was largest at the front of the curve:

  • 1-year: 4.44%, down 10bp
  • 2-year: 4.78%, down 10bp
  • 5-year: 5.01%, down 8bp
  • 10-year: 5.24%, down 5bp
  • 30-year: 5.61%, down 3bp

The gap between the 2-year and 10-year widened to 46bp from 41bp, the widest of this run. Unexplained We found no single confirmed catalyst for a 10bp fall in the 2-year. The day’s data, if anything, pointed the other way. ISM’s prices-paid index jumped nearly 7 points, and jobless claims stayed near 57-year lows. Jefferson repeated that inflation has been “too high for too long.” Prediction markets and a CME FedWatch reading cited by one crypto outlet put the chance of an October 28 hike at about one in three, little changed from Wednesday. Those readings carry no reliable timestamp. A pullback after the overnight extreme is the plainest description of what happened.

Stocks followed the turn in yields. The S&P 500 was lower in the first hour and finished up 0.19%. Equal-weight RSP (+0.47%) and small-cap IWM (+0.41%) beat the cap-weighted SPY (+0.18%) for the first time in several sessions. Rate-sensitive stocks had been hurt most by the selloff, and they are the ones that recovered.

Why it matters One reversal does not change the trend. The 30-year is still within 3bp of its post-2002 closing high, and HYG closed within 51 cents of its 52-week low. Still, the overnight peak at 5.34% came on no new US data and then failed. If Friday’s payrolls are soft, that peak may hold as the top. If they are strong, it is the level the market will test again before next week’s auctions.
What’s next Payrolls Friday at 8:30, then 3-, 10- and 30-year Treasury auctions Tuesday through Thursday.

Nike beats on the quarter but cuts its year: revenue down high-single digits and EPS of $1.15–$1.35

The full-year earnings guide is about 25% below consensus, and Nike announced a new cost program, “Pace.” The stock had closed Thursday 13 cents above its 52-week low.

Nike’s release, filed at 4:15 PM and read directly for this edition, reported the following for the quarter ended August 31:

  • Revenue: $11.2 billion, down 4%, or 5% excluding currency effects. Consensus was about $11.3 billion.
  • Diluted EPS: $0.48, against about $0.44 expected.
  • Gross margin: 42.8%, up 60bp on lower warehousing and logistics costs.
  • Nike Direct: down 8%, with digital sales down 13%.
  • Converse: down 28%.
  • Greater China: down 22% to $1.18 billion, or 26% excluding currency, per secondary accounts of the call materials.

North America grew. Inventories fell 3% to $7.8 billion.

The outlook is the news. Nike now expects fiscal 2027 revenue to fall by a high-single-digit percentage. It expects adjusted EPS of $1.15–$1.35, which excludes about $0.15 of restructuring costs. Analysts had expected about $1.65. Pace is an operating-model overhaul: three geographic regions instead of the current structure, a new campus in India, and supply-chain modernization. Nike says it will save about $2.5 billion cumulatively through fiscal 2031, at a cost of about $1.0 billion in pre-tax charges, $0.3 billion of them this year. Chief executive Elliott Hill said: “We have more work to do in NIKE Sportswear, Jordan Brand and Greater China.” The 8-K also carries Item 2.05, the filing item for exit and restructuring costs.

The shares closed at $35.15, down 0.7%, on 2.8 times normal volume. They were about 5% lower after hours in early trading, per secondary reports. The 52-week high is $76.97.

Why it matters This is the third year of a turnaround, and the guide says the decline is not over. The quarter’s margin beat came from costs, not demand. For the wider consumer trade, China down by a quarter and digital sales down 13% are the details to carry forward.

Brent jumps about 4% back above $102 as Iran holds to its terms and more US forces head for the Gulf

Oil fell in the morning on recovering Gulf exports, then rose more than 5% from its low. Several possible drivers arrived at once.

December Brent settled at $102.31, up 4.4%, and November WTI at $92.87, up 2.7%, according to CNBC. Yahoo’s late quotes were $102.38 and $92.93. Earlier in the day Brent had traded near $96 on reports that Gulf exports were back near pre-war levels. Likely contributor Three developments came during the rally, and we cannot rank them:

  • The Wall Street Journal reported, and the AP confirmed, that more US forces are heading to the region, with a third carrier possible by late October.
  • Iran’s deputy foreign minister said “the ball is in the US court.” He repeated that Hormuz reopens only after the war ends on all fronts and the US blockade is lifted.
  • Reuters reported that Chinese refiners suspended October fuel exports.

The rally lifted energy shares. XLE rose 1.95% and USO 3.0%.

The Treasury Department added Iran sanctions on Thursday. The actions target Iran’s auto, rail and industrial sectors, and the “A7” shadow-banking network is now designated a transnational criminal organization, with a proposed FinCEN rule to follow. “If you facilitate illicit finance for America’s adversaries, you will lose access to the U.S. financial system,” Secretary Bessent said.

Why it matters Reuters listed oil among the drivers of the global bond selloff, and today’s ISM prices index shows energy costs are reaching factories. Jefferson named energy as his main inflation worry. Eight OPEC+ members meet on Sunday and are expected to keep November targets unchanged, per anonymous sources cited by Reuters.

The Economy

Factories. ISM manufacturing for September was 54.5. Reports put August at 54.6, where our calendar had carried 55.2, so the August comparison is uncertain. Either way the index is well above 50, the line between expansion and contraction. Consensus was 54.8–55.0. New orders rose to 55.3 from 53.7 and employment to 52.7 from 51.2. Prices paid rose to 77.9 from 71.1, one of the larger one-month jumps in the series. The figures come from accounts of ISM’s release on PR Newswire and FXStreet. S&P Global’s final manufacturing PMI was 55.9, revised down from its 57.0 flash. That is still its strongest since May 2022, per TradingEconomics. The ISM prices index is a diffusion index: it counts how many firms report paying more, not how much more they pay.

Labour. Initial jobless claims fell 1,000 to 197,000 in the week to September 26. The prior week was revised to 198,000, and FRED’s series confirms both. The four-week average is 200,000. Continuing claims fell 11,000 to 1.701 million, per Reuters. Layoffs remain rare. Friday’s payrolls will show whether hiring is slowing.

Construction. Construction spending rose 0.9% in August to a $2.203 trillion annual rate, and July was revised up, per Reuters. That is a nominal level, still 1.7% below a year earlier.

The Fed. Vice Chair Philip Jefferson’s text, read on federalreserve.gov, describes inflation as above target “for more than five years.” He said: “I remain concerned about the risk of higher energy prices leading to a persistent rise in inflation more broadly.” He called September’s quarter-point increase, to 3.75–4.00%, “an important step” for expectations. He said future moves depend on “the data, the evolving outlook, and the balance of risks.” On bonds, he said the rise in yields across the curve is “a sign that investors are reassessing the evolving macroeconomic landscape.” He did not call it disorderly. Governor Waller’s talk at FRED Con was about data and AI, with no policy content. Vice Chair Bowman, speaking on bank regulation, said the recalibrated leverage ratio had given banks nearly $5 trillion of balance-sheet room and that dealer Treasury holdings had grown past $700 billion. Governor Cook, by one Newsquawk headline, said supply shocks have had “surprisingly persistent effects.”

The Treasury. Treasury sold $195 billion of 4- and 8-week bills today, settling October 6, and we did not find the results. The government is funded through December 11. Bowman’s figures matter for next week’s 3-, 10- and 30-year auctions. They say dealers have more room to absorb supply than in past selloffs, and that is the buyer base the market doubts. Treasury’s other releases today were the Iran sanctions and the completion of “Trump Accounts” auto-enrolment for more than 60 million children.

Rates and credit. The ICE BofA high-yield spread (FRED) widened to 3.12% on Wednesday from 3.08%. That is 46bp wider in seven sessions and the widest of this run. The 10-year TIPS real yield was 2.93% and the 10-year breakeven 2.36% on Wednesday. Long yields have risen mostly through real yields, not inflation expectations. SOFR was 3.90%.

The United States

A federal judge rules the president cannot fire a court-appointed US attorney and reinstates Seattle’s Roger Rogoff

The case appears to be the first ruling of its kind on the removal power, and an appeal to the Ninth Circuit is expected.

Judge Stanley Bastian of the Eastern District of Washington, hearing the case for the Western District, granted a preliminary injunction from the bench on Thursday. The order returns Roger Rogoff to the job of US attorney in Seattle, according to CNN, KOMO, Bloomberg Law and Law360. Judges of the Western District appointed Rogoff in July under the statute that lets district courts fill a vacancy after an interim appointment expires. The administration removed him within about an hour. The question is whether a prosecutor appointed by the court can be removed by the president. Bastian’s ruling says no, at least at this preliminary stage. We have not read the written order.

Separately, on Wednesday Judge Haywood Gilliam in Oakland blocked the $100,000 fee on new H-1B visas. He found that USCIS and the State Department skipped notice-and-comment rulemaking, per Reuters. He is the second judge to block the fee.

Congress leaves town with no full-year budget; the government is funded to December 11

There is no shutdown. The Senate adjourned late Wednesday until November 9. The appropriations bills, the defense authorization and the farm bill all wait for the lame-duck session.

The fiscal year began Thursday under the stopgap Congress passed in early September: the Senate voted 90–6, the House 370–48, and the president signed it on September 2. It holds spending at current levels through December 11. Before leaving, Senate Democrats blocked last-minute Republican votes on several bills, The Hill and the Washington Post report. Supporters of those votes said the bills had bipartisan backing. Opponents called them campaign messaging. The House is out until November 9 as well.

The midterms are 33 days away. Polling averages put Democrats ahead on the generic congressional ballot by roughly 6 to 8 points, depending on the average. Cook Political Report now rates Senate control a toss-up after moving three races toward Democrats in the past week. Republicans hold the Senate 53–47. The president began what his schedule describes as a 32-day campaign swing. Thursday’s stops were a Peterbilt truck plant in Denton, Texas, and a rally in Durant, Oklahoma.

The World

NATO rejects Russia’s Kaliningrad note as Moscow opens its winter campaign on Ukraine’s grid

Russia warned of “the entire arsenal” if NATO blockaded its Baltic exclave. NATO says no blockade is planned and called the nuclear language irresponsible.

Russia’s note to NATO on Wednesday claimed “information that NATO is preparing an air and naval blockade” of Kaliningrad, AP and Reuters reported. It warned that Russia would use its full arsenal, “including nuclear weapons,” and raised the prospect of striking “decision-making centers” in NATO states. NATO spokesperson Allison Hart said the alliance “strongly denounce[s] the threat of force, including any irresponsible nuclear rhetoric,” and replied in writing that its exercises pose no risk to Russia. Secretary-General Mark Rutte said he did not take the threat seriously. More NATO Baltic exercises are planned for October, and Latvia votes in a parliamentary election on Saturday.

On the ground, Russia launched nearly 190 drones and ballistic missiles against Kyiv and the surrounding region on Wednesday. A Kyiv thermal power plant was hit and emergency blackouts followed. Death tolls vary by source, from at least four (AP, for the Kyiv region) to seven nationwide (the Washington Post, citing Zelenskyy). On Thursday a drone struck a Kyiv school while pupils sheltered in the basement, and no injuries were reported. Moldova briefly closed its airspace after its third drone incursion in nine days. Ukraine says its counteroffensive near Lyman has retaken more than 125 square kilometres, a claim not independently verified.

Science

Bats began in Europe. A study in Nature by 137 researchers combined 103 bat genomes with 44 fossil species. It places the origin of bats in Europe 65–60 million years ago, finds that echolocation evolved early, and reconstructs the ancestral bat genome, according to the Museum für Naturkunde and Stony Brook University.

A 40-year-old prediction measured. Caltech physicists used laser-trapped atoms as a quantum simulator to measure directly the energy-level ratios that conformal field theory predicted for the Ising and tricritical Ising models in the 1980s. The paper is in Nature, per ScienceDaily. The method could let simulators test field theories that are too hard to calculate.

About 1,000 Duquesne Light workers are on strike, and no talks are scheduled

Members of IBEW Local 29 walked out at 12:01 AM Thursday when their contract expired, the Post-Gazette, WPXI and KDKA report. Twelve days left for lawmakers to act on skill games before police start seizing machines

The state Supreme Court ruled in June that the games are slot machines and gave the General Assembly until October 13 to act. State Police say that from October 14 they will seize unlicensed machines, an estimated 70,000 of them, and that criminal charges are possible, according to Spotlight PA.
