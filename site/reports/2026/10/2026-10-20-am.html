Top Stories

Korean chipmakers slide and Nasdaq futures fade a day after the chip rebound

Monday’s rally in semiconductors ended a nine-day losing streak. Overnight, Seoul’s memory makers fell hard, and the neocloud data-centre stocks are lower again before the bell.

At 6:33 AM ET, Nasdaq 100 futures traded at 29,595.25, down 0.43% from Monday’s settlement. S&P 500 futures were at 7,728.75 (−0.07%), Dow futures at 52,262 (+0.15%) and Russell 2000 futures flat. Reuters’ premarket report had CoreWeave down about 3% and Nebius about 3%, with Apple down 1% and Nvidia slightly lower. It said the slip in AI names coincided with higher oil and the approach of the week’s big tech earnings.

The sharpest move was in Seoul. The Kospi closed down 3.73% at 5,552.74, according to CNBC, and Yahoo’s quote agrees. That erased Monday’s 1.8% gain and more. SK Hynix fell about 4.2% and Samsung Electronics about 3.0%, and foreign investors were reported as heavy net sellers. Taiwan’s Taiex fell 1.7%, Japan’s Nikkei 1.3% to 64,075 and Hong Kong’s Hang Seng 1.3% on its return from Monday’s holiday. Shanghai edged up 0.1%. Investing.com’s Asia wrap said AI jitters hit the Korean chipmakers ahead of US tech earnings. That describes the selling; no new fact about AI demand or financing was reported overnight.

Monday’s US gains were narrow. The S&P 500 rose 0.41% and the Nasdaq 1.11%, but the equal-weight S&P fell 0.32%, and decliners beat advancers 3,038 to 2,218 across about 5,400 stocks.

Why it matters The market is again leaning on a handful of mega-caps while most stocks drift lower. Korean memory makers are the most direct read on AI hardware demand outside the US, and they have now fallen hard on two of the last five sessions.
What’s next Netflix and SpaceX report tonight. Meta, whose capex guide is the week’s real test of AI spending, reports on Wednesday after the close with Tesla, IBM, Lam Research and Texas Instruments. Futures are not a guaranteed open.

Brent climbs past $111 as Iran and the US trade claims over the strait

Iran says more tankers hit mines. Washington says the strait is clear of mines and ships are being hit by projectiles. No independent party has confirmed either account, and crude is at a five-month high.

December Brent traded at $111.61 a barrel at 6:33 AM, up 1.9% from Monday’s $109.55 settlement. CNBC describes the price as near its highest since May. December WTI was $100.92, up 2.4%. The November WTI contract expires today and is quoted higher, so “the” WTI price depends on which month you read. Gold fell 0.5% to $4,137.60.

Unconfirmed Iran’s Revolutionary Guard says two more tankers, each carrying about two million barrels, hit mines on Monday, following a supertanker on Saturday. That account comes from Iranian state media, carried by Tasnim and Al Arabiya. US Central Command says the strait has been cleared of mines and that ships are being hit by projectiles. Its commander, Adm. Brad Cooper, said the strait “is controlled by the United States”. The UK maritime agency says many attacked ships do not report, so counts are probably low. The think tank JINSA counts 26 Iranian attacks on ships from Oct. 1 to Oct. 19, twice September’s total.

Unverified Separately, Yemen’s Houthis claim strikes on Saudi Aramco’s Yanbu terminal, the Shaybah field and two Saudi airports. Saudi authorities have not confirmed them, and Reuters says it could not verify them. Bloomberg reports that the East-West pipeline, which lets Saudi crude bypass Hormuz through Yanbu, is running normally.

Diplomatically, Foreign Minister Araghchi says Iran’s seven-day plan, sent through Qatar, is “still on the table”. Under it the strait would reopen on day six. Tehran wants the US naval blockade lifted, oil sanctions suspended and a formal ceasefire. President Trump has called the plan not acceptable, and US officials say they are “not in a rush”.

Why it matters September CPI was 3.7% largely because of energy, and Treasury Secretary Bessent has argued the shock fades once the war ends. Brent above $110 pushes that relief further out, just as the Fed weighs whether to hike again on Oct. 28.

GE Aerospace raises its outlook on every line after orders jump 49%

Adjusted EPS of $2.25 beat by about 25 cents. The company now guides to $8.00–8.10 a share for 2026 and expects commercial services to grow at a low-double-digit rate next year.

GE Aerospace’s 8-K, filed at 6:30 AM, reports third-quarter orders of $19.0 billion (+49%) and GAAP revenue of $14.1 billion (+16%). Adjusted revenue was $13.3 billion (+18%) and operating profit $3.0 billion (+30%), a 22.5% margin. Adjusted EPS was $2.25 (+36%) against a consensus near $2.00–2.02 (Benzinga). Free cash flow fell 14% to $2.0 billion.

Full-year guidance went up across the board:

  • Adjusted EPS $8.00–8.10, from $7.65–7.85.
  • Operating profit $10.9–11.0 billion, from $10.55–10.75 billion.
  • Free cash flow $9.1–9.4 billion, from $8.9–9.2 billion.
  • Adjusted revenue growth of about 19–20%, from “high teens”.
Why it matters GE is the clearest read on airline fleet demand and the jet-engine aftermarket. A raise this size, with oil above $110, says airlines are still flying and still paying for engine maintenance. GE closed Monday at $311.28.

3M, Northrop and GM all beat, and two of them raise guidance

3M’s raise is partly an accounting change. Northrop’s backlog hit a record $110 billion. GM narrowed its profit range and authorised a $20 billion buyback.

3M reported adjusted EPS of $2.50 (+12%) against a consensus near $2.41, on adjusted sales of $6.6 billion with 3.3% organic growth. It raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $9.10–9.15. Read the raise with care: 3M now treats intangible amortisation as a special item, which lifts adjusted figures for every period. The old range of $8.80–8.95 was on the old basis. The release also discloses an expanded data-centre deal with Amazon. 3M issued Amazon a warrant for up to 3.2 million shares, which vests as Amazon’s cumulative purchases of optical-connectivity and other data-centre products reach $6 billion. Benzinga had the stock up about 4% premarket from Monday’s $162.01.

Northrop Grumman reported diluted EPS of $7.89 against estimates near $7.18–7.25. Sales rose 7% to $11.1 billion, in line, and net awards of $16 billion gave a book-to-bill of 1.5. Backlog reached a record $110 billion. Mark-to-market-adjusted EPS guidance rose to $29.35–29.75 from $28.60–29.10.

General Motors reported revenue of $50.0 billion against $48.7 billion expected and adjusted EPS of $3.70 against $3.63 (LSEG via CNBC). It narrowed 2026 adjusted EBIT guidance to $14.5–15.5 billion from $14.0–16.0 billion, which leaves the midpoint unchanged. The board authorised a new $20 billion share repurchase. We could not confirm the stock’s premarket move.

Long yields climb back toward their highs before Wednesday’s 20-year sale

The 10-year par yield rose 6 basis points on Monday to 5.15%, retracing nearly half of last week’s rally. Rate-sensitive stocks took the hit.

Treasury’s par curve for Monday shows the 2-year at 4.77% (+3 bp), the 10-year at 5.15% (+6) and the 20-year at 5.53% (+6). The 30-year was 5.48% (+5). The curve steepened, with 2s10s widening to 38 bp from 35, because long yields rose faster than short ones. Ten-year inflation breakevens held at 2.35%, so the move was in real yields rather than inflation expectations. Utilities fell 2.0%, real estate 1.2% and the long-bond ETF TLT 0.7%.

The supply test is Wednesday at 1 PM: a $13 billion reopening of the 20-year bond. A Substack preview says September’s 20-year reopening cleared at 5.420%, the highest since the maturity returned in 2020; we could find only that one source. The par curve has the 20-year 11 bp above that now. A $26 billion 5-year TIPS auction follows on Thursday. Today’s only sale is a $95 billion 6-week bill.

The Fed is in its quiet period before the Oct. 27–28 meeting. A FedWatch-based reading cited by Mitrade on Tuesday put an October hike at about 17% and a hike by December near 90%. Before the blackout, Governors Jefferson and Waller and New York Fed President Williams signalled that October could be a hold. Overnight, the People’s Bank of China kept its one-year loan prime rate at 3.00% and its five-year rate at 3.50%, the 17th month without a change and in line with all 22 economists Reuters polled.

France’s unrest reaches the bond market

The government has ordered partial curfews after a weekend of riots. French government bonds had their worst day in nearly a month, and European stocks are falling for a second day.

The protests began among students and spread into a broader cost-of-living movement. Interior Ministry figures cited by Foreign Policy put the weekend at about 500,000 marchers in more than 140 cities, nearly 1,000 police injured and about 2,400 arrests. Prime Minister Lecornu ordered partial curfews in several cities from Monday evening and has floated a state of emergency.

Markets responded on Monday. The Stoxx 600 fell 0.8% to 639.56 and the CAC 40 about 1.1%. Reporting cited by Investing.com described French bonds’ worst sell-off in nearly a month. One source puts the 10-year OAT near 4.84%, against about 3.42% on the German Bund; we could not confirm that spread with a second source. At 6:30 AM ET today the Stoxx 600 was down another 0.6%, the CAC 0.6% and the DAX 0.7%.

Why it matters France’s budget was already the euro area’s weak point. A spread move driven by street unrest rather than fiscal data is harder for policymakers to answer, and the ECB meets on Oct. 29.

Justice Department charges 28 in California and seeks emergency order on mail ballots

The department says everyone charged is a green-card holder on its list of about 20,000 names. California says the federal data is wrong and the request has no legal basis.

On Monday the Justice Department announced charges against 28 people in California. The counts include unlawful voting by a non-citizen, falsely claiming citizenship to register or vote, and depriving residents of a fair election. These are charges, not convictions. NPR and ABC report that several apparently never voted. President Trump wrote on Truth Social that they “will all go to jail for a long period of time”.

In United States v. California, filed Oct. 15 in the Central District of California, the department on Monday asked for a temporary restraining order. It would require the state to set aside about 20,000 mail ballots linked to the names on its list. The department’s theory rests on federal preemption and the federal law barring non-citizen voting. California officials say the list contains errors and have refused to set the ballots aside. No ruling had been reported by this morning. Election Day is Nov. 3, and early in-person voting opened Monday in several states, including Texas.

The United States

Supreme Court. Monday’s order list denied review in Wilcox v. Trump. That leaves standing an appeals ruling that limits on the president’s power to remove National Labor Relations Board members are unconstitutional; Bloomberg called it a win for the administration. The Court also turned away Cemex v. NLRB. It agreed to hear an equal-protection challenge to magnet-school admissions in Montgomery County, Maryland, and a litigation-funding dispute, Burford German Funding v. FinancialRight.

The midterms, 14 days out. Early in-person voting opened Monday in states including Texas, Colorado and South Carolina. Unofficial tracking data cited by NewsNation shows first-day turnout well above 2022 in Georgia and North Carolina. The World

Yemen. A Houthi ballistic-missile strike on a security-forces training camp in Shabwa province on Monday killed at least 53 people and wounded more than 200, according to local authorities cited by Asharq Al-Awsat; Al Jazeera’s earlier count was 18. Most of the dead were recruits. The Houthis claim Saudi officers ran the camp and were among the casualties but offered no evidence. The toll is not independently verified.

Russia and Ukraine. Russia struck Ukraine’s power system again overnight. Interfax-Ukraine reports 16 ballistic missiles and about 270 drones, more than 10 energy sites hit and about 550,000 households without power. Accounts of the toll range from two to three dead, and bne IntelliNews reports a Kyiv data centre destroyed. The Kremlin said Monday that Putin, Xi and Trump “could meet” at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on Nov. 18–19 but that nothing has been agreed. A senior US official says the president has not agreed, and Ukraine’s foreign minister objects to any meeting without Kyiv. Last week the president imposed the Graham Act sanctions on Russia and waived them at once. Bessent says that is not sanctions relief.

Elections abroad. President Trump endorsed Prime Minister Netanyahu before Israel’s Oct. 27 vote, and polls there are deadlocked. Albertans rejected starting a separation process, about 76% to 24% on an unofficial count. Brazil’s Sunday runoff between Flávio Bolsonaro and President Lula is a statistical tie in every recent poll.

AI & Technology

Anthropic sets aside 5% of its shares for a public-benefit fund. On Sunday the company announced the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, a wholly owned LLC rather than a charity. The 5% grant needs stockholder approval. The board may add 2% for every 50% rise in the company’s value above a baseline, up to about 20% in total. The fund’s equity cannot be used for lobbying or political giving; displaced workers are named as one possible beneficiary. The baseline and governance have not been disclosed. The move comes ahead of an IPO reported for as soon as November. Reuters reported last week that some investors were weighing a $1.5 trillion valuation against a reported target above $2 trillion. Read-through: Amazon and Alphabet are major investors.

Single source FTC demands. Axios reported last week that the FTC sent civil investigative demands of more than 50 pages each to OpenAI and Anthropic. They seek records on safety incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy. A demand is a request for information, not a finding. OpenAI said it would “engage constructively”.

Nvidia. The company released new open-weight models for always-on enterprise agents and small “Bolt” models for local PCs, per TechCrunch. Nvidia closed Monday at $217.09, up 1.4% but well below its record above $243.

Science

Gene editing in the body cuts swelling attacks. The Oct. 15 New England Journal of Medicine carries Intellia’s Phase 3 trial of lonvoguran ziclumeran, a one-time CRISPR therapy for hereditary angioedema. It reports a significantly lower attack rate than placebo. We have not read the full paper, and the exact figures should come from it. Read-through: Intellia (NTLA).

A step toward fault-tolerant quantum computers. A paper in Science demonstrates “magic state cultivation” on a superconducting processor. The technique cheaply produces the special states a quantum computer needs for operations that error correction alone cannot supply.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

County Council votes tonight on 12 weeks of paid parental leave

The ordinance would require employers with 15 or more eligible workers to provide 12 weeks of fully paid leave for birth, adoption or stillbirth. It was cut from 18 weeks in the original draft, and workers would need six months on the job, up from 30 days. It would take effect 180 days after passage or at the employer’s next budget cycle. The Board of Health approved it unanimously, and Council’s health committee advanced it 5–1. It needs eight votes and then goes to County Executive Innamorato. The Pennsylvania Chamber asked for a delay; council members told WESA they expect the vote to proceed.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

PRT board votes Friday on closing the Wabash Tunnel for good

Pittsburgh Regional Transit staff propose closing the tunnel permanently on Dec. 1. Its lighting, ventilation and electrical systems are rated poor to critical, and a long-term rehabilitation would cost about $43 million. About 1,500 vehicles a day use it, and no scheduled transit runs through it. The Federal Transit Administration has already approved the closure. The tunnel would not be demolished, and the Wabash park-and-ride stays open.

Pennsylvania

House passes a school cellphone ban and limits on licence-plate cameras

On Monday the state House passed SB 1014, restricting student cellphone use in schools, 141–62. The Senate passed a different version earlier, so the chambers must still agree on one text. The House also passed HB 2771 (Rep. Jennifer O’Mara), 190–13. It limits government use of automated licence-plate readers to law enforcement, some parking enforcement and secure-area access, and requires non-relevant data to be deleted within 72 hours. Only four session days remain before the two-year session ends Nov. 30, and a Republican senator told The Center Square there is “no logistical way” the plate-reader bill finishes in time.
