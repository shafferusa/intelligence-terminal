Top Stories

Intel and Amazon push Nasdaq futures down 2.4% and take the chip trade with them

This week the market has punished three giants for AI spending that their cash flow does not cover. This morning, for the first time, the selling has reached the companies that supply the build-out.

At about 6:30 AM, Nasdaq 100 futures were near 28,865, down 2.4% from Thursday’s settlement, and S&P 500 futures near 7,672, down 1.1%. Dow futures were unchanged and Russell 2000 futures were down 0.6%. Premarket indications at about 6:40 AM had Intel at about $69, 25% below Thursday’s close, and Amazon at about $227, down 6.8%. Reuters estimates the two would lose about $300 billion of market value between them if the moves hold. Chipmakers followed: AMD was indicated down 8.1%, Marvell 5.9%, Micron 5.1%, ASML 4.8%, Broadcom 3.6%, TSMC 3.4% and Nvidia 2.8%, and the VanEck semiconductor fund (SMH) 4.6% lower. Microsoft and Alphabet were down less than 1% (Yahoo Finance).

Asia traded the same way overnight. Korea’s Kospi fell 3.5% to 5,730.74 after dropping more than 5% intraday, which triggered a temporary curb on program selling; SK Hynix fell 7.1% and Samsung Electronics 3.0%, and foreign investors sold a net ₩1.6 trillion (Seoul Economic Daily). Japan’s Nikkei fell 2.1% to 63,290.11, led by SoftBank Group (−6%), Advantest and Tokyo Electron (Reuters), and Taiwan’s index fell 2.5%. Hong Kong rose 1.5%.

The week has a single theme. Last week Meta was marked down for selling stock to fund AI. On Thursday Alphabet had its worst day since its 2004 listing after free cash flow turned negative, while Microsoft, which pays for its build-out from cash flow, held steady. Overnight, Amazon reported trailing free cash flow of −$42.4 billion and Intel a forecast well below estimates. What is new this morning is that the suppliers are falling too: until now the market had treated their customers’ capital spending as a guaranteed source of revenue for them.

Why it matters Chip and AI-infrastructure stocks are the largest group by weight in the Nasdaq 100, and they have carried this year’s gains. Thursday’s rotation — equal-weight S&P (RSP) up 1.2% and small caps 1.0% while the Nasdaq fell 1.4% — shows the money moving within the market, not leaving it. Our call: the Nasdaq Composite closes today below Thursday’s 26,184.72 (75–88%), given futures down 2.4% three hours before the open; a large rebound in the hour after the open would undo it. Futures are not a guaranteed open.

Intel is indicated 25% lower after forecasting a weak holiday quarter and a shrinking PC market

Third-quarter results beat on the strength of data-center sales. The forecast for the fourth quarter fell well short, and executives said the PC slump will last into 2027.

Intel reported third-quarter revenue of about $16.5 billion, up 21%, and adjusted earnings of $0.52 a share, with data-center revenue up about 78% (company results via TradingKey, CNBC). The forecast drove the stock: fourth-quarter revenue of $15–16 billion against about $17 billion expected, and adjusted earnings of $0.25 a share against about $0.43. The company said it expects the PC market to shrink about 12% this year and about 10% in 2027, and the chief financial officer said the slowdown will last at least through the first half of 2027. Intel also said a roughly two-week production halt in Israel in October will cut fourth-quarter revenue by about $300 million (Bloomberg, CNBC). Reuters reports the company warned of cash burn this year.

The shares closed Thursday at $92.07, after falling 3.6% in the session, and were indicated near $69 at 6:40 AM. A 25% fall would be among the largest one-day declines in the company’s history; the after-hours estimates had ranged from 15% to 23%, so selling deepened overnight.

What’s next The PC forecast matters beyond Intel: it tells AMD, Micron and the memory makers that consumer demand will not cushion them if data-center orders slow. AMD is indicated 8% lower this morning, more than Nvidia.

Intel Form 8-K (SEC EDGAR, filed 4:08 PM Oct. 29); CNBC; Bloomberg; Reuters; TradingKey; Yahoo Finance.

Amazon is indicated 7% lower: AWS grew 39%, but free cash flow over the past year was −$42 billion

Its cloud business grew at its fastest pace in about five years. Earnings and the holiday-quarter profit forecast fell short, and spending is going higher next year.

Amazon reported third-quarter revenue of $201.9 billion, up 12% and slightly below estimates, and earnings of $1.89 a share against about $1.96–1.97 expected. Amazon Web Services grew 39% to about $46.0 billion, at a record margin. Advertising growth slowed to 12% from 26%. Free cash flow over the trailing twelve months was −$42.4 billion, against +$14.8 billion a year earlier, as the company holds its 2026 capital plan at about $220 billion. The fourth-quarter guide was revenue of $240–247 billion, with operating income at a midpoint of about $31 billion against roughly $33.2 billion expected, and Reuters reports the company signalled higher capital spending next year (Amazon 8-K; Reuters; Forbes).

The stock fell 4.3% to $243.79 during Thursday’s session, before the report, and was indicated near $227 at 6:40 AM. The comparison with Microsoft is the one the market is making: Azure grew 48% while Microsoft produced $18.6 billion of free cash flow in a single quarter.

Amazon Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (SEC EDGAR, filed 4:05 PM Oct. 29); Reuters; CNBC; Forbes; Yahoo Finance.

Treasuries rally as stocks fall; the 10-year heads for its biggest monthly drop since 2023

The 10-year is about 4.92% this morning, down from 5.13% on Oct. 21. The Employment Cost Index at 8:30 is the last wage reading before the jobs report on Nov. 6.

The 10-year Treasury yield was about 4.91–4.93% in Asian and European trade (Reuters, CNBC), down roughly 6 basis points from Thursday’s official close of 4.98%. Reuters reports that would leave it down about 37 basis points for October, the largest monthly fall since December 2023. Thursday’s official curve had already rallied after in-line inflation data and a clean seven-year auction: the 2-year fell 6 basis points to 4.61% and the 30-year 5 to 5.32%. Fed funds futures now price a December hike at about 75–80% (CME FedWatch, as reported by Fox Business and FXStreet), down from about 84% on Wednesday.

At 8:30 the Labor Department publishes the third-quarter Employment Cost Index. Forecasters expect a rise of 0.8% for the quarter after 0.9% in the second, when it was up 3.4% from a year earlier. With September’s core PCE at 3.1% and payroll growth stalled at 29,000, a wage reading at or below consensus would support the market’s move away from a December hike; a reading of 1.0% or more would revive it.

What’s next Our calls: the 10-year par yield closes today below Thursday’s 4.98% (70–85%), given it is already about 6 basis points lower; and the ECI rises 0.8% or more for the quarter (45–60%), given the consensus and the second quarter’s 0.9%.

The Bank of Japan holds at 1.25% and says inflation is now in line with its target for the first time since 2013

One board member voted to hike. The yen strengthened slightly, and markets now treat December as the likely date for the next increase.

The Bank of Japan left its policy rate at 1.25% by an 8–1 vote, with board member Takata dissenting in favour of a rise to 1.50% (Bloomberg, Japan Times). Its quarterly outlook judged underlying inflation “broadly in line” with the 2% target, the first such assessment since 2013 (Reuters). Governor Kazuo Ueda said the bank has “not reached our goal yet,” but that this “doesn’t mean we will just sit and wait,” and he gave no timing (Reuters). The dollar fell to about 157.1 yen from 157.8 and was 157.3 at 6:38 AM. MUFG estimates a December hike was about 80% priced before the meeting (via FXStreet).

Bloomberg; Reuters; Japan Times; FXStreet; Yahoo Finance (USD/JPY). The Bank of Japan’s own statement could not be retrieved this morning.

Oil rises as Iran claims three tankers hit mines near Hormuz and the US says none did

The December Brent contract expires today near $116, and OPEC+ meets on Sunday. Brent is heading for a fourth straight monthly gain.

US Central Command said “no ships have hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz” and that its forces have cleared the major lanes. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre separately confirmed one tanker struck by an unidentified projectile and one dhow lost Disputed. Chevron and Exxon beat estimates on a refining boom

The war-driven spread between crude and fuel prices has made refining the oil majors’ best business this year. Both shares are indicated about 1.5% higher.

Chevron earned $9.9 billion, or $5.06 a share, in the third quarter, and $5.28 a share adjusted, against about $4.89 expected (CNBC). Its downstream (refining and marketing) earnings rose to $4.9 billion from $1.1 billion a year earlier, its US refineries ran at record throughput at 98% utilisation, and free cash flow was $12.0 billion. Production fell to 3.98 million barrels a day from 4.07 million in the second quarter. It said its Venezuelan joint ventures plan to invest $7 billion over five years (Chevron 8-K). Exxon Mobil reported $15.4 billion, or $3.72 a share, and $4.23 excluding identified items against estimates of about $4.03–4.12, with refining earnings roughly doubling from the second quarter (company release, Reuters). At 6:40 AM Chevron was indicated 1.5% higher and Exxon 1.5% higher.

Chevron Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (SEC EDGAR, filed 6:16 AM Oct. 30, primary); ExxonMobil earnings release via Reuters; CNBC; Yahoo Finance.

Binance discloses a theft that outlets put at about $6.3 billion; bitcoin falls toward $77,000

If the figure holds, it would be the largest crypto theft on record. About half has reportedly been frozen.

Binance said in a statement early Friday (02:25 UTC) that its initial investigation points to a compromised laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer, which let an attacker submit unauthorised withdrawals; it says no private keys were exposed. Crypto outlets put the loss at about $6.3 billion; Chainalysis estimates about $5.9 billion at the time of the theft, and about $3 billion, including $2.3 billion of stablecoins, has reportedly been frozen with help from partners. The blockchain analytics firms TRM Labs and Chainalysis attribute the attack to North Korea’s TraderTraitor unit, linked to the Lazarus Group; TRM says it has “high confidence.” No government has confirmed the attribution, and Binance has not confirmed the dollar figure (CoinDesk, Blockonomi, Bitcoin.com, FinanceFeeds).

About $1.5 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated across exchanges in 24 hours, mostly bitcoin longs (CoinGlass via Bitcoin.com). Bitcoin was near $77,500 at 6:38 AM, down from $81,268 at Wednesday’s close, and total crypto market value was about $2.60 trillion, down 5.6% in 24 hours (CoinGecko).

Binance statement as reported by crypto outlets; Chainalysis and TRM Labs via Blockonomi and FinanceFeeds; CoinGlass via Bitcoin.com; CoinGecko; Yahoo Finance.

Federal prosecutors subpoena California election officials to testify the day after the midterms

The grand jury subpoenas concern the screening of registrants whom the government lists as likely noncitizens. No one has been charged.

The Justice Department has issued grand jury subpoenas to state and local election officials in California, including staff of the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder, requiring testimony on Nov. 4. According to NBC, NPR and ABC, the subpoenas seek the officials’ correspondence with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter, and the county’s procedures for screening noncitizens. The department also sent warning letters to 57 of California’s 58 counties, and Minnesota’s secretary of state’s office received a subpoena for voter records. The department declined to comment on what it called leaks. California Attorney General Rob Bonta called the subpoenas an attack on election officials. Separately, a divided Ninth Circuit panel this week refused the department’s emergency request to make California set aside about 20,000 mail ballots, after a district judge denied the same request; the department has appealed again.

The most fragile assumption. This morning’s tape assumes the damage stays inside technology: Dow futures are flat, small caps only slightly lower and credit spreads near their tightest. If the chip selling spreads into the industrials and utilities that have been sold as “AI power” plays, or if high-yield spreads widen on the crypto losses, the rotation becomes a broad sell-off. A Dow that turns lower by more than 1% in the first hour, or a VIX above 20, would be the sign.

The Economy

Wages at 8:30. The Employment Cost Index measures what employers pay in wages and benefits, adjusted for changes in the mix of jobs, which makes it the Fed’s preferred wage gauge. The consensus is +0.8% for the third quarter after +0.9% (3.4% a year) in the second. The labour data around it are mixed: payrolls rose 29,000 in September after 133,000 in August and a decline of 10,000 in July, unemployment rose to 4.2%, but weekly jobless claims of 194,000 for the week to Oct. 24 show few layoffs (FRED). The October jobs report is on Friday, Nov. 6 (BLS). Chicago’s purchasing managers’ index follows at 9:45; it was 58.8 in September, and forecasts for October range from 54.8 to 56.1.

The Fed path. The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% on Wednesday, with Governor Cook and President Hammack dissenting in favour of a hike. No Fed officials are scheduled to speak today. Futures price a hike at the Dec. 9 meeting at about 75–80% and another by March. The case for one rests on inflation: September’s headline PCE was 3.6% a year and core 3.1%, with core rising 0.3% for the month, and headline CPI rose 0.65% in September (FRED, nominal index). The case against is the labour market and now a softer stock market.

Treasury. No auctions today. The Treasury’s quarterly refunding begins Monday with its borrowing estimates, according to market previews, followed by the refunding statement on Wednesday, Nov. 4, which will set coupon auction sizes for the coming quarter. In August the Treasury estimated it would borrow $628 billion in October–December and end the year with $850 billion in cash. One question for the statement is whether it keeps the larger buyback capacity it added in September. SOFR was 3.94% on Oct. 28, in line with the fed funds range.

Global central banks. The Bank of Japan held at 1.25% with one vote to hike; the ECB held at 2.50% on Thursday, when Christine Lagarde said she will step down on Feb. 7, about nine months before her term ends; the Bank of Canada raised its rate on Wednesday. Australia’s third-quarter producer prices rose 1.1%, adding to the hot consumer-price data that has traders betting on a hawkish central bank there.

Risks & Scenarios

Base case — the rotation continues (about 50–60%). The Nasdaq falls 1.5–3% while the Dow and equal-weight indexes hold up, Treasuries keep their gains and credit stays calm. This matches the pattern since Wednesday and today’s futures. Our calls logged for grading: the Nasdaq Composite closes below 26,184.72 (75–88%), the 10-year par yield closes below 4.98% (70–85%), and the VIX closes above 18.0 (50–65%), given it was about 18.5 early and closed at 17.20 on Thursday.

Downside — a broad sell-off (about 20–30%). The chip selling spreads into AI power and industrial names and into credit; the Dow falls more than 1% and the VIX rises above 20. The crypto losses are the wild card: forced selling by leveraged holders can spread to stocks of crypto-linked companies and brokers.

Upside — the dip is bought (about 15–25%). A soft ECI and lower yields lift rate-sensitive stocks, and buyers step into chipmakers after the open. The basis: Thursday’s session, when equal-weight stocks and small caps rose while the Nasdaq fell, shows buyers are still active outside megacap technology.

Into next week. The weekend carries three event risks: OPEC+ on Sunday, Iran’s response to the US letter, and Tuesday’s midterms. Apple reports Monday and the Treasury sets its auction sizes on Wednesday.

Science

A Jurassic swimmer. This week’s Nature cover describes Megacauda sungei, a roughly 165-million-year-old relative of early mammals whose skeleton shows it both dug and swam, like a modern platypus (Luo, Zhou, Martin and colleagues). It adds to evidence that mammal relatives filled varied ecological roles well before the dinosaurs died out.

An Alzheimer’s candidate, early. Nature Medicine published preclinical work and a phase 1 trial of VG-3927, a small-molecule drug that activates TREM2, a receptor on the brain’s immune cells. In healthy volunteers it reached the central nervous system, engaged its target and was tolerated. Phase 1 trials test safety, not benefit; whether it slows the disease is unknown.

Also published. Science carried a study dating a Martian meteorite to at least 4.1 billion years ago and two condensed-matter papers on magnetic and topological states in engineered materials. Nature reported that the brain clears large numbers of faulty neurons around birth.

Space

The 100th US spacewalk. NASA’s Anil Menon and the Canadian Space Agency’s Joshua Kutryk spent 7 hours 11 minutes outside the International Space Station on Thursday, installing the seventh of eight new roll-out solar arrays. The eighth goes up on a spacewalk set for Nov. 5; with all eight, the station’s power rises from about 160 kilowatts to about 215 (NASA).

Launches. SpaceX plans its next Starlink launch from Vandenberg tonight at 11:48 PM ET; it flew its 117th Falcon 9 mission of the year on Tuesday (Space.com). United Launch Alliance targets Nov. 13 for Vulcan’s return to flight, after a nine-month grounding over its solid rocket boosters, carrying 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites in its first six-booster configuration (Spaceflight Now). For Amazon the launch matters as Leo is the next big item in its capital plan; for SpaceX shareholders it marks the return of a competitor for satellite launches.

Also in the News

Measles. The CDC counts 4,588 US cases this year as of Oct. 22, up 178 in a week, with 479 hospitalisations and 43 outbreaks. Pennsylvania has 1,317 cases; the state counts five deaths, three of them confirmed by the CDC (Reuters). The next update is expected Wednesday.

Weather. The Atlantic hurricane season is quiet, with no storms expected for the next seven days (National Hurricane Center). Moisture from the remnants of Pacific Tropical Storm Vance is bringing rain to the southern and southwestern US and a risk of flooding in Mexico.

Wegmans will anchor a rebuilt Galleria of Mt. Lebanon

Roadside Development, the Washington, D.C., firm that bought the Galleria on Washington Road in April, and Wegmans announced on Thursday that Wegmans will anchor a redevelopment of the two-story, roughly 168,000-square-foot mall with a store of about 110,000 square feet. It would be the chain’s second in the Pittsburgh area, after Cranberry. Existing tenants stay open during planning. Reports differ on timing: one says the store opens in 2027, another that renovation begins in 2028. Yesterday’s edition said Wegmans had bought the property; most reports describe it as the anchor tenant of Roadside’s project (see Corrections).

Pennsylvania

The legislature leaves for the election with the fewest bills passed in at least 50 years

The 2025–26 General Assembly is on track to pass fewer bills than any session since at least 1973, WESA reports. Skill-game regulation never got a vote, and the Senate passed none of the data-center measures. The House returns Nov. 9 and the Senate Nov. 17 for the post-election session. Gov. Josh Shapiro signed a bell-to-bell ban on cell phones in schools on Monday, effective next school year. On Tuesday Shapiro faces Stacy Garrity; the latest independent polls had him ahead by 23–25 points (Franklin & Marshall, Oct. 7–18; Quinnipiac). Locally, Rep. Chris Deluzio faces Beaver County Sheriff Tony Guy in the 17th District. Mail ballots must reach county election offices by 8 PM Tuesday.
