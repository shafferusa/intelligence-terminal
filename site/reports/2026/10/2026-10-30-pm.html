Friday, October 30, 2026 · 34 min read

Intel fell 28.6% and the selling in AI hardware spread to almost every chipmaker, giving the S&P 500 its worst day since June and the Nasdaq its worst month since March 2025, while defensive stocks and oil producers rose, wage growth matched forecasts and Treasury yields ended October about a third of a point lower.

Top Stories

Intel falls 28.6%, its worst day since 1974, and the market finally sells the companies that supply the AI build-out

Chipmakers lost about $1 trillion of value in a session, as reported. For three days investors had punished the spenders; today they marked down the suppliers too.

Intel closed at $65.76, down $26.31 from Thursday’s $92.07, on about 448 million shares — more than four times its 20-day average (Yahoo Finance). The Associated Press called it the stock’s worst day since 1974. It was down about 30% at the low. The company had guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16 billion against about $17 billion expected, said the PC market will shrink into 2027, cited higher memory costs and a two-week production halt in Israel, and warned of cash burn this year (Intel 8-K; Reuters). Intel is now down 45% in October and 54% below its 52-week high.

The selling did not stay at Intel. AMD fell 10.9%, Marvell 10.1% (after 13.2% on Thursday), ASML 6.6%, TSMC 6.0%, Broadcom 5.5%, Micron 5.3% and Nvidia 4.2%; the VanEck semiconductor fund (SMH) fell 7.0% and Reuters put the chip index down 7.2%. Sandisk fell 13.5% despite beating estimates and announcing an 8-for-1 split, and Reuters reported Dell down 10.8% and HP 9.6%. Lumentum, reportedly in talks to be bought by Marvell, fell 8.6%. Vertiv, an AI power-equipment supplier that rose 16.5% on Wednesday, fell 5.5% (Yahoo Finance).

Nvidia was the single largest drag on the S&P 500, Reuters and AP reported. The technology sector fund (XLK) fell 4.9%, and Reuters said technology had its worst month since March 2025, with the Nasdaq 100 losing more than $2 trillion of value in October. The Nasdaq Composite’s 2.9% fall was its largest since June 5, according to Reuters, and leaves it about 8% below its Oct. 6 record close.

The week’s sequence matters. Meta was marked down last week for selling stock to fund AI; on Thursday Alphabet had its worst day since 2004 after free cash flow turned negative; overnight Amazon showed trailing free cash flow of −$42 billion. Until today, the companies selling chips, memory and equipment to those spenders had held up. Intel’s PC warning, and a market that no longer assumes every capital budget keeps rising, changed that.

Why it matters. Semiconductors and AI-infrastructure names carried most of this year’s index gains and are the largest group by weight in the Nasdaq 100. When the suppliers fall with the spenders, the cap-weighted indexes have no AI leg to stand on: the equal-weight S&P fell only 0.8% today and is up 5.0% in October, while the Magnificent Seven fund is down 4.5%.
What’s next. AMD reports Tuesday after the close, Qualcomm Wednesday and Arm Thursday; Apple reports Monday. This morning’s call that the Nasdaq would close lower landed. Our call: the S&P 500 closes Monday above today’s 7,580.07 (45–60%) — a near coin flip after a 1.8% fall, with weekend Iran and OPEC+ risk on one side and oversold chips on the other.

Yahoo Finance (closing prices and volume); Intel Form 8-K, Oct 29 (SEC EDGAR); Associated Press; Reuters via Yahoo Finance; CNBC; Benzinga.

Amazon recovers most of its overnight loss and closes down 1.4%, while Reddit, Roblox and Twilio do not

The market forgave one cash-burning spender and punished three companies whose growth is slowing.

Amazon was indicated about 7% lower before the open after an earnings miss and a fourth-quarter profit guide below estimates. It closed at $240.35, down 1.4% (Yahoo Finance), on 103 million shares, close to three times average volume. AWS grew 39%, its fastest in about five years, and chief executive Andy Jassy projected growth above 40% in 2027 and 2028 (8-K); reports described buyers stepping in through the day. Yesterday’s call that Amazon would close below $243.79 landed, but by far less than the after-hours price implied. Microsoft fell 1.6%, Meta 1.5%, Alphabet 1.4% and Apple 1.2%.

Thursday’s other reporters had a worse day. Reddit fell 29.2% to $91.73 after its daily active users of about 130 million fell short of the roughly 134 million expected, as reported. Roblox fell 18.1% on a weaker fourth-quarter sales outlook and Twilio 15.2% on slower expected organic growth (CNBC, Bloomberg). Each was a growth story priced for acceleration; each guided to deceleration.

Yahoo Finance (closes, volume); Amazon Form 8-K, Oct 29 (SEC EDGAR); CNBC; Bloomberg; Yahoo Finance reporting.

Wage growth matches forecasts at 0.8%, the slowest annual pay gain since 2021; Treasuries end October with their biggest monthly fall since 2023

The 10-year par yield fell to 4.94%, from 5.29% at the end of September. Futures trimmed the odds of a December Fed hike to about 72%.

The Employment Cost Index, which measures employers’ costs for the same mix of jobs over time and is the Fed’s preferred wage gauge, rose 0.8% in the third quarter, seasonally adjusted, after 0.9% in the second (BLS). Wages and salaries rose 0.7% for the quarter and 3.1% from a year earlier; Haver Analytics, cited by Reuters, called the annual wage figure a five-year low. Benefit costs rose 0.9% and 3.9% for the year. Total compensation was up 3.4% for the year, unchanged. Adjusted for inflation, private-sector wages fell 0.6% over the year (BLS). This morning’s call that the index would rise 0.8% or more landed, exactly at the line.

The reading matters for the Fed because it cuts against the inflation data. September’s core PCE prices rose 3.1% from a year earlier and headline 3.6%. Pay rising 3.1% while prices rise faster means workers are losing ground and the wage-price spiral the hawks fear is not visible in this report. Chicago’s purchasing managers’ index fell to 55.3 in October from 58.8, below the 55.8–56.1 forecast but still well above the 50 line between expansion and contraction, as reported.

Treasuries extended their rally. The official par curve: 3-month 4.16% (−2 bp), 2-year 4.56% (−5), 10-year 4.94% (−4), 30-year 5.31% (−1). That leaves the 10-year down 35 basis points and the 2-year down 32 in October (Treasury; FRED for Sep. 30) — the largest monthly fall since December 2023, Reuters reported this morning — and the morning’s call for a 10-year close below 4.98% landed. CME FedWatch odds of a December hike were reported at about 72–73%, from about 80–83% on Thursday (ActionForex, GuruFocus); the Fed holds at 3.75–4.00% after Wednesday’s two dissents in favour of a hike.

What’s next. The October jobs report on Friday, Nov. 6, and October CPI on Nov. 10 are the last two big readings before the Dec. 9 decision. Our call: the 10-year par yield is below 5.00% at the close on Nov. 6 (65–80%), given it is 6 basis points below that level with a softer labour market and a risk-off tape; a hot jobs report is the main risk to it.

BLS Employment Cost Index release, Oct 30 (primary); US Treasury daily par yield curve, Oct 30; FRED; Reuters; Haver Analytics via Reuters; CME FedWatch via ActionForex and GuruFocus; Investing.com and FX.co (Chicago PMI).

Oil ends October with a fourth monthly gain as expiring Brent settles near $118 and the US weighs options on Iran

A small boat attached explosives to a tanker in the strait, the UK maritime agency said. Iran’s claim that four tankers hit mines remains disputed.

December Brent, which expired today, settled at $117.64, up $2.76, and December WTI at $104.12, up $2.12, according to a single report; Yahoo Finance’s last WTI quote was $103.05 at 3:44 PM, and January Brent, the new benchmark from Monday, was $112.36. That gap is a contract roll, not a fall in prices. Reports put Brent’s October gain at about 14% and WTI’s at about 15%. Energy (+1.9%) was the best sector today and Exxon and Chevron rose 2.2% and 1.8% (Yahoo Finance), touching all-time highs during the session, CNBC reported.

In the Strait of Hormuz, the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said a tanker was hit by explosive devices attached by a small boat. Iran’s forces now say four tankers hit mines south of the strait on Thursday; US Central Command calls that false, and its commander, Adm. Brad Cooper, said in a video statement today that US forces have cleared Iranian mines from the main shipping lanes. No independent monitor has said what damaged the vessels Disputed. The Wall Street Journal reported that Cooper briefed President Trump on Thursday on options to resume strikes; officials say no decision has been made, and the White House has said there will be no attack before Tuesday’s election. A Qatari delegation arrived in Tehran today, Iranian state media reported; Iran has not formally answered Vice President Vance’s letter.

What’s next. OPEC+ meets Sunday on December output; one trade report says the group is preparing to stop raising its targets. Our call: OPEC+ announces no increase in its December production targets (55–70%), given that report and prices already above $100. Watch Monday’s January Brent quote, not the expired December one.

Binance says $5 billion of the roughly $6.4 billion stolen is now frozen; bitcoin touches a six-week low and crypto stocks fall

Three blockchain-analysis firms attribute the theft to North Korea. No government has confirmed it.

Binance said early today that about $3 billion had been frozen with partners’ help, including stablecoins frozen by Tether and Circle, and raised that to $5 billion in the afternoon, according to CNBC and TechCrunch. TRM Labs said an attacker moved about $6.36 billion out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains, most of it in about 20 minutes; Chainalysis put it at about $6.4 billion, or $5.9 billion at the time of the theft. TRM, Chainalysis and Elliptic attribute the attack to North Korea’s TraderTraitor group, which the FBI blamed for the $1.5 billion Bybit theft in February 2025. Binance says it is working with law enforcement to confirm the identity and that user losses will be covered; withdrawals were paused. If the total holds it is the largest crypto theft on record.

Bitcoin fell to about $76,400, a six-week low, and was near $76,800 at 4:40 PM, down 8% in October (Yahoo Finance). About $1.6 billion of leveraged crypto futures positions were liquidated, CoinDesk reported. Coinbase fell 10.6% and Robinhood 7.2%; the Grayscale Bitcoin Mini Trust fell 4.9%.

CNBC; TechCrunch; NPR; CBS News; TRM Labs, Chainalysis and Elliptic as reported; CoinDesk; Yahoo Finance.

A second appeals-court panel refuses to set aside 20,000 California ballots as early voting runs 10 million ahead of 2022

The Justice Department is expected to ask the Supreme Court. Three Los Angeles County election workers are due before a grand jury the day after the election.

A divided Ninth Circuit panel today again rejected the Justice Department’s request that California set aside the ballots of about 20,000 voters that federal agencies flagged as likely noncitizens, according to NBC News and Democracy Docket, which called it the administration’s third straight loss on the question. A panel turned down an earlier version on Monday, 2–1, calling the emergency “at least in part, a self-constructed one,” and District Judge David O. Carter has also refused. Reports say the department is expected to go to the Supreme Court; we found no filing as of this afternoon. The Los Angeles County registrar has confirmed that three employees received grand jury subpoenas to testify on Nov. 4 in an investigation that cites Homeland Security Investigations; no one has been charged, and the registrar says his staff are acting within state and federal law.

Turnout is running high. An Associated Press analysis found nearly 10 million more Americans had voted by five days before the election than at the same point in 2022, with the largest increases in states with competitive Senate races; early voting is up about 30% in Iowa and Kansas. President Trump, who had switched Sunday events in those states to telephone rallies, will now hold them in person, AP and CBS reported. Polling averages of the generic congressional ballot show Democrats ahead by about 8 points (Decision Desk HQ, D+8.1). Polls are not predictions.

NBC News; Democracy Docket; CNN; ABC News; NPR; Associated Press; CBS News; Decision Desk HQ.

What Moved Markets

Before the open. Nasdaq 100 futures were down about 2.4% on Intel and Amazon, and Asian chip stocks had already fallen hard (the Kospi lost 3.5%). Chevron and Exxon beat estimates; AbbVie and Colgate reported strong quarters at about 7:50 AM.

8:30 AM and the open. The wage report matched forecasts and Treasury yields edged lower. Stocks opened with the S&P 500 down about 1.2% and the Nasdaq about 1.4%, while the Dow was nearly flat (TheStreet). Chicago’s PMI at 9:45 came in a little soft.

Midday. The selling deepened. By late morning Intel was down about 28–30%, the S&P 500 down more than 2% at its low and the Nasdaq 100 down more than 3%; the Dow turned lower. Defensive sectors — staples and health care — were higher throughout, as was energy (Benzinga, AP).

Afternoon and close. Stocks recovered part of the loss into the close, led by Amazon, which pared a 7% premarket loss to 1.4%. The VIX closed at 19.42, up 2.22, and the dollar index was little changed at 101.90.

The Economy

Today’s data. Employment Cost Index +0.8% q/q (consensus +0.8%, prior +0.9%); wages +0.7%, benefits +0.9%; 3.4% a year, with wages 3.1% and real wages −0.6% (BLS). Chicago PMI 55.3 (forecast about 55.8–56.1, prior 58.8). The University of Michigan’s final October sentiment reading was published on Oct. 23 (48.0), not today. Nominal versus real is the story of the ECI: pay is growing more slowly than prices, which squeezes households and argues against a wage-led inflation spiral at the same time.

The Fed. No Fed officials spoke today, and the post-meeting blackout is over. With the funds rate at 3.75–4.00%, futures put the chance of a December hike at about 72%, down about 10 points in a day, as reported. The case for a hike is inflation still accelerating (headline PCE 3.6%, core 3.1%); the case against is payroll growth of 29,000 in September, unemployment at 4.2%, falling real wages and now falling stock prices.

Treasury. The quarterly refunding begins next week: borrowing estimates are expected Monday afternoon, following the usual pattern (the date is our inference from past practice), and the refunding statement, which sets coupon auction sizes for the coming quarter, is Wednesday, Nov. 4 (Treasury). The rally helps the Treasury’s interest bill at the margin: the 10-year is 35 basis points below where it started the month. SOFR was 3.94% on Thursday.

Rates & credit. The curve steepened slightly between 2 and 10 years, to 38 basis points; 3-month to 10-year is 78. The 10-year’s breakeven inflation rate was 2.35% on Thursday and its real yield 2.63% (FRED). The high-yield spread widened to 3.26 percentage points on Thursday from 3.14, its first notable widening this month; HYG fell 0.2% today while investment-grade LQD was flat.

Global. The Bank of Japan held at 1.25% this morning by 8–1 and judged underlying inflation broadly in line with its 2% target; the yen closed little changed at 157.5 per dollar. Europe’s Stoxx 600 fell 0.4% to 646.75 but had its best month since June; European technology stocks fell 2.75%, their worst day since July (Irish Times). Germany’s DAX fell 0.9% and London’s FTSE 100 was flat (Yahoo Finance). China’s official October PMIs come out Saturday morning Beijing time; September’s manufacturing index was 50.1.

AI & Technology

The cost of compute, in the market. Today’s chip sell-off and Thursday’s Alphabet collapse are covered in Top Stories. The week leaves a clear ranking: investors will pay for AI spending funded from operating cash (Microsoft) or showing fast cloud growth (Amazon), and are cutting exposure to suppliers whose order books depend on that spending staying on its current path.

Anthropic’s IPO filing. Anthropic’s S-1, filed Oct. 26 for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT, has not yet set a share count or price range. CNBC reports the filing shows Anthropic will pay SpaceX about $3.1 billion a month for Nvidia-GPU compute once all contracted capacity is delivered. Reports of a mid-November listing date appear only in aggregators and are not confirmed.

Nvidia and Meta. Nvidia is investing $3 billion in Meta’s equity offering as part of an expanded partnership on Meta’s AI agents, with AMD adding $1 billion, CNBC and Reuters reported this week; reports differ on whether Meta’s total raise is $95 billion or $97.5 billion.

Cyber. The Binance theft (Top Stories) is the week’s largest security incident. Binance traced it to a compromised laptop belonging to one engineer — an operational-security failure, not a cryptographic one.

Science

Dengue returns even after vaccination. A human challenge study in Science Translational Medicine, a peer-reviewed journal, found that dengue virus can re-emerge in vaccinated volunteers, as described in an accompanying Nature news piece. We could not read the paper’s numbers, so we report only its finding as described; challenge studies are small by design, and the result bears on how long vaccine protection lasts rather than whether vaccines work.

Light, sweeteners and the ageing brain. Two large observational studies publicised this week link heavy artificial-sweetener use to faster memory decline (about 13,000 adults) and more daytime light exposure to a 16% lower dementia risk (about 87,000 adults), according to ScienceDaily summaries. Both are associations, not proof of cause, and we could not confirm the journals.

Space

India’s navigation satellite. ISRO scheduled its GSLV-F18 rocket to launch the NVS-03 navigation satellite from Sriharikota at 11:51 PM India time (2:21 PM ET) today, aiming to restore its NavIC regional positioning system after NVS-02 failed to reach its working orbit in January 2025; ISRO now opens the satellite’s propulsion valves before launch, removing the step that failed last time. We had not seen a confirmed result at press time.

Starship and Artemis. SpaceX completed full-duration static fires of the Flight 15 booster on Oct. 25 and the ship on Oct. 27, Space.com reported; Elon Musk has said the flight will come before Thanksgiving. NASA stacked the Artemis III core stage with its solid rocket boosters this week; the agency’s plan keeps Artemis III as a mid-2027 Earth-orbit docking test with the SpaceX and Blue Origin landers, with a crewed landing in 2028.

Local

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Mayor O’Connor signs the first phase of Pittsburgh’s zoning overhaul, ending parking minimums

Mayor Corey O’Connor on Thursday signed two zoning ordinances that City Council passed this week, WPXI reported. The first adds incentives for affordable housing, eliminates minimum off-street parking requirements and allows accessory dwelling units such as backyard cottages and basement apartments; the second modernises the zoning code. They are the first phase of the housing executive order he issued on taking office. Ending parking minimums lets builders decide how much parking a project needs, which supporters say lowers the cost of new housing; critics in some neighbourhoods worry about on-street parking.

WPXI; City of Pittsburgh mayoral schedule.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

County voters decide Tuesday whether council members may spend county money on staff and offices

A ballot question asks whether to repeal the Home Rule Charter’s ban on county funds for council members’ personal staff, district offices and benefits. Its sponsor, Councilwoman Bethany Hallam, says passage would free about $800,000; the county’s Government Review Commission unanimously recommends a no vote, as do the county Republican Party and Councilman DeWitt Walton. A yes vote would not itself appropriate any money (WESA, Axios Pittsburgh).

WESA; Axios Pittsburgh; Allegheny County Elections Division.

Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania’s measles outbreak reaches 1,398 cases and five deaths, the state reports

As of Oct. 28 the state Department of Health reports 1,398 cases, 265 hospitalisations and five deaths, and is investigating a further death in Franklin County, Reuters reported. CNN calls it the largest and deadliest US outbreak in more than 30 years. The CDC’s national dashboard shows three measles deaths nationwide this year, a gap between state and federal counts that has not been explained; each figure here is attributed to the agency that published it.

Reuters via US News; CNN; Pennsylvania Department of Health as reported.
