Top Stories

SpaceX’s spectrum buy knocks a tenth off the big three wireless carriers

Investors treated a roughly $8 billion licence purchase as the moment satellite phone service became a direct competitor. T-Mobile fell 13%, AT&T and Verizon about 10%, and tower owners rallied.

SpaceX said Thursday evening it will buy Grain Management’s nationwide 800 MHz portfolio: up to 14 MHz of paired low-band spectrum across 3,224 counties. It will use the airwaves for Starlink Mobile, its phone-to-satellite service. Low-band signals travel far and get through walls, which is what a satellite network needs to reach phones indoors.

  • Price: not disclosed. The Wall Street Journal and Reuters report about $8 billion in cash.
  • Approval: the deal needs the FCC. Chairman Brendan Carr called it “really good news” for consumers.
  • Provenance: Grain bought these licences from T-Mobile in August for $2.9 billion plus 600 MHz spectrum, and held them for about two months.
  • Context: the FCC reportedly cleared SpaceX’s own direct-to-cell constellation of up to about 15,000 satellites this week.

How the carriers closed (Yahoo):

  • T-Mobile: −13.3%, to $148.58, on about 4 times normal volume
  • AT&T: −10.8%, to $22.18, on about 3 times normal volume
  • Verizon: −10.1%, to $41.65, on about 4 times normal volume

CNBC has slightly smaller falls for AT&T and Verizon in its own figures. Either way, it calls this the worst day for Verizon since July 2002, for AT&T since 2000 and for T-Mobile since 2013. The communication-services sector fund (XLC) lost 1.5%, the only sector fund down more than 0.3%. AST SpaceMobile, which builds satellite phone service with the carriers’ own spectrum, fell 10.5% to $50.97, close to its 52-week low. SpaceX (SPCX) rose 1.3% to $162.57, on the day its reported post-IPO lockup expired.

Market narrative Tower owners rose sharply: Crown Castle +15.6%, American Tower +9.3%, SBA +7.3%. One market wrap ties the rise to the spectrum news, but we found no explanation of why a satellite competitor helps companies that rent tower space to the carriers. We cannot confirm the cause.

Why it matters

The carriers’ valuations rest on owning scarce spectrum and the only networks that reach every phone. A satellite operator with its own nationwide low-band licences, and permission to sell directly, challenges both. By our arithmetic from share counts, roughly $60–70 billion of market value came off the three companies on the strength of one licence purchase. Satellite capacity per user is far below a ground network’s, so the near-term threat is to rural and coverage-gap customers, not city users. Whether today was an overreaction depends on what SpaceX charges and how much traffic it can actually carry.

What’s next

The FCC review, and the carriers’ third-quarter calls later this month, where they will be asked about it. Verizon and AT&T’s dividend yields have jumped with the drop. Investors will watch for any change in buyback or spectrum-auction plans.

Sources: SpaceX and Grain announcements via Via Satellite and Fierce Network; Wall Street Journal and Reuters (price, reported); CNBC; NBC News; Forbes; Yahoo Finance (prices).

Delta cuts its year by a fifth on fuel, and the stock ends flat

The first big airline report of the energy shock missed its own range, but the shares recovered a 4–5% early drop as management said demand is still strong.

Delta’s 8-K, filed this morning, shows third-quarter adjusted earnings of $1.72 a share. That was below the $2.00–2.50 range it gave in July and below analyst estimates, which vary by survey from about $1.75 to $1.96. Fuel was the reason:

  • Adjusted fuel expense rose 62% from a year ago, to about $4.1 billion
  • The company is absorbing roughly $6 billion of extra fuel cost this year
  • Fourth-quarter guidance assumes about $4.25 a gallon
  • Full-year EPS is now $5.10–5.60, cut from $6.50–7.50; free cash flow about $2.5 billion
  • Revenue was about $17.6 billion

The shares traded 4–5% lower before the open. They closed at $82.17, up 3 cents, on almost twice normal volume. United finished flat, American −0.3% and Southwest +0.7%. Likely contributor Chief executive Ed Bastian told CNBC that demand remains strong, and the cut was close to what fuel futures already implied. Investors appeared to treat fuel as a cost that will pass, not a demand problem.

Why it matters

This is the first major earnings report to show the cost of $100 oil. Airlines can pass fuel into fares only with a lag, so margins carry the shock first. The stock’s recovery says investors were already braced for this. Bank earnings next week will show whether consumers are still spending through higher fuel prices.

Sources: Delta Air Lines 8-K (SEC EDGAR) and press release; CNBC; Quartz; Benzinga; Yahoo Finance (prices).

Humana jumps 12% on its star ratings, and Alignment falls 13%

Medicare’s 2027 quality scores moved billions of dollars of bonus payments between insurers. The money arrives in 2028.

CMS posted its 2027 Medicare Advantage star ratings on Thursday. Humana said in an 8-K that 95% of its members will be in plans rated four stars or higher, up from 20% this year. JPMorgan analysts had expected 60–70%. Four-star plans earn the bonus payments that fund richer benefits; Evercore ISI estimates Humana’s at about $4.8 billion in 2028.

  • CVS: −1.9%

Health care (XLV +1.6%) was the second-best sector of the day.

Sources: Humana 8-K and release (Business Wire); Reuters; CNBC; Evercore ISI and JPMorgan via news reports; Yahoo Finance (prices).

Isaias strengthens to a Category 3 and closes 71% of Gulf oil output

Landfall is expected between Pensacola and Destin between 9 PM and midnight Eastern. Oil barely reacted.

The National Hurricane Center’s 11 AM ET advisory put Isaias at 120 mph with a central pressure of 959 mb. A Hurricane Hunter aircraft confirmed the pressure at midday. At 2 PM ET it was about 145 miles south of Pensacola, moving north-northeast at 16 mph. Warnings in force:

  • Hurricane warning: Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Bay/Gulf County line in Florida
  • Storm-surge warning: the mouth of the Mississippi River to the Suwannee River
  • Surge: local forecasters say 6–9 feet is possible around Pensacola Beach

Florida, Alabama and Georgia have declared states of emergency, and Okaloosa and Escambia counties have ordered evacuations of their lowest zones. We found no federal emergency declaration yet.

Offshore, Reuters reports that 71.5% of Gulf of Mexico oil production and 58.8% of gas production were shut in on Friday, up from 62.9% of oil on Thursday. Crews have left 129 of 371 staffed platforms. Oil hardly moved on it: Brent ended the day roughly flat near $104. Offshore output usually returns within days of a storm passing, unless platforms are damaged. The larger risk to fuel prices is refineries and ports from Mobile to Pensacola.

What’s next

Landfall tonight, then damage reports Saturday. The offshore regulator’s daily shut-in figures will show how fast output returns. Isaias’s remnants are forecast to bring heavy rain to western Pennsylvania on Sunday (see Local).

Sources: National Hurricane Center (via ABC, CBS, CNN, NPR, Fox Weather); Reuters via BOE Report (shut-ins); WEAR and WKRG (evacuations).

OpenAI says $70 billion by year-end, and the chip bounce fades

A day after a report put its revenue run-rate near $50 billion, OpenAI told investors it expects at least $70 billion by the end of 2026.

Bloomberg reported Friday that OpenAI expects annualized revenue of at least $70 billion by the end of 2026. On Thursday the Financial Times reported, and CNBC confirmed, that the company told investors it was running at about $50 billion at the end of September. That is well short of the roughly $68 billion that had circulated. CNBC’s source said the difference is mostly accounting: the higher number counted cloud partners’ gross sales. The investor materials reportedly cite 77% run-rate growth in the quarter. None of these figures is audited, and OpenAI has not commented on the record.

CNBC reported heavy put buying in Micron and Nvidia. Money went instead to the rest of big tech: Amazon +3.3%, Microsoft +2.4% and Tesla +2.1%.

Sources: Bloomberg; Financial Times; CNBC; Quartz; Yahoo Finance (prices).

Consumer sentiment slides toward its record low as inflation expectations rise

The early-October Michigan survey was reported at 46.3, below forecasts. Households expect 4.7% inflation over the next year.

Figures as reported by Trading Economics and Yahoo Finance (we could not open the University of Michigan release itself):

  • Sentiment: 46.3, against a consensus of 47.6 and September’s 48.1. The record low is 44.8, set in May.
  • Current conditions: 44.7, down from 50.9
  • Expectations: 47.3, up from 46.3
  • Year-ahead inflation expectations: 4.7%, from 4.6%
  • Five-to-ten-year expectations: 3.5%, from 3.4%

The current-conditions collapse fits with gasoline and diesel prices that rose through the survey window. The longer-run expectations figure is the one the Fed watches most. At 3.5% it supports the officials, Waller and Musalem among them this week, who say more tightening will be needed. Futures moved little: CME FedWatch put the odds of an October 28 hike at about 18% on Thursday, with roughly 85% odds of at least one hike by December. The 2-year yield rose 5bp.

Tehran’s answer to Washington is still pending. The attacks on Gulf shipping are spreading beyond Hormuz.

Pennsylvania’s Supreme Court refuses to delay the skill-game ban

The machines must be removed by Tuesday. State Police say seizures can begin Wednesday.

In one-sentence orders on Friday, the court denied two requests:

  • Lawmakers’ request to extend its stay to February 10, 2027. Senators Yaw and Williams and Representative Fritz filed it, withdrew it, then filed it again this week.
  • An emergency petition from VFW and American Legion posts, clubs and fire companies

The Attorney General’s office opposed both, saying they came too late and that the lawmakers lack standing. Machine maker Pace-O-Matic supported them. The October 13 deadline stands, and State Police say enforcement can begin October 14. The House and Senate have each passed a bill to regulate the machines but have not agreed on one.

Sources: WJAC; Local21 News (WHP); Pennsylvania State Police.

Navi Pillay’s Nobel Peace Prize draws praise from the UN and condemnation from Israel

The 85-year-old South African jurist, a former UN human rights chief and international criminal judge, won the prize for work to ensure war crimes and genocide are prosecuted. She dedicated it to “survivors of international crimes.” Reactions:

  • Praise from UN Secretary-General Guterres, UN rights chief Türk and South Africa’s President Ramaphosa
  • Israel’s Foreign Ministry called it a “grotesque weaponisation.” Pillay chaired the UN commission that found Israel committed genocide in Gaza, a finding Israel rejects.
  • President Trump did not congratulate her and reposted a message saying the committee had “shamed itself”

The economics prize is announced Monday at 5:45 AM ET.

Sources: Al Jazeera; CNN; Time; NBC News; Euronews; nobelprize.org.

Science

A mouse whose cortex is largely human tissue

A peer-reviewed paper in this week’s Nature describes a “xenocortical” mouse. Most of its own cortex and hippocampus was genetically removed and replaced by a graft of a human cortical organoid, which formed circuits wired into the host brain. It is a research model for studying human brain development and disease in a living animal, not a cognitive enhancement. It will also sharpen the ethical debate over human–animal brain chimeras. Replication and behavioural results are the next steps.

Source: Nature vol. 658, issue 8135 (October 8).

A phase 3 trial of a pill for hereditary angioedema attacks

The Lancet published a randomized phase 3 trial of oral deucrictibant to treat attacks of hereditary angioedema, a rare genetic disorder that causes sudden, sometimes life-threatening swelling. Most on-demand treatments today are injections. We have seen only the listing, not the effect sizes. Separately, NASA released the full Artemis II lunar science dataset, more than 800 GB.

Sources: The Lancet Online First; NASA.

Space

Starlink Mobile is now a spectrum owner

The Grain deal (Top Stories) gives SpaceX its own nationwide low-band licences for phone-to-satellite service. It needs FCC approval. Together with the FCC’s reported approval this week of up to about 15,000 direct-to-cell satellites, and a waiver letting SpaceX sell service without a carrier partner, SpaceX now has the pieces to compete as a mobile operator. It still has to show the capacity. Satellite cells are large, so per-user throughput in dense areas stays well below a ground network’s.
