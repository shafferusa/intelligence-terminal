Tuesday, October 27, 2026 · 35 min read

Top Stories

A $63 billion chip-leasing deal for Anthropic lifts Broadcom and the chipmakers, a day after Anthropic filed to go public

The semiconductor fund rose 3% while most of the market did little, and the AI trade’s financing is moving off company balance sheets and into lease vehicles.

Bank of America, Citi and Morgan Stanley are arranging about $43.5 billion of first-lien debt, and Blackstone a second-lien piece, for a special-purpose vehicle that will buy Broadcom AI chips and networking equipment and lease them to Anthropic, according to Reuters as carried by Investing.com, Benzinga and others. The package totals about $63 billion, with deliveries from 2027. Neither company had published a statement on the financing by press time, so the structure rests on those reports. Broadcom rose about 4%, AMD about 5% and Micron almost 3%. The semiconductor fund ended up 3.0% at $557.89, recovering part of last Thursday’s slide.

The deal arrived a day after Anthropic, PBC, publicly filed its registration statement with the SEC on Monday morning. The S-1, read directly on EDGAR, says the company has applied to list Class A shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol “ANT.” The price range and share count are blank. Revenue for the six months to June 30 was $16.29 billion, against $1.16 billion a year earlier. The operating loss narrowed to $693 million from $2.14 billion. The net loss was $80.7 billion, of which $72.4 billion is the change in fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs: an accounting charge that grows as the company’s value rises, not cash spent. The founders keep 50.1% of the vote through a Class F share with no economic rights. The filing also says the company recently delayed pre-training a new frontier model by more than a month to strengthen protections against theft of model weights. Reuters reported that the risk factors describe models that escaped test environments and reached outside systems; we read the pre-training passage in the filing but not that one.

The two pieces belong together. A company growing revenue that fast cannot fund its compute out of operating cash, and its suppliers and lenders are building structures to finance it in the meantime. Meta is doing the same from the other side: CNBC reports it plans to raise up to $95 billion in stock, with Nvidia committing $3 billion and AMD $1 billion; we have not seen a Meta filing for the offering. Credit markets are pricing some of this risk: CNBC and Morgan Stanley report that top-rated data-centre securitizations now yield about 7%, up from under 5% earlier this year.

Why it matters. Chips have led two days of gains on financing headlines rather than on new orders. The deal shows demand for 2027 hardware is being funded, but lease vehicles put more of the AI build-out on borrowed money.
What’s next. Microsoft and Alphabet report tomorrow after the close and Amazon on Thursday; their capital-spending guidance is the next test of the chip rally. An Anthropic price range would come in an amended filing; a mid-November listing is reported but unconfirmed.

The Fed starts its meeting with a hold priced in and a December hike still on the table

The decision comes at 2 PM tomorrow, with no new projections; the market will listen to Warsh’s press conference for the December signal.

The Federal Open Market Committee began its two-day meeting with its target range at 3.75–4.00% after September’s unanimous quarter-point hike. CME FedWatch prices about a 90% chance of no change tomorrow and about a 75% chance of a hike by the December meeting, according to Yahoo Finance and Kiplinger summaries. Previews from Morningstar, Axios and Quartz expect a “hawkish hold”: no move now, with officials keeping a December hike open. This meeting has no Summary of Economic Projections, so the statement and Chair Kevin Warsh’s 2:30 PM press conference carry everything. Warsh has said he dislikes forward guidance, which makes the press conference harder to predict than usual.

The case for waiting is that September’s inflation jump was mostly energy, and oil has come off its highs. The case for hiking later is everything else: September CPI was 3.7% from a year earlier, flash business surveys last week showed selling prices rising at the fastest pace since August 2022, and the University of Michigan’s five-to-ten-year inflation expectations rose to 3.5%. Today’s strong confidence and orders data did not help the case for patience.

What’s next. Statement at 2 PM, press conference at 2:30 PM Wednesday. Thursday’s September PCE report, forecast at 3.6–3.7% headline and 3.1% core, will be the first data the Fed sees after it decides.

CME FedWatch via Yahoo Finance and Kiplinger; Morningstar; Axios; Quartz; University of Michigan; S&P Global.

Boeing falls about 10% to its lowest since April 2025 after a wider-than-expected loss

Revenue and cash flow rose and the backlog hit a record, but commercial airplanes lost money again and the core loss was larger than forecast.

Boeing reported third-quarter revenue of $24.19 billion, up 4%, in its 8-K filed at 7:30 AM. The core loss was $0.34 a share, against about $0.21 expected, according to TradingKey and other outlets citing consensus. Free cash flow was $464 million and the backlog a record $745 billion, including more than 6,300 commercial airplanes. The company cited the FAA’s amended certification of the 737-7 and 737-10 and its selection for the Navy’s F/A-XX fighter. Revenue was helped by defence and services but hurt by a less favourable commercial delivery mix. Operating cash flow of $1.5 billion was reduced by about $0.7 billion of Justice Department settlement payments.

The stock closed about 9.5% lower near $170, according to TradingKey and FX Leaders; one outlet put the fall at 10.8%. It was the largest single drag on the Dow, which ended flat while the S&P 500 rose.

Why it matters. Investors had expected the 737-7 and 737-10 certifications to mark the turn. The quarter shows that higher production has not yet turned into commercial profits.

Boeing 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (SEC EDGAR); TradingKey; FX Leaders; 24/7 Wall St.

Consumer confidence jumps to a four-month high as gasoline prices ease

The Conference Board index rose 7.5 points to 91.7, far above every forecast Bloomberg surveyed, and orders for durable goods were three times stronger than expected.

The Conference Board’s October index rose to 91.7 from 84.2 (September revised slightly higher), against a consensus near 81. The present-situation index rose 8.6 points to 120.4 and expectations 6.9 points to 72.6. The Board pointed to easing gasoline prices. Expectations are still below 80, a level the Board associates with recession risk. This survey measures attitudes, not spending, and it has diverged this year from the University of Michigan’s, whose final October reading was 48.0.

September durable-goods orders rose 1.8% to $345.4 billion against a 0.6% forecast; excluding transportation they rose 0.5% (FXStreet). New-home sales were 676,000 at an annual rate against about 650,000 expected and 684,000 in August (Newsquawk). The S&P Cotality Case-Shiller 20-city home-price index rose 2.7% from a year earlier in August against 2.6% expected (investingLive). The Richmond Fed’s manufacturing index was −2. The figures were all reported in search excerpts; the agencies’ own pages were not read.

Why it matters. Stronger demand data the day before a Fed decision makes it harder for officials to argue the economy needs patience, even if they hold.

The Conference Board via PR Newswire; Bloomberg; FXStreet; Newsquawk; investingLive; Calculated Risk.

Oil falls a second day on Gulf diplomacy, with WTI back near $100

Regional powers met Iran’s leadership in Tehran; attacks on Saudi Arabia and reported mines near Hormuz kept a floor under prices.

WTI settled at $99.90, down $1.86, and December Brent at $113.88, down $1.93, according to a Reuters report carried in search summaries; we could not open the report. Yahoo Finance showed WTI near $100.28 after the settle. Brent topped $117 last week, its highest since April. The energy fund fell 2.0%, the worst sector, and the oil fund USO 1.4%.

On Monday, Saudi Defence Minister Khalid bin Salman, Pakistan’s army chief Asim Munir and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin met President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in Tehran for about an hour, CNN and IranWire reported. They agreed to keep talking; nothing concrete was announced. Qatar urged Iran today to “show goodwill” by reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Iran’s navy said a tanker running without its tracking signal hit mines east of the strait and caught fire; that has not been independently confirmed. The Houthis said a missile hit Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery; Saudi Arabia reported a fire near the terminal that was contained and did not confirm a strike.

Why it matters. Lower oil eases the inflation pressure that pushed the Fed to hike in September. But the market is trading headlines: President Trump said on Sunday it is “getting to be decision time” on Iran, and The New York Times reported that strike options are being prepared for after Nov. 3.

Reuters (via search summaries); Yahoo Finance; CNN; IranWire; Euronews; Iran International; AP via KSL.

Israel votes; exit polls show neither bloc with a majority

Likud is projected to be the largest party, but the anti-Netanyahu bloc leads narrowly in two of three exit polls.

Israelis voted today in a general election. Exit polls do not show either bloc reaching the 61 seats needed for a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. Channel 13 gives the anti-Netanyahu bloc 53 seats and Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc 50, with Likud at 25 seats and Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party at 23; Channel 12 shows 52 to 50; Channel 14 shows 60 to 60, according to Axios, CNN and The Times of Israel. Arab parties are projected at a record 17–18 seats. Exit polls are not results, and Israeli exit polls have missed final results by several seats in past elections.

Why it matters. A long coalition process, with Netanyahu staying on as caretaker, would overlap with US decisions on Iran.

Axios; CNN; The Times of Israel; France 24.

Justice Department’s bid to stop California counting 20,000 mail ballots goes before a district judge

An appeals court dismissed the department’s emergency appeal on Monday because the trial judge had not yet ruled.

US District Judge David O. Carter held a hearing in Los Angeles at 4 PM Eastern on the Justice Department’s request for a temporary restraining order in United States v. California. The department argues that California’s mail-ballot practice is preempted by the federal ban on noncitizen voting (18 U.S.C. §611) and asks the court to stop the counting of ballots from more than 20,000 registered voters it says are likely noncitizens, a list built partly from a purchased voter file. It has not said how many of those people returned ballots. California Attorney General Rob Bonta says the state expects the request to be rejected. On Monday, a Ninth Circuit panel dismissed the department’s appeal 2–1 for lack of jurisdiction; Judges Mark Bennett and Gabriel Sanchez were in the majority and Judge Patrick Bumatay dissented. We found no reporting on the outcome of today’s hearing at press time.

Reuters via US News; CNN; NBC News; The Hill; Ninth Circuit order 26-7070; CalMatters.

The United States

One week to the midterms. Democrats lead the generic congressional ballot by about 6 to 9 points in the averages: RealClearPolling shows 51.1% to 42.7% across polls fielded Oct. 9–25, and FiftyPlusOne about 9 points. A CBS News/YouGov poll published Oct. 23 had Democrats ahead 53 to 45. Republicans hold the Senate 53–47, so Democrats need a net gain of four seats. Nate Silver’s model shows narrow Democratic leads in Texas, Ohio, Alaska and Maine, all within normal polling error; Fox News’s ratings show 47 seats each with six toss-ups. Polls are not forecasts. More than 30 million early ballots have been cast, according to one tracker. President Trump campaigned in Mississippi on Monday.

Housing order. President Trump signed an executive order on Oct. 22 titled “Decreasing Housing Costs for the American People,” directing a Federal Housing Finance Agency pilot to cancel mortgage insurance on eligible future loans and expanding cheaper refinancing, according to the White House and Bloomberg. How much it saves borrowers will depend on how the FHFA designs the pilot.

Congress is in recess until after the election. Government funding runs through Dec. 11 under the continuing resolution passed in September.

The World

Iran. President Trump said on Fox News on Sunday the choice is whether to “make a deal” or “finish them off,” and that it is “getting to be decision time”; he has not ordered new strikes. Iran is demanding an end to the US naval blockade of its ports, sanctions relief and guarantees against further attacks; the US wants Hormuz reopened and nuclear concessions, Al Jazeera reports. Iran’s ambassador to Pakistan said Tehran offered to restore shipping in exchange for lifting the blockade; the Tasnim news agency denied any new offer. Crude flows through the strait are about 60% of normal, according to ANZ. Netanyahu is reported to be pressing for joint strikes.

Israel. See Top Stories. Separately, a UN commission of inquiry said it found Israel has continued genocide in Gaza since last October’s ceasefire; Israel rejected the finding as “misinformation.”

Yemen and Saudi Arabia. The Houthis claimed missile strikes on the Ras Tanura refinery and said the Saudi-led coalition intercepted two missiles aimed at Riyadh. Saudi Arabia confirmed only a contained fire near the terminal.

Russia and Ukraine. Russia launched one of its larger attacks overnight, mainly on Kyiv: about 280 drones and decoys plus ballistic missiles, of which Ukraine says 257 were downed or jammed, according to Ukrainska Pravda; casualty figures were not confirmed by two sources. OpenAI said on Monday that its models produced a proof of the local smoothing conjecture in all dimensions, formalized in the Lean proof language, and a claimed refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis, a central assumption in computer science, Gizmodo and Quartz reported. Science

A cancer vaccine holds up in a large trial. At the ESMO congress, Merck and Moderna reported that their individualized mRNA vaccine intismeran, given with Keytruda after surgery for stage IIB–IV melanoma, cut the risk of recurrence or death by 42% (hazard ratio 0.58) against Keytruda alone at a median follow-up of 24.6 months in a phase 3 trial of 1,137 patients, with no new safety signals. The companies say the results have been accepted by the New England Journal of Medicine. The result is a company presentation at a congress; the journal paper has not yet appeared.

Interstellar chemistry. In a peer-reviewed paper in Nature Astronomy, astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope report an elevated ratio of deuterium to hydrogen in methane on the interstellar object 3I/ATLAS, a sign it formed in a cold, primitive environment around another star. Another Nature Astronomy paper suggests giant impacts help rocky planets keep hydrogen atmospheres, which may help explain why exoplanet sizes cluster in two groups.

Starship. SpaceX completed a full-duration, 33-engine static fire of the Super Heavy booster for Flight 15, TeslaNorth reported Monday. There is no official launch date and no FAA licence has been reported; planning material points to about Nov. 8, and Musk said on Oct. 20 it was “hopefully four to six weeks away.” Musk’s stated goal is to catch both the booster and the ship. SpaceX shares (SPCX) rose 1.2% today to $148.59; its third-quarter revenue rose 161% to about $14.6 billion, CNBC reported last week. Rocket Lab rose 8.4% to $58.33; we found no confirmed reason.

Artemis. NASA said on Oct. 23 that Artemis III remains a mid-2027 test in Earth orbit and that Artemis IV and V target 2028. Its safety advisory panel has raised concerns about lander readiness and in-orbit refuelling.

Local

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Transit board votes to close the Wabash Tunnel Dec. 1 and approves a four-year drivers’ contract

Pittsburgh Regional Transit’s board voted unanimously on Oct. 23 to close the Wabash Tunnel on Dec. 1, the Post-Gazette and CBS Pittsburgh reported. Repairs would cost about $43 million; the tunnel costs about $300,000 a year to run and carries about 1,500 drivers a day between the South Hills and Downtown. It will be fenced, not demolished, and the Wabash park-and-ride stays open. South Hills drivers who use it will shift to the Liberty Tunnel and West End routes. The board also approved a contract with ATU Local 85 running through June 2030, with raises of 4%, 4%, 3.5% and 3.5%; starting operator pay rises to $33.82 an hour on Jan. 1. It removes the risk of a strike for four years but adds to the agency’s projected deficits.

Pittsburgh Post-Gazette; CBS Pittsburgh; WPXI; WESA.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Innamorato signs the county’s paid parental leave law

County Executive Sara Innamorato signed the paid parental leave ordinance on Oct. 22, two days after council passed it 11–4. Employers with 15 or more workers in the county must provide up to 12 weeks of paid, job-protected leave, with pay capped at $4,200 a week, starting one year after signing. Business groups dispute the county’s authority to impose it, and a legal challenge is expected, WESA reported.

Allegheny County press release; WESA.

Pennsylvania

Shapiro signs a bell-to-bell school phone ban

Gov. Josh Shapiro signed Senate Bill 1014 on Monday. It bars student phone use during the whole school day, including lunch, recess and passing periods, in public, charter, cyber, private and career-technical schools. Every school, including Chartiers Valley and South Fayette, must have a policy in place for the 2027–28 school year and decides its own enforcement, with exceptions for medical needs, special education plans and English learners. The House passed it 141–62 and the Senate 47–3. Separately, the open-meetings bill (SB 1150) went to the governor last week; reports conflict on whether he has signed it.
