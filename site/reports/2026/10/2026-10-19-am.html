Tech futures are bouncing after last week’s AI-debt rout while Europe slips. China’s third-quarter growth beat forecasts. Fifth Third opened the week’s bank earnings with clean credit, ahead of Wednesday night’s Meta and Tesla reports.

The Brief

Top Stories

Tech futures bounce after the Nasdaq’s worst week since June

The rebound is led by the same chip and data-centre names that sold off on AI-debt worries. The test comes on Wednesday night, when Meta, Tesla, IBM and two chip-equipment makers report.

At 6:22 AM ET, Nasdaq 100 futures traded at 29,694.50, up 1.03% from Friday’s settlement. S&P 500 futures were at 7,740.25 (+0.47%), Russell 2000 futures up 0.40%, and Dow futures slightly lower at 52,178. Stocktwits’ 4 AM premarket rundown had Micron, SK Hynix, Broadcom, Qualcomm and TSMC up 2–5%. The same rundown said Nebius and CoreWeave, the “neocloud” data-centre landlords at the centre of last week’s credit scare, were bouncing too. CNBC’s overnight blog called it investors buying the dip in the AI trade. That describes the trading; it does not explain it. No new fact about AI financing came out over the weekend.

The bounce follows a bad week. The S&P 500 fell 1.95% to 7,659.44 and the Nasdaq Composite 4.48% to 26,140.03, the worst week for both since early June. The VanEck semiconductor fund fell nine sessions in a row. Friday’s breadth was worse than the index: across about 5,600 US stocks, decliners outnumbered advancers nearly two to one (3,608 to 1,870). Only about 29% of stocks with enough history sat above their 50-day trend.

Europe is not joining in. By 6:17 AM the Stoxx 600 was down 0.85%, the DAX 1.27% and the FTSE 100 0.97%. Futures are not a guaranteed open, and the gap between New York and Frankfurt is the first thing to watch at 9:30.

Why it matters Last week’s selloff was about who pays for the AI build-out, not about demand for chips. A Monday bounce in the chipmakers does not answer that question. Meta’s capex guidance does. The company’s last guide was $130–145 billion for the year, and it reports Wednesday after the close.
What’s next Netflix reports Tuesday after the close. Meta, Tesla, IBM, Lam Research and Texas Instruments report Wednesday night, the same day as a $13 billion 20-year bond auction. CNBC counts about 70 S&P 500 companies reporting this week.

China’s growth quickens to 4.5% as shoppers return but investment keeps shrinking

Third-quarter GDP beat the 4.4% poll and retail sales more than doubled expectations. Investment and property are still contracting, and Beijing is expected to leave lending rates alone on Tuesday.

China’s National Bureau of Statistics said the economy grew 4.5% from a year earlier in the third quarter, up from 4.3% in the second, which was the weakest in more than three years. Reuters had polled 4.4%. September industrial output rose 5.5%, as forecast, and retail sales rose 1.1% against a 0.6% forecast, the fastest since March. Fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% over the first nine months, and property investment fell 20.6%. Reuters and CNBC both carried the figures; one CNBC page address reads “4.6%”, but its headline and every other outlet say 4.5%.

Shanghai closed up 0.39% at 3,730.92 and the Kospi rose 1.76%, while the Nikkei fell 0.62% as Tokyo Electron slid. Hong Kong was shut for the Chung Yeung holiday, according to AFP. All 22 respondents in a Reuters poll expect the People’s Bank to hold its one-year loan prime rate at 3.00% and the five-year at 3.50% on Tuesday, which would be the 17th unchanged month.

Why it matters Copper is up about 0.9% this morning to $6.59 a pound, which fits a firmer read on Chinese demand. Shrinking investment means growth is coming from exports and consumers, not from building. That matters for iron ore, steel and the commodity exporters.

National Bureau of Statistics via Reuters/US News and CNBC · Reuters LPR poll · Yahoo Finance index closes.

Brent eases below $110 as Iran’s mined-tanker claim stays unconfirmed

The Revolutionary Guard says two loaded supertankers struck mines and burned. No ship has been named, and neither US Central Command nor the UK’s maritime agency has confirmed it. Meanwhile Tehran says its seven-day plan to reopen the strait is still on offer.

Brent crude for December was $108.71 a barrel at 6:22 AM, down 1.0% from Friday’s settlement of $109.86, its highest since May. Front-month WTI quotes range from about $97.50 to $100.40 depending on the source and contract month, because the November contract expires this week. Gold was little changed at $4,186 an ounce.

Unconfirmed Iran’s IRGC Navy said, via the Tasnim agency, that one supertanker hit a mine on Friday evening and a second on Saturday. It says each carried about two million barrels, sailed an “unauthorised route” and caught fire. Al Jazeera, Iran International and Middle East Monitor all report the claim. None names a vessel, and Euronews said CENTCOM and the UKMTO did not comment.

On the diplomatic track, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told an Organisation of Islamic Cooperation meeting in Baghdad on Sunday that Iran had sent Washington a seven-day plan through Qatar, according to CBS and Fox News. The strait would reopen on day six and talks would begin on day seven. The reported conditions are that the US lifts its blockade and oil sanctions and ends hostilities on every front, including Lebanon. He said Washington had replied and Tehran was studying the response. The US position is that the strait is already open. Separately, CNN reports Iranian commanders saying they are preparing for another round of fighting and naming undersea cables, AI data centres and regional energy sites as possible targets.

Kpler data cited by Reuters put flows through Hormuz at 11.62 million barrels a day in the week to Oct. 13, against a pre-war baseline of about 17.15 million.

Why it matters Oil above $100 is what turned last week’s inflation reports hot: import prices rose 8.1% from a year earlier. A route that carries about a third less oil than normal, plus an unverified claim that mines have been laid, keeps a risk premium in every barrel. Energy stocks were the best sector on Friday, and XLE closed within a few cents of its 52-week high.

Yahoo Finance futures (delayed) · Tasnim via Al Jazeera, Iran International and Middle East Monitor (claims) · CBS and Fox live coverage · CNN · Kpler via Reuters.

Fifth Third opens the week’s bank earnings with steady credit and a wider bond-portfolio loss

Adjusted earnings were $1.13 a share and net charge-offs 0.31%. The unrealised loss on its securities grew by $1.6 billion in a quarter when long yields rose.

Fifth Third Bancorp filed its third-quarter results with the SEC at 6:30 AM. It reported diluted earnings of $0.90 a share, or $1.13 excluding items it lists as one-offs. Net income available to common shareholders was $828 million, against $763 million in the second quarter. Net interest income rose 3% from the prior quarter to $2.28 billion, but the margin slipped to 3.34% from 3.36%. Net charge-offs were 0.31% of loans, and the nonperforming-asset ratio edged up to 0.64%. Core deposits grew 3%. Consensus estimates vary by source: Zacks had $1.09 and Benzinga $0.88, so “beat” depends on which you use. The adjusted figure is above both.

One line stands out given where rates went. Accumulated other comprehensive loss, the unrealised loss on the bank’s securities and hedges, widened to $4.92 billion from $3.29 billion at the end of June, and tangible book value per share fell to $22.01 from $23.22. Chief executive Tim Spence said in the release that “competition for deposits is heating up.” The bank says it has completed the conversion of Comerica’s systems.

What’s next Cleveland-Cliffs had guided to about $575 million of adjusted EBITDA; its results and 8:30 AM call had not appeared on EDGAR at press time. Steel Dynamics, Zions, Western Alliance, W.R. Berkley and Crown Holdings report after the close.

Anthropic’s slowing growth and debt-heavy chip financing keep AI credit in focus

Investors are reportedly marking down the private AI lab’s IPO valuation, and its default-swap spreads show CoreWeave still priced like a distressed borrower.

Reuters reported on Oct. 15 that Anthropic told prospective IPO investors third-quarter revenue was $16.8 billion. That is up 45% from $11.6 billion in the second quarter, and the company said it was profitable on an adjusted basis. The same report said investors are now discussing a valuation of about $1.5 trillion, against roughly $2 trillion expected for a November listing. Anthropic did not comment. Anthropic is private, so there is no share price; only reported valuations exist.

Secondhand An industry newsletter, citing the Financial Times, reports that Anthropic offered Blackstone a 10% coupon on $9 billion of junior debt within a $60 billion chip-financing package. We have not seen the FT report itself.

CoreWeave’s five-year default-swap spread was about 1,038 basis points in CNBC’s Oct. 15 reporting, up from 368 on Oct. 6. Other outlets cite an intraday peak near 1,195, and the figures differ between sources. The stock closed Friday at $61.39, just above its 52-week low. Oracle closed at $128.23, and the Motley Fool reports its own default-swap spread is near a record of about 300 basis points.

Why it matters The AI build-out is increasingly paid for with debt rather than cash flow. That is why a credit story moved equity indexes last week, and why a chip rally this morning does not settle it.

Reuters (via The Star) · CNBC · FourWeekMBA citing the FT (secondhand) · Motley Fool · Yahoo Finance closes.

Bessent calls September inflation an energy shock that will pass with the Iran war

On the Sunday shows the Treasury secretary said oil could fall before year-end. He also declined to call Ukraine an ally.

Scott Bessent said September’s inflation, 3.7% on the headline CPI against 0.3% month-on-month for core, reflects an “energy shock.” He said prices should ease on “the other side of the Iran conflict,” and oil could fall before the end of the year depending on the war, according to The Hill and Benzinga. On NBC’s Meet the Press he declined to describe Ukraine as a US ally and called President Zelensky’s remarks about Starlink “very reckless.”

Why it matters Treasury is making the case for looking through headline inflation one week before a Fed meeting where markets had briefly priced a 50% chance of a hike. Treasury does not set rates. What matters is whether core inflation stays near 0.3% a month while energy feeds into import prices.

The Hill · Benzinga · NBC News.

Ukraine’s drone strike sets St. Petersburg’s oil terminal ablaze, and Trump turns on Zelensky

One person was killed and about 20,000 lost power. The president wrote that Zelensky “doesn’t want to end this war, and probably never did.”

Overnight into Sunday, Ukrainian drones hit the Petersburg Oil Terminal and the Ruchyi fuel depot. Governor Alexander Beglov reported one dead, five injured (state media later said ten) and about 20,000 people without power. A VK data centre and an Ozon logistics hub were also hit. Russia’s Defense Ministry claims it downed 1,015 drones nationwide; that count is Moscow’s and cannot be independently checked. The Kyiv Independent, ABC News and TASS all reported the strike.

President Trump wrote on Truth Social that Zelensky “doesn’t want to end this war, and probably never did.” Zelensky said he had spoken with envoys Witkoff and Kushner. He said Russia had struck Ukraine’s energy system 76 times since the US-brokered energy ceasefire. He also said the US had resumed sharing ballistic-missile intelligence after a pause of several days.

Why it matters Hitting Baltic export capacity tightens refined-product markets while Hormuz is already restricted. A US–Ukraine rift lowers the odds of a negotiated pause in strikes on energy infrastructure.

Kyiv Independent · ABC News · TASS · CNN · Zelensky on X and NBC.

Before the Open

What futures price. S&P futures at 7,740 imply an open about 1% above Friday’s cash close of 7,659.44. The curve. Friday’s par curve: 3-month 4.26%, 2-year 4.74%, 5-year 4.89%, 10-year 5.09%, 20-year 5.47%, 30-year 5.43%. The 2s10s spread is 35 basis points, down from 44 a week earlier, and the 3-month/10-year is 83. The rally was led by the long end, which flattened the curve as hike fears faded. The 10-year real yield was 2.78% and the 10-year breakeven 2.35% on Oct. 15, so most of the fall in yields has been in real rates, not inflation expectations. The 20-year is the curve’s most expensive point to borrow, at 5.47%, and Wednesday’s reopening tests it.

Volatility. The VIX was 18.65 this morning, down from Friday’s 19.99 close. Friday’s term structure was nearly flat: 9-day 20.12, 30-day 19.99, 3-month 20.50. When the 9-day sits above the 30-day, investors are paying more for protection this week than for the month. That fits a week with this many big earnings reports.

Credit. High-yield spreads, per the ICE BofA index, were 3.23 percentage points on Oct. 15, wider than 3.11 on Oct. 12 but still tight by historical standards. The broad junk market has not followed CoreWeave. The stress is concentrated in the AI-infrastructure names and in private credit. Moody’s says counting payment-in-kind interest and maturity extensions lifts the middle-market CLO default rate to 5.8% from 4%.

The most fragile assumption. This morning’s bounce assumes last week was a positioning flush, not a repricing of AI funding. That reading fails if CoreWeave or Oracle credit widens again while the stocks rise, or if Meta trims capex guidance on Wednesday. Either would show the market cannot finance the build-out at last month’s prices. It would also be in trouble if Brent went back above $110 on a confirmed mine strike, which would reopen the inflation and hike argument the bond rally has just closed.

Science

A galaxy with no stars? Astronomers using China’s FAST radio telescope report a compact hydrogen cloud with no detectable stars. It is a candidate “dark galaxy,” held together by dark matter that never formed stars. It is one detection, as reported by Phys.org. Claims like this usually need deeper optical imaging before they hold.

Coral extinction risk. Research reported in Nature finds that nearly half of reef-building coral species face elevated extinction risk. A separate study in Nature Ecology & Evolution, from King’s College London, finds ecosystems have less functional “backup” than assumed when species are lost.

Medicine. A New England Journal of Medicine trial in African adults who struggled with daily pills found long-acting injectable HIV therapy matched oral treatment. Viral suppression at 48 weeks was 91.0% against 89.2%, a difference inside the margin of error. That supports injections as an option for patients who struggle with daily pills, not as a better treatment.

Pennsylvania

Legislature returns with skill-game machines now subject to seizure

Lawmakers come back to Harrisburg this week with no deal on skill games. The state Supreme Court’s 120-day stay ran out on Oct. 13, and State Police began enforcement on Oct. 14. Machines can be seized and owners charged, and unplugging a machine does not make it legal. Six operators or distributors have signed compliance agreements, and four legislators have asked the court for another 120 days. The two regulation bills, HB 2213 and SB 1079, would impose a $500 monthly fee per terminal and a 50,000-machine cap, but both have stalled. The session ends Nov. 30.
