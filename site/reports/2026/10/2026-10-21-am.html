Top Stories

SpaceX jumps about 10% before the open after its blowout quarter, and Musk says both companies have committees weighing a Tesla merger

Musk called a combination “a good idea” but said it is up to independent directors at both companies and needs a majority of all Tesla shares. No terms have been agreed and nothing has been filed.

Shares of Space Exploration Technologies (Nasdaq: SPCX) were up about 9–10% in premarket trading, according to CNBC and Benzinga, after Tuesday’s close of $147.79. In after-hours trading on Tuesday the stock reached as high as $161.56 (TradingKey). The quarter, reported in an 8-K at 4 PM, had revenue of $14.58 billion, up 161%, against estimates near $12.7–13.0 billion. Net income was $4.4 billion, or $0.35 a share; one analysis service puts about $1.6 billion of that below the operating line. More than half the revenue came from the AI segment.

The news that moved Tesla came on the call. Musk said SpaceX and Tesla have each formed special committees of independent directors to look at combining the companies. “I think it would be a good idea, but it’s really up to the special committees of both companies,” he said, as quoted by CNBC. Bloomberg, Axios and TechCrunch report that the talks are early, that no terms are agreed and that Musk is recused. Musk warned against treating a deal as certain: Tesla would need approval from 50% of all outstanding shares, not just those voted, which he called “quite a high hurdle.” Tesla shares were up about 2% before the bell. Tesla reports its own quarter after today’s close.

The committees are Musk’s account. Neither company has filed anything with the SEC about them, so far as we found, and Tesla’s proxy for its Dec. 3 annual meeting, filed Monday, lists no merger proposal. Electrek reports that the proxy does show one Tesla director who chairs its compensation and governance committees, Ira Ehrenpreis, sitting on SpaceX’s board since February. That overlap is the kind of conflict an independent committee exists to manage.

Why it matters Two of the largest companies on the Nasdaq-100 are, in effect, being priced as one possible company. SpaceX now earns more from AI computing than from Starlink. A merger would put Tesla shareholders into the AI-infrastructure bet with SpaceX’s $19.6 billion a quarter of capital spending. Expect heavy trading in both stocks and questions on Tesla’s call tonight.
What’s next Tesla’s call at 5:30 PM ET. Any formal step, such as a committee hiring advisers or an 8-K, would be the first document behind the talk.

Tesla (call at 5:30 PM) is expected to earn about $0.43–0.45 a share, after missing estimates last quarter at $0.33. Watch auto gross margin, energy storage, robotaxi timelines and any word on the merger committee.

Why it matters This week’s reports have rewarded guidance and cash, not beats: GE Aerospace, General Motors and Genuine Parts all beat and fell. Our Sunday forecast is that Meta holds or raises its capex outlook (80–90%).

Korea’s chipmakers rebound 5% as Super Micro lifts its forecast by more than a quarter

The Kospi recovered nearly all of Tuesday’s 3.7% fall, and Super Micro is up about 13% before the US open. Hong Kong went the other way.

Super Micro Computer raised its forecast for the September quarter late Tuesday. It now expects revenue of $18.5–19.5 billion, up from $14.5–15.5 billion, against about $15 billion expected, and a gross margin of 12–12.5%, up from 10.4–10.8% (company preliminary results, as reported; full results Nov. 3). The stock was up about 13% premarket, and Dell rose about 6% after hours, according to Benzinga.

In Seoul the Kospi rose 3.29% to 5,735.66, triggering a buy-side “sidecar” curb on program trading. Samsung Electronics rose 5.67% and SK hynix 5.81% (Seoul Economic Daily). Korean coverage tied the move to Super Micro and to easing worries about AI spending. The Nikkei rose 0.85% to 64,621. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng fell 2.2% to 23,652, with Alibaba down about 3.6% by one report. We found no single explanation for that fall, and midday reports of its size varied widely.

Why it matters A server maker’s revenue is the most direct read on how many AI systems are actually being shipped. A raise of more than 25% one week after the neocloud credit scare says the hardware orders are still coming.

UK inflation jumps to 3.5% on fuel, strengthening the case for a Bank of England hike

Diesel is at its highest since July 2022. Oil is now in another major economy’s inflation data.

UK consumer prices rose 3.5% in the year to September, up from 3.1% in August and above the 3.4% consensus. Prices rose 0.4% on the month. The Bank of England held at 3.75% in September, with three of nine members voting to hike. Coverage this morning (FXStreet, MoneyWeek) reads the data as strengthening the case for a November hike.

The United States

Still no ruling on the California ballots. In United States v. California (C.D. Cal., No. 2:26-cv-12025), the Justice Department wants about 20,000 mail ballots set aside, from voters on a federal list of suspected non-citizens. As of this morning we found no ruling on that request and no reported hearing date. California filed its opposition Tuesday, calling the request at best a “reckless political stunt.” The suit makes a single claim: that federal law overrides state procedure. It does not invoke the federal voter-registration statutes. The department points to the 28 people it charged on Monday; those are charges, not convictions. Election Day is Nov. 3.

Farm aid and a whistleblower fund. The Agriculture Department announced an $8 billion “Harvest Fuel Relief” program: one-time payments to farmers hit by diesel and fertilizer costs. President Trump unveiled it at a Kansas rally on Monday (USDA release; CNN). Separately, the president and Vice President Vance announced a $25 million Justice Department program that will pay up to $30,000 for verified fraud tips.

Congress is out until after the election. The stopgap law signed Sept. 2 funds the government through Dec. 11. A shutdown is not in play until December. Thirteen days out, the generic-ballot averages give Democrats a lead of about 7 to 8 points: RealClearPolling D+8.3 (Sept. 28–Oct. 19) and FiftyPlusOne D+8. Polling averages are not forecasts.

Science

The UN’s emissions-gap report says 1.5°C will be overshot. The UN Environment Programme’s 2026 report, “The Point of Return,” finds that current policies point to about 2.8°C of warming this century. Countries’ unconditional pledges point to 2.7°C. Every pledge and net-zero target, if all were met, points to 2.1°C. Even on the lowest-overshoot path, warming peaks at 1.7–1.8°C around mid-century, with roughly even odds of falling back to 1.5°C by 2100. That path requires emissions 18% below 2025 levels by 2030 and 45% below by 2035. This is an institutional assessment built on modelled scenarios, not a peer-reviewed paper, and its numbers depend on those scenarios’ assumptions.

Euclid finds its first comet. The European Space Agency’s Euclid survey telescope, built to map dark matter, has discovered a long-period comet, C/2026 S1 (Euclid). It is about 1.3 billion km away, between Jupiter and Saturn, and was confirmed by the Minor Planet Center on Oct. 8 (ESA/INAF).

Space

Japan’s Mars moons mission is on its way. JAXA’s H3 rocket, on its tenth flight and the first in its most powerful configuration, launched the Martian Moons eXploration spacecraft from Tanegashima on Monday afternoon (Eastern time). The spacecraft separated about 71 minutes after liftoff. It is due at Mars around August 2027 and is meant to bring at least 10 grams of soil from the moon Phobos back to Earth in 2031 (JAXA). That would be the first sample returned from the Martian system.

Starship next month, Musk says. On SpaceX’s earnings call, Elon Musk said Flight 15 is expected next month and will try to catch both the booster and the ship. He put the odds of catching the ship at “at least 50 or 60 percent.” No date is set.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

County Council passes 12 weeks of paid parental leave, 11–4

Allegheny County Council voted 11–4 on Tuesday night to require employers with 15 or more workers to give up to 12 weeks of paid leave for a birth, adoption or stillbirth. Leave is paid at full regular wages, up to $4,200 a week. Full-time workers qualify after six months and part-timers after 625 hours. Four members voted no: Democrats Aaron Adams, Pat Catena and Nick Futules, and Suzanne Filiaggi, council’s only Republican. County Executive Sara Innamorato’s office says she will sign it Thursday. Employers then have up to 12 months to comply, so the rules would bite around late 2027. Supporters call it the first county mandate of its kind. The Allegheny Conference and the Pittsburgh Regional Alliance oppose it as bad for jobs and investment. Adams warned of a possible legal challenge; none has been filed.

WESA; PublicSource; WPXI; Axios Pittsburgh.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Transit workers ratify a four-year contract; Friday’s board meeting also takes up the Wabash Tunnel

About 2,300 Pittsburgh Regional Transit operators, mechanics and office staff in ATU Local 85 approved a four-year contract by a two-to-one margin. It gives raises of 4%, 4%, 3.5% and 3.5%, and shortens the time to reach the top of the pay scale to three years from four (Post-Gazette; KDKA). The PRT board is expected to approve it on Friday. The same meeting will vote on staff’s recommendation to close the Wabash Tunnel for good on Dec. 1 rather than spend about $43 million to repair it.

Pittsburgh Post-Gazette; KDKA; WESA.

Pennsylvania

House passes a bell-to-bell school phone ban; it goes back to the Senate

The state House voted 141–62 on Monday for Senate Bill 1014. It would ban phones and most personal internet devices, including tablets, smartwatches and handheld game consoles, from the first bell to the last in all public and private schools, starting in fall 2027. Medical and special-needs uses are exempt, and schools would choose how to enforce it. Democrats split 78–25 in favour and Republicans 63–37. Because the House amended the bill, the Senate must vote again; the session ends Nov. 30. Governor Shapiro has said he will sign a bell-to-bell ban.

Philadelphia Inquirer; CNHI/Tribune-Democrat; abc27; Pennsylvania House roll calls.
