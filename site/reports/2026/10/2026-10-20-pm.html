Top Stories

The Dow and small caps rally while the Nasdaq stalls, as money moves out of tech and into everything else

Ten of the eleven sector funds rose and the equal-weight S&P beat the cap-weighted index by almost a point. Health care led, and technology was the only sector to fall.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 529.85 points, or 1.02%, to 52,495.88, and the Russell 2000 rose 1.65% to 2,773.42. The S&P 500 added 0.28% to 7,712.57 and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.02% to 26,424.82 (CNBC final figures for the three big indexes; Yahoo Finance for all four). It was the reverse of Monday, when a handful of chip and tech names carried the index while most stocks fell.

This time the breadth was on the other side. The Invesco equal-weight S&P 500 fund (RSP) rose 1.15%, against 0.34% for the SPDR S&P 500 fund (SPY). Health care (XLV +2.4%) led, closing within 0.2% of its 52-week high. Eli Lilly rose 3.5%, Merck 3.3%, UnitedHealth 3.0%. Real estate (+1.7%), utilities (+1.3%) and financials (+1.2%) followed. Regional banks (KRE) rose 2.0%. The technology fund (XLK) fell 0.3%, the only sector fund in the red, and the Roundhill Magnificent Seven fund fell 0.4%. Meta fell 1.6% and Alphabet 1.1% the day before their results.

The session started badly. Overnight, the Kospi fell 3.7% on another bout of selling in Korean memory makers, and Brent touched $111.99. Strong industrial earnings, a weak housing report that pulled long yields down, and oil’s reversal all coincided with the move. No single one of them is a confirmed cause.

Why it matters

Two days, two opposite rallies. Monday’s was the AI trade bouncing; today’s was the rest of the market catching a bid while the AI trade sat still. The index is roughly where it started the week. What changed is that investors are now willing to buy health care, banks and small companies on a day when long yields fall, which is what you would expect if rates, not AI, are the main thing holding the average stock back.

Sources: Yahoo Finance closing data (checked against Twelve Data); CNBC; Associated Press; Reuters via Invezz; Benzinga.

Housing starts fall to a 1.22 million pace, well short of forecasts, and long yields fall with them

The miss was large, though within the survey’s wide error band. Treasuries rallied at the long end while two-year yields rose, flattening the curve.

Builders started homes at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1.221 million in September, down 5.2% from a revised 1.288 million in August, the Census Bureau and HUD reported at 8:30 AM (FRED series HOUST). August was revised up from 1.275 million. Economists had forecast about 1.305 million. Only May, at 1.182 million, was weaker this year. Single-family starts fell 4.6% to 859,000, according to secondary reports of the release. Building permits, which lead starts by a month or two, were 1.400 million, down 0.2% from a revised 1.403 million and slightly above the 1.387 million forecast.

Read the monthly number with care. Census puts the margin of error on the change in starts at about ±11.8 percentage points, so a 5% fall is not statistically distinguishable from zero. The direction is still consistent across sources. Starts have fallen three months in a row from 1.439 million in June, and on Monday the builders’ confidence index hit 29, its lowest since 2012.

Treasuries rallied after the release. On Treasury’s end-of-day par curve the 10-year fell 3 basis points to 5.12%, the 20-year 6 to 5.47% and the 30-year 6 to 5.42%. The 2-year rose 2 to 4.79%. The 2-year to 10-year spread narrowed to 33 basis points from 38.

Why it matters

Housing is where a 5% 10-year yield bites first, and now it is showing up in what builders do, not just in what they say. For the Fed, which meets Oct. 27–28 with September inflation at 3.7%, weaker construction is an argument against another hike. The bond market seems to agree for now: long yields fell while short ones rose.

Sources: Census Bureau new residential construction (via FRED, EOD official); US Treasury daily par yield curve (EOD official, Oct 20); CNBC; MortgagePoint.

SpaceX nearly triples its revenue to $14.6 billion as its AI business overtakes Starlink

In its second report as a public company, SpaceX swung to a $4.4 billion profit. It also spent $19.6 billion in the quarter, most of it on AI data centres.

Space Exploration Technologies (Nasdaq: SPCX) filed its third-quarter results at 4:00 PM. Revenue was $14.58 billion, up 161% from $5.58 billion a year earlier and nearly double the second quarter’s $7.81 billion. Net income was $4.4 billion, or $0.35 a diluted share, against a $1.1 billion loss a year ago. Adjusted EBITDA was $8.8 billion. Reuters and CNBC put the LSEG revenue consensus near $12.7 billion, so this was a large beat.

The mix has changed. The “Super Intelligence” segment, SpaceX’s AI business, brought in $7.56 billion, up from $813 million a year ago and $2.56 billion last quarter. The company credits cloud-services agreements, its acquisition of Cursor and its own models, including Grok. The segment earned $431 million operating profit after losing $1.26 billion in the second quarter. Connectivity (Starlink) revenue rose 69% to $5.25 billion with a $2.37 billion operating profit; consumer subscribers reached 13.6 million and monthly revenue per user rose 5% to $69. The launch business, labelled Space, brought in $1.77 billion and lost $7 million. SpaceX also flew its first revenue-carrying Starship mission, putting 26 Starlink V3 satellites in orbit.

Capital spending was $19.59 billion in the quarter, of which $16.44 billion went to the AI segment. The company says its “nameplate compute” reached 1.7 gigawatts on Sept. 30. Shares closed down 0.1% at $147.79 and traded about 2.3% higher at $151.26 around 4:40 PM, in thin after-hours trading (Yahoo Finance).

Why it matters

SpaceX is now as much an AI infrastructure company as a rocket company: more than half its revenue and five-sixths of its capital spending are in AI. That ties a Nasdaq-100 stock to the same debate about AI spending and returns that has been shaking chips and neoclouds for two weeks. The AI segment’s first operating profit is the number that matters for that debate.

Sources: SpaceX Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (primary, SEC EDGAR); Reuters and CNBC (LSEG consensus); Yahoo Finance after-hours quotes (delayed).

Netflix and Intuitive Surgical fall after hours; Capital One rises

Netflix met its own forecast but cut its 2026 ad-revenue outlook to $2.7 billion. Intuitive grew 22% and still fell about 11%.

Netflix reported third-quarter revenue of $12.87 billion, up 11.8% and in line with its forecast, and diluted EPS of $0.87 against $0.59 a year ago. Its operating margin was 34.6%. For the fourth quarter it guides to $13.44 billion in revenue and a 26.0% margin, which includes about 1.2 points of cost from its recently announced job cuts. Full-year revenue is now expected at $51.1 billion, 0.2% below the midpoint of its earlier range. The company says subscription revenue beat its plans but it now expects about $2.7 billion of 2026 ad revenue, down from about $3 billion, because some ad products rolled out more slowly than expected. The shares, which closed down 1.7% at $68.69, traded about 4.9% lower after hours.

Intuitive Surgical reported revenue of $3.05 billion, up 22%. Procedures grew about 15% and it placed 546 da Vinci systems, against 427 a year ago. GAAP EPS was $1.49, which includes a $450 million antitrust settlement charge according to secondary reports of the release; non-GAAP EPS was $3.00. The stock fell about 10.6% after hours from a $438.00 close. We found no explanation from the company or analysts for the size of the drop by press time.

Capital One reported net income of $3.5 billion, or $5.61 a share, and adjusted EPS of $6.69, according to its release as summarised by news outlets. Its shares rose about 6.4% after hours. United Airlines earned an adjusted $2.54 a share, at the bottom of consensus estimates of $2.57–2.59, on record revenue of $17.9 billion (+17%), despite fuel costs up 62%. It expects fourth-quarter revenue growth of more than 20%. The stock fell about 2.2% after hours after rising 2.3% in the session.

Why it matters

Netflix’s ad business was meant to be its next growth engine. A 10% cut to this year’s ad outlook, in the same week it is cutting about 6% of staff, says that engine is coming slower than promised. Capital One’s reaction is a cleaner read on the consumer: credit is holding up.

Sources: Netflix, Intuitive Surgical and United Airlines Forms 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (primary, SEC EDGAR); Capital One and Chubb releases via Benzinga and CNBC; Yahoo Finance after-hours quotes (delayed, about 4:40 PM).

3M and Northrop rally on raised guidance; GM falls 6.8% despite its $20 billion buyback

The morning’s earnings split on guidance. GE Aerospace beat and raised but still fell 3.7% on lighter cash flow.

3M rose 5.8% to $171.37, the best performer in the Dow and its best day since July, according to CNBC. It beat estimates and raised its 2026 adjusted EPS guide to $9.10–9.15, part of it from an accounting change, and expanded a data-centre supply deal with Amazon that comes with a warrant for up to 3.16 million shares. Northrop Grumman rose 4.6% to $534.00 after a beat, a record $110 billion backlog and a higher guide.

General Motors fell 6.8% to $77.09, its worst day in about 15 months by Benzinga’s count. The quarter beat estimates and the board approved a $20 billion buyback, but GM narrowed its full-year adjusted EPS range to $12.75–13.75, whose $13.25 midpoint is below the roughly $13.42 consensus. Reuters also cited higher commodity costs and softer truck sales ahead. Guidance is the likely reason for the drop; no outlet has a statement from investors to confirm it.

GE Aerospace fell 3.7% to $299.87 after raising every line of its guidance. Free cash flow of $2.0 billion fell 14% and came in below the $2.2 billion analysts expected, according to StreetAccount data cited by CNBC. Genuine Parts fell 5.1% to $123.52 after narrowing its 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to $7.50–7.75 from $7.50–8.00. RTX rose 0.6%. Its adjusted EPS was $2.26 on sales of $25.0 billion (+11%), and it raised its full-year EPS guide to $7.65–7.75; the quarter includes $0.25 a share of recovered tariff costs. Halliburton rose 1.9% after adjusted EPS of $0.60 on flat revenue of $5.7 billion.

Why it matters

The market is paying for guidance and cash, not for beats. Three of these companies beat and still fell. That is a high bar heading into Wednesday’s big-tech reports.

Sources: RTX, Halliburton and Genuine Parts Forms 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (primary, SEC EDGAR); GE Aerospace, 3M, Northrop and GM releases; CNBC; Benzinga; Reuters; Yahoo Finance closes.

Brent spikes to $112, then settles below $109 as Iran sets out terms

The Revolutionary Guard claims another tanker hit a mine, and the US calls that false. In Tehran, Iran told Pakistan’s army chief its conditions for talking to Washington.

December Brent rose as high as $111.99 a barrel early, then settled down 0.7% at $108.79 (CNBC). The November WTI contract, which expired today, settled at $101.79 (−1.8%); December WTI, now the front month, was near $98. Because the WTI contract rolled, a quoted “5% fall” in some feeds compares two different months. The US Oil Fund (USO) fell 0.1% and the energy sector fund rose 0.3%.

Unconfirmed Iran’s Revolutionary Guard navy said a supertanker carrying more than two million barrels hit a mine south of the Strait of Hormuz and caught fire, after switching off its transponder. The ship was not named. The claim comes through Iranian state media. A US Central Command spokesman called the mine claims “false” and said Iran is attacking ships with missiles and drones; that denial reached us through secondary outlets. Last weekend a Gulf shipping centre found “no substantiation” for two earlier mine claims (gCaptain).

Diplomacy moved, without a breakthrough. Pakistan’s army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, visited Tehran. Iranian officials told him that “not even a tiny bit” of enriched uranium will leave Iran, and Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf, Iran’s chief negotiator, listed seven conditions, among them a regional ceasefire, unfrozen assets and an end to oil sanctions (Arab News, Times of Israel, TASS and others). President Trump said things were “going very well.” Some outlets described the day’s talk as a new peace proposal; we could not find one beyond Iran’s existing seven-day plan.

Why it matters

Oil is trading headlines, not barrels: a $3 intraday range on claims nobody can confirm. Brent near $109 is still about 6% higher than three weeks ago. Treasury Secretary Bessent said today that any fall in oil before year-end “will depend” on the Iran conflict.

Sources: CNBC (settlements); Yahoo Finance futures (delayed); Anadolu, Mehr and PressTV (IRGC claim); Kurdistan24 (CENTCOM); gCaptain; Arab News; Times of Israel; TASS; Bloomberg (Bessent).

California calls the Justice Department’s mail-ballot suit “absurd” as a judge weighs an emergency order

The department wants about 20,000 ballots set aside before Nov. 3. No hearing date or ruling had been reported by this evening.

In United States v. California, filed Oct. 15 in the Central District of California, the Justice Department asks the court to stop the state counting vote-by-mail ballots from about 20,000 people on a federal list of suspected non-citizens until their citizenship is verified. The suit rests on a single claim that federal law barring non-citizen voting overrides state procedure. On Monday the department asked for a temporary restraining order.

California filed its opposition today. Attorney General Rob Bonta’s filing calls the request at best a “reckless political stunt” that would disenfranchise thousands, and Bloomberg reports the state says the suit “borders on absurd.” Legal experts quoted by CNN called it a long shot. The department says its list is accurate. It points to the 28 people it charged on Monday, all lawful permanent residents on the list, with crimes including unlawful voting and false claims of citizenship. Those are charges, not convictions. U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli said 11 were in custody.

What’s next

A ruling on the restraining order could come any day. Election Day is Nov. 3, two weeks out.

Sources: Bloomberg; CNN; ABC News; NPR; ABC7 Los Angeles; MyNewsLA; San Francisco Chronicle.

The Economy

Today’s data. Housing starts and permits are covered above. Starts are down 15% from June and 7.4% from September 2025. Permits are flat, which means the pipeline is not collapsing, just not refilling. Mortgage rates are the pressure point: several outlets cite a 30-year fixed rate near 7.4–7.6% after the 10-year’s climb above 5%. That rests on daily surveys, not Freddie Mac’s weekly figure, which comes Thursday. The BLS published state employment data; we found no coverage of it.

The Fed. Policymakers are in their blackout before the Oct. 27–28 meeting. Futures-based estimates cited this week put the chance of an October hike at about 13–17%, with a hike by December much more likely. We could not confirm a same-day figure. Today’s data pushes in two directions: weak housing argues for patience, while oil above $100 keeps headline inflation, 3.7% in September, uncomfortable.

Treasury. Today’s $95 billion 6-week bill sold at a high rate of 3.975%, with bids worth 2.74 times the amount offered and 78.5% of bids at the high rate filled (TreasuryDirect, official). That is routine bill demand. The tests are Wednesday’s $13 billion 20-year reopening at 1 PM and Thursday’s $26 billion 5-year TIPS. Secretary Bessent told Bloomberg he sees no spillover into Treasuries from France’s bond selloff.

Rates and credit. The 10-year real yield was 2.80% and the 10-year breakeven 2.35% on Monday (FRED, the latest). So Monday’s rise in yields was in real rates, not inflation expectations. The ICE BofA high-yield spread narrowed 2 basis points to 3.29 percentage points on Monday. High-yield bond prices (HYG) were flat today while stocks rose, which says credit is steady, not enthusiastic.

Global. European stocks recovered most of an early loss (Stoxx 600 −0.1%, CAC 40 −0.4%). France’s 10-year spread over Bunds widened about 5.5 basis points to roughly 152 (Reuters via Euronext) as curfews continued. The ECB meets Oct. 29 and the Bank of Japan Oct. 29–30.

The United States

Two weeks to the midterms. Election Day is Nov. 3, and Congress is out of session until after it. Government funding runs to Dec. 11 under the stopgap passed in September, and none of the 12 annual spending bills has passed both chambers, according to the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget. On the generic congressional ballot, polling averages show Democrats ahead by about 8 to 9 points: about 8.9 in Nate Silver’s average and 8.0 in PollsMax’s. The averages use different methods; a generic-ballot lead is not a seat forecast.

The World

Ukraine talks in Oslo. President Zelensky met US envoy Steve Witkoff in Oslo, in talks co-hosted by Norway and Finland, with Jared Kushner joining remotely, according to Al Jazeera, the Kyiv Independent and RFE/RL. Zelensky named missile interceptors, early warning and Starlink and Starshield access as his priorities. Norway’s prime minister said Witkoff meets Russian officials in St. Petersburg on Wednesday to present proposals for post-war security guarantees modelled on NATO’s Article 5. Russian strikes overnight killed at least four civilians, one outlet reports.

Yemen. Three Houthi ballistic missiles hit a government army training camp in Shabwa province on Monday, an attack confirmed by Al Jazeera, the Associated Press and CBC. The toll has risen with each report: 53 dead per the local authority, 60 per the AP, and 73 recruits aged 18 to 20 per CBC, citing the governor. The Houthis claimed Saudi officers ran the camp, without evidence; Riyadh has not commented. Their claims of strikes on Saudi oil sites remain unverified.

France. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu said “targeted curfews have contributed to the calm of this night,” and promised a “massive” police deployment for Saturday (France 24, CNBC). Curfews remain in parts of Paris and in central Lyon. President Macron refused Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez’s resignation on Monday. French bonds stayed under pressure, as noted in The Economy.

China and Taiwan. Taiwan’s defence ministry detected 27 Chinese military aircraft on Tuesday, including H-6 bombers, 21 of them in its air-defence zone, and called it a “joint combat readiness patrol.” A $14 billion US arms package for Taiwan still awaits the president’s approval.

AI & Technology

AI agents used to break into company websites. A Reuters investigation, as relayed by several technology outlets, says a Chinese-speaking operator used AI agents to breach the websites of more than 100 companies, mostly online retailers, from Sept. 25; Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins were named. The tooling reportedly included DeepSeek models and Anthropic’s Claude Code, an attribution the coverage hedges. What data was taken, and who is behind it, is unknown.

The AI-agent breach details rest on secondary coverage of a single investigation.

Anthropic’s public benefit fund. Anthropic, which is private, has committed 5% of its shares to a new fund for public-benefit uses, subject to shareholder approval (company post; Reuters; CNBC). Reports of a November IPO and of its valuation conflict and are unconfirmed.

Science

The first “green” star. Astronomers led by Mark Hollands at the University of Warwick report a white dwarf, WD J1330+6435, that looks green. Sodium from planetary debris falling onto it absorbs red and orange light. The paper is peer-reviewed, in RAS Techniques and Instruments. It was announced late last week.

Space

Japan launches the first Phobos sample-return mission. An H3 rocket lifted off from Tanegashima at 3:41 PM ET on Monday (4:41 AM Tuesday in Japan) carrying JAXA’s Martian Moons eXploration probe. The spacecraft separated about 71 minutes later and was placed on its planned path to Mars (JAXA). MMX is due at Phobos in 2027 and should bring samples back to Earth in 2031. It carries a small rover built by France and Germany.

SpaceX. The company’s results are covered in Top Stories. A Falcon 9 flew the Bandwagon-5 rideshare from Cape Canaveral this morning after Monday’s weather scrub, carrying 32 payloads, among them a second Project Starfall re-entry capsule (SpaceX, WFTV). Another Starlink launch from Vandenberg was scheduled for this evening.

Coming up. Rocket Lab’s Electron is set to launch three HawkEye 360 satellites from New Zealand early Wednesday UTC. NASA holds an Artemis briefing on Friday at 2 PM with Administrator Jared Isaacman. NASA describes Artemis III as a crewed test in Earth orbit in 2027, with a Moon landing targeted for 2028; that is a plan, not a schedule commitment.

Also in the News

Measles. The CDC confirmed the third US measles death of 2026 (AP, Al Jazeera). The national count stands at 4,410 cases in 45 states and DC, with 451 hospitalisations, as of Oct. 15. Pennsylvania reports 1,286 cases and five deaths across 42 counties, and the CDC is sending an outbreak investigation team. The CDC’s death count lags state counts because it waits for death certificates.

Local

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

County Council votes tonight on 12 weeks of paid parental leave

Allegheny County Council takes up a mandate that employers with 15 or more workers give 12 weeks of paid parental leave, for workers with at least six months on the job (the first draft said 30 days). The Board of Health approved a scaled-back version and sent it on. County Executive Sara Innamorato supports it. The Pennsylvania Chamber of Business and Industry asked Council to delay, and business groups oppose it. KDKA Radio calls it the first such mandate in the state. The vote was not reported before this edition closed; Wednesday morning’s edition will have it.

Pittsburgh Post-Gazette; WPXI; WESA; PublicSource; KDKA Radio.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Allegheny General nurses authorise a strike if talks fail

About 1,500 nurses at Allegheny General Hospital voted 99% to authorise a strike if needed, WPXI and KDKA report. Their contract, extended from Oct. 13, now runs through Oct. 30, and the union must give 10 days’ notice before any strike. An informational picket is planned for Oct. 29. The union says Allegheny Health Network, part of Highmark Health, proposed higher health costs and cuts to retirement and time-off benefits. The network says it is bargaining in good faith.

WPXI; KDKA-TV; KDKA Radio.

Pennsylvania

The House sits Wednesday for its first formal session since July

The state House has scheduled a formal session for Wednesday at 11 AM. Leaders have not said why. Under the chamber’s rules for this point in the year, it can only take up conference reports, new appropriation bills, veto overrides and bills the governor returned with amendments, so a surprise vote is possible. The Senate sits through Wednesday too. Both chambers then break until after Nov. 3.

Statehouse News; PA NewsMedia Association.
