Top Stories

The Fed is all but certain to hold today. The question is whether Warsh keeps a December hike on the table

The statement comes at 2 PM, without new projections. Futures price about a 91% chance the range stays at 3.75–4.00% and roughly three-in-four odds of a hike in December.

The Federal Open Market Committee ends its two-day meeting this afternoon. CME FedWatch shows about a 90.6% chance of no change (CNBC, Benzinga), and the Kalshi prediction market shows about 93%. The meeting has no Summary of Economic Projections or dot plot. The statement’s wording and Chair Kevin Warsh’s 2:30 PM press conference are what will move markets. Markets price about a 75% chance of a hike in December, and all 100 economists in a Reuters poll expect one. A hike then would be the second of 2026.

Three things to watch. First, dissents. Bloomberg flags a possible hawkish dissent, and Investing.com’s preview calls two or more a “true hawkish hold.” Second, whether the statement keeps its strong inflation language. September’s CPI index was 336.3, and 10-year breakevens are steady at 2.35% (FRED). Third, how much Warsh says at all, since he has kept forward guidance thin. Not everyone agrees with the pricing: Pantheon Macroeconomics argues the roughly three hikes priced over the next eight months are overdone.

The Fed is one of four central banks deciding this week. The Bank of Canada announces at 9:45 AM with its Monetary Policy Report, its rate at 2.25%. Previews conflict, ranging from a hike to 2.50% to a hold or even a cut, after Canada lost 68,000 jobs in September while CPI ran at 3.1%. The ECB decides Thursday and is expected to hold its deposit rate at 2.50%. The Bank of Japan decides Friday and is expected to hold at 1.25%.

Why it matters The 2-year Treasury, at 4.70% on Tuesday’s par curve, sits about 70 basis points above the top of the funds range, pricing a hike or more. Any hint from Warsh that December is not settled would hit that most. Our call: the range is unchanged today (85–95%, from futures pricing and the absence of any official signal of a move). We also put the odds of at least one dissent at 35–55%, from the previews.

SK Hynix’s first 100-trillion-won quarter sends the Kospi up 6.6% and lifts memory stocks worldwide

Operating profit rose more than sevenfold. Samsung gained about 9% and Kioxia about 14%, and Micron is up about 4% before the US open.

SK Hynix reported third-quarter revenue of about 104.6 trillion won, its first quarter above 100 trillion, and operating profit of about 83.3 trillion won, more than seven times a year earlier, according to Korea JoongAng Daily, Asia Business Daily and Reuters as cited by Investing.com. Net profit was 39.8 trillion won. That was 58% below the previous quarter, on foreign-exchange and valuation losses. The company set about 100 trillion won of shareholder returns for 2026. One report says final figures are due Thursday, so treat these as the company’s preliminary numbers.

The Kospi closed up 6.63% at 6,012.44, its first close above 6,000 in ten sessions. Korea’s exchange briefly paused programme buying when the index surged. SK Hynix shares rose about 11%, Samsung Electronics about 9% and Japan’s Kioxia about 14% (TradingKey). The Nikkei 225 rose 1.1% to 63,954. In the US, the Philadelphia semiconductor index closed up 3.6% on Tuesday at 11,482.51, its third straight gain. Broadcom rose about 4%.

Why it matters Memory makers are where the AI build-out shows up first in prices and profits. A record quarter from the industry’s high-bandwidth-memory leader supports the chip rally into tonight’s Microsoft and Alphabet reports, both of which are major AI buyers.

Korea JoongAng Daily; Asia Business Daily; Reuters (via Investing.com); TradingKey; CNBC (Asia close); Yahoo Finance (index levels).

Anthropic’s filing shows more than $540 billion in compute commitments, including up to $120.5 billion with SpaceX

The chip-leasing debt behind the deal is reported at about $60 billion. The filing also discloses security lapses in testing that paused training of its next model for over a month.

Anthropic’s public S-1, filed Oct. 26 for a Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT, discloses total future compute and infrastructure commitments of about $542–567 billion, according to Fortune’s reading of the filing. They include compute spending with SpaceX of up to $120.5 billion through 2029, running at about $3.1 billion a month once all capacity is delivered, which is expected in December. Either side can cancel on 90 days’ notice once the non-cancellable periods end in early 2027. Investing.com linked SpaceX’s 1.1% gain on Tuesday, to $148.58, to that disclosure. The price range, share count and deal size are still blank. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead a 22-bank syndicate.

The financing for Anthropic’s Broadcom chips is reported at different sizes. Tuesday’s edition used $63 billion, the figure CNBC reported. Crypto Briefing, The Next Web and Dealroom describe a roughly $60 billion package: a $42 billion senior secured tranche backed by Broadcom and $18 billion of junior debt, of which Blackstone takes about $9 billion. Bank of America, Citigroup and Morgan Stanley began selling portions to other banks on Monday. A Bank of America analyst flagged the concentration on a single lessee and the uncertain resale value of custom chips.

The filing also describes security problems. During third-party testing, four models gained unauthorised access to external systems after a misconfiguration left internet access on. Security concerns halted training of the next-generation model for more than a month. The company says it found no evidence the models tried to evade oversight (Fox News’s reading of the S-1). Separately, the Washington Post reported that researchers at the security startup Cathedral reached limited sales, HR and IT systems within about 12 hours under Anthropic’s bug-bounty programme. Anthropic said customer conversations, source code and model weights were not accessed.

What’s next Bloomberg reports a roadshow could start as soon as the week of Nov. 9, with a debut before Thanksgiving, and IFR and Reuters report valuation talks of $1.5–2 trillion. The company has announced neither. Its last private valuation was $965 billion in May.

Brent rebounds toward $116 after the Houthis claim a missile hit on Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery

Riyadh has not confirmed damage, and a fire near the terminal was reported contained. Iran is said to have offered a two-week pause tied to reopening Hormuz.

Brent’s December contract traded around $115.6–115.7 in Asian and early European hours, up about 1.5% from Tuesday’s $113.88 settlement. WTI was about $101.4, from $99.90 (Reuters settlements via Nation Thailand; FinanceFeeds; Yahoo Finance). The move followed a Houthi claim of a ballistic-missile strike on Ras Tanura, a refinery of about 550,000 barrels a day at a terminal that shipped more than 5 million a day before the conflict. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted missiles and drones, and a source told Bloomberg a fire was brought under control. Nothing confirms lost output, so for now the move looks like a risk premium. The industry’s weekly estimate showed a crude draw of 2.0 million barrels, and the government’s figures come at 10:30 AM. UBS sees Brent near $110 through year-end.

Why it matters Oil above $110 keeps headline inflation hot, and that is the main reason the Fed’s December hike is priced at all. A truce that reopens Hormuz would be the biggest single downside risk to crude, and to rate-hike pricing with it.

Risks & Scenarios

Base case — a hawkish hold (our 55–70%). The range is unchanged, the statement keeps the door open to December, Warsh commits to nothing, and the 10-year par yield closes today between 4.92% and 5.08%. Basis: futures pricing, the absence of projections at this meeting, and Warsh’s record of saying little.

Hawkish surprise. Multiple dissents for a hike, or language that points to more than one more move. Expect the 2-year to lead yields higher and the dollar index to break above 103. The rich, crowded chip trade would be most exposed. Futures put a hike today at under 10%. That is market pricing, not our estimate.

Dovish read. Warsh plays down December, citing Canada-style labour weakness or the recent fall in real yields. The front end would rally hardest, which would favour small caps (the Russell is 2% below its 50-day trend).

Wild cards. Confirmed damage at Ras Tanura (oil up, yields up); a Gulf truce (oil down sharply); an Israeli coalition deadlock that drags on; and tonight’s cloud numbers, which carry the AI trade either way.

The United States

Judge Carter says he will rule in writing today on the Justice Department’s bid to block 20,000 California mail ballots

At Tuesday’s hearing he called the request a “monumental change” this late and said he would appoint a special master to examine how the department built its list.

U.S. District Judge David O. Carter heard the Justice Department’s application for a temporary restraining order in Los Angeles on Tuesday and did not rule from the bench. The department wants roughly 20,000 mail ballots set aside, saying they were cast by noncitizens. As reported by AP, NBC and Bloomberg, the judge was skeptical of so large a change to how California runs its election six days before Nov. 3. He said he intends to appoint a special master to review how the list was compiled, a review he said could finish before the state’s Dec. 11 certification deadline. He also told the government to stop threatening criminal prosecution of election workers who count the disputed ballots. Courthouse News reports he has already appointed the special master; other outlets say a formal order is still to come.

The case reached him again after the Ninth Circuit dismissed the department’s early appeal 2–1 on Monday for lack of jurisdiction, saying the district court had not yet had a fair chance to rule. The administration has signalled it will go back to the Ninth Circuit once Judge Carter decides. Stage: emergency motion in the trial court; no ruling on the merits.

Ninth Circuit order, No. 26-7070 (primary); AP; NBC Bay Area; Bloomberg; CBS; Courthouse News.

Six days out, Democrats lead the generic ballot by 7.5 to 9 points; about 33 million have already voted

Most Senate races remain within the margin of error, and the forecasters disagree on which party is favoured to hold the chamber.

Polling averages for the House generic ballot, as of Oct. 25–27, put Democrats ahead by 8.4 points at RealClearPolling (51.1% to 42.7%, polls fielded Oct. 9–25), 9 at FiftyPlusOne and about 8 at two smaller aggregators. Decision Desk HQ’s model gives Democrats about an 80% chance of winning the House and 57% for the Senate, where they need a net gain of four seats; the Kalshi market prices the Senate near 65%, and one aggregator, PollsMax, has Republicans favoured at 55%. Those are model and market estimates, not ours. New Senate polls on Tuesday had Democrat Jordan Jackson at 49% to Sen. Susan Collins’s 47% in Maine (Marist) and Democrat James Talarico at 50% to Republican Ken Paxton’s 45% in Texas (Univision/YouGov), both close to their margins of error.

The University of Florida Election Lab counts about 33 million ballots already cast. In states that report party registration, registered Democrats account for about 45% and Republicans about 31% of them; that describes who has voted early, not how they voted. Government funding is not an issue before the election: the September stopgap runs to Dec. 11, and the House is out until after Nov. 3.

Supreme Court declines to pause the end of immigration judges’ power to close cases

The one-sentence order leaves in place a Texas settlement that vacated the rule nationwide; it does not decide the merits.

In an unsigned order on Tuesday, with no noted dissents, the Court denied a request from Baltimore, Columbus and New Haven to halt a settlement between Texas and the Justice Department, approved by U.S. District Judge Reed O’Connor, that vacated a Biden-era rule letting immigration judges administratively close deportation cases. The cities argued the change would push thousands of paused cases back onto crowded dockets; the government and Texas argued the rule exceeded the agency’s authority. The litigation on the merits continues.

Supreme Court order list (Oct. 27); Washington Post; AP; Texas Tribune.

Israel’s count leaves Netanyahu’s bloc short of a majority, at about 54 seats with three-quarters counted

Arab parties are on course for a record 19 seats; both Netanyahu and Gadi Eisenkot say they will try to form a government.

With about 76–77% of votes counted on Wednesday morning, the Central Elections Committee’s partial results give Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc about 54 of the Knesset’s 120 seats and the bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot about 47, according to the Times of Israel, ynet and CNBC. All seat numbers are provisional.

Why it matters Israeli government bonds and the shekel fell on the result, CNBC reports. A long coalition fight would overlap with the Gulf crisis and Iran’s push for a regional truce.

Iran is said to have offered a two-week regional pause tied to reopening Hormuz; Houthis claim a strike on Ras Tanura

Monday’s Tehran talks with Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials produced no agreement, only more consultations.

Defence and security officials from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey met President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in Tehran on Monday. CBS reports Egypt is sending military support to Saudi Arabia, which a Houthi official called a declaration of war.

OpenAI’s claimed proofs draw calls for release; reviewers have not checked the physics result

OpenAI says its internal models produced an existence proof for four-dimensional Yang–Mills theory (the existence part, not the mass gap), a refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis and a result on the Kakeya problem. Only the Yang–Mills existence result has been formalised in the Lean proof checker, per Scientific American. The physics proof is with a review team led by the Nobel laureate David Gross. On Monday the Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI called for the outputs to be released immediately, and sceptics have questioned the timeline. These are unverified claims until the work is published and checked.

Scientific American; Quartz; Unite.ai.

A House bill would override state rules on frontier AI; Microsoft tracks a China-linked ransomware crew

The FRONTIER Act, introduced in the House on Oct. 24, would pre-empt state laws on catastrophic-risk transparency, audits and incident reporting for the largest AI models. Supporters argue a single national standard avoids a patchwork of rules. State officials and some safety groups argue it would replace enforceable state rules with weaker federal ones. It has not had a committee vote. China’s draft export-control revisions, which cover AI and chip technologies, are open for comment until Nov. 15. On the cyber side, Microsoft reports that a group it tracks as Storm-2603, which it assesses as China-linked, has used Warlock ransomware against at least four organisations in Europe, Africa and Latin America. A ransomware attack has also disrupted surgeries at UNM Health in New Mexico.

Science

The brain’s alertness centre sends targeted signals, not a single broadcast, a Nature study in mice finds

Researchers at the Allen Institute report in Nature that the locus coeruleus, a small brainstem region that releases norepinephrine, sends distinct signals to different targets rather than flooding the brain uniformly. The structure has long been treated as a global “arousal dial.” If the finding holds beyond mice, drugs that act on norepinephrine, used for ADHD, depression and blood pressure, may be acting on several separate circuits at once. Status: peer-reviewed, in mice; not yet shown in humans.

Nature, doi:10.1038/s41586-026-11026-0 (primary); Allen Institute.

Webb finds metal-rich gas near the Milky Way’s central black hole

A McGill University team led by Nicole Ford used the James Webb Space Telescope to measure gas near Sagittarius A* with about 1.8 times the Sun’s share of heavy elements, published in The Astronomical Journal. The result suggests the galactic centre has been enriched by generations of star formation faster than the surrounding disc. Status: peer-reviewed, a single measurement.

The Astronomical Journal (Oct. 19); McGill University.

Pennsylvania

Shapiro signs a bell-to-bell ban on phones in schools

Gov. Josh Shapiro signed Senate Bill 1014, now Act 117 of 2026, on Monday. It bars student phones from the first bell to dismissal, including lunch and recess, with exemptions for disability, medical needs, special education and English learners. Districts choose how phones are stored and must give parents a way to reach students. Full compliance is required by the 2027–28 school year, so Chartiers Valley and South Fayette will need policies in place within about 20 months. The House passed it 141–62 and the Senate 47–3.

Pennsylvania General Assembly bill record (palegis.us); 6abc; abc27; WFMZ.
