Top Stories

Seoul’s chip rout deepens: the Kospi falls 5.5%, SK hynix about 10%, and the selling reaches Tokyo, Europe and Nasdaq futures

A second, larger leg of Monday’s selloff in the world’s memory makers. The trigger most often cited is a Chinese rival’s claim of denser memory; the bigger driver may be how far these stocks had run.

South Korea’s benchmark closed at 6,035.35, down 348.89 points or 5.47% (Yahoo Finance, exchange close). It fell as much as 7.4% during the session, according to figures relayed from Investing.com. Korea JoongAng Daily, which called it “Black Tuesday,” reports Samsung Electronics down 7.39% to 232,000 won and SK hynix down 10.09% to 1.443 million won. Those component moves come from search summaries rather than the exchange’s own data. The index is now roughly 14% below its Oct. 6 close, after two sessions in which foreign investors sold heavily.

The selling spread. Tokyo’s Nikkei 225, closed Monday for Sports Day, fell 1.82% to 67,777.52, with SoftBank among the decliners. In Europe, where the broad market is up, Reuters reports ASML down about 3.3% and STMicroelectronics and Infineon about 5% each. In New York, Nasdaq 100 futures were near 30,754 at about 6:23, down 0.47% from Monday’s settlement, while Dow futures were up 0.69%.

Disputed cause Three explanations are circulating, and no one can prove which matters most. First, China’s CXMT said it has made progress on a denser DRAM cell design and plans a server-memory module this year. It gave no specifications, and analysts quoted by CNBC say CXMT remains behind the leaders. Second, plain profit-taking: Seoul had more than doubled over the year, and Samsung had just reported a record quarter. Third, Monday’s US chip losses, which Seoul then followed. Some analysts call the selloff overdone.

A further data point is pressure from a different direction. An IDC count, reported by Reuters, puts Nvidia’s share of China’s data-centre AI-chip market at 34% in the first half, on unit shipments down 44% from a year earlier. Huawei shipped 572,000 units, up from about 300,000.

Why it matters Memory has been the most leveraged part of the AI build-out: Micron, Sandisk and SK hynix rose many times over in a year. When that end of the chain cracks, the question is whether it is a valuation reset or the first sign that AI spending is slowing. Nothing in the data says spending is slowing; Microsoft is planning gigawatt-scale deployments of its own chip for 2027. Exposure, not direction: Micron, Sandisk, Western Digital, Seagate, the optical names (Ciena, Lumentum), equipment makers (ASML, Applied, Lam), and the semiconductor fund SMH, which closed Monday at 590.99, 12% below its 52-week high.
What’s next How US memory names open; ASML’s results and order guidance before Wednesday’s open; TSMC on Thursday, where the fourth-quarter outlook matters more than the quarter it has already pre-announced. Samsung’s full results follow Oct. 29.

Oil gives back two dollars as Saudi Arabia says exports are flowing, though Riyadh’s airport stays shut until Saturday

Monday’s one-month-high settlement is already under water. Brent is pricing a war that hits airports and ships, not oil terminals.

Brent was $104.50 at about 6:22, down $2.15 or 2.0% from Monday’s $106.65 settlement, and WTI was $91.85, also down 2.0% (Yahoo Finance, delayed). OilPrice.com points to Saudi assurances that exports continue, higher loadings through Hormuz and the kingdom’s East–West pipeline still running. FXEmpire reports that Russia’s deputy prime minister said diesel exports could reach 3.0 to 3.5 million tonnes, which would ease the market that Ukraine’s refinery strikes have tightened.

The fighting has not stopped. Saudi Arabia extended the closure of Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport through Oct. 17 and opened an inquiry into why its air defences failed (AP via PBS). The coalition said it intercepted a missile over Riyadh and two over Khamis Mushait on Monday (CNN, Fox). Death tolls from the airport strikes still differ: AP counts at least 13 in the main attack, Saudi authorities 12. The Houthi-run health ministry says Saudi strikes killed 9 in al-Jawf, including 5 children; that figure has not been independently confirmed. The president said after a call with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman that the attacks were “not acceptable” and that the US “may” join strikes. We found no sign that it has. The Houthis threatened a “swift and painful” response if it does.

At sea, the UK maritime agency reported another outbound tanker struck by a projectile on Monday, with no one claiming it. Al Jazeera, citing the agency, counts nine tanker attacks in October. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard says it struck an LPG tanker that used an “illegal route” (CBS). US Central Command says a US jet struck a cargo ship while enforcing the blockade on Iran. Tehran is sending two messages on the US reply to its Hormuz conditions: its security council chief calls it insufficient, while the foreign ministry says it is still reviewing it.

Why it matters Lower oil is the best news the bond market has had in a week, a day before a CPI report in which gasoline is expected to lift the headline. The shape of the war has not changed. Every strike so far has hit people, airports and ships; none has hit a Gulf export terminal. That is why Brent can fall $2 on reassurance and could rise $10 on a single hit.

The IMF holds global growth at 3.1% and says the world’s resilience is “reaching its limits”

The Fund’s baseline assumes Hormuz reopens from March 2027. Its oil assumption is now $96, and AI investment is doing much of the work.

The World Economic Outlook, released at the IMF and World Bank meetings in Bangkok, puts 2026 global growth at 3.1%, a tenth above July’s update, and 2027 at 3.4% (CNBC, The National, IndexBox). It has US growth at 2.3% in both years. Oil is assumed to average about $96 a barrel this year and about $87 next. Global inflation is put at 4.7% for 2026. The Middle East and North Africa is the only region forecast to shrink this year, by about 2%. Saudi Arabia’s 2026 forecast falls to −3.3% from +1.7% in July, and Kuwait, Qatar and Bahrain face double-digit contractions.

The Fund estimates the Hormuz closure would have taken 0.9 points off world growth without rerouted supply, higher output elsewhere and inventory drawdowns; it puts the actual hit at about 0.3 points. Chief economist Silvana Tenreyro said resilience is “reaching its limits.” Growth is also narrowing: 32 economies now produce 95% of it, down from 54 in April. The Fund warns that global oil inventories are falling toward critical levels. An adverse scenario has oil near $130 through the first quarter of 2027.

Why it matters Brent is $8 above the Fund’s assumption even after this morning’s fall. The forecast relies on AI investment to offset the war, which is why a crack in chip stocks is a macro story and not only a sector one.

No charges and no new statement a day after federal agents searched Rep. Ilhan Omar’s home

The Justice Department calls it part of an ongoing criminal investigation; Omar calls it political targeting three weeks before the midterms.

Homeland Security Investigations agents searched the Minnesota Democrat’s Washington-area home on Monday morning under a “judicially authorized search warrant secured by the Department of Justice,” a department spokesperson said. The statement did not name Omar. The search was confirmed by NPR, NBC, ABC, CBS, CNN and others. No one has been charged. The case is at the investigation stage.

What is being investigated has not been stated on the record. Unnamed sources told ABC and CBS it concerns immigration paperwork, partly forms filed by her late father when she was a child. NBC’s sources describe a years-long inquiry involving the US attorney in Minneapolis that “goes beyond” immigration and fraud. Border adviser Tom Homan confirmed an investigation on Fox News but referred questions to Attorney General Todd Blanche and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin. Omar said the search was “an attempt to target political enemies and weaponize federal law enforcement.” House Democratic leaders called it “deeply disturbing” and noted it came less than 30 days before the Nov. 3 election. We found no statement from Speaker Johnson.

What’s next Whether prosecutors file charges or unseal anything. Long-standing Justice Department practice discourages overt investigative steps close to an election.

Moscow still has not confirmed the energy truce; a Russian drone hits a Kyiv apartment block

The White House says the truce is in place. Russia’s strikes on Ukraine’s power system say otherwise.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov repeated on Monday that “contacts are ongoing” and would not say whether Russia had agreed to the halt on energy strikes that the president announced on Sunday (NBC, PBS). Euronews reports this morning that the president “insists” the truce is in place. Russia struck Kharkiv’s CHP-5 power plant at least twice after the announcement, and a plant near Kyiv, according to ABC, CNN and PBS. The EU’s foreign-policy chief said she saw no sign that Russia would respect it.

On Monday evening a Russian drone hit a 25-storey apartment building in Kyiv’s Darnytskyi district. Prosecutors say two people were killed and at least seven injured, including a 17-year-old; Mayor Vitali Klitschko put the injured at 11 (Ukrainska Pravda, Euronews). A similar announcement in September did not hold.

Microsoft’s Maia 300 and Meta’s first frontier models put the AI buyers ahead of the AI chipmakers

Monday’s split — Meta up 3.4%, Nvidia down 1.3% — has a product story behind it.

Microsoft unveiled Maia 300, its third in-house accelerator, built mainly for inference. The company says it will become the main system running AI in its products from 2027, in “gigawatt-scale” deployments, and claims 40% more tokens per megawatt than unnamed “top GPU systems” (Reuters via TheNextWeb, UPI). Those are vendor claims that no one has tested. The chip is built on TSMC’s N2P process with HBM4 memory, so it shifts money within the chip supply chain more than out of it.

Meta released Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, the first frontier-class models from its Superintelligence Labs. They accept text, image, audio and video and have a context window of about a million tokens. They are available through Meta’s API and coding product, not as downloadable weights (TechCrunch, CNBC, Reuters). Meta closed up 3.38% at $742.96 and Microsoft up 1.14%, while Nvidia fell 1.29% (Yahoo Finance closes).

Science

Human neurons in a mouse brain. Sergiu Pașca’s lab at Stanford grafted human cortical organoids into mice engineered to lack most of their cortex and hippocampus, producing what the team calls “xenocortical” mice (Nature, Oct. 8 issue). The work is a step toward studying human brain circuits and disorders in a living system. It also sharpens the ethical questions about how much human tissue an animal model should carry.

Ageing brains, across species. A Nature highlight on Oct. 9 reports that chimpanzee and gorilla brains keep their mass with age, unlike human brains. That points to a human-specific vulnerability rather than a universal primate pattern.

The Nobel week, for the record. Medicine went to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for light-gated ion channels and optogenetics. Physics went to Francis Halzen for the IceCube observatory and high-energy astrophysical neutrinos. Chemistry went to Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai for non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric synthesis. Economics went to Robert Hall and Michael Woodford. These come from secondary reporting; we could not open the Nobel Foundation’s own pages this morning.

Pennsylvania

The skill-game stay expires today; State Police can seize machines from tomorrow

The state Supreme Court’s 120-day stay of its June ruling that skill games are illegal slot machines ends today. On Oct. 9 the court, in one-sentence orders without explanation, refused a bipartisan request from Sens. Gene Yaw and Anthony Williams and Rep. Jonathan Fritz for another 120 days, and an emergency petition from VFW, American Legion and fire-company groups (PA Capital-Star, WKOK). Attorney General Dave Sunday opposed any extension, and the Legislature has passed no regulation bill. The dispute now is over what compliance means. The attorney general and State Police say unplugging a machine is not enough and owners should have distributors remove them; penalties include seizure of machines and cash, and criminal charges. Pace-O-Matic, the largest supplier, says the ruling requires the games to be “rendered inoperable,” not removed. No court has settled that question. Removals are already under way in Philadelphia, Altoona and some Dauphin County VFW posts, and WTAJ reports layoffs at an Altoona operator.

Two budgets, no tax increases: the county’s $1.25 billion plan and the city’s reserve drawdown head to council

County Executive Sara Innamorato’s 2027 operating budget is $1.25 billion, up 4.7%, or about 2% excluding a one-time bond payment and juvenile-services costs the state largely reimburses. Neither council has voted.
