Investors are splitting the AI spenders in two — Alphabet is indicated about 10% lower and Microsoft about 3% higher — as Nasdaq futures slip about 1% before the first GDP and inflation numbers since the Fed, an ECB decision, a seven-year auction and Amazon tonight.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

Top Stories

Investors split the AI spenders: Alphabet indicated 10% lower, Microsoft 3% higher

Both companies are growing cloud revenue fast. The difference the market is pricing is who pays for the build-out out of cash flow, and who is selling stock and bonds to do it.

Alphabet shares were indicated at about $291 before the open, about 10% below Wednesday’s close of $324.89, which would erase roughly $400 billion of market value (AP). Its third-quarter revenue rose 25% to $127.6 billion, just short of estimates, and Google Cloud more than doubled to $30.7 billion. But earnings per share were $2.12 on a GAAP basis, against consensus near $3.00, after an $8.7 billion loss on equity holdings, and free cash flow was −$9.1 billion after $51.4 billion of capital spending (8-K). Analysts had expected positive free cash flow of about $2.7 billion (CNBC). Alphabet kept its 2026 capital budget at $195–205 billion and said 2027 spending will “increase significantly,” with details on its next call; a figure of $300 billion circulating in some reports is not a company number. On the call, executives said search revenue growth of 12% reflected more use of its AI Mode.

Microsoft was indicated about 2.7% higher, at about $546. Azure grew 48%, disclosed in dollars for the first time at about $33.2 billion, and Microsoft guided December-quarter Azure growth to 49–50% in constant currency, against about 44.6% expected, saying demand still exceeds supply. It guided revenue to $96.0–97.1 billion. Free cash flow was $18.6 billion. Chief financial officer Amy Hood said quarterly capital spending would top $50 billion and trimmed the calendar-2026 total to about $170 billion from $175 billion because third-party capacity is arriving late, as reported by Fortune and others. Shares of CoreWeave and other rented-capacity providers fell after hours on that comment.

The split reaches past the two companies. Nasdaq 100 futures are down about 1% while Dow futures are up. Alphabet is one of the largest weights in both indexes, so its move alone accounts for much of the futures’ dip. Chip suppliers ran up into the reports — the VanEck semiconductor fund is up 8.5% in five sessions — and both companies’ spending plans remain enormous, which is supportive for them.

Why it matters Meta was punished last week for issuing stock to fund AI, and now Alphabet for burning cash. Amazon reports tonight with a capital plan of about $220 billion; its free cash flow and AWS growth (against Microsoft’s 48% and Google Cloud’s 103%) will show which side of the line it sits on. Our call: the Nasdaq Composite closes lower today than Wednesday (60–75%), given futures down about 1% three hours before the open and a 10% indicated drop in its second-largest weight; a strong GDP print or Amazon expectations could narrow it.

GDP and inflation at 8:30: the first test of the Fed’s December hike pricing

Consensus is 3.2% growth for the third quarter and 3.6% headline PCE inflation for September, with core rising 0.3% for the month.

The Commerce Department releases the advance estimate of third-quarter GDP and September personal income and spending at 8:30 AM. Forecasters expect annualised growth of 3.2% (range 2.7–3.5%) after 2.2% in the second quarter, and the Atlanta Fed’s GDPNow model was near 3.3% in one preview. PCE inflation, the Fed’s preferred gauge, is expected at 3.6% year on year (from 3.4%) with a 0.5% monthly rise; core PCE at 3.1% (from 3.0%) and +0.3% for the month (AP, Investing.com). Weekly jobless claims are expected near 196,000–199,000 after 194,000.

The inputs point the same way: September’s CPI rose 0.65% for the month and 3.7% from a year earlier, and core CPI 0.33% and 2.6% (FRED). Payroll growth has stalled, with 29,000 jobs added in September and unemployment at 4.2%. Record imports in September’s trade data will weigh on the GDP headline, so final sales to private domestic purchasers is the cleaner read of demand. The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% on Wednesday with two officials voting to hike, and futures price a December hike at roughly 81–85% (CME FedWatch as reported by CNN, Reuters and TradingKey).

What’s next Our call: September core PCE rises 0.3% or more for the month (55–70%), based on the consensus and core CPI’s 0.33%. We put the chance that advance GDP comes in at 3.0% or higher at 45–60%, as logged Wednesday.

Risks & Scenarios

1. Inflation plus supply (the main risk). Core PCE at 0.4% or more and a seven-year tail would lift the 10-year above its 5.13% October high and pull growth stocks down further. We put the chance the seven-year auction tails — clears above its 1 PM when-issued yield — at 45–60%, based on September’s weak sale and this week’s long-end selloff, offset by yields now high enough to draw buyers.

2. The AI funding question spreads. If Amazon tonight shows weak free cash flow beside a rising capital budget, the market’s penalty for spending could move from individual names to the whole megacap group that dominates the index weights. Suppliers that ran up 10–25% this week are exposed if spending forecasts are questioned.

3. Gulf escalation after Tuesday. Reports of both a US offer of talks and a possible post-midterm strike campaign leave oil with two-way risk. Brent already trades about $13 above where Goldman expects it at year-end ($90) and in line with UBS ($110). OPEC+ meets Sunday.

4. The upside case. A soft core reading with solid real growth would ease hike pricing, lower long yields and let the rotation into cyclicals and small caps run without the tech drag. We see this as the less likely outcome today.

The United States

Judge Carter denies the Justice Department’s bid to set aside 20,000 California mail ballots; the department appeals

The written order says separating a vote is itself the harm. The department says it will ask the Ninth Circuit for an emergency order.

US District Judge David O. Carter of the Central District of California on Wednesday denied the Justice Department’s request for a temporary restraining order. The department had asked that California and Los Angeles County separate the mail ballots of more than 20,000 people it had flagged as “likely noncitizens.” Those ballots will now be processed like any others. Carter wrote that “once a vote is segregated, lasting harm has occurred,” that the request would disrupt an election already under way, and that the claimed emergency was the department’s own doing. He pointed to remedies that already exist: federal prosecution of individuals who vote illegally, and California’s procedure for cancelling ineligible registrations (Washington Post, NBC, CBS, CNN).

First Assistant US Attorney Bill Essayli said the department has filed a notice of appeal and will “immediately seek an emergency order from the Ninth Circuit.” On Monday a Ninth Circuit panel dismissed the department’s first appeal 2–1 because Carter had not yet ruled. The case is at the stage of a denied restraining order with an appeal noticed. The department sued on Oct. 15, arguing that federal law barring noncitizen voting overrides the state’s handling of the ballots.

Washington Post; NBC; CBS; CNN; Democracy Docket and Law Dork (Ninth Circuit dismissal).

Science

Heat deaths. The 2026 Lancet Countdown, published in The Lancet on Wednesday by researchers from 30 countries, attributes almost 3 million heat-related deaths in 2016–2023 to warming and links more than 70% of 2023’s heat deaths to human-caused climate change. The attribution rests on modelling, with the uncertainty that implies. The World Meteorological Organization separately reported that atmospheric carbon dioxide reached a record 426.8 parts per million in 2025.

A slower-leaking solar material. An Oxford team reports in Nature that bismuth sulfide, a cheap and non-toxic semiconductor, loses its charge carriers about 75% more slowly than conventional absorbers at room temperature. No working cell has been built from it yet, so any efficiency gain is still to be shown.

Medicine. Geron stopped its Phase 3 trial of imetelstat in myelofibrosis after its data monitoring committee said it was unlikely to meet its overall-survival goal (company 8-K). Genmab said an interim look at its LiGeR-HN1 trial showed petosemtamab cut the risk of death by half in first-line head and neck cancer; that is a company release, not yet peer-reviewed.

Space

Vulcan returns Nov. 13. United Launch Alliance is targeting Friday, Nov. 13, for Vulcan’s first flight since a solid-rocket-booster nozzle anomaly in February, carrying 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites from Cape Canaveral, pending range approval (Spaceflight Now). It is the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon has bought. ULA says it changed the nozzle design but has not published its findings, and the Space Force will review flight data before putting national-security payloads back on the rocket.

SpaceX’s compute business shows up in a rival’s filing. Anthropic’s S-1 discloses an agreement to spend up to $120.5 billion through 2029 with SpaceX.AI for Nvidia-GPU computing, about $3.1 billion a month once capacity is fully delivered, which the filing expects in December; the contracts become cancellable on 90 days’ notice after early 2027. Fitch left SpaceX’s BBB+ rating unchanged, putting its cash at $89.9 billion and debt at $38.0 billion at Sept. 30. SpaceX shares closed at $145.23 on Wednesday, 36% below their 52-week high.

Vietnam became the 78th country to sign the Artemis Accords.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

A councilwoman proposes a 2% city tax on unearned income; the mayor questions whether it is legal

Pittsburgh City Councilwoman Barb Warwick introduced a 2% tax on unearned income — dividends, gains from property sales, estate and trust distributions, and rental income — which she estimates would raise about $51 million a year, with collection probably starting no earlier than 2028. Mayor Corey O’Connor said he was not told in advance, questioned its legality and said he would not count on the money; Warwick says it fits the city’s taxing authority, and WPXI reports a legal challenge is expected if it passes. It has been introduced, not voted on. The mayor’s preliminary 2027 budget said the city’s fiscal crisis is arriving sooner than expected.

Pittsburgh Post-Gazette; WPXI; CBS Pittsburgh.
