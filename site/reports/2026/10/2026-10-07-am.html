Top Stories

A $39 billion 10-year auction, then the Fed’s minutes: the long end’s hardest hour of the week

Tuesday’s 3-year sale cleared without a tail and yields eased, which helped the S&P 500 to a record. This morning yields are back up, and the two biggest tests of the week come an hour apart.

Treasury reopens its August 10-year note for $39 billion at 1:00 PM. At 2:00 the Federal Reserve releases the minutes of its September 15–16 meeting, the one at which it raised its target range a quarter-point to 3.75–4.00%. It was the first increase since 2023, and all 12 voters backed it. In the projections released then, 12 of 18 officials penciled in another hike this year (meeting statement and summary of projections, as reported in previews).

Tuesday went well for bonds. Treasury’s fiscal data shows the $58 billion 3-year sold at 4.932% with a bid-to-cover of 2.62, down from 2.72 last time. Indirect bidders, the category that includes foreign central banks, took 57.6% of the competitive bids, below their recent average, and direct bidders took 31.7%. The yield was the highest at a 3-year auction since 2006, but it cleared slightly below where it traded beforehand. On the official curve the 3-year fell 9 basis points to 4.88%, the 2-year 5 to 4.79% and the 10-year 4 to 5.27%. The 30-year ended at 5.64%.

That relief has partly reversed. CNBC puts the 10-year near 5.31% this morning, up about 3 basis points, and FXStreet’s headline describes futures falling “as US Treasury yields rebound ahead of FOMC Minutes.” That is a description of timing, not a confirmed cause. The 10-year’s official close of 5.31% on Monday was its highest since 2002; it touched about 5.33% intraday on October 1.

Fed officials have kept their options open. San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly, speaking Tuesday, backed September’s hike and said whether more are needed depends on whether the shocks “fade or compound.” If tariffs, oil prices from the Middle East conflict and AI investment “prove to be conventional shocks,” she said, “then we may not need more” (Reuters via AOL; FXStreet). Last week Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the range may need to rise “an additional 50 basis points or more,” while New York Fed President John Williams and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson said the Fed has time. CME FedWatch on Tuesday priced about a 22% chance of a hike on October 28, down from about 51% a week earlier (Benzinga, citing CME). FXStreet put the odds of a hike by December near 70%.

Why it matters Most of the rise in long yields has come from real yields and term premium, not inflation expectations: the 10-year real yield was 2.95% on Monday and 10-year breakeven inflation 2.36% (FRED). That is the profile of buyers demanding more pay to hold long bonds. A sloppy 10-year sale would say that demand is still not there at 5.3%. Minutes showing broad support for further hikes would make it harder for the front end to rally if the auction goes badly.
What’s next Results post at about 1:00; watch the yield against its 1:00 pre-auction level (the “tail”), the bid-to-cover and the indirect share. Minutes at 2:00. Fed Governor Christopher Waller speaks on the outlook Thursday morning, a few hours before the $22 billion 30-year reopening.

Go deeper

What a reopening means. Treasury is selling more of a note first issued in August, which now has 9 years and 10 months to run. Reopenings tend to draw slightly less fresh interest than new issues. The coupon auctions settle October 15. Treasury also sells a $75 billion 17-week bill at 11:30.

What the minutes could change. The statement and dots are already known. The minutes add the argument: how many officials wanted a bigger move or a pause, how much weight they put on oil and tariffs versus a softening labor market, and whether “many” or “some” participants saw further increases as likely. Since that meeting, the labor data have weakened sharply. September payrolls rose 29,000 with 60,000 of downward revisions, and unemployment rose to 4.2%. But price indicators have risen: ISM’s services prices index hit 74.0, its highest since July 2022. The minutes predate both reports, so read them as a description of the committee’s reasoning, not its current view.

The curve. At Tuesday’s close the 2s10s spread was 48 basis points, 3-month to 10-year 106 and 5s30s 61. All three are near the steepest of this cycle, with the steepening coming from the long end rising, not the short end falling.

Oil is back above $101 as ship attacks around Hormuz reach nine this month and Houthi missiles target Gulf airports

Gulf exports are recovering under US escort even as the attacks increase. Overnight, the war in Yemen reached Aden’s airport and the skies north of Riyadh.

Brent crude for December was near $101.88 a barrel at about 6:30 AM, up about 1.3% from Tuesday’s settlement near $100.58. US crude (WTI) was near $89.99. UK Maritime Trade Operations has counted nine attacks on commercial ships so far in October, already half of September’s total, according to Bloomberg. Late Monday a Saudi-flagged tanker and a South Korean-owned tanker were hit minutes apart by unidentified projectiles as they left the Gulf. Iran has not claimed the attacks; Bloomberg, NBC and CNBC attribute them to Iran.

The flows are moving the other way. Bloomberg calculates Middle East crude exports averaged 18.6 million barrels a day over the latest 14 days, back to pre-war levels, with traffic relying on a US-escorted route along the coast of Oman. CNBC reports Gulf exports averaged about 10.3 million barrels a day in the week to Saturday, about 23% below pre-war. The two figures measure different things and should not be compared directly. Both agree that attacks are rising even as volumes recover.

In Yemen, government forces backed by Saudi Arabia say they launched an offensive Monday aimed at the capital, Sanaa, and have taken Mocha on the Red Sea coast. The Houthis say they have lost no ground. Early Wednesday, Yemen’s Transport Ministry said Houthi missiles and drones targeted Aden International Airport, with one missile landing near the runway and a Cairo flight diverted to Jeddah. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a ballistic missile north of Riyadh (The National). On Tuesday the Houthis claimed attacks on Saudi forces in Jazan and on the airports at Abha and Riyadh; the coalition said it downed four drones (France24).

Why it matters Brent has held above $100 for most of the past two weeks despite the G7 and IEA agreeing on October 2 to release up to 100 million barrels over four months. The Energy Information Administration raised its 2026 Brent forecast to $96.32 from $91.01 in its October outlook. For the Fed, this is the energy-inflation channel Daly and Jefferson have both named.
What’s next Official US inventory data at 10:30. The industry group API reported a 2.1-million-barrel crude draw on Tuesday evening (one report). Iran’s foreign minister set out a seven-day plan last week to reopen the strait in exchange for a ceasefire, sanctions relief and the release of frozen assets; no US response has been reported.

Black Hills signs on to power a Google data center in Wyoming with $1.8 billion of new gas generation

The second Google power deal in two days. Monday it was 20 years of nuclear from Constellation; Tuesday evening a regional utility filed for a gas-fired build in Cheyenne.

Black Hills Corp. said in an SEC filing at 4:19 PM Tuesday that it has signed definitive agreements, effective September 30 and running through 2048, to serve a planned Google data center in Cheyenne, Wyoming. The utility will build about $1.8 billion of company-owned natural-gas generation and provide up to 590 megawatts of grid service, and will manage about 2.1 gigawatts of third-party resources through a private microgrid. It expects the arrangement to add about $150 million of net income in 2030 and about $2.4 billion of unlevered free cash flow through 2048. The shares rose about 5% after hours, Benzinga reported.

It follows Google’s 20-year agreement with Constellation Energy, announced Tuesday, for power from new and uprated nuclear capacity in the PJM grid. Constellation closed up 12.2% and Vistra 10.8%, and the utilities ETF (XLU) rose 3.0%. Bloomberg framed the Constellation deal as a response to PJM’s proposal that large new loads bring their own power.

Why it matters Hyperscalers are now paying utilities directly to build generation rather than waiting for grid connections. That moves part of the AI capital-spending cycle onto regulated utility balance sheets, with long contracts behind it, and gives investors a second route into the AI build-out beyond chips.

Tropical Storm Isaias forms in the Gulf and is forecast to hit the coast this weekend as the season’s first hurricane

Forecasters expect hurricane watches for part of the northern Gulf Coast later today, with landfall anywhere from Louisiana to the Florida Panhandle.

Tropical Depression Nine formed Tuesday in the southern Gulf of Mexico and became Tropical Storm Isaias early Wednesday. At 5:00 AM it was about 285 miles west of Progreso, Mexico, moving east-northeast at 8 mph with 40 mph winds, according to the National Hurricane Center advisory as relayed by AccuWeather and CNN. The forecast has it becoming a hurricane Thursday, turning north and approaching the northern Gulf Coast late Friday or early Saturday, with peak winds near 100 mph. Outlets disagree on whether it would still be a Category 2 or a Category 1 at landfall. It would be the latest first Atlantic hurricane of the satellite era, in a season suppressed by a strong El Niño.

Why it matters The track runs across offshore oil and gas platforms and toward the refining belt of Louisiana. That matters for gasoline and diesel at a time when the Strait of Hormuz is already constraining supply. It also matters for property insurers. These are exposures, not predicted price moves.

Russia fires about 50 missiles and 120 drones at Ukraine on Putin’s birthday

The overnight attack came a day after Ukraine sent hundreds of drones at the Moscow region. The death toll differs by source.

Ukraine’s air force said it shot down 5 ballistic missiles, 39 cruise missiles and 117 drones overnight into Wednesday, out of a larger salvo. Reuters counts at least eight killed: two in Kyiv’s Darnytskyi district, four in Pryluky in the Chernihiv region and two in the Poltava region. President Volodymyr Zelensky put the toll at 11, and Ukraine’s UNN news agency at 12, with more than 40 injured. A missile collapsed the entrance of an apartment block in Pryluky, and Kyiv’s Dovzhenko Film Studio was damaged. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said Russia’s generals had given Vladimir Putin, who turned 74 today, “his favorite gift — war crimes.” Russia’s Defense Ministry had said it would intensify strikes on Kyiv.

The night before, Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said about 650 drones flew toward the Moscow region and that 105 were downed near the capital. A fire hit the Volodarskaya oil pumping station, which supplies jet fuel to Moscow’s airports. Russian reports put the dead at two to three. Zelensky said on October 4 that the US has proposed technical-level talks among the US, Ukraine and Russia by the end of October; Russia has not confirmed them.

The Pentagon says it has stopped using Anthropic’s AI

The phase-out ordered in February is complete, a Defense Department official told the BBC. It comes as Anthropic is reported to be preparing a November listing.

A Defense Department official told the BBC on Tuesday that the department “has ceased the use of Anthropic products.” President Trump ordered federal agencies in February to stop using the company’s technology, with a six-month phase-down for the Pentagon, after Anthropic refused to drop restrictions on mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth designated it a supply-chain risk. Reuters reported earlier this year that Anthropic’s Claude was built into Palantir’s Maven Smart System, which the military uses for intelligence analysis and targeting. The BBC’s sources say it was used as recently as last week in operations against Iran. Single source The BBC report has not yet been matched by a second outlet.

Why it matters For Palantir, it marks the end of a migration off a model built deep into one of its flagship defense products. For Anthropic, which Bloomberg and Reuters report is targeting a November IPO, it settles the status of its US defense business before investors see the prospectus. Anthropic is private; we do not quote a price.

Risks & Scenarios

Hawkish minutes. We put the odds that the minutes describe a majority of officials expecting further increases this year at 65–80%. Basis: 12 of 18 projected another hike, the vote was unanimous, and Logan and others have since argued for more. The market reaction depends on how much weight officials put on the softening labor market, which the minutes cannot reflect fully.

An October 28 hike. We put the odds that the Fed raises rates again at its October 28–29 meeting at 15–30%. Basis: futures price about 22%; the September payroll miss and Daly’s conditional language argue for waiting; services prices and oil above $100 argue the other way. The September CPI on October 14 is the swing factor.

Isaias. We put the odds that Isaias makes landfall on the US Gulf Coast as a hurricane by October 11 at 65–80%. Basis: the hurricane center’s forecast of hurricane strength Thursday and a northern-Gulf approach Friday night, against disagreement on intensity at landfall and the usual track uncertainty three days out.

What would change the picture. A credible step toward reopening Hormuz would lower oil and, with it, the case for more Fed hikes. A confirmed strike on Saudi export infrastructure, or Isaias shutting Gulf refining, would do the reverse. In equities, the risk is concentration: the index is at a record while most stocks are below their 50-day averages.

The United States

Trump says his super PAC will pay for ads the government had funded. After criticism from both parties, President Trump said Tuesday that his super PAC, not taxpayers, will pay for a series of ads promoting his administration. Congress is out. A stopgap funds the government at current levels through December 11, so there is no shutdown. The Senate’s next voting session is November 9.

The midterms, 27 days out. Polling averages of the generic congressional ballot show Democrats ahead: Nate Silver’s average D+9.6 (updated October 6) and FiftyPlusOne D+8. Averages are not forecasts, and generic-ballot leads do not translate directly into seats.

Science

Nobel Prize in Chemistry: mirror-image molecules. The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences this morning awarded the chemistry prize to Henri B. Kagan of Université Paris-Saclay, 95, and Kenso Soai of the Tokyo University of Science “for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis.” Many molecules come in left- and right-handed forms that behave differently in the body, and drugmakers usually need only one. Kagan showed that a catalyst that is not perfectly one-handed can still produce a product that is more one-handed than itself. Soai found a reaction in which a slight excess of one hand in the product catalyzes more of the same, amplifying it. That is one candidate explanation for why life uses only one hand of amino acids and sugars. Nobelprize.org, NPR and CNN confirm the award.

This week’s other prizes. Physics went Tuesday to Francis Halzen of the University of Wisconsin–Madison for the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and its discovery of high-energy neutrinos from beyond the galaxy. Medicine went Monday to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for optogenetics. Literature is Thursday and Peace Friday.

South Korea’s Nuri flies again. Korea’s homegrown Nuri rocket launched 15 satellites from the Naro Space Center on Tuesday night local time. It was the country’s first launch of 2026 and Nuri’s fourth straight success (Space.com).

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Innamorato proposes a $1.25 billion county budget with no tax increase

County Executive Sara Innamorato introduced a 2027 operating budget Tuesday of about $1.25 billion, up 4.7% from this year. It holds the property-tax rate flat and has no layoffs. It eliminates about 150 vacant positions to save about $9 million. It adds about $21 million for juvenile placement, child protection and family services, and makes a one-time payment on a 2010 bond. The Post-Gazette frames it as cuts made as “federal policies hit home.” Separately, the acting controller’s report on 2025 says last year’s 36% property-tax increase raised $132 million but gave only temporary relief. County Council must pass the budget before year-end.

Why it matters If council agrees, Bridgeville and South Fayette homeowners face no county tax increase next year. The controller’s warning suggests the pressure returns in 2028.
