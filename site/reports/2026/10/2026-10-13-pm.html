Top Stories

The banks split: Goldman crushes estimates, JPMorgan misses for the first time in nearly three years, and Citi falls on a beat

Trading desks had a strong quarter, led by equities. The stocks traded on the details, and all four chief executives sounded a warning about inflation and rising rates.

The four biggest US banks reported before the open, and the results did not move together.

  • Goldman Sachs earned $17.01 a share on net revenue of $18.68 billion. Consensus was about $13.07 to $13.65 and $16.84 billion, depending on the survey (company release; Newsquawk; Reuters). Equities trading revenue rose 72% from a year earlier to $6.44 billion; fixed income was flat at $3.47 billion and below forecasts. The bank says earnings would have been about $18 a share without a $450 million accelerated charitable contribution. Chief executive David Solomon called the quarter “very strong,” with “the second highest” net revenue in its history. The stock gave up an early gain of about 4% and closed down 0.2% at $891.91.
  • JPMorgan Chase earned $15.3 billion, or $5.57 a share. Excluding $658 million of securities losses, it earned $5.75. Both are below the $5.90–5.94 consensus. Reuters called it the bank’s first quarterly miss in nearly three years. Managed revenue rose 8% to $50.9 billion, slightly under forecasts; net interest income rose 13% to $27.1 billion, and the bank guided to about $28 billion for the fourth quarter. Expenses rose 12%. Jamie Dimon wrote that “large fiscal deficits, sticky inflation, elevated asset prices, rising interest rates and geopolitical conditions continue to shift under the surface,” and said trading and banking activity “did moderate somewhat.” The stock fell 3.5% before the open, briefly turned higher, and closed down 0.9%.
  • Citigroup earned $2.85 a share on $23.8 billion of revenue, against $2.65 and about $23.7 billion expected (company release; Newsquawk). Profit rose about 40%. The quarter includes a $726 million goodwill impairment tied to the sale of a 25% stake in Banamex, and the bank raised its full-year return-on-tangible-equity goal to “above 11%.” The stock fell 5.6% to $124.53, its worst day on our watchlist in weeks. Reuters had it down about 1.8% in the morning; we found no reporting on why the decline deepened in the afternoon.
  • Wells Fargo earned $1.87 a share, slightly above forecasts, but revenue of $21.87 billion and net interest income of $12.52 billion both missed (StockStory via Yahoo). It set aside $1.03 billion for credit losses and kept its full-year net interest income guidance of about $50 billion. The stock fell 3.1%.

Fortune and the Washington Times both noted that all four banks warned about higher rates and persistent inflation. That is a notable chorus the day before a CPI report, from the lenders that see consumer and corporate credit first.

Why it matters

Goldman’s quarter says the market-making machine is running hot: heavy trading volumes in a volatile, war-priced market. JPMorgan’s miss and Wells Fargo’s revenue shortfall say the more ordinary banking business is feeling a 5% ten-year and a slower deal pace. The KBW bank index had already lagged the S&P 500 by about 11 points over three months (TheStreet), and today’s reactions did not change that. We did not find provision and charge-off figures for JPMorgan or Citi in time for this edition, so the credit read is incomplete.

What’s next

Bank of America, Morgan Stanley and BlackRock report before tomorrow’s open. Bank of America is expected to earn about $1.10 a share; Morgan Stanley estimates range from $2.83 to $3.12.

Sources: company releases and 8-K filings (JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup); Reuters; CNBC; Newsquawk; StockStory via Yahoo Finance; Fortune; Washington Times; TheStreet; Finnhub (closing prices).

Chip-equipment stocks take the next leg down as the chip index falls for a fifth day

After the Kospi’s 5.5% fall, the selling in New York moved from memory makers to the companies that equip them. Nvidia and AMD rose.

The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell about 1.2%, its fifth straight decline (Reuters). Applied Materials led the losses, down 6.7% at $464.24; KLA fell 3.2% and Lam Research 2.7%. Micron fell 3.6% to $953.19, about 4% below the $1,000 mark it lost on Monday. ASML’s US shares fell 1.9% before its results tomorrow. CNBC had Seagate and Western Digital down about 6% during the session; both recovered most of that, closing down 0.8% and 1.6%, and Sandisk finished down just 0.3%.

The bigger names went the other way. Nvidia rose 1.0%, AMD 1.9% and Broadcom 0.7%, and the semiconductor fund SMH closed down only 0.5% at 588.19. That is 12% below its 52-week high.

Disputed cause Reporters tied the selling to China’s CXMT and its claim of a denser DRAM design, and to Broadcom chief executive Hock Tan’s remark on Monday in Singapore that some AI investing resembles a casino. 24/7 Wall St called the equipment selling profit-taking, noting Applied had risen about 80% this year. None of these is established as the cause.

The IMF added a macro frame. Its Global Financial Stability Report, released today in Bangkok, warns that a correction in AI-linked stocks could tighten financial conditions and spill into the real economy (Reuters).

What’s next

ASML reports before tomorrow’s open; consensus is roughly $12.07–12.47 a share on about $13.2 billion of sales. The company no longer reports bookings, so its outlook for 2027 carries the weight. TSMC reports Thursday.

Sources: Reuters; CNBC; 24/7 Wall St; IMF Global Financial Stability Report (via Reuters); Finnhub and Twelve Data (closing prices).

Brent eases to $105.69 on the Russian diesel deal, while WTI edges up

The two benchmarks settled in opposite directions. The Gulf war is unchanged; what moved was the prospect of more Russian diesel.

Brent settled at $105.69, down 96 cents or 0.9%, and WTI at $94.14, up 40 cents, its highest settlement since Sept. 24 (Reuters). Brent traded near $104.50 this morning, so it recovered some ground during the day. Reuters pointed to hopes that the arrangement letting Russia supply diesel, with US sanctions on Russian fuel temporarily lifted, would free up supply. Gasoline futures fell about 4–5% in Yahoo Finance data. That figure may partly reflect a change in the front-month contract, and we could not confirm a settlement.

The deal drew fire in Washington. About a dozen senators, six from each party, urged the president to cancel the arrangement for 4.8 million metric tons of Russian diesel through mid-April and to withdraw the related Treasury sanctions waiver. They called it “a gift to our adversary and a betrayal of our Allies” (CBS, CNBC). Sen. John Barrasso defended the president, who he said “is balancing and has to make tough decisions.”

The Gulf did not quiet. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three cruise missiles and two drones over the Riyadh region and the south, and ballistic missiles near Dhahran, where Aramco is headquartered. No damage was reported (Arab Times, CBS, Jerusalem Post). The Houthis said Saudi Arabia attacked Sanaa after the coalition warned civilians away from an oil depot (Reuters). The president said the Houthis were “behaving badly” and trying to push up oil prices before the midterms; he again declined to say whether the US will strike (Jerusalem Post; CBS and Al Jazeera for the general remarks).

Why it matters

Brent is about $10 above the IMF’s new $96 assumption for this year. The bond market got a small reprieve from today’s easing, but a sharp jump in oil, the most inflation-sensitive input, is one Gulf strike away.

Sources: Reuters (settlements); CBS News; CNBC; Arab Times; Jerusalem Post; Al Jazeera; Yahoo Finance (gasoline futures, delayed).

Axios: the president and Netanyahu discussed a joint strike on Iran before the elections; the White House says that is false

The report, citing US and Israeli officials, describes a call last week. The White House says the president has ruled out attacking Iran before the midterms.

Axios, citing two US officials and an Israeli official, reported that the president and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu discussed a possible major joint operation against Iranian military and nuclear targets before the US and Israeli elections. A US official quoted the president telling Netanyahu to “go ahead and get ready,” which the official said “could mean now… later… never.” Axios said Defense Secretary Hegseth and Central Command were the strongest advocates for resuming strikes.

A White House official called the report “false” and said the president “has said that we will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the midterm elections.” Netanyahu’s office did not deny that the call took place and said it does not comment on their conversations; the Times of Israel reports the office denied that he pressed for war before the election. Israel’s Channel 12 relayed similar reporting.

Iran has not answered the US reply on its Hormuz conditions. On Monday its foreign ministry said it was still reviewing the reply and would answer through Qatar and Pakistan; its security council chief had already called it “insufficient.” Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claimed a third strike in two days on a “non-compliant” tanker. Further UK maritime notices on Tuesday reported more ships hit, though the count differs across live blogs.

Why it matters

A US–Israeli strike on Iran is the largest oil risk on the board, larger than the Houthi campaign. The White House denial is on the record; so is the reporting. We treat the operation as discussed, not decided.

Sources: Axios; Times of Israel; The Media Line (Channel 12); White House statement (via Axios); Reuters via US News (Iran foreign ministry); IranWire; CBS News; ABC News.

Johnson & Johnson raises its outlook and falls 5.7%, dragging health care lower

A beat on sales and adjusted profit was overshadowed by a weaker medical-devices business. GAAP earnings nearly vanished under a talc charge.

J&J reported third-quarter sales of $25.4 billion, up 5.9%, and adjusted earnings of $2.59 a share against about $2.53 expected (8-K, via GuruFocus and Investing.com summaries). It raised its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $11.05–11.15 and sales guidance to $101.0–101.4 billion. GAAP net earnings fell 96% to $210 million, or 9 cents a share, because of a talc-resolution charge and acquisition costs. Innovative Medicine grew about 7.7%; MedTech grew about 2.5%, which coverage described as a miss. The stock closed down 5.7% at $248.61, and Reuters called health care the market’s biggest drag. AbbVie and Merck each fell 2.4%.

UnitedHealth also reported, with adjusted earnings of $4.69 a share against $4.14 expected, and raised its full-year guidance to at least $20.50. It closed down 0.2%. One outlet had it up about 4.5% before the open; we could not confirm that move.

Sources: Johnson & Johnson 8-K (via GuruFocus, Investing.com, Quartz); UnitedHealth release; LSEG; Reuters; Finnhub (closing prices).

Two cargo ships are hit off Bulgaria; one sinks with 11 crew missing

The second such attack in the western Black Sea in a week. Bulgaria summoned Russia’s ambassador but says it lacks evidence to blame anyone.

Suspected drones struck two cargo ships in Bulgaria’s part of the Black Sea on Tuesday. The HS Dignity, flagged in Guinea-Bissau, sank about 17 nautical miles east of Krapets; five crew were rescued and 11 are missing, according to a preliminary count from the regional administration. The Malta-flagged, Ukrainian-owned Chelsea 3 was hit about 18 nautical miles east of Byala; all 15 crew were rescued (Euronews, France 24). Prime Minister Rumen Radev summoned Russia’s ambassador but said, “We lack solid, clear evidence” to accuse any party. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called it “another front of escalation of the Russian war in Ukraine.” On Oct. 6 the Alfa Watan sank in a similar attack, and its crew of about 10 was never found.

Why it matters

Attacks inside EU and NATO waters widen the shipping war beyond the Gulf. War-risk insurance for Black Sea grain and fuel routes is the market channel to watch.

Sources: Euronews; France 24; GreekReporter.

Bumble agrees to go private at $6.05 a share, more than double its pre-bid price

The stock jumped 57% to $5.95, just under the offer.

Bumble agreed to be acquired by Ithaca Holdings, the vehicle of investors Ravi Gupta and John Hegeman, for $6.05 a share in cash, about $1.3 billion. That is a 138% premium to the Oct. 9 close, according to the company’s release filed with the SEC. Blackstone affiliates and founder Whitney Wolfe Herd have already supplied the shareholder approval needed; regulatory approval is pending. The shares closed at $5.95. The 10-cent gap to the offer suggests the market sees a high chance the deal closes.

Sources: Bumble press release (SEC 8-K, Ex. 99.1); Finnhub (closing price).

The Economy

Small business sours a little. The NFIB optimism index fell 0.8 points to 97.9 in September, just under its 52-year average of 98 and below the 98.1 forecast. Its uncertainty index rose a point to 90, far above its average of 68 (NFIB). It was the day’s only US release.

The Fed: inflation talk, no new signal for October.

  • Richmond’s Tom Barkin, speaking at Howard University, called inflation “enduring,” partly because of AI investment, and said acting “makes sense.” He said 2% is still achievable (FXStreet; Investing.com). He does not vote on policy this year. Whether he endorsed a move this month is unclear from the coverage.
  • Governor Christopher Waller, in New Delhi overnight, said inflation pressure from AI spending “could last anywhere from two to three years,” that productivity gains are “still a few years out,” and that higher long-term yields are doing some of the tightening (Scotsman Guide, ActionForex, Bloomberg).
  • New York’s John Williams told Bloomberg he still sees one more hike this year as appropriate, but none needed in 2027 (as relayed by TheStreet and Confluence; we did not see the interview).
  • Boston’s Susan Collins was listed to speak this evening; we found no coverage before press time.

Futures-implied odds of a quarter-point hike on Oct. 28 are about 18–20%, per third-party trackers of CME FedWatch as of Monday. The policy range is 3.75–4.00% after September’s hike.

Tomorrow’s CPI. Consensus (Dow Jones, via CNBC) is +0.6% for September headline and +0.2% core, taking the annual rates to 3.6% and 2.5% from 3.4% and 2.4%. The gap between headline and core is gasoline. A 0.6% headline with 0.2% core would mean energy, not broad prices, is driving the rise. The Fed can tolerate that more easily, unless it spreads into expectations. The 10-year breakeven was 2.33% on Friday, steady, while the 10-year real yield was 2.91% (FRED). A nominal ten-year above 5.2% is still mostly a real rate, not an inflation scare.

Ukraine: heavy overnight strikes despite the “energy ceasefire.” Ukraine’s air force counted five ballistic missiles, nine guided missiles and 108 drones overnight; it said 88 drones were stopped and 17 sites hit. Science

Webb finds a cluster that matured too early. Three papers on the galaxy cluster XLSSC 122, about 10.4 billion light-years away, describe the most distant known cluster that acts as a strong gravitational lens. Its unusually dense core suggests some structures formed faster than the standard cosmological model predicts (Caltech/IPAC release). At least one paper is a preprint, and it is a single object: a puzzle, not an overturned model.

Human fingerprints on warming, reviewed. A University of East Anglia review in Earth’s Future, a peer-reviewed journal, concludes after 30 years of “fingerprint” studies that the evidence for human-caused warming is stronger than ever. It is a synthesis of existing work, not new measurements.

Still current: last week’s Nature paper from TU Wien and Germany’s PTB, describing a thorium-229 nuclear clock that ran on its own for more than 24 hours. It is a step toward clocks that could test fundamental physics, though it does not yet match the best optical clocks.

The 120-day stay of the state Supreme Court’s June ruling that cash-paying skill games are illegal slot machines runs out tonight.
