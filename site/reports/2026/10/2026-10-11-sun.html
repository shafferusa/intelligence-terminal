Today’s News

A missile kills 12 at Riyadh’s main airport, an American among them, and Trump says the US will “look at” joining strikes on the Houthis

It is the deadliest attack on a Gulf Arab state since the Iran war began, and the third hit on the same airport in a week. Flights there are suspended until at least Monday night.

Saudi Arabia’s General Authority of Civil Aviation said early Sunday that a missile struck the terminal complex at King Khalid International Airport on Saturday, killing 12 people and wounding 309, some critically. The dead were four Saudis, two Bangladeshis and one person each from the United States, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, the Palestinian territories and Sudan. Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed that an American was killed. The authority said damage was still being assessed and extended its suspension of flights until at least midnight Monday, Saudi time (5 PM ET Monday). Arab News, Gulf News, Al Jazeera, CNBC and others all carry the same figures. Some reports, citing Western officials, say they could still rise.

The Saudi-led coalition blames Yemen’s Houthis, and a diplomatic source told France 24 the missile was a Houthi one. Unlike Thursday’s attack on the same airport, which killed three Saudis, the Houthis had not claimed this one by Sunday morning, according to The National and France 24. Asked on Saturday, before the toll was confirmed, whether the US would join Saudi strikes on the Houthis, President Trump said: “We’re going to look at it.” Single source Two Saudi officials told MS NOW the kingdom has asked Washington for “urgent defense support.”

The Gulf war produced two more incidents. UKMTO reported an outbound tanker hit by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz late Saturday (about 3:20 PM ET), setting it on fire; the crew is safe and nobody has said who fired. And US Central Command said a US warplane disabled the propulsion of the Panama-flagged Ocean Molica in the Gulf of Oman after it left an Iranian port, with no crew hurt. CENTCOM says it has now disabled four ships and turned back 135 since the blockade resumed on July 14. Iran has still not delivered its promised reply to Washington’s response on Hormuz.

Why it matters Last Thursday the president pledged no US strike on Iran before the Nov. 3 election. That pledge said nothing about the Houthis, and an American death in Riyadh puts US action against them on the table. A US–Houthi exchange is a different risk from a US–Iran one, but it would land in the same oil market. Brent ended Friday at $104.72, and oil futures reopen at 6 PM ET tonight, the first market test of the weekend.
What’s next Whether the Houthis claim the strike; any US decision on joining Saudi strikes; a Saudi request for air-defence support; the 6 PM open; Iran’s reply on Hormuz, which its foreign minister has said will come “within days.”

Trump says Ukraine should “get a new president” after Zelensky attacks the Russian diesel deal

The remark came as a Russian guided-bomb attack killed 20 people in Zaporizhzhia, and two days after Washington licensed Russian diesel exports.

Speaking to reporters at the White House on Saturday before flying to a rally in Tennessee, the president said “it’s time for Ukraine to get a new president,” and blamed Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refineries for high diesel prices. The Washington Post, CNN, Fox News, CBC and others carried the remarks. Axios, citing a US official, reports that the diesel deal with Moscow followed Zelensky ignoring “half a dozen” US requests to stop the refinery strikes. Zelensky had called the deal a gift to Putin and “not fair and not honest.” Republican Reps. Don Bacon and Michael McCaul warned it could strengthen Russia’s war effort, and Britain’s prime minister told Zelensky he stood in “complete solidarity.”

On the ground, Ukraine’s State Emergency Service says Saturday morning’s Russian guided-bomb attack on Zaporizhzhia killed 20 people, three of them children, and injured 32. Sunday is a day of mourning there. Kyiv was hit by drones and then missiles around 2 PM local time; Mayor Vitali Klitschko reported at least seven injured. The AP counts more than 60 killed by Russian strikes over four days. Zelensky’s offer stands: Ukraine stops hitting refineries if Russia stops hitting power plants. A Ukrainian official says US envoys threatened to cut intelligence sharing, which a US official denies. Single source Russia’s TASS reports that envoys Witkoff and Kushner may visit Moscow within two weeks.

Why it matters Washington is now openly leaning on Kyiv over fuel prices before the midterms. For markets the question is narrow: whether Ukraine’s refinery campaign, which has squeezed Russian diesel exports, slows. That would ease the diesel prices in Delta’s cost warning and in rising inflation expectations.

Isaias’s toll rises to four; about half of the 870,000 outages are restored

The storm’s remains are raining on western Pennsylvania today. The Gulf of Mexico’s oil restart has not yet been reported.

CNN, CBS, NBC and Fox Weather now report at least four deaths: two in Escambia County, Florida, which Gov. Ron DeSantis confirmed, and two in Alabama. Two were caused by trees falling on homes, and one Florida man was found in a closed garage with a running generator. Outages peaked at about 870,000 customers on Saturday morning (Florida 438,911, Alabama 307,743, Georgia 121,191, per a CNN tally) and were down to roughly 480,000 by Saturday night. President Trump approved an emergency declaration for Georgia on Saturday, after Florida and Alabama on Friday. These are emergency declarations; no major-disaster declaration has been reported.

The energy question is still open. The latest offshore figure is Friday’s: 71.5% of Gulf of Mexico oil output, about 1.46 million barrels a day, shut in. No post-storm restaffing or restart report has been published, and the two refineries closest to the track, Chevron’s Pascagoula plant and Vertex’s Mobile plant, have not reported their status. GasBuddy’s Patrick De Haan expects output to “resume significantly by early next week.”

Top Themes

1. September CPI is the last big test before the Fed meets. Wednesday’s report is the final inflation reading before the Oct. 27–28 meeting. Aggregator consensus is a hot headline (+0.6% for the month, 3.6% on the year, up from 3.4%) because of gasoline, and a tame core (+0.2%, 2.5% on the year). Futures price about a one-in-five chance of an October hike and about 85% for one by December. The Fed’s minutes said energy was the risk “many” officials worried about, the worry being that it spreads into other prices. So the core number decides the reaction more than the headline. A 0.3% or higher core would revive October; a 0.2% core keeps the Fed on December.

2. The Gulf war has a new front, and the US is weighing it. Saturday’s missile on Riyadh’s airport killed 12, an American among them. The president’s pledge of no strike on Iran before Nov. 3 does not cover the Houthis, and he said the US would “look at” joining Saudi strikes on them. Add a tanker set on fire in Hormuz on Saturday and a US warplane disabling a blockade runner. Brent held above $100 all last week with a war premium that did not move; tonight’s 6 PM open is the first test of whether the weekend adds to it.

3. Banks open earnings season, with the consumer as the question. JPMorgan, Goldman, Citi and Wells Fargo report Tuesday; Bank of America, Morgan Stanley and BlackRock Wednesday; PNC, US Bancorp and Schwab Thursday; Travelers and regional banks Friday. FactSet expects third-quarter S&P 500 earnings growth of 29.6%. Trading revenue should be strong after a volatile quarter. The things to read are card charge-offs, consumer spending commentary and loan demand, in a quarter when credit-card balances shrank and sentiment hit near-record lows.

4. TSMC and ASML test the AI trade after its first crack. Chip stocks lost 4.3% last week after a report put OpenAI’s revenue below the figure that had circulated, and ASML and TSMC’s US shares each fell more than 4%. ASML reports Wednesday and TSMC Thursday. TSMC’s monthly sales already point to a quarter above its own guidance, so its guidance for the fourth quarter and its spending plans matter more than the beat.

5. A quiet bond calendar, and a Fed that goes quiet on Friday. The bond market is closed Monday and there are no coupon auctions this week, so last week’s 5.30% ceiling on the 10-year is untested by supply. The Fed’s communications blackout starts Saturday, and Chair Warsh’s last public word before it is a fireside chat with the IMF’s Kristalina Georgieva in Bangkok, late Thursday night Eastern time.

The Economy Ahead

Where we start. Hiring has nearly stopped but layoffs have not started, and prices are rising fastest where fuel feeds in:

  • Payrolls rose 29,000 in September; unemployment is 4.2%.
  • Jobless claims are near 197,000, their lowest four-week average since 2022.
  • ISM services prices paid hit 74.0, the highest since July 2022.
  • Michigan sentiment is 46.3, near its record low, and households’ five-to-ten-year inflation expectations rose to 3.5%.

The Fed’s target range is 3.75–4.00%. The minutes of September’s meeting said “most participants” expect another hike by year-end, and Governor Waller said hikes need not come at consecutive meetings. CME FedWatch, as relayed Friday, put October at about 18% and December at about 85%.

CPI, Wednesday 8:30. The date is confirmed on the BLS calendar. August showed +0.4% headline and 3.4% on the year, with core +0.3% and 2.4%; gasoline rose 3.9% in the month. Consensus for September, from secondary aggregators, is +0.6% headline and 3.6% on the year, core +0.2% and 2.5%. One aggregator has the headline at 3.7%. The Cleveland Fed’s nowcast is 3.60% headline and 2.39% core. What a surprise would mean: the Fed has said it can look through energy if it does not spread. A core of 0.3% or more, especially in services, is the spreading it fears, and the 2-year would move first. A 0.1% core would push December odds down and pull the dollar off its 18-month high.

Retail sales and PPI, Thursday 8:30. Retail sales jumped 1.2% in August, a figure lifted by gasoline prices. Consensus for September is +0.3%, with the control group (which feeds GDP) also +0.3%. The Chicago Fed’s tracker projects sales excluding autos up 0.4% in dollars but down 0.7% after inflation, which would mean households are paying more for less. PPI consensus is +0.5% headline, +0.3% core. The Philadelphia Fed’s factory index is expected to fall to 26.5 from 37.8, which would still be strong.

The rest. The Beige Book (Wednesday 2:00) gives the regional banks’ anecdotes on hiring and on whether firms are passing on fuel costs. Industrial production (Friday 9:15) and import prices (Friday 8:30) close the week; no consensus was found for either.

Scenarios

Base case, about 45–55%: inflation hot at the top, calm underneath. CPI matches consensus, with a headline near 3.6% and a 0.2% core. The Fed stays on course for December and October odds stay below 30%. The 10-year holds between 5.10% and 5.35%. The banks beat, TSMC beats and guides steadily, and the S&P 500 makes a marginal new record. Brent holds roughly $100–110 as Gulf strikes continue without hitting energy infrastructure. Confirmers: core at 0.2%, FedWatch October under 25%, a 10-year between 5.15% and 5.30%. Invalidators: core at 0.3% or higher, a Brent settlement above $110.

Bull case, about 15–20%: inflation cools and AI demand holds. Core CPI at 0.1–0.2% and soft retail sales pull December odds lower; the 10-year falls toward 5.10%. TSMC raises its outlook, chips recover and breadth widens. Small caps and regional banks lead. Confirmers: 2-year below 4.70%, SMH regaining last week’s loss, KRE up on the week.

Bear case, about 20–30%: a hot core and a hawkish Warsh. Core CPI at 0.3% or more revives an October hike, with FedWatch above 30%. The 2-year jumps, the dollar breaks higher and the 10-year retests 5.30–5.35%. Bank reports show rising card losses, and the S&P falls 2–3% from its record, led by small caps and consumer stocks. Confirmers: a 2-year above 4.90%, high-yield spreads above 3.25%, Warsh not pushing back on October.

Shock case, about 5–10%: the Gulf widens. US strikes in Yemen draw an Iranian response, or a strike on Saudi or Emirati oil facilities is confirmed. Brent goes above $110, stocks sell off, and Treasuries are caught between safety buying and inflation fear. Confirmers: a Pentagon announcement, a Saudi government statement on an energy facility. This would override the others.

What would change the outlook. Iran formally replying on Hormuz with terms Washington accepts; a Houthi announcement that it is stopping attacks on Saudi Arabia; Warsh signalling either an October move or explicit patience; or claims above 230,000.

Local Week Ahead

Pennsylvania

Skill games: the deadline is Tuesday, and “unplugged” may not be enough. The state Supreme Court’s ruling that skill games are illegal gambling devices takes full effect Tuesday, Oct. 13. State Police say seizures and possible criminal charges can begin Wednesday. Both the State Police and Attorney General Dave Sunday say switching a machine off does not comply: it must be physically removed by its vendor or distributor, and money tied to it can be seized. Pace-O-Matic, the largest supplier, disputes that and says the games need only be “rendered inoperable.” Some distributors are already pulling machines. The legislature is not in session until Oct. 19, so no fix can come before the deadline.
