Top Stories

Alphabet falls 12.6%, its worst day since its 2004 listing, as investors punish AI spending that outruns cash

About $500 billion of market value went in a day. Microsoft, which reported the same evening, gave up a premarket gain and closed slightly lower.

Alphabet’s Class A shares closed at $283.82, down $41.07 from Wednesday’s $324.89, on volume of about 137 million shares, more than five times their 20-day average (Yahoo Finance). Benzinga, Yahoo Finance and Reuters described it as the company’s worst day since its 2004 public offering and put the value lost at roughly $480–500 billion, among the largest one-day losses ever for a US company. The stock is now 31% below its 52-week high.

Nothing new came out of the company today; the selling was a verdict on Wednesday night’s report. Google Cloud revenue doubled to $30.7 billion, but free cash flow was −$9.1 billion after $51.4 billion of quarterly capital spending, the second negative quarter in a row, and Alphabet said 2027 spending will “increase significantly” (8-K). Analysts had expected positive free cash flow. The company has been selling shares through an at-the-market program and issuing bonds to fill the gap.

The contrast with Microsoft narrowed during the day. Microsoft was indicated almost 3% higher before the open after guiding Azure growth to 49–50%, but it slipped in the first hour and closed at $530.14, down 0.2%. Meta, which was sold last week for issuing stock to fund AI, rose 2.0%, and Nvidia fell 0.8%. The communication-services sector fund, where Alphabet and Meta are the largest weights, fell 1.9%; the Roundhill Magnificent Seven fund fell 2.2%.

Alphabet’s fall alone explains most of the gap between the indexes. It is among the largest weights in both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq 100, while the Dow, which does not hold it, fell only 0.25% and the equal-weight S&P rose 1.2%.

Why it matters. The market has drawn a clear line this earnings season: AI spending paid for out of operating cash is rewarded, and spending financed by new stock and debt is not. That line now runs through the index’s largest companies, and Amazon, with negative trailing free cash flow, reported tonight on the wrong side of it.
What’s next. Alphabet’s 2027 capital plan comes on its next call; Apple reports Monday, Nov. 2, after the close. This morning’s calls that Alphabet and the Nasdaq would close lower both landed.

Yahoo Finance (closing prices and volume); Alphabet Form 8-K, Oct 28 (SEC EDGAR); Benzinga; Reuters via the Detroit News; Yahoo Finance live coverage.

Amazon falls about 5% after hours: AWS grows 39%, but earnings miss and trailing free cash flow is −$42 billion

Revenue matched forecasts at $201.9 billion and operating margin hit a record 13.9%; advertising missed and capital spending more than doubled.

Amazon’s third-quarter net sales rose 12% to $201.9 billion, at the top of its own guidance and in line with the roughly $202 billion consensus, according to its 8-K. Amazon said Prime Day’s move into the second quarter cost about 4 percentage points of growth. AWS revenue rose 39% to $46.0 billion, its fastest growth in nearly five years and above the roughly $45.3 billion StreetAccount estimate cited by 24/7 Wall St, and AWS operating income rose to $17.8 billion from $11.4 billion. Total operating income was $28.1 billion, up from $17.4 billion.

Net income fell to $20.7 billion, or $1.89 a share, against consensus near $1.96; a year ago it included $9.5 billion of gains on Amazon’s Anthropic stake. Advertising revenue of about $19.6 billion missed estimates of about $20.8 billion, as reported; Amazon blamed Prime Day timing and guided to ad growth in the high teens in the fourth quarter. Purchases of property and equipment were $77.3 billion in the quarter, against $35.1 billion a year earlier, and trailing twelve-month free cash flow was an outflow of $42.4 billion, against an inflow of $14.8 billion a year ago. Amazon kept its 2026 capital-spending plan at about $220 billion.

Fourth-quarter guidance is for sales of $240–247 billion, growth of 12–16%, and operating income of $28.5–33.5 billion, against $25.0 billion a year earlier. Chief executive Andy Jassy said AWS revenue growth is projected at more than 40% in both 2027 and 2028. The shares closed the regular session at $243.79, down 4.2% into the report, and were about $232 at 4:42 PM, roughly 5% lower (Yahoo Finance); one report said they were down as much as 8–10% in the first minutes. After-hours prices are thin and the call began at 5 PM.

Why it matters. AWS growth of 39% against Microsoft Azure’s 48% and Google Cloud’s 103% keeps Amazon in the race, and its operating profit is rising fast. But on the measure investors punished Alphabet for today — cash out the door — Amazon, on a trailing twelve-month basis, shows the largest outflow of the three.
What’s next. Our call: Amazon closes Friday below today’s $243.79 (70–85%), given the after-hours level, though the 5 PM call could narrow the move.

Amazon Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1, Oct 29 (SEC EDGAR, primary); 24/7 Wall St; Yahoo Finance (after-hours price).

Growth beats at 3.4% and inflation lands on forecast; Treasuries rally and the seven-year auction clears cleanly

The 10-year par yield fell back to 4.98%, and futures trimmed the odds of a December Fed hike to about 80%.

The economy grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter, after 2.2% in the second and above the 3.2% median forecast, according to the Commerce Department’s advance estimate (our calculation from the real GDP series on FRED agrees). Consumer spending grew at 3.8%, CNBC reported, and final sales to private domestic purchasers — the measure that strips out trade, inventories and government — rose 4.9%, which AP-style reports called the strongest since 2021. This is a first estimate; the second comes Nov. 25.

September’s personal income report matched the forecasts that mattered. The PCE price index rose 0.5% for the month and 3.6% from a year earlier, up from 3.4% in August; core PCE, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3% (0.32% unrounded) and 3.1% for the year, from 3.0%. Inflation is still accelerating, in other words, but not faster than markets had priced. Incomes rose only 0.2% and spending 0.5%, both a little below forecast, and real disposable income fell 0.3%, leaving the saving rate at 3.8%, as reported by CNBC, Morningstar and Fox Business. Weekly jobless claims were 194,000, against about 200,000 expected (FRED).

Treasuries rallied from the release onward. The 10-year par yield fell to 4.98% from 5.05% and the seven-year to 4.87% from 4.95% (Treasury). At 1 PM the Treasury sold $44 billion of seven-year notes at a high yield of 4.894%, matching the when-issued yield exactly — no tail, as Newsquawk reported — with 2.46 bids for every dollar offered, against 2.42 in September and a six-auction average of 2.49. Foreign and other indirect bidders took 57.8%, below average; dealers were left with 9.4%, less than their usual 12%. September’s sale had cleared at 5.085%, the highest since 1993. The 30-year mortgage rate fell to 7.24% from 7.32% in Freddie Mac’s weekly survey (via FRED).

Fed pricing eased a little. CME FedWatch odds of a quarter-point hike in December were reported at 79.6%, from 84.2% on Wednesday (Fox Business). No Fed officials spoke today.

Why it matters. The long end had been the market’s pressure point all week. An in-line inflation print and a clean auction took the 10-year back below 5% and gave rate-sensitive sectors — real estate, utilities, regional banks, small caps — a broad lift. The inflation trend has not turned: headline PCE rose from 3.4% to 3.6%, and the Fed’s December decision still leans to a hike.

The ECB holds at 2.50% and Lagarde says she will leave on Feb. 7, nine months early

The Governing Council debated a hike and decided against it; the euro touched a 2026 low on the decision and then recovered.

The European Central Bank left its deposit rate at 2.50% after two hikes this year, the latest in September. Its statement said energy prices pose “upside risks to inflation” and “downside risks to growth,” and that “financing conditions have tightened” since the last meeting, as the French bond selloff lifts borrowing costs (ECB statement as reported by Euronews and AP). Euro-area inflation is 3.8%. At her press conference Christine Lagarde said the council had debated whether a hike was appropriate and played down the risk that inflation would spread beyond energy, according to FXStreet and The Edge. Most economists expect one more hike in December (Euronews).

Lagarde also announced that she will step down on Feb. 7, 2027; her eight-year term runs to October 2027. Euronews and CNN reported that she gave as her reason a wish to speak more freely on matters beyond monetary policy. The European Council appoints her successor, which opens a contest among euro-area governments at a moment when France’s 10-year spread over Germany was about 157 basis points on Wednesday, up from about 142 earlier in the week.

The euro fell to about $1.111, a fresh 2026 low, on the decision, then recovered to about $1.116–1.119 in the afternoon as the dollar weakened after the US inflation data (FXStreet; Yahoo Finance). The dollar index fell 0.3% to 101.90.

Why it matters. An early, scheduled exit at the ECB puts the succession into the same months as a December hike decision and an unresolved French budget. Whoever follows her inherits the question of how far the bank would go to cap one member’s borrowing costs. This morning’s call of a hold landed.

ECB monetary policy statement (as reported); Euronews; CNN; AP via the Manila Times; FXStreet; The Edge Singapore; Pound Sterling Live; Yahoo Finance.

Software’s worst day in memory: ServiceNow falls 27% and Fortinet 20%

Both beat on revenue. Investors sold the outlooks, and the selling spread to the AI-exposed parts of the software group.

ServiceNow closed at $109.67, down 27.0%, on 75 million shares, six times its average (Yahoo Finance), what Reuters and CNBC called its worst day on record. It guided growth in current remaining performance obligations, its main measure of contracted revenue due within a year, to 18.5% in constant currency for the fourth quarter, below the roughly 19.8% expected, and missed its earnings estimate by two cents for the first time in six quarters. It raised its free-cash-flow margin target to 36% and nudged up its subscription revenue guidance. Benzinga cited fears that AI agents will erode demand for seat-based software as part of the selling; that is a market narrative, not something the company said.

Fortinet closed at $139.10, down 20.1%. It beat estimates and raised its full-year billings and revenue guidance a third time, but cut its services revenue guidance and reported a gross margin of 79.6%, down from 81.6%, on higher component costs (company results via Reuters and Chartmill). Oracle fell 3.5% and the technology sector fund 1.2%.

Yahoo Finance (closes and volume); Reuters; CNBC; Benzinga; Chartmill; The Globe and Mail.

Caterpillar falls 9.3% on a beat, because it did not raise its outlook

This morning we found no reason for the premarket drop. The answer was the fourth-quarter guidance and a margin squeeze from tariffs.

Caterpillar closed at $647.39, down $65.99, on three times its usual volume (Yahoo Finance). Its third quarter was strong — sales up 17% to $20.7 billion and adjusted earnings of $7.20 a share against about $7.10 expected (8-K) — but it kept rather than raised its full-year outlook, guided to only “moderate” fourth-quarter sales growth against consensus near $21.4 billion, and said its adjusted operating margin would land near the bottom of its target range as tariff costs rise, Benzinga and TIKR reported. The stock had risen into the report on data-centre power demand; it is now 40% below its 52-week high.

Caterpillar Form 8-K (SEC EDGAR); Benzinga; TIKR; Yahoo Finance.

Eli Lilly jumps 6.6% after raising its 2026 revenue forecast by $5.5 billion

Health care was the day’s best sector; Merck rose 3.8% and Mastercard 4.2% to a record on their own results.

Lilly’s third-quarter revenue rose 39% to $24.4 billion, against about $22.5 billion expected, though about $900 million of it was one-time, including $410 million of Jardiance milestone payments, as CNBC reported. Mounjaro sales rose 65% to $10.72 billion; Zepbound brought in $5.35 billion at lower US prices, and its new oral obesity pill, Foundayo, sold $184 million, below expectations. Lilly raised full-year revenue guidance to $91–92 billion from $85–87 billion and guided non-GAAP earnings to $35–36 a share; the guidance absorbs a $4.57-a-share research charge, so headlines describing a cut are misleading. The stock closed at $1,373.70, up 6.6% (Yahoo Finance).

Merck’s adjusted earnings of $2.49 a share and revenue of $18.1 billion beat estimates of $2.08 and $17.3 billion (CNBC, LSEG), though about $550 million of Keytruda sales were advance customer purchases. It closed up 3.8%. Mastercard closed at $635.70, up 4.2%, near its record; one report put adjusted earnings at $5.31 a share against $5.16 expected and cross-border volume up 11%, which we could not confirm elsewhere. The health-care sector fund rose 2.4% to within a whisker of its 52-week high.

Eli Lilly results release via CNBC; Merck via CNBC and LSEG; Mastercard via KuCoin and Benzinga Pro (single-sourced); Yahoo Finance (closes).

Intel falls about 16% after hours on a weak outlook and a shrinking PC market

Data-centre and AI revenue rose 78%, but Intel guided fourth-quarter earnings to about half of what analysts expected.

Intel reported revenue of about $16.5 billion, in line, and adjusted earnings of $0.52 a share against about $0.40 expected, with its Data Center and AI segment up 78% to $7.32 billion, according to Quartz, Benzinga and Yahoo Finance. But it guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16 billion against about $17.05 billion expected and adjusted earnings to $0.25 a share against $0.43, and said it expects the PC market to shrink about 10% in 2027 after about 12% this year as memory shortages raise prices. Shares closed at $92.07 and were about $77.17 at 4:45 PM (Yahoo Finance).

Why it matters. Intel is the clearest casualty so far of the memory squeeze that is making SK Hynix and Samsung record profits: the same scarcity that lifts memory makers raises the cost of every PC.

Quartz; Benzinga; Yahoo Finance (results summaries and prices); Intel’s release was not read directly.

The Economy

Growth, in real terms. Real GDP rose at a 3.4% annual rate (about 0.8% for the quarter, not annualised), the fastest of the year, after 2.2% in the second quarter. Final sales to private domestic purchasers at 4.9% say domestic demand is stronger than the headline, since record imports subtracted from it. Inside the report, the PCE price index rose at a 2.1% annual rate for the quarter and core at 2.5%, as reported; those quarterly rates are lower than September’s year-on-year readings because the summer’s energy spike hit in the second quarter.

Inflation, level versus rate of change. On a year-on-year basis the picture is acceleration, not disinflation: headline PCE 3.6% (from 3.4%), core 3.1% (from 3.0%); headline CPI is 3.7% and core CPI 2.6% (FRED). The monthly rates — 0.5% headline, 0.3% core — are each above the pace consistent with the Fed’s 2% target. Real incomes fell for the month, and the saving rate at 3.8% is low: consumers are spending ahead of their incomes.

Labour. Initial claims of 194,000 for the week to Oct. 24 follow 195,000 (revised up from 194,000) the week before; layoffs remain low even though payroll growth stalled at 29,000 in September. October’s jobs report is due Friday, Nov. 6.

The Fed. No speakers today. Futures price a December hike at about 80%, from 84% before the data (CME FedWatch via Fox Business). The White House panel on removing Governor Lisa Cook meets Nov. 5. Our December-hike call of 70–85% from Wednesday stands.

Treasury and rates. The curve fell almost in parallel: 2-year 4.61% (−6 bp), 5-year 4.78% (−7), 10-year 4.98% (−7), 30-year 5.32% (−5). The 2s10s spread is 37 basis points and 3-month to 10-year 80. The seven-year sale was the last coupon auction before next Wednesday’s quarterly refunding announcement, where the size of future auctions is the question. SOFR was 3.94% on Wednesday, above the 3.88% effective fed funds rate, a sign of firm month-end repo demand. The 30-year mortgage rate fell 8 basis points to 7.24%.

Credit. The high-yield spread tightened to 3.14 percentage points on Wednesday, the tightest this month (ICE BofA via FRED); the high-yield fund HYG was flat today and the investment-grade fund LQD rose 0.1%. Credit is not confirming any equity stress.

Global. The ECB held at 2.50% (above). The Bank of Japan decides tonight, expected to hold at 1.25% with 83% of economists in a Bloomberg survey looking for a December hike; the yen is near 157.7. The Reserve Bank of Australia meets Tuesday after 4.3% inflation. The Bank of Canada raised its rate to 2.50% on Wednesday.

The United States

Alaska’s governor orders a state inquiry into the Peltola campaign over spoiler-candidate reports

A key source for the New York Times story has retracted her statements, according to reports. The Peltola campaign and the DSCC deny involvement.

Gov. Mike Dunleavy, a Republican, late on Wednesday ordered the Alaska Department of Public Safety to examine whether state law was broken and whether criminal or civil action is warranted, after a New York Times report that a project backed by Rep. Mary Peltola’s Senate campaign and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee recruited spoiler candidates, including a second “Dan Sullivan.” Dunleavy said the reporting “substantially supports” earlier findings by the state elections board; his order did not cite specific statutes (Anchorage Daily News, NBC, Washington Post). President Trump had said on Wednesday that Peltola should be prosecuted.

Peltola’s campaign says it “has no involvement with either Sullivan campaign,” and the DSCC told TIME it was not involved with the second candidate. Elisa Rios, a former Peltola campaign manager quoted by the Times as saying Peltola was “definitely aware,” later contacted the paper and retracted her statements about the race, according to reports. The Times noted that “ballot stacking” is common and generally legal. Stage: a state inquiry has been ordered; no one has been charged. More than 50,000 Alaskans have already voted, and the RealClearPolling average has Peltola at 49.4% and Sen. Dan Sullivan at 48.2%.

Anchorage Daily News; NBC News; Washington Post; TIME; PBS/AP; Alaska Public Media; RealClearPolling.

Five days out: Democrats lead the generic ballot by about 9 points, and the Senate map is tight

About 41 million ballots have been cast, ahead of 2022’s pace.

The PollCheck average (updated Oct. 27) puts Democrats ahead on the generic congressional ballot 51.1% to 42.4%, and a Reuters/Ipsos poll of 1,164 likely voters fielded Oct. 26 had them ahead 53–44. Senate averages from RealClearPolling show close races in Maine (Jackson 48.8, Collins 47.4), Ohio (Brown 48.2, Husted 44.9), Texas (Talarico 48.4, Paxton 46.9) and Alaska, with Cooper well ahead in North Carolina (50.6 to 40.7). The Washington Post described Democrats as heavily favoured for the House and the Senate as much tighter. Polls are not predictions, and averages in single states rest on few surveys. Axios reported that early turnout is ahead of 2022 at the same point.

In California, the Justice Department’s second appeal over 20,000 mail ballots it flagged as “likely noncitizen” is pending at the Ninth Circuit after Judge David O. Carter’s 26-page denial on Wednesday; we found no ruling or briefing order today.

PollCheck; Reuters/Ipsos; RealClearPolling; Washington Post; Axios; NBC early-vote tracker; NPR; Democracy Docket.

The World

Iran says it has received Vance’s letter but has not replied; a tanker is struck near Hormuz

Saudi Arabia intercepted another Houthi missile, the second in three days.

Iran’s Tasnim news agency said on Thursday that Tehran received the letter Vice President JD Vance sent through Qatar to Mohsen Rezaei, proposing direct talks with the nuclear issue on the table, but has not responded, and that the US has not met Iran’s “seven conditions” (Al Arabiya, Jerusalem Post, Al Jazeera). President Trump said on Wednesday he had not directed Vance to open negotiations and that the outreach concerned medical supplies; the Wall Street Journal’s account differs. The Journal and AP also reported that US officials told Gulf states to prepare for a “short, intense” campaign against Iran after Tuesday’s election; the White House said all options remain open.

Britain’s UKMTO maritime agency said a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile while leaving the Strait of Hormuz, with no injuries; the attacker is unknown. Iran’s navy said four tankers hit mines on an “unauthorized” route; that claim is Iran’s alone and unverified. Saudi air defences shot down a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at the Eastern Province early Thursday, as reported by HNGN and a CBS live blog. Oil fell for much of the day: CNBC reported Brent down about 2% near $113 and WTI below $100 in the afternoon. We could not confirm settlement prices, and our own futures feed showed WTI roughly flat, so treat the size of the move as unsettled.

Why it matters. Oil is pricing two tracks at once — talks and a strike campaign — and the December Brent contract expires on Friday with OPEC+ meeting Sunday and the US vote Tuesday.

Tasnim via Al Arabiya, the Jerusalem Post and Al Jazeera; Wall Street Journal and AP via Anadolu and The New Arab; UKMTO as reported; HNGN; CBS News; CNBC; Yahoo Finance.

AI & Technology

Google previews Gemini 4 Carbon, its strongest model, to vetted cyber defenders only

An independent index ranks it first by one point. There is no date for general access.

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on Wednesday as its next frontier model, aimed at cybersecurity and long-running coding agents, following Gemini 4 Argon in September. Access is limited to vetted security teams through Google DeepMind’s Fairwind programme, with more partners to follow, and Google said it took part in the US government’s voluntary pre-release testing (Google blog, The Hacker News, VentureBeat). Google’s own benchmarks show large gains on vulnerability-finding tests, run without guardrails, VentureBeat noted. Independently, Artificial Analysis’s intelligence index puts Carbon first at 59, one point ahead of Anthropic’s Claude Opus 5.5 and two ahead of OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra; Vals AI ranks it second of 29. Listed pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, described as introductory; some trackers list $4 and $20.

Why it matters. Alphabet’s products are not the question investors are asking; its cash flow is. The model news did nothing for the stock today.

Google blog; The Hacker News; VentureBeat; Artificial Analysis; Vals AI.

Science

Ancient plants under a Baffin Island ice cap suggest a small ice advance that warming cut short

A peer-reviewed study in Science by Nicolás Young, Gifford Miller and colleagues at the University of Colorado dated plants emerging from beneath the Barnes Ice Cap and concluded that ice there began re-expanding at least about 1,000 years ago, a small-scale glacial inception, until industrial-era warming halted it. The finding rests on one ice-cap remnant and on the authors’ definition of inception; it does not show that people prevented an ice age, and Shaun Marcott told Smithsonian that the next large inception was not expected in this cycle anyway. Some headlines overstate it.

University of Colorado INSTAAR; Science; Smithsonian.

A single molecule that links an electron spin to light, a step towards quantum networks

An open-access paper in this week’s Nature reports a spin–photon interface built from a single triplet-carbene molecule held in a tailored crystal. It is peer-reviewed proof of concept, not a working network component; we read only the journal’s summary.

Nature, vol. 658, issue 8138.

Space

The 100th US spacewalk installs another ISS solar array; Vulcan returns to flight Nov. 13

NASA astronaut Anil Menon and the Canadian Space Agency’s Joshua Kutryk began the 100th US spacewalk at 7:57 AM to install the seventh of eight roll-out solar arrays on the station’s P4 truss, NASA said; it was Kutryk’s first and Canada’s first spacewalk in about seven years. We had not seen NASA’s confirmation of the result at press time. The last spacewalk of the series is set for no earlier than Nov. 5; with all arrays in place, station power rises from about 160 to about 215 kilowatts.

United Launch Alliance said its Vulcan rocket will return to flight on Friday, Nov. 13, after a nine-month grounding over solid-rocket-booster problems, carrying Amazon Leo broadband satellites (Spaceflight Now). SpaceX fell 0.9% to $143.89; NASASpaceflight reports no firm date yet for Starship’s 15th flight, which aims to catch both the booster and the ship. India’s GSLV is set to launch the NVS-03 navigation satellite on Friday at about 2:21 PM ET.

NASA space station blog; CBC; Space.com; Spaceflight Now; NASASpaceflight; Yahoo Finance (SPCX close).
