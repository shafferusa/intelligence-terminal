Top Stories

A report on OpenAI’s revenue knocks the AI trade, and money rotates into the rest of the market

OpenAI reportedly told investors it was running at about $50 billion a year, not the roughly $68 billion widely reported. Chip stocks fell 3–5%, while the average stock rose.

The Financial Times reported, and CNBC separately confirmed through its own source, that OpenAI told investors its annualized revenue was about $50 billion at the end of September. Figures of about $68 billion had been widely reported late last month, and CNN cites $70 billion. CNBC’s source said the higher number counted partners’ gross revenue. OpenAI is private, so there is no share price, but the companies that sell to it are public.

How they closed (Yahoo):

  • Micron −4.8%
  • Broadcom −4.4%
  • AMD −3.9%
  • TSMC −3.0%
  • Nvidia −2.9%
  • Microsoft −1.4%
  • Oracle about −5% at midday (CNBC); we have no close

The Nasdaq was down about 1.4% at its low, on pace for its worst day since July (CNN). It closed down 1.25%.

Likely contributor CNBC and CNN both tie the selloff in these names to the report, and the timing fits. Broadcom was already in the news: the Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday night that it is arranging more than $50 billion of private credit so OpenAI can buy its chips.

Underneath, the market did not sell off; it rotated. Outside the AI names:

  • The equal-weight S&P (RSP) rose 0.6% while the cap-weighted SPY fell 0.4%, a gap of about one point
  • The Dow and the Russell 2000 finished flat to slightly higher
  • Eight of eleven sector funds rose
  • Energy +3.0% and staples +2.1% led
Why it matters

The AI build-out is increasingly financed with debt: Broadcom’s reported $50 billion for OpenAI, SpaceX’s reported $40 billion for Nvidia chips, and Oracle’s talks with lenders. That debt rests on the buyers’ revenue growing into it. A customer earning materially less than the market believed is exactly the kind of fact that reprices the chain. One report is not a trend, and CNBC’s source suggests the gap is partly about how revenue is counted. It is still the most important new fact about AI demand this quarter.

What’s next

TSMC’s September sales, filed today, were strong (see AI & Technology). Its full third-quarter results come in mid-October, and the big cloud companies report at month-end. Watch whether OpenAI or its investors dispute the figure.

Sources: Financial Times (reported); CNBC (independent confirmation); CNN; Wall Street Journal via investingLive; Yahoo Finance (prices).

Trump says no US attack on Iran before the midterms; oil keeps most of its gain

The pledge came a day after reports that the White House had asked for strike options usable before November 3. The blockade stays, and the tanker attacks have spread to Qatar’s waters.

In a Truth Social post on Thursday, the president said the US will not attack Iran “at any time prior to the Midterm Elections on November 3.” He cited “productive discussions” with Tehran. Multiple outlets confirmed the post, including CNBC, ABC, Reuters, The Hill and UPI.

What did not change:

  • The president said the US naval blockade stays.
  • He said Iran will not be allowed a nuclear weapon.
  • The White House says “all options” remain available.
  • He said nothing about what happens after the election.

The pledge follows Wednesday’s reports: The Atlantic said the White House asked the Pentagon for options usable before the midterms, and Axios said Central Command had been told to prepare.

The oil market. Brent had jumped more than 5% early, above $105, after the attack on the tanker Acers north of Qatar and the hurricane shut-ins. Likely contributor It eased after the post, and the 10-year yield turned lower at about the same time (CNBC). Late in the day:

  • Brent futures were near $103.35 (Yahoo, about 4:45 PM). Bloomberg-sourced reports put the settlement at $104.28, up 4.1%.
  • WTI was about $90.73. Its reported settlement was $91.49, up 3.6%.

Either way, crude kept most of its gain. The market took the pledge as lowering one risk, a US strike, while the supply problems at sea and in the Gulf of Mexico remain.

What’s next

Iran says it will answer the latest US proposal “within the next few days.” Its atomic-energy chief says enrichment is not negotiable, which is the core US demand. This morning we put the chance of new US strikes before November 3 at 20–40%. Today’s pledge argues for the low end of that range. We will grade the forecast as written.

Sources: Truth Social post as reported by CNBC, ABC, Reuters (US News), The Hill, UPI; CBS; NewsNation; Euronews; Al Jazeera; Yahoo Finance and Bloomberg via Euronews (prices).

The 30-year sells at 5.618%, the highest since 2000, with a 0.1bp tail; bonds rally anyway

The sale was adequate rather than strong. It still cleared the week’s last coupon test, and long yields fell 6–7bp on the day.

The results of the $22 billion reopening of the 30-year bond (CUSIP 912810UW6), from TreasuryDirect:

  • High yield: 5.618%, against 5.308% at September’s reopening
  • Tail: 0.1bp over the 5.617% when-issued level just before the 1:00 PM deadline (investingLive, Newsquawk)
  • Bid-to-cover: 2.54, against 2.61 last time

Of the competitive bids accepted:

  • Indirect bidders: 72.3%
  • Direct bidders: 20.9%
  • Primary dealers: 6.8%, against a record-low 2.2% in September

Peter Boockvar called it “decent but nowhere close to as good as the 10 yr auction yesterday,” and Wells Fargo’s Tony Miano made the same comparison (CNBC). This morning we gave 50–65% odds the sale would come at or through the when-issued yield. It tailed by a tenth of a basis point, so on the strict wording that forecast missed.

The day’s path matters more. Late in the morning the 10-year was up about 3bp, near 5.305% (Reuters), as oil climbed. It reversed after the Iran pledge and the auction. Treasury’s official closing curve:

  • 10-year: 5.22%, down 6bp
  • 30-year: 5.60%, down 7bp
  • 2-year: 4.75%, down 2bp
  • 3-month bill: 4.23%, up 1bp

The 2s10s spread narrowed to 47bp from 51bp. That is a bull flattening: long rates falling faster than short ones.

Why it matters

The week’s $119 billion of coupon supply (3-, 10- and 30-year) is done, and every sale found buyers at yields not seen since about 2000. The 10-year has fallen 14bp from Wednesday’s 5.365% intraday high. For now, buyers are stepping in at these levels. September CPI on October 14 is the next test.

Sources: TreasuryDirect auction results (primary); Treasury par yield curve (primary); investingLive; Newsquawk; CNBC; Reuters via Investing.com.

Isaias shuts in about 63% of Gulf of Mexico oil output

About 1.28 million barrels a day was offline as of late morning, up from about 512,000 on Wednesday. Shell is shutting Mars, Olympus, Ursa, Vito and Appomattox.

Operator reports compiled by the federal offshore regulator, as of 11:00 AM CDT, put shut-ins at:

  • Oil: 62.89% of Gulf production, about 1.28 million barrels a day
  • Gas: 57.35%
  • Evacuated: 121 platforms and 5 rigs

Reuters and Oilprice reported the figures. Press reports name the agency differently, and we did not find the release itself. The next update is Friday afternoon.

CNN reports the industry is also watching the Gulf Coast refineries in the storm’s path. The National Hurricane Center forecasts strengthening before landfall, and some forecasters raise the chance of Category 2 or 3. Offshore output usually returns within days of a storm passing if platforms are undamaged. A direct hit on refining would matter more for gasoline and diesel than for crude.

Sources: federal offshore shut-in statistics via Reuters (BOE Report) and Oilprice; Rigzone; CNN; National Hurricane Center.

The United States

Gulf states declare emergencies as Isaias, now an 85 mph hurricane, heads for the Mississippi–Florida coast

It is the first hurricane of the 2026 Atlantic season. Landfall is expected late Friday or early Saturday, and the National Hurricane Center forecasts more strengthening first.

At its 1:00 PM CDT intermediate advisory, the National Hurricane Center put Isaias near 24.0°N, 89.8°W. It was a Category 1 hurricane with 85 mph winds, moving east-northeast at about 10 mph. Warnings in force:

  • Hurricane warning: Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Bay/Gulf County line in Florida
  • Storm-surge warning: the mouth of the Mississippi River to the Suwannee River

CBS, citing the forecast, reports surge of up to 7 feet on parts of the Mississippi and Alabama coast and up to 5 feet on the western Panhandle. It also reports 3–7 inches of rain, locally 15.

State response. Governors have declared emergencies:

  • Florida: 25 counties
  • Alabama: 40 counties
  • Georgia: statewide, with up to 500 Guard troops, as reported

Escambia (including Pensacola Beach and Perdido Key), Okaloosa, Walton and Franklin counties in Florida ordered mandatory evacuations (CNN).

Federal response. We found no federal emergency declaration yet. That is “not found,” not “not made.”

What’s next

Landfall Friday night into Saturday, then the remnants move northeast. That reaches Pittsburgh as Sunday rain (see Local). The offshore-oil effect is covered in The Economy.

Sources: National Hurricane Center advisory 8A (primary); CBS; CNN; NPR; WSFA.

The World

Russian strike on two buses in Kramatorsk kills 33, Ukrainian officials say

The toll rose through the day. Ukraine’s negotiators meet US envoys Kushner and Witkoff in the United States from Friday.

A Russian strike hit two passenger buses in Kramatorsk, in Donetsk region, on Thursday. Ukraine’s State Emergency Service and the regional governor, Vadym Filashkin, put the toll at 33 dead and 18 injured. Earlier counts were 12, then 30. The figures come from Ukrainian officials, and we saw no independent count or Russian comment. Police accounts disagree on the weapon; one names a glide bomb. Public transport in Kramatorsk and Sloviansk is suspended.

Other strikes today:

  • Kyiv: Ukrinform reports 3 killed and 14 injured (one source).

President Zelensky said Ukraine’s delegation will hold two days of talks in the US with Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff from Friday, with Europeans present. The first deadline for US sanctions decisions under the Graham Russia–Iran sanctions act falls on October 18.

Sources: Ukraine State Emergency Service and Donetsk regional administration via NBC, Al Jazeera, Meduza and Ukrainska Pravda; Ukrinform; Kyiv Independent.

In this week’s Nature: a gut microbe that curbs cholesterol production, in mice

A peer-reviewed study reports that a gut bacterium secretes a molecule that lowers the host’s own cholesterol production. It is a mouse study, so whether the same holds in people is untested. A separate Stanford paper in the same issue finds that gut species vanishing from industrialized populations evolved alongside humans and spread worldwide with them.

Source: Nature, vol. 658, issue 8135 (peer-reviewed; read at summary level).

FCC staff clear SpaceX’s own direct-to-phone satellite network; AST SpaceMobile falls 6%

The approval lets SpaceX serve phones from orbit on spectrum it bought from EchoStar, with no carrier partner required.

According to 24/7 Wall St and Benzinga, the FCC’s Space Bureau authorized:

  • A direct-to-cell constellation of about 15,000 satellites
  • Use of the spectrum SpaceX acquired from EchoStar

Launches would start in late 2027, with full service no earlier than 2035. We could not find the order on the FCC’s site, so the satellite count is as reported. A bureau-level order can still be reviewed by the full commission.
