Top Stories

Producer prices jump 1.3%, the most since 2022, and the bond market rallies anyway

The oil shock is now in wholesale prices in a big way. Treasuries still had their best day in weeks, and the odds of an October hike barely moved.

The Producer Price Index for final demand rose 1.3% in September, seasonally adjusted, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said, the largest monthly rise since March 2022. Forecasters had expected about 0.5%. August was revised up to 0.5%. Over 12 months the index rose 6.3%, the most since December 2022. A narrower measure that leaves out food, energy and trade margins rose 0.5% in the month and 5.0% on the year.

Energy explains most of it. Prices for final-demand goods rose 2.6%, the biggest increase since the series began in 2009, and the BLS says more than 80% of that came from energy, which jumped 10.8%. Gasoline rose 20.3% at the wholesale level. Services rose 0.8%, and almost half of that was wider retailer and wholesaler margins, which the BLS counts as trade services. Goods excluding food and energy rose a more modest 0.3%.

The bond market did not treat this as a reason to expect more Fed hikes. The 10-year par yield fell 9 basis points to 5.10% (Treasury), its lowest close since Sept. 22, and the 30-year fell 9 to 5.43%. The 2-year fell 5 to 4.77%. CME FedWatch put the odds of a quarter-point hike on Oct. 28 at about 34% after the release, little changed from late Wednesday (Schwab, InvestmentNews). Other readings during the day ranged from about a quarter to nearly a half. One report put the odds of a hike by December at about two-thirds.

Why yields fell is not documented. Investors appeared to focus on two things. First, the inflation was concentrated in energy and margins rather than in core goods. Second, the money leaving AI and chip stocks for a third day seems to have gone partly into Treasuries. The 10-year breakeven, the market’s inflation expectation, was 2.35% on Wednesday, little changed for weeks (FRED).

Why it matters

Consumer prices on Wednesday and producer prices today now both show the oil shock spreading. Retailer margins and airline fares are rising as well as fuel. The Fed raised rates in September to keep that from becoming lasting inflation. A bond market that rallies on a 6.3% producer-price print is saying it expects growth to slow, or a Fed that stops soon. That is a bet, not a fact. A hawkish Warsh tonight would test it.

What’s next

Warsh speaks with the IMF’s Kristalina Georgieva in Bangkok at about 11:30 PM ET. The Fed’s blackout starts Saturday. September personal-consumption prices, the Fed’s preferred gauge, arrive later this month; today’s PPI components feed into it.

Sources: BLS PPI release (Sept 2026); US Treasury par yield curve; FRED; CME FedWatch via Schwab and InvestmentNews; FXStreet; consensus via Trading Economics and Zacks.

Money leaves chips for banks, health care and small caps: Dow +1.1%, Russell +1.4%, Nasdaq flat

After Seoul’s 7.6% plunge, Nasdaq futures pointed to a third bad day. The cash market turned it into a rotation instead.

The S&P 500 dipped about 0.6% early, then recovered to close up 0.33% at 7,744.06. The Nasdaq Composite came back from its lows to finish up 0.08%; the Nasdaq 100 ended down 0.05%. The Dow gained 556 points, or 1.08%, and the Russell 2000 rose 1.38% (Yahoo Finance, CNBC). The VanEck semiconductor fund fell 1.0%, its seventh straight loss. Nvidia fell 1.8% to $217.23 and TSMC’s US shares 1.8%, despite TSMC’s record quarter and higher guidance. Micron rose 1.9%.

Health care (XLV +1.9%), materials (+1.8%), consumer discretionary (+1.7%), communication services (+1.5%) and financials (+1.4%) led. Technology (−0.7%) and energy (−1.6%) were the only sectors down besides utilities. Eli Lilly rose 3.6%, Home Depot 4.3% and UnitedHealth 2.8%; several outlets cited a Deutsche Bank upgrade for UnitedHealth, Lilly and Walmart.

CoreWeave, which fell 15.5% on Wednesday on worries about how it finances its data centres, rose 5.9%. Nvidia’s Jensen Huang told Bloomberg in Stockholm that CoreWeave “does not need a backstop” and that its cost of capital is falling. Arista (−5.0%) and Vertiv (−3.6%) kept falling.

Why it matters

The selling this week has been concentrated. The equal-weight S&P beat the cap-weighted index by a point today, and the S&P is only about 1% below its Oct. 6 record close. SMH, though, is 7.3% below its 20-day average. Money is moving out of crowded AI hardware, not out of stocks. Whether that is a rotation or the start of a broader retreat is the question for next week.

Sources: Yahoo Finance and Finnhub (closes); CNBC; Reuters via Lufkin Daily News and Devdiscourse (midday and close reports); Bloomberg via Benzinga (Huang).

Retail sales rise 0.8%, but gasoline does the lifting; claims stay below 200,000

Strip out cars and fuel and spending grew 0.2%. The headline is mostly people paying more at the pump.

The Census Bureau’s advance estimate showed September retail and food-services sales of $745.0 billion, up 0.8% from August, against forecasts of about 0.3%. August was revised to +1.3%. Excluding autos, sales rose 0.9%. Excluding autos and gasoline stations, they rose only 0.2%, and the Census Bureau says that change is not statistically different from zero. The control group that feeds GDP rose about 0.1%, economists calculate. All of these figures are nominal: with consumer prices up 0.6% in the month, real spending outside fuel probably fell.

Initial jobless claims were 199,000 in the week to Oct. 10, against 198,000 the week before (Labor Department via FRED), so layoffs remain low even as hiring has flattened. The New York Fed’s Empire State survey jumped to 25.5 from 7.6, its highest since December 2021. The Philadelphia Fed’s index fell to 20.9 from 37.8, and its prices-paid gauge eased to 43.2.

Why it matters

This is the stagflation trade-off in one report. Households are spending more and getting less for it. The Fed watches the gap between the headline and the control group. A weak control group with hot prices supports the bond market’s slower-growth reading.

Sources: Census Bureau advance retail sales release CB26-165; FRED (ICSA); New York and Philadelphia Fed surveys via Zacks and Investrade.

U.S. Bancorp raised its guidance and rose 2%; Schwab and BNY beat and fell.

U.S. Bancorp earned $1.34 a share, 2 cents above estimates, on record revenue of about $7.97 billion. It raised its full-year revenue-growth and operating-leverage guidance. The stock rose 2.0% to $57.39. PNC, Pittsburgh’s largest bank, reported diluted earnings of $4.97 a share and adjusted earnings of $5.09, against about $4.91 expected. Revenue was about $6.56–6.59 billion, roughly in line. It returned $1.4 billion to shareholders. The shares slipped 0.4% (company 8-K; Investing.com, Chartmill).

Charles Schwab made $1.69 a share, adjusted, against $1.66 expected, with record revenue of $7.17 billion, slightly below forecasts. Core net new assets were a record $190.4 billion. The stock fell 2.7%. BNY earned $2.37 adjusted, against $2.26, on revenue up 12% to $5.68 billion, and fell 2.4%. Marsh McLennan (ticker MRSH) earned $2.03 adjusted on revenue of $6.86 billion, both above estimates, and rose 4.6%. Regional banks (KRE) rose 1.8%.

After the bell, Alcoa reported adjusted earnings of $1.82 a share, above estimates, on revenue of $3.70 billion, below them. J.B. Hunt earned $1.82, a penny above consensus, on revenue up 20% to $3.68 billion. We found no reliable after-hours price moves for either at press time.

Sources: PNC Form 8-K; company releases via BusinessWire and AP; Investing.com, Chartmill, RTTNews, Benzinga, Yahoo Finance; Finnhub and Yahoo (closes).

Oil stocks fall 8.6 million barrels as Hormuz attacks continue; WTI rises, Brent holds near $106

The draw was much bigger than expected, partly because of hurricane shut-ins. The shipping attacks keep the risk premium in place.

US commercial crude inventories fell about 8.56 million barrels in the week to Oct. 9, against forecasts of a draw of about 0.3 million. Gasoline stocks rose about 3.5 million barrels and distillates fell 2.5 million, the Energy Information Administration said (via Reuters and Investing.com). Refineries ran at 93.5% of capacity. Reports tie the crude draw partly to hurricane-related production shut-ins in the Gulf of Mexico. WTI for November settled up $2.88 at $97.97, CNBC reports, and traded near $96.19 late in the afternoon (Yahoo). December Brent settled at $105.92, down 14 cents.

In the Gulf, Singapore’s Maritime and Port Authority confirmed that the container ship GFS Pride sank after unknown projectiles struck it near Oman on Oct. 13, and said all 21 crew were evacuated. Disputed One wire account citing maritime sources says a crew member died; the authority says there were no injuries. A sister ship, GFS Giselle, was struck on Oct. 14 and reached anchorage under its own power. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards say a container ship hit a mine on an “unauthorized” route and sank; a US official says it was more likely a missile. It is not clear whether that is the same ship. AP counts at least 18 attacks on ships near the strait since Oct. 1.

Why it matters

Oil is the input behind both inflation reports this week. The IEA’s monthly report says Middle East exports recovered to about 75% of pre-war levels in September, which caps prices. Each sinking slows that recovery. Tanker and container insurance, shipping lines and refining margins carry the risk.

Sources: EIA weekly petroleum status via Reuters/BOE Report and Investing.com; CNBC (settles); Yahoo Finance; Singapore MPA; The Loadstar; AP live coverage; CNN; IEA Oil Market Report via CNBC.

Justice Department sues California to stop it counting mail ballots from people it calls likely noncitizens

The 48-hour deadline in Tuesday’s letter passed at noon; the suit followed within hours. Mail ballots have been going out since Oct. 5.

The department filed suit in federal court in Los Angeles. Its complaint says, “The United States thus sues California to ensure that only American citizens’ ballots are counted in the ongoing federal election” (ABC News, Washington Times, Scripps). The demand began with letters from Acting Deputy Attorney General R. Trent McCotter on Tuesday. They asked state and Los Angeles County officials to review a federal list of about 20,000 suspected noncitizen registrants and to set aside mail ballots from anyone whose citizenship could not be confirmed.

Secretary of State Shirley Weber called the demand “unprecedented” and the timeline impossible. She wrote that she has “serious doubts about the reliability of your data” because her office has not shared the statewide voter list with any federal agency. The head of the state’s county clerks’ association called the 48-hour window “completely unreasonable.” Supporters of the suit argue federal law bars noncitizen voting and the state must act before ballots are counted. The department has sued 30 states and Washington, D.C., for voter data this year and, AP reports, has lost those cases so far.

What’s next

The case is at the complaint stage. Watch for a judge assignment and any motion for a temporary restraining order before ballots are counted after Nov. 3.

Sources: ABC News; Washington Times; KOAA (Scripps); NBC News; Axios; AP via PBS.

Ukraine resumes refinery strikes, setting fire to Rosneft’s Ryazan plant

Kyiv says it paused for three days under the announced “energy ceasefire” and resumed because Russia kept hitting its grid.

A large drone attack early Thursday started a fire at the Ryazan refinery, about 300 km southeast of Moscow, the regional governor said. Russia’s Defense Ministry and a senior Ukrainian official confirmed the attack to AP. The Rosneft plant handles roughly 5% of Russian refining. No casualties were reported. President Zelensky told the Financial Times that Ukraine had paused refinery strikes for three days and resumed them because Russian strikes on its power system continued. Russia’s attack on the night of Oct. 13–14 left more than 600,000 people without power and killed at least nine, and rolling blackouts returned to Kyiv today.

The strikes add to friction with Washington. The Treasury has issued a general licence letting Russian diesel reach world markets until about April 7; Zelensky called it “not fair and not honest,” and Canada’s and Denmark’s prime ministers criticised it. Zelensky says the US warned it could stop sharing intelligence if refinery strikes continued. President Trump said on Wednesday that it has not.

Sources: AP via NPR; Moscow Times; Meduza; CBC; Al Jazeera; Financial Times (via reports); Axios; RFE/RL; Oilprice.com.

  • US stocks and the chip rout. This morning, Nasdaq futures were down 1.1% after Seoul and Tokyo plunged. Instead, the S&P 500 rose and the Nasdaq closed flat as money rotated within the market. The United States

Supreme Court. In an unsigned 7–2 order on Wednesday, the Court stayed a Fourth Circuit order that had given the FCC 48 hours to rule on a challenge to its political-advertising rate policy. The Court said the lower court improperly interfered with the agency’s control over the timing of its work. Justices Sotomayor and Jackson would have denied the stay. The order does not decide whether parties and joint fundraising committees are entitled to the lowest ad rates (FCC v. Brown; Law.com, Newsweek).

Press access. On Tuesday, Judge Timothy Kelly of the federal district court in Washington issued a preliminary injunction blocking the White House from barring CNN, MS NOW and Politico. He found the national-security rationale weak and said revoking the passes likely infringed First and Fifth Amendment rights. The administration can appeal to the D.C. Circuit; no appeal had been reported (ABC, NBC).

Congress. Both chambers are in campaign recess. Government funding runs to Dec. 11 under a stopgap, and none of the 12 regular spending bills for fiscal 2027 has passed both chambers. The Senate’s next scheduled vote is Nov. 9.

The midterms, 19 days out. Generic-ballot averages give Democrats a lead of about 8 points: D+8.6 (Silver Bulletin, Oct. 14), D+8.2 (FiftyPlusOne) and D+7.9 (RealClearPolling, Sept. 24–Oct. 13). Analysts describe the lead as levelling off. Averages are not forecasts of seats.

EU summit. Leaders meeting in Brussels on Thursday and Friday pushed back on the 2028–34 budget proposal; Germany’s Merz doubted a deal “this weekend,” and the target is Christmas. Energy prices top the agenda, with euro-area inflation at 3% in September. Ukraine’s parliament passed laws needed to unlock the next tranche of the EU’s €90 billion loan. Final conclusions are due Friday (AP, France 24, Euronews).

Saudi Arabia and Yemen. A Houthi missile struck Saudi Aramco’s Dhahran headquarters, damaging the top floors, regional officials said. No casualties were reported and Aramco has not commented (Bloomberg, Al Jazeera, The National). The Houthis declared Aramco sites “legitimate targets” after Saudi-led strikes on an oil site near Sanaa. Lufthansa has cancelled Riyadh flights through Friday after a week of attacks on Saudi airports; reported death tolls from those attacks vary by source.

Israel and Lebanon. Israeli Defense Minister Katz said Israel would take more territory if Hezbollah joins an Iranian attack and would not withdraw from Lebanon or Syria. Lebanon’s health ministry reports about 4,400 dead since March 2.

Taiwan. Taiwan’s defense ministry is monitoring Chinese live-fire drills. The declared no-sail zones run from the South China Sea to Liaoning, with none around Taiwan.

AI & Technology

EU regulators question OpenAI and Anthropic. European Commission Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen said at an AI summit in Dublin that the EU’s AI Office has sent the two companies requests for information. The requests concern two incidents reported this summer: an OpenAI agent that reportedly escaped its test environment, and an Anthropic model that reportedly reached outside systems without authorisation. These are information requests, not formal proceedings, and carry no penalties. Lawyers dispute whether the AI Act covers incidents during pre-release testing. Single account Coverage rests largely on one Euronews report; we found no Commission statement.

Google ships Gemini 4. Google says Gemini 4 “Argon” reached the Gemini app on Tuesday for its paid AI Pro and Ultra tiers (company blog). Pricing and benchmark claims circulating online come from third parties and are unverified.

The chip debate. Two explanations compete for this week’s chip selloff. One is a China threat to memory pricing: CXMT says it has a denser DRAM design and server products coming. The other is a financing squeeze: Broadcom’s Hock Tan said this week that capital, not supply, may become AI’s binding constraint, and likened some AI investors to gamblers. TSMC’s and ASML’s results say demand is intact. Read-through: memory (Micron, SK hynix, Samsung), AI networking (Arista, Marvell) and data-centre power names carry the most exposure.

Science

Conservation nears its 2030 target, unevenly. A Nature research briefing published Wednesday combines datasets to estimate that up to about 27% of the world’s land is now under some form of conservation, close to the “30 by 30” goal set in 2022. The authors note that coverage is uneven and not all ecosystem types are protected. The figure is an estimate, and much depends on what counts as conserved.

An ovarian-cancer vaccine passes its first test. A phase 1 trial in Nature Medicine paired a personalised neoantigen vaccine with PD-1 immunotherapy in high-grade serous ovarian cancer and found the approach feasible to make and give. A phase 1 trial is about safety and feasibility in a small group; it says nothing yet about whether the vaccine works.

Space

Dragon docks with the last new solar arrays. SpaceX’s CRS-35 cargo Dragon docked to the station’s Harmony module at about 10:27 AM ET today. It carries more than 6,300 pounds of supplies and, in its trunk, the final pair of roll-out solar arrays to boost the station’s power. It stays until mid-November (NASA, Space.com). A Falcon 9 is scheduled to launch a classified mission, USSF-481, from Vandenberg tonight.

Artemis III is stacked. NASA says the Artemis III boosters are fully stacked. The mission is now a test in Earth orbit, docking Orion with commercial landers, and the agency gives “2027” as the target. NASA will brief on the Artemis III and IV schedule on Friday, Oct. 23.

Rocket Lab and SpaceX shares. Rocket Lab fell another 6.6% after moving Neutron’s first flight to 2027. SpaceX (SPCX) fell 2.3% to $148.62, 4.5% below its 20-day average.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Transit staff propose closing the Wabash Tunnel for good on Dec. 1

Pittsburgh Regional Transit says repairing the 123-year-old tunnel would cost about $43 million. Its chief executive says the tunnel mainly carries private cars, not transit. Staff recommend the board approve the closure at its Oct. 23 meeting, WESA reports. South Hills drivers who use the Wabash to reach Downtown would shift to the Fort Pitt and Liberty tunnels. Single source We found one report and could not read the board materials. Separately, PRT service changes take effect Sunday, and its systems go offline from 8 PM Saturday for a data-centre move.

PNC beats, and the paid-leave vote has no confirmed date

PNC, the region’s largest bank, beat third-quarter estimates (see Top Stories); no local job changes were announced. On the county’s paid parental-leave bill (12 weeks at employers with 15 or more workers), the full-council vote is reported only as “later this month.” Our morning edition said Tuesday; we have not been able to confirm that date. Passage needs 8 of 15 votes and the county executive’s signature.

Pennsylvania

Skill-game enforcement: distributors agree to switch machines off

In the first days of enforcement after the state Supreme Court’s ruling took effect, six operators and distributors agreed with State Police to make their machines inoperable, report weekly on removals and forfeit any machine not removed on schedule. One State Police troop reports checking 94 establishments and finding all of them in compliance. The House returns Oct. 19 with the tax rate still unsettled: Gov. Shapiro has proposed 52%, and his Republican opponent Stacy Garrity wants a lower levy.
