Top Stories

Flávio Bolsonaro beats the polls and leads Lula into Brazil’s October 25 runoff

Final surveys had Lula ahead by one to three points. Bolsonaro instead finished nearly two points in front, and his allies won key governorships, which sets Brazilian markets up for a strong open.

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro took 47.03% of valid votes in Sunday’s first round, 56.1 million votes. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva took 45.16%, 53.9 million, according to the state news agency Agência Brasil. Neither reached the 50% needed to win outright, so the two meet again on October 25. CNN, NPR, the Washington Post and Bloomberg all report the same result. The Rio Times puts abstention at 21.08%, the highest in a first round since 1998, and says Bolsonaro carried 15 states to Lula’s 12.

The polls had it the other way round. Datafolha’s final survey had Lula at 45% of valid votes and Bolsonaro at 42%, and Quaest had 46–45. The right also did well further down the ballot. Tarcísio de Freitas was re-elected governor of São Paulo with 62.65% (Rio Times). CNN reports Bolsonaro’s Liberal Party won at least five governorships. Lula said he had been “convinced I would win the election in the first round.” Bolsonaro declared “the end of the PT era.” Each campaign now has three weeks, and surveys taken before the vote showed the runoff roughly even.

Why it matters Investors have treated a change of government as the fiscally tighter outcome for Latin America’s largest economy. Brazilian assets rallied into the vote: the Ibovespa rose 2.6% Friday to 192,115, and the Brazil ETF (EWZ) closed at $38.19. One market-news service reports EWZ up about 10% in overnight trading. That is a single source and a very large move, so look for the cash open before relying on it. Analysts surveyed by Reuters expect the real to gain 2–4% against the dollar and stocks 4–6%.
What’s next B3 opens at 10:00 AM local time (9:00 ET). Lula’s campaign has three weeks to regroup and is likely to try to win over voters who backed eliminated candidates. The official count from the Superior Electoral Court is final once certified.

Go deeper

What the runoff math looks like. About 7.8% of valid votes went to other candidates. To pass 50%, Bolsonaro needs roughly four in ten of those voters and Lula roughly six in ten; the gap between them is about 2.2 million votes. Abstention is the wild card: a 21% rate leaves a lot of room for turnout to swing a three-week campaign. Pre-vote runoff polls disagreed. Datafolha had Lula 47–46, Quaest had Bolsonaro 44–42, and both results were inside the margin of error. A betting market puts Bolsonaro around 82%. That is a price, not a poll.

What we are not saying. The overnight rally is consistent with investors pricing a fiscal-policy shift, but we have no confirmed catalyst beyond the result itself. Watch the real and Brazil’s long-dated local-currency bonds. A large move in the DI futures curve would show how much fiscal risk premium the market thinks a change of government removes.

The long end starts its test: $119 billion of coupons this week, with the 10-year at 5.28%

Last week the 10-year rose 11 basis points even as soft data pulled October hike odds down by about 50 points. This week’s auctions show who is willing to hold long bonds at these yields.

Treasury’s official curve closed Friday at 4.19% for the 3-month bill, 4.83% for the 2-year, 5.06% for the 5-year, 5.28% for the 10-year and 5.63% for the 30-year. A week earlier, on September 25, those maturities stood at 4.24%, 4.81%, 4.98%, 5.17% and 5.49%. The front end barely moved; the long end did nearly all the selling. The 2s10s spread is now 45 basis points and the 3-month-to-10-year gap 109, both near the steepest of this run.

TreasuryDirect’s schedule is fixed. Today brings $95 billion of 13-week bills and $82 billion of 26-week bills at 11:30. Then come $58 billion of 3-year notes Tuesday, a $39 billion reopening of the 10-year Wednesday and a $22 billion reopening of the 30-year Thursday, all at 1:00 PM. The coupons settle October 15. Wednesday’s auction comes an hour before the Fed releases the minutes of its September 15–16 meeting. At that meeting it raised rates a quarter-point to 3.75–4.00%, unanimously, its first hike since 2023.

The pressure is global. On Monday Japan’s Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi opened the Diet promising to restrain debt issuance and “enhance market trust,” with Japanese government bond yields at record highs (FXStreet). In Europe, France’s 10-year premium over Germany has been above 130 basis points since Wednesday. Spain now faces an election, and Britain’s 30-year gilt has traded above 6% in recent sessions.

Why it matters Mortgage rates, corporate borrowing costs and stock valuations all key off the 10-year. The real 10-year yield, after inflation, was 2.88% on Thursday (FRED), and 10-year breakeven inflation was 2.36%. Most of the move is in real yields and term premium, not in inflation expectations. That points to supply and a demand for extra pay to hold long bonds, rather than an inflation scare.

Schneider Electric agrees to buy PTC for $205 a share in cash, its largest deal ever

The 42.3% premium values PTC’s equity at about $22.6 billion. Schneider’s own shares fell about 7% in Paris.

Schneider Electric, the French maker of electrical and automation equipment, said Monday it will buy PTC, the Boston-based maker of industrial design and product-lifecycle software (Creo, Windchill, ThingWorx), for $205 a share in cash. Bloomberg, Reuters and GuruFocus report the deal. PTC closed Friday at $144.03, so the premium is 42.3%. Reuters and Bloomberg put the equity value at $22.6 billion. One deal tracker gives $23.7 billion, which probably includes debt. Schneider expects €250 million of cost savings by the third year and about €800 million of extra revenue, and expects to close around the third quarter of 2027.

The financing is heavy. Morgan Stanley and Société Générale are providing a bridge loan, to be refinanced with €5–6 billion of new Schneider shares and €16–17 billion of new debt. Investing.com reports Schneider shares down about 7% in Paris, which is consistent with investors balking at the price and the dilution.

Why it matters This is the latest bet that factory and grid software becomes worth more when combined with AI. It also prices industrial software at a premium even with 10-year yields above 5%. Expect arbitrage funds to price PTC just under $205. The regulatory reviews in the US and EU, and a close almost two years away, set the size of the remaining spread. Rivals Autodesk, Dassault Systèmes, Siemens and Rockwell may trade in sympathy.

Spain calls a snap election for November 29 as the euro slides toward a 17-month low

Spain joins France as a source of political risk in Europe. The dollar index is up about 0.3% this morning.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called a general election for November 29. The announcement came less than 72 hours after Congress rejected two housing decrees his minority government had issued in response to weeks of protests over rents and evictions. CNN, CNBC, Al Jazeera and Bloomberg report the call. Polls cited by those outlets make a government led by the conservative People’s Party, relying on Vox, the most likely result. Sánchez has led the country since June 2018.

The euro fell as low as about $1.116 in Asian trading, its weakest since May 2025, according to a Reuters-sourced report, and is near $1.121 this morning (Yahoo). France’s bond spread over Germany has stayed above 130 basis points since the €43 billion austerity package. Saxo says that pressure is now reaching Italian and Belgian bonds. Paris’s CAC 40 is down about 1% this morning and the Euro Stoxx 50 about 0.4%. London is slightly higher.

Why it matters A weaker euro means a stronger dollar, which tightens financial conditions for US multinationals and emerging markets. Political risk is spreading from France to Spain, while the European Central Bank is already facing 3.8% inflation after its September hike.

Trump names intelligence chief Jay Clayton as AI czar to lead a new “Super Intelligence Force”

The FTC chairman and the Pentagon’s research chief join the group, so the new post spans national security and antitrust.

President Trump said over the weekend that Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton will be the White House AI czar and lead a new “Super Intelligence Force” to coordinate the government’s work on AI. NPR, CNBC, UPI and MS NOW report the announcement. Clayton chaired the SEC and was US attorney in Manhattan before becoming DNI. The force also includes FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Under Secretary for Research and Engineering Emil Michael and OPM Director Scott Kupor. Trump said the goal is to “ensure that America continues to lead the World in Super Intelligence.” Clayton has said publicly that superintelligence “is a national security issue.” The group has 120 days to report.

Why it matters With an antitrust regulator inside the group, industry structure is in scope as well as national security. That matters for Nvidia, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta, and for the private labs: Anthropic, which Bloomberg reports is preparing an IPO, and OpenAI, which is reported to be raising money. No rules or orders have been issued. This is a structure, not a policy.

The Supreme Court opens its term with the Boulder climate-liability case

The question is whether federal law blocks state-law suits seeking damages from oil companies for climate harms.

The justices hear Suncor Energy v. Commissioners of Boulder County at 10:00 this morning. Boulder and other Colorado governments sued Suncor and ExxonMobil under state law for climate-related damage. The companies argue that federal law precludes state claims over interstate and international emissions. Suncor says at least 60 state and local governments have filed similar suits. The court also hears Johnson v. U.S. Congress, on whether veterans can bring constitutional challenges to benefits laws in district court. An orders list was issued this morning; we have not read its contents.

Why it matters A ruling for the companies would end most of the climate-damage suits against oil producers. A ruling for Boulder would let dozens go to trial in state courts. It affects Exxon, Chevron, Suncor and other producers. A decision is likely by June.

Risks & Scenarios

Weak long-end auctions (the main risk this week). We put the odds that the 10-year ends the week, on Friday October 9, at or above 5.20% at 70–80%. Basis: it is at 5.28%, the front end is anchored by a Fed that just hiked, the coupon supply is heavy, and foreign long-end yields are rising. A drop below 5.20% would most likely need a sharp fall in oil or a very soft ISM.

Oil. We put the odds that Brent settles above $100 on Friday at 60–75%. Basis: it has held above $100 all of last week despite the G7 release and record-level Middle East exports. Iran’s conditions for reopening the strait are unchanged, and tanker attacks continue. The downside case is a credible diplomatic step or proof that the strait is open in practice; the upside case is an attack on Saudi infrastructure that someone can confirm.

Brazil. We put the odds that Flávio Bolsonaro wins the October 25 runoff at 55–65%. Basis: he leads by 1.9 points, his allies did well in the governor and Senate races, and he beat his polling, but pre-vote runoff polls were tied within the margin of error. This is a judgment, not a poll.

Europe. A second southern-European election with France’s budget still unresolved raises the risk that the bond selloff spreads within the euro area. A run of lower euro lows would tighten dollar funding conditions worldwide.

The Gulf. A confirmed US strike on the Houthis, which Axios says the Saudis are pressing for, would widen the war beyond Iran itself. It is not priced as a base case.

Science

The Nobel for medicine goes to optogenetics. The Karolinska Institute awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine to Karl Deisseroth of Stanford, Peter Hegemann of Humboldt University Berlin and Georg Nagel of the University of Würzburg “for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics.” Hegemann and Nagel found channelrhodopsin, a light-sensitive protein in a single-celled alga. Deisseroth put its gene into neurons so that a flash of blue light can switch individual brain cells on or off in a living animal. That made it possible to test which circuits drive memory, fear, movement and reward rather than just watching them. The prize is 12 million Swedish kronor. STAT, CNN, ETV Bharat and China Daily report the award. Physics is announced Tuesday and chemistry Wednesday.

Bats. A Nature paper published October 1 reports genomes for 103 bat species covering all 21 families. It places the origin of bats about 60–65 million years ago, in Europe.

The Duquesne Light strike enters its first full week with no deal reported

About 1,000 members of IBEW Local 29 have been on strike since 12:01 AM Thursday, October 1. They voted 592–14 to authorize it. We found no statement from the PUC, Governor Shapiro, Mayor O’Connor or County Executive Innamorato.

Pennsylvania

Three session days left before skill games become seizable

The General Assembly is in session today through Wednesday. These are its last scheduled days before October 13, when the 120-day stay of the Pennsylvania Supreme Court’s ruling that skill games are illegal slot machines runs out. State Police have told operators to remove the machines by then and plan to begin seizures October 14. About 70,000 machines sit in bars, clubs and restaurants. The two sides are far apart on the tax rate. Governor Shapiro proposed 52%, Senate Republicans 35%, and a House bill from Rep. Russ Diamond 20% with a five-machine cap. They also disagree on where the money goes: the General Fund under the Senate plan, transit under House Democrats’. Senate Majority Leader Joe Pittman says he is “not overly optimistic.” House Majority Leader Matt Bradford says the House is waiting on the Senate.

Why it matters A deal would legalize and tax machines found in many South Hills bars and clubs, and could send new money to Pittsburgh Regional Transit. Without one, the machines go dark after next Monday.
