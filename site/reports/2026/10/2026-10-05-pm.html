Top Stories

The Nasdaq closes at a record while the 10-year Treasury yield reaches its highest since 2002

Equities and long yields rose together again. The 10-year finished at 5.31% on Treasury’s curve after touching about 5.35% intraday, the night before the first of $119 billion of coupon auctions.

The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.05% to 27,477.31, above its previous record close of September 22, and the Nasdaq-100 also closed at a record (CNBC, TheStreet). The S&P 500 rose 0.66% to 7,773.95, about half a percent below its 52-week high of 7,816.70. Nvidia set an all-time high, and Nvidia and Tesla each rose about 2% (Yahoo Finance). The Dow lagged, up 0.18%.

The bond market went the other way. Treasury’s official closing curve:

  • 3-month bill: 4.22%, up 3bp
  • 2-year: 4.84%, up 1bp
  • 10-year: 5.31%, up 3bp
  • 20-year: 5.70%, up 3bp
  • 30-year: 5.66%, up 3bp

CNBC put the 10-year’s intraday high at about 5.347%, the highest since April 3, 2002. The 2s10s spread widened to 47bp on the par curve. That is a bear steepener: long yields rising faster than short ones, which usually points to term premium, supply and inflation risk rather than to expectations for the Fed’s next move. The 10-year TIPS yield was 2.92% and the 10-year breakeven 2.36% on Friday’s FRED data, so more than half of the 10-year yield is real yield rather than inflation compensation.

Outlets described stocks as “shrugging off” or “looking past” higher yields (Yahoo Finance, CNBC). Market narrative Commentators pointed to Friday’s weak payrolls report, which cut the odds of an October Fed hike, and to continued enthusiasm for AI-linked stocks. Neither is a confirmed cause of today’s gain.

Why it matters A 10-year near 5.3% and an S&P near its record leave stocks priced at a thin premium to bonds. Rising together is sustainable only while earnings expectations rise faster than the discount rate. The technology leaders are carrying that, and the equal-weight index and small caps remain below their 50-day averages.
What’s next The $58 billion 3-year on Tuesday, the $39 billion 10-year reopening and the September FOMC minutes on Wednesday, and the $22 billion 30-year on Thursday, all at 1:00 PM except the minutes at 2:00 PM.

Sources: Treasury par yield curve (primary, Oct 5); FRED (DFII10, T10YIE); CNBC; Yahoo Finance; TheStreet.

Brazil’s market posts a record after the first-round upset; EWZ jumps 12.5%

The Ibovespa rose 7.7% and the real about 4% as investors priced a stronger chance that Flávio Bolsonaro wins the October 25 runoff.

With nearly all votes counted, Flávio Bolsonaro took about 47% to President Lula’s 45%, according to Brazil’s electoral authorities (AP, NPR). Polls had shown Lula ahead. The two now go to a runoff on October 25.

The market reaction was among the largest single-day moves in Brazil in years:

  • Ibovespa: +7.70%, to a record 206,911.89, with an intraday high of 209,605.70 (CNN Brasil)
  • Dollar–real: about R$5.00 at the close, down roughly 4.1–4.4% depending on source, meaning a stronger real (CNN Brasil, Yahoo)
  • EWZ (iShares MSCI Brazil): +12.54%, to $42.98, on about 187 million shares against a three-month average near 27 million (Yahoo)
  • Petrobras ADRs: up more than 11% at the open (24/7 Wall St.)

Investors appeared to focus on the prospect of a more market-friendly government and on state-owned companies such as Petrobras, where a change of government would most directly change policy. That is the reading in market coverage, not a stated platform: we found no detailed economic programme from the Bolsonaro campaign. Lula said on Sunday night that he had been “convinced that I would win” in the first round. The runoff campaign started today. Neither the third-place finisher, Augusto Cury, nor two other eliminated candidates has endorsed anyone, and both camps are courting the centrist parties known as the Centrão (ND+, Euronews).

Why it matters Today priced much of a Bolsonaro win in one session. Three weeks of campaigning remain, and a two-point first-round lead is not decisive. If polls tighten, the reversal could be just as sharp.

Sources: CNN Brasil; AP via Washington Times; NPR; Euronews; ND+; Yahoo Finance (EWZ, BRL=X); Reuters via MarketScreener.

Services activity cools slightly, but the ISM prices index reaches its highest since July 2022

The headline missed by a fraction. The inflation signal inside the survey is what matters for a Fed that hiked in September.

The Institute for Supply Management’s services PMI for September came in at 54.9, down from 55.4 and just below the 55.2 consensus (ISM, Reuters). Readings above 50 mean expansion. The components:

  • Prices paid: 74.0, up from 72.6, the highest since July 2022
  • Employment: 50.1, up from 47.8, back above 50 after two months of contraction
  • New orders: 59.8, down 1.1 points
  • Business activity: 56.5, down 5.2 points

Respondents listed copper, diesel, steel, petroleum products and memory chips among items rising in price. Those levels are diffusion indexes: 74 means far more firms reported paying higher prices than lower ones. It says nothing about how much prices rose. Taken together, the survey describes services demand cooling from a high level while input costs accelerate, which pulls the opposite way from Friday’s soft payrolls.

Why it matters The Fed raised rates in September. Friday’s weak jobs report cut the market’s odds of another hike on October 28 to somewhere around 15–25%, depending on the source. A survey showing the fastest input-price growth in over three years keeps December in play and gives the minutes on Wednesday more weight.

Sources: ISM Services ROB (via PR Newswire); Reuters via Investing.com; AP; FXStreet.

A third tanker is hit near Hormuz in 48 hours, and Iran’s president calls US talks “meaningless”

Brent rose above $103 early after a Houthi strike claim on Saudi oil sites, then fell 1.9% to settle at $100.32.

The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency reported a tanker struck by an unidentified projectile on Monday, causing an engine-room fire and no casualties. Two tankers were hit on Sunday (UKMTO, via Dawn, the Times of Israel and others). Separately, Iran’s Revolutionary Guard hailed a tanker about 11 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, and ordered it to turn back; it complied. Iran’s Fars agency, which is affiliated with the Guard, says 13 “violator” tankers have been targeted over seven days. That claim is single-sourced and from an interested party.

President Masoud Pezeshkian said talks with the United States are “meaningless,” saying the US attacked Iran three times after earlier talks and wants to overthrow the Islamic Republic (state media via Korea Times and Daily Sabah). Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said on Sunday the strait stays closed until Washington meets seven conditions. The White House has disclosed no decisions from Friday’s meeting at Camp David.

Oil traded on both sides of that. Likely contributor Brent reached about $103.06 early after Yemen’s Houthis claimed missile and drone attacks on Saudi Aramco facilities near Riyadh and at Khurais. The Saudi-led coalition called the claims “misleading,” and we found no Saudi damage assessment. Brent then settled down 1.9% at $100.32 and WTI down about 1.8% at $89.43 (CNBC). Coverage pointed to rising Middle East crude exports from outside the Gulf and the G7 agreement on Friday to release up to 100 million barrels. Aramco’s chief executive told CNBC that rebuilding global inventories could take up to two years.

Sources: UKMTO advisories via Dawn, Times of Israel, Cyprus Mail; Fars (Iranian state-affiliated); Korea Times; Daily Sabah; CNBC; investingLive.

The dollar index rose toward the top of its 52-week range, and Spain’s spread over German bonds widened modestly.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called a snap general election for November 29 after his government lost a vote on emergency housing measures (NPR). In a televised address he argued a broader progressive majority could tackle the country’s largest problems. The opposition Popular Party’s leader, Alberto Núñez Feijóo, said “At last, the Spanish people will decide.”

The euro touched about $1.1118, its weakest since May 2025, and was near $1.122 late in New York (Reuters via Yahoo; Yahoo). The ECB’s reference rate was $1.1204. The ICE dollar index rose 0.22% to 102.16, close to its 52-week high of 102.54. Spain’s 10-year yield was about 4.08%, with the spread over Bunds 3bp wider. Coverage linked the euro’s fall to Spain and to continuing French debt concerns. The euro is down nearly 5% on the year.

Sources: NPR/OPB; Reuters via Yahoo Finance; CNBC; Frankfurter (ECB reference rates); Yahoo Finance (DX-Y.NYB).

The Supreme Court opens its term with a divided hearing on Boulder’s climate suit against oil companies

The question is whether federal law blocks state-law lawsuits over global emissions. Coverage of how the justices leaned differed widely.

In Suncor Energy (U.S.A.) Inc. v. County Commissioners of Boulder County (No. 25-170), the justices heard argument on a Colorado Supreme Court ruling that let Boulder’s state-law suit against Suncor and ExxonMobil go forward. The companies, supported by the federal government, argue the Clean Air Act and federal law on interstate pollution preempt such suits. Boulder argues its claims are ordinary state-law claims about deception and local harm.

Reports on the argument conflicted. NBC described the justices as “stumped,” The Hill as skeptical of the oil companies, and the Daily Signal and Daily Caller as questioning Boulder’s claims. Justice Kavanaugh cited “a wall of precedent” treating interstate pollution as a federal matter, and Justice Jackson suggested the case might be premature for the Court. A ruling is expected by June 2027; the case is at the merits stage after the Colorado court’s decision. For energy companies, dozens of similar suits by states and cities ride on the outcome.

The Court’s first orders list of the term denied review in several closely watched cases: former Baltimore State’s Attorney Marilyn Mosby’s appeal of her federal perjury convictions, Roy Moore’s appeal over a defamation verdict that an appeals court had wiped out, a students’ free-speech case over “Let’s Go Brandon” sweatshirts, and a challenge to Minnesota’s refusal to honor out-of-state concealed-carry permits (Fox Baltimore, The Hill, CNN, Daily Caller News Foundation). We could not open the orders list itself to check for grants or noted dissents.

Sources: Supreme Court docket; NBC; The Hill; Washington Examiner; Daily Signal; Daily Caller; CNN; Fox Baltimore.

The Economy

Services. The ISM services PMI fell to 54.9 in September from 55.4, slightly below the 55.2 consensus. AI & Technology

AI companies testify under oath in New York. Senior staff from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta gave sworn testimony on AI risks today at a hearing of the full New York City Council, which coverage described as the first such appearance by the major labs before a legislative body (CNBC, Quartz, CBS New York). Several companies initially declined and agreed only after Council Speaker Menin authorized subpoenas. Witnesses included OpenAI’s Morgan Dwyer and Anthropic’s Logan Graham, head of its Frontier Red Team, as well as independent researchers, one of whom, a former Anthropic employee described as a whistleblower, said it is “more likely than not that humanity loses control to these AIs.” The hearing follows disclosures over the summer of model “containment failures” at several labs. A city council has limited power over AI developers; the significance is the sworn record. OpenAI and Anthropic are private.

Chip startups. Etched, a private maker of AI inference chips, is fielding offers at a valuation of $40 billion or more, about double the $21 billion of its last round two months ago, according to TechCrunch, citing people familiar with the matter. Single source

Cybersecurity. Citrix disclosed on Sunday a critical flaw in NetScaler ADC and Gateway appliances (CVE-2026-88779) that attackers exploited before a fix was available. It affects devices configured for SAML single sign-on. The federal cyber agency CISA added it to its list of known exploited vulnerabilities and gave federal agencies until Wednesday to patch (BleepingComputer, SecurityWeek, Tenable). No one has been named as responsible. It is the third exploited NetScaler flaw in recent weeks, and widely used remote-access gateways are a common way into corporate networks. Citrix’s owner, Cloud Software Group, is private.

Science

The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went to Karl Deisseroth of Stanford, Peter Hegemann of Humboldt University of Berlin and Georg Nagel of the University of Würzburg “for their discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics” (Nobel Assembly, via Nature, STAT and AP). Hegemann and Nagel found channelrhodopsin, a light-sensitive protein in a green alga that opens a channel through the cell membrane when light hits it. Deisseroth’s lab put it into mammalian neurons in 2005, so that individual brain cells could be switched on with a pulse of light. That made optogenetics a standard tool for working out which circuits drive behaviour, and it is now being tested for restoring partial sight. The physics prize is announced tomorrow and chemistry on Wednesday.

Bat origins. A study in this week’s Nature sequenced the genomes of 103 bat species across all 21 families. It dates the origin of bats to about 60–65 million years ago, in Europe, soon after the extinction of the dinosaurs. We read it only through the journal’s listings.
