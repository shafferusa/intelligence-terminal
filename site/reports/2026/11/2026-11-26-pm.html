Closing Edition

Europe closes near 2% higher and U.S. futures hold their gains on a closed Thanksgiving

Thursday, November 26, 2026 · 28 min read

With New York shut for the holiday, Europe’s markets finished about 1.6–2.1% higher and U.S. stock futures stopped trading at 1 PM still half a percent up, while the ECB’s minutes showed broad support for another rate rise, eurozone leaders backed Spain’s Pablo Hernández de Cos to run the central bank, drones killed two Italian contractors in Kuwait, and Altria said it may bid for Imperial Brands.

The Brief

S&P 500 futures halted at 1 PM at 7,234.50, up 0.54% from Wednesday’s settlement, almost exactly where they stood at dawn. Stocks reopen Friday for a session that ends at 1 PM.

The ECB’s account of its October meeting shows “large support” for a rise even then, and members said another increase would likely prove necessary; a December 17 move is widely expected. Eurozone leaders agreed to recommend Pablo Hernández de Cos as Christine Lagarde’s successor. Tokyo’s November inflation figures arrive at 6:30 PM, with core prices expected at 3.0%.
  • Rates and the Fed. No Treasury trading today; Wednesday’s 10-year par yield of 4.85% stands. Ten-year note futures slipped 0.20% before their halt, pointing to slightly higher yields on Friday. A quarter-point Fed rise on December 9 is priced at about 98%.
  • Biggest risk. The Gulf. Drones killed two Italian technicians at an air-defence site in Kuwait; Italy calls the drones Iranian-made, and Iran denies involvement. Satellite images show smoke over Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery, and one shipping source says loadings were suspended, but damage is unconfirmed. U.S. crude rose 1.5% to $84.91.

Top Stories

Europe’s rebound holds to the close, and U.S. futures stop trading with their morning gain intact

A thin holiday session gave the overnight bounce no reason to fade. Banks, miners and chip stocks led in Europe; London had a takeover story of its own.

The Stoxx Europe 600 closed up 1.75% at 636.98, a little above where it stood this morning, and recovered all of Wednesday’s 1.1% fall. Germany’s DAX rose 2.08% to 25,215.52 and the mid-cap MDAX 2.51%; London’s FTSE 100 gained 1.75% to 10,609.30 and the CAC 40 in Paris 1.61% to 8,108.74, where advancing shares outnumbered decliners by 338 to 126 (Yahoo Finance closes; dpa-AFX and Investing.com as summarized). One summary of CNBC data has the Stoxx 600 up 1.83% at 637.50, and one report puts the DAX gain at 2.14%; the exchange levels above are the ones we could check.

Outlets attributed the rally to two things already visible in Asia overnight: a reading of Wednesday’s U.S. Section 301 tariff proposals as less damaging for allies than feared, and a bounce in technology and chip shares after heavy AI-related selling, helped by Wall Street’s recovery on Wednesday. Banks and miners led in London. These are the explanations outlets gave; with New York shut and volumes thin, we cannot show they drove the move.

In the U.S., the abbreviated futures session ended at 1 PM. S&P 500 futures settled the day at 7,234.50, up 0.54% from Wednesday; Nasdaq-100 futures were up 0.46% at 26,221.25, Dow futures 0.43% at 50,443 and Russell 2000 futures 0.54% at 2,720.80 (Yahoo Finance, delayed). That is within a fraction of where they stood at 6:30 AM. Ten-year Treasury note futures fell 0.20%, which implies yields a couple of basis points above Wednesday’s 4.85% when the cash market reopens.

Why it matters Friday’s half-day session starts from a market that has now risen two days running from Wednesday’s 3.2% intraday low, with Europe, Asia and U.S. futures all pointing the same way. The test is volume: Friday after Thanksgiving is one of the thinnest sessions of the year, and the VIX closed Wednesday at 28.29, still a stressed level.
What’s next Tokyo’s November inflation figures at 6:30 PM tonight; U.S. stocks trade 9:30 AM to 1 PM on Friday, bonds until 2 PM, with no major U.S. data. The Fed’s pre-meeting quiet period starts Saturday.

ECB minutes show broad support for raising rates in October, and another rise “would likely prove necessary”

The account makes a December 17 increase look close to settled. Separately, eurozone leaders lined up behind Spain’s Pablo Hernández de Cos to succeed Christine Lagarde.

The European Central Bank’s account of its October 28–29 meeting, published at 7:30 AM Eastern, shows what Reuters called “large support” for an increase even at that meeting, when the Governing Council held its deposit rate at 2.50% after rises in June and September. Members said data since September had strengthened the case for tightening, that an October move would only have brought forward an increase, and that another rise “would likely prove necessary” unless the inflation outlook improved significantly. Euro-area inflation was 3.8% in September and 4.0% in October, and some members doubted it would return to the 2% target even by the end of 2028, with energy costs well above the bank’s assumptions. The ECB repeated that it is not committing in advance to a path (Reuters headline via syndicated outlets; summaries of the account). We could not open the account itself, and the summaries give no count of how many members favoured a rise.

At the euro summit in Brussels, eurozone leaders agreed to recommend Pablo Hernández de Cos, the head of the Bank for International Settlements and former governor of the Bank of Spain, as the next ECB president after the Netherlands withdrew Klaas Knot, Euronews and Il Sole 24 Ore report. The same statement reportedly covers board nominations for Laurence Boone and Markus Brunnermeier. It is a recommendation: euro-area finance officials and then all EU leaders must still approve it formally. Lagarde’s term ends in October 2027.

Why it matters Europe tightening alongside the Fed, the Bank of Korea and probably the Bank of Japan means the global rise in borrowing costs has not peaked. For markets, an orderly succession at the ECB removes one source of uncertainty for 2027; Hernández de Cos is seen as a mainstream choice.

Drones kill two Italian contractors at an air-defence site in Kuwait as Gulf attacks spread

Italy says the drones were Iranian-made; Iran denies it. Satellite images show smoke over Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery, but no one has confirmed damage.

Two Italian technicians working for Rheinmetall Italia, who maintained radar for Kuwait’s Skyguard air-defence system, were killed on Thursday when drones struck an air-defence detection site; falling debris injured a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani worker. Kuwait said it engaged several hostile drones. Italy’s foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, said the drones were Iranian-made and noted that “numerous pro-Iranian militias operate in the region.” Iran’s armed forces spokesman denied involvement, and its foreign minister offered a joint investigation. It is reported as the first deadly attack on Kuwaiti territory since July (Al Jazeera, CBC, ABC Australia, Al-Monitor). Attribution disputed

On the Houthi claim of a strike on Ras Tanura, Saudi Arabia’s largest refinery (about 550,000 barrels a day) and a main export terminal: NASA and Planet Labs satellite images reviewed by Reuters and CNN show a large black smoke plume over the site on Wednesday, and one shipping source told CNN that terminal loadings were suspended. The Saudi-led coalition says it intercepted four drones and that falling debris caused no injuries; it has not said the refinery was hit, and Aramco has not commented. We found no report today on whether loadings have resumed. Damage unconfirmed

In the Strait of Hormuz, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards again claimed that tankers on “unauthorized” routes hit mines north of Oman’s Khasab port; reports differ on whether two or three ships were claimed today. No ship has been named, and U.S. Central Command has repeatedly called the mine claims false. The UK’s maritime security agency, UKMTO, separately reported an unidentified projectile exploding near a tanker about 50 nautical miles west of Dubai, with no stated link to the Iranian claims.

Oil rose modestly. U.S. crude futures were $84.91 a barrel at the 2:30 PM halt, up 1.5% from Wednesday’s settlement, and February Brent $91.73, up from about $91 this morning (Yahoo Finance, delayed). One aggregator puts the expiring January Brent contract at $96.57, up $1.96; we could not confirm that figure from a major outlet.

Why it matters The attacks now reach three Gulf states in two days, and the Kuwait deaths involve citizens of a NATO country. Oil is still pricing them largely as noise; a confirmed outage at Ras Tanura would change that quickly. OPEC+ meets on Sunday, and Reuters sources say the group may simply roll over its current targets into early 2027.

Altria says it is weighing a cash bid for Imperial Brands; the shares jump 13%

It would be one of the largest tobacco deals in years. Imperial says it has received no proposal, and Japan Tobacco is reported to be looking too.

Altria, the maker of Marlboro cigarettes in the U.S., confirmed under British takeover rules that it is considering a possible offer for Imperial Brands, likely in cash, and that it is in talks with a third party about part of Imperial’s business. Imperial, whose brands include Davidoff and Gauloises, said it has received no proposal. Reuters reports Japan Tobacco is also weighing a bid. Imperial’s shares rose as much as 17.6% to 3,562 pence and closed up about 13.3% at 3,432 pence. Under the UK code Altria has until 5 PM on December 24 to make a firm offer or walk away (Reuters, lse.co.uk, as summarized).

Why it matters Altria’s U.S. shares have not traded since the announcement; Friday’s session will show how investors weigh a large cash deal against its dividend. A competing bid from Japan Tobacco would raise the price.

Altria and Imperial Brands statements under the UK Takeover Code, via Reuters and London market reports (as summarized). Deal not agreed; Japan Tobacco interest single-sourced.

China “reserves the right” to respond to the new U.S. tariff proposals but announces no retaliation

Beijing also pressed Washington to carry out September’s tariff-cut deal, which the proposals exempt.

Commerce ministry spokesman He Yadong said China “firmly opposes” the Section 301 proposals on 14 economies, called them unilateral, urged dialogue and said China reserves the right to take necessary measures, according to Xinhua as summarized. He noted that the proposals exempt the roughly $30 billion of goods covered by the bilateral tariff-cut arrangement agreed in September, and urged that it be implemented quickly. Reports still differ on the proposed rate for China: 20% on top of existing duties in most accounts, rising to as much as 40% if the trade truce lapses. Public comments are due January 5; nothing has taken effect.

China’s Ministry of Commerce via Xinhua (as summarized) · CNBC · USTR proposal as reported. Stage: proposed.

EU leaders fail to agree a budget or the €45 billion Ukraine loan on the summit’s first day

France’s president said there would be no deal at this summit; leaders try again on December 17–18.

The special European Council in Brussels, called to settle the 2028–34 long-term budget, Ukraine’s financing for 2027 and the ECB succession, made progress only on the last. On the budget, reported at about $1.76 trillion, Emmanuel Macron said “there will be no agreement at this summit,” and Italy’s Giorgia Meloni described the sides as “light-years away”; Euronews reported a boycott by the “frugal” member states. On a proposed joint loan of up to €45 billion for Ukraine, Germany is pressing every member to contribute, while Hungary, Czechia and Slovakia are staying out and Bulgaria will join but not its military strand (AP via WTOP, Al Jazeera, France 24, Euronews). Council President António Costa expects conclusions on Friday.

Why it matters Ukraine’s budget for 2027 depends on this money, and Russia has stepped up strikes on its power grid. A slip to December leaves little margin before the year ends.

European Council (agenda) · AP, Al Jazeera, France 24, Euronews (as summarized).

The Thanksgiving storm cuts power to hundreds of thousands and snarls flights in the East

The worst of the wind falls on one of the busiest travel days of the year, and disruption is expected into Friday.

Utility counts aggregated by outlets put outages above one million customers early Thursday, falling to roughly 660,000–800,000 later in the day, with Virginia worst hit at more than 200,000 and a wet snowstorm cutting power to more than 120,000 in Missouri. Gusts topped 60 mph in Delaware, North Carolina, Virginia and West Virginia. By noon about 400 U.S. flights had been cancelled and more than 1,200 delayed; Baltimore–Washington International scrubbed about one in four morning departures (Weather.com, NBC News, Fox Weather, NPR, as summarized). Philadelphia cancelled its Thanksgiving parade; Macy’s parade in New York runs Saturday at 8:30 AM. CNN calls the Macy’s move the first weather postponement in the parade’s history, while one outlet says it is the first since 1934; we cannot settle which is right. We found no confirmed storm deaths.

The United States

The president’s holiday message. President Trump posted a Thanksgiving message on Truth Social that, as reported by Reuters and Newsweek, extended greetings to political opponents he accuses of wrongdoing and criticised immigrants and the federal government; critics called it a tirade, and the White House has not commented further. He also reposted the joint statement he and NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte issued after meeting at Mar-a-Lago on Wednesday, urging Russia to avoid actions that “expand the war” and to seek peace quickly. The World

Ukraine. Russia attacked overnight with ballistic missiles and 113 drones, according to Ukraine’s air force; a shopping centre in Kyiv’s Desnianskyi district was hit, and Mayor Vitali Klitschko said seven people were injured. Reported deaths nationwide range from one to at least three, and the Kharkiv toll differs between outlets. Science

Drained peatlands. A study in Nature on Wednesday estimates that draining the world’s peatlands since 1800 accounts for about 2.7% of present-day human-caused warming, because drained peat keeps releasing stored carbon long after the land is converted; MIT’s César Terrer is among the authors quoted. The finding is an estimate from models and inventories, and it strengthens the case that rewetting peat is a durable climate lever (Nature World News, Ecoticias, as summarized).

DNA repair. Researchers at Spain’s CNIO and Stanford report that the enzyme PRIMPOL binds the main replication helicase to restart DNA copying when it stalls; a possible link to chemotherapy resistance is proposed, not shown. This rests on a single secondary summary.
