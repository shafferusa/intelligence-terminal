Nvidia’s beat lifts futures as Wall Street turns to Anthropic’s pricing and the Fed minutes

Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · 31 min read

Nvidia’s results and a $130 billion forecast have Nasdaq futures up about 1% after the S&P 500’s lowest close since July, while gold, copper and oil slide and the day builds toward the Fed’s minutes at 2:00 PM and the largest IPO pricing on record tonight.

Top Stories

Nvidia beats and guides to $130 billion, and the chip trade rallies before the bell

The first report under an interim chief executive cleared every revenue bar. The softer margin guide, a reported SEC inquiry and the leadership question remain.

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.84 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, against about $109.1 billion expected, and adjusted earnings of $2.68 a share against about $2.47 (company release, as reported by Benzinga and Reuters). For the quarter to January it guided to $130 billion plus or minus 3%, a range of $126.1–133.9 billion, against an average forecast of about $125 billion. The guide includes an extra week in the quarter and assumes no data-centre revenue from China: the company said it no longer ships licensed products there, and no licensed Hopper chips are in the forecast because of the new Commerce Department export controls.

The weak spot was margin. Nvidia guided adjusted gross margin to 71–72%, below the 74.9% it has recently run at, which several outlets called a warning. It also added $150 billion to its share buyback, leaving $227.3 billion available, and interim chief executive Colette Kress said it intends to use the new authorisation by the end of fiscal 2028. Sources disagree on what the company said about growth next year (one report says about 75%, another “70%-plus”), so we do not print a figure.

On leadership, the board said it will not reinstate Jensen Huang, who resigned on November 12, and the call named no successor (Reuters). Separately, the Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday that the SEC has opened a civil inquiry into whether Nvidia’s disclosures misled investors, citing people familiar with the matter; Nvidia has not confirmed it. The export inquiry into Singapore’s Megaspeed is now criminal, according to the same reporting.

Before the bell the shares were up about 3.7% at $161.19 (Benzinga, 5:53 AM), against Tuesday’s close of $155.46, which left the stock down nearly 25% for November (Investing.com). Peers followed: AMD about +3%, Broadcom +2.5%, Micron +3.4%, Marvell +4.4% (Reuters), and the cloud-compute renters CoreWeave, Nebius and Iren up 6–7%.

Why it matters The chip sell-off since mid-November rested on two fears: that the export rule would cut demand, and that Nvidia’s leadership crisis hid something in the numbers. A $130 billion guide without China answers the first for now. The margin guide and the SEC inquiry keep the second open.
What’s next Premarket gains in the chip group have faded twice this month. Watch whether SMH holds a gain through the 2:00 PM minutes, and whether the rally reaches beyond chips: Tuesday’s losses hit every sector.

FINRA’s daily short-sale volume was 23.6% of Nvidia’s trading on Tuesday, against 46.9% across all names. Low short volume in a stock that fell 25% in a month fits with holders selling rather than new short positions, but short volume is not short interest and proves nothing on its own. Nvidia closed $6.66 above its 52-week low of $148.80.

What would undo the read: a close in the red after a gap up, as Cisco and Applied Materials did after beats last week, would say the market is selling strength in the group regardless of the numbers.

Anthropic prices tonight, with demand reported at about seven times the shares on offer

Bankers are said to expect a price at or above the top of the $70–78 range. Trading on Nasdaq as ANT starts Thursday.

Anthropic is selling 825 million Class A shares, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading. At $78 that would raise about $64 billion, alongside a separately reported $26 billion private placement. Bloomberg reported last week that the book was about six times covered and that the company expects to price at or above the top of the range. CNBC’s David Faber says above $78 is likely, and the Wall Street Journal says the range could be lifted above $80. All of these are anonymous-source reports before pricing. A $1.4 trillion valuation has appeared in one report and is not confirmed. No change to the share count has been announced.

Why it matters The listing pulls cash from the rest of the market. Tuesday’s slide in software (Salesforce −9.45%, ServiceNow −6.5%) followed reports of investors selling to fund allocations, and a pricing above range would make the deal the largest IPO on record.

Target beats on a tariff refund and lifts its forecast; Lowe’s raises sales guidance

The two retailers reported before the open. TJX follows later this morning.

Target earned $2.65 a share in its third quarter, against about $2.06–2.08 expected, but $0.50 of that came from a $301 million refund of tariffs collected under the emergency-powers law (IEEPA). Net sales rose 7.1% to $27.1 billion. The company reports comparable sales up 5.6%, with traffic up 5.0% and digital up 9.8%; one summary attributed that figure to a different quarter, so it should be checked against the release. Target raised its full-year earnings guide to $10.65–11.40, including about $2.14 of refunds so far, and lifted sales growth to about 6%.

Lowe’s earned an adjusted $3.10 a share against $2.88–2.91 expected, on sales of $22.8 billion, up 9.3% with the help of acquisitions; comparable sales rose 1.8%. It raised its full-year comparable-sales guide to about +0.5% from flat and its sales guide to about $92.5 billion, but trimmed its operating-margin guide to about 11.1% from 11.2%. Premarket moves for both had not been reported at press time. Home Depot, which reported Tuesday, fell 2.45%.

Gold hits a one-year low and copper drops 4% as the dollar firms

Metals and oil fell together overnight while the dollar index pushed toward its high.

Front-month gold futures were at $3,768.60 at about 6:28 AM, down 2.5% and just above their 52-week low of $3,759.90; spot gold hit its lowest since September 2025 (Reuters, via Business Recorder). Silver fell 3.4% to $45.69, near its own 52-week low. Copper fell 3.9% to $5.74 a pound, which dragged London’s miners. The ICE dollar index rose 0.27% to 103.77, against a 52-week high of 103.86, and the yen stayed near 159.3 per dollar. The outlets we read tied the metals’ fall to the firm dollar and to a 96% probability of a Fed rate rise in December. No single confirmed catalyst explains the size of the overnight move.

Oil fell too: Brent was down 3.5% at $89.87 and WTI 2.6% at $83.78 (Yahoo; Reuters had Brent at $92.53 earlier, against a different contract base). The American Petroleum Institute reported a 5.28-million-barrel crude build for the week to November 13, against an expected draw, plus a 5.12-million-barrel rise in gasoline; the government’s figures come at 10:30 AM.

Why it matters Real yields near 2.9% and a dollar at a high are a hard combination for metals, and gold has fallen 6.5% in a week. Falling oil is the Fed’s one piece of good inflation news this month, if it lasts.

Iran targets a U.S. base in Kuwait after an American strike on Qeshm Island

The Gulf exchange widened overnight to a third country, and Gulf states have asked the UN Security Council to act.

After an Iranian attack on Tuesday on a U.S. radar in Bahrain, a U.S. fighter struck a Revolutionary Guards missile-launch site on Iran’s Qeshm Island, according to AP. Early Wednesday Iran’s army said it targeted U.S. drone and missile sites at Kuwait’s Ali Al Salem air base. Kuwait said it intercepted the drones, reported no immediate damage, and its foreign ministry called the attack a “flagrant violation” and a “dangerous escalation.” Bahrain said it intercepted Iranian attacks again; outlets count this as the third or fourth time in about a week. The Gulf Cooperation Council condemned the attacks and asked the Security Council to act.

At sea, the Abu Dhabi oil company ADNOC says one crew member is missing and 11 are injured after Tuesday’s attack on a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran also claims three more tankers struck mines on routes it calls unauthorised; it has offered no evidence, and the U.S. says it has cleared the main routes. Iran says the strait remains closed; the U.S. says its blockade force is “in full force,” now with a third carrier. Mediation by Qatar and Oman is stuck on sequencing: Iran wants the blockade lifted first, and the U.S. wants simultaneous, verifiable steps.

Why it matters A U.S. strike on Iranian territory and an Iranian attack on Kuwait both mark a wider exchange than last week’s claims and interceptions, yet Brent fell 3.5% overnight. Oil is trading inventories and next year’s glut forecasts, not the war. That leaves room for a sharp move back if a cargo is lost.

Xi meets Trump at the airport as APEC opens; Putin and Xi pledge a “multipolar” order

Thursday brings the Trump–Xi talks and a three-way meeting with Putin. Chips and tariffs are the market questions.

APEC’s leaders’ meeting opened in Shenzhen on Wednesday. Xi Jinping broke with protocol by greeting President Trump in person at the airport (CNN, CNBC, Nikkei) and opened the summit calling for free trade and cooperation on AI. In a separate meeting, Xi and Vladimir Putin pledged stable ties and a “fairer multipolar world order”; in a business speech Putin called “tools of economic coercion” a “thing of the past” (Al Jazeera, SCMP). Thursday’s schedule, as reported from the White House itinerary, is a Trump–Xi meeting and working lunch, then the three-way meeting with Putin on nuclear arms, from which a senior U.S. official said last week no agreement is expected.

The trade truce runs to January 10, 2027. Beijing has suspended an extra 24% levy on U.S. goods for two months from November 10, and has promised countermeasures “if needed” to this week’s U.S. licence rule for AI chips. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has proposed a separate AI track, including testing models before release; that proposal is reported, not confirmed by both sides.

The Economy

The Fed minutes. At 2:00 PM the Fed publishes minutes of its October 27–28 meeting. Markets price a quarter-point rise on December 9 at about 96–97% (CME FedWatch, as reported), up from 87% a week ago, with a half point at about 4%. The questions for the minutes: how many officials argued for a rise in October, whether a half point was discussed, and how they read the 6.5% annual rise in producer prices. Since then, St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem has said several rises are likely before spring and that policy may be up to a point too low, New York Fed President John Williams that a further rise is likely, and Governor Christopher Waller that a half point is on the table if inflation does not improve. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh spoke about AI on Monday but made no comment on policy.

Housing. October housing starts and permits are due at 8:30 AM. With the 10-year above 5%, they are the clearest read of rate damage to construction. Results will be in the closing edition.

Treasury. The $16 billion new 20-year bond auctions at 1:00 PM. The previous 20-year sold at 5.469% in October with a bid-to-cover of 2.73, and last week’s 3-, 10- and 30-year auctions all tailed, selling at higher yields than the market expected. A fourth weak long-end sale an hour before the minutes would test the two-day rally in bonds. A $19 billion 10-year TIPS reopening follows on Thursday.

Recent data, level versus change. October retail sales rose 0.2%, with the control group up 0.6%, firmer than expected; industrial production fell 0.4%, though manufacturing rose 0.3%. Consumer prices were 337.476 on the index in October, up 0.35% on the month; core rose 0.30% (BLS via FRED). Payrolls rose 165,000 in October (FRED, preliminary) and unemployment fell to 4.1%. Jobless claims were 200,000 in the week to November 7, up from 191,000; Thursday brings the next figure.

Abroad. British CPI at 3.6% leaves the Bank of England under pressure after its November rise. Eurozone final October inflation is due today; the flash estimate was 4.0%, the highest since 2023. Japan’s finance minister repeated warnings against one-sided yen moves, with the currency near 160; there has been no new confirmed intervention.

Risks & Scenarios

Anthropic prices at or above the top of its range ($78 or more). Probability 70–85%. Basis: three outlets report a book many times covered and bankers guiding to the top or above; deals this oversubscribed rarely price inside the range. If it prices below $78, read it as a demand warning for the whole AI trade.

The chip rebound holds through the close (S&P 500 closes above Tuesday’s 7,340.53). Probability 55–70%. Basis: futures imply a higher open and Nvidia’s guide cleared expectations, but premarket chip gains faded after beats last week and only 27% of stocks are above their 50-day average. Invalidated by a red close led by financials and small caps.

Hawkish minutes. A clear majority favouring further rises, or discussion of a half point, would push the 2-year back toward last week’s 4.88% and hit rate-sensitive stocks. With a quarter point almost fully priced, only a half-point signal moves the December odds much.

Science

Genetics. A study in Nature reports that a gene variant inherited from Neanderthals is associated with smaller volume in parts of the brain and lower risk of schizophrenia. It is an association from genetic data, not a demonstrated mechanism, and needs replication before it says anything about treatment.

Ageing. A Nature paper found that aged mice lacking the enzyme granzyme K had better heart and liver function and less kidney damage. It is a mouse result; whether blocking the enzyme is safe or useful in people is untested.

The outer solar system. Astronomers report a possible large dwarf planet beyond Pluto, catalogued 2026 ED138, found in a crowded patch of sky. Its size and orbit need follow-up observations to pin down.

Allegheny County

Innamorato proposes a $1.25 billion county budget with no tax increase

County Executive Sara Innamorato presented a $1.25 billion 2027 operating budget to County Council this week. It holds the property-tax rate flat and has no layoffs, and closes 150 vacant positions to save about $9 million. Spending rises about 2% excluding those items, and most departments get less than in 2026. It adds $2.5 million for more child-care places. Council hearings come next; last year’s budget passed unanimously in early December. The city’s own $872.6 million budget, which draws about $28 million from reserves and would temporarily suspend its 10% fund-balance rule, is in council hearings.
