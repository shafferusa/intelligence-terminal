Chip stocks slide again as Nvidia’s turmoil spreads; AMD falls 9% and the 10-year hits 5.15%

Friday, November 13, 2026 · 35 min read

A softer-than-expected headline producer-price reading lifted stocks at the open, but a second day of selling in chipmakers, led by AMD, Broadcom and Nvidia itself, pulled the Nasdaq down 0.9%. The rest of the market held up and the Dow finished higher, while the 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.15%, bitcoin settled below $70,000 and the yen sat at 160 to the dollar.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • Inflation and the Fed. October producer prices rose 0.3%, below the 0.4% expected, but the annual rate rose to 6.5%, the highest since 2022. A narrower core measure rose 0.5%. Futures still price a December hike as near-certain, with roughly a 10% chance of a half point.
  • Rates, dollar, crypto. The 10-year par yield closed at 5.15% and the 2-year at 4.88%. Over the week they rose 29 and 37 basis points.   • Next week. Retail sales and Nvidia’s results, the first under interim CEO Colette Kress, are both on Tuesday. Wednesday brings the Fed’s minutes and the expected pricing of Anthropic’s IPO, and Walmart reports Thursday. This weekend, ULA tries again to launch Vulcan, at 12:32 PM Saturday, after a ground-equipment fault scrubbed today’s attempt.
  • Beyond markets. Sen. Chris Van Hollen is challenging Chuck Schumer for Senate Democratic leader. In Dublin, European leaders picked a five-country group to represent Europe at the next round of Ukraine talks. Iran says it destroyed a U.S. radar in Bahrain, and U.S. Central Command says it shot down the two drones and nothing was hit.

Top Stories

Chipmakers take a second day of losses as Nvidia’s leadership crisis spreads beyond Nvidia

On Thursday money moved from Nvidia into its rivals. On Friday the selling hit almost the whole AI-hardware chain, while the rest of the market rose.

AMD fell 8.75% to $534.06 (Finnhub, EOD official), giving back more than Thursday’s 5.9% gain. CNBC and Benzinga summaries put its loss in market value at about $100 billion at the low. Broadcom fell 4.76% to $347.01, Marvell 4.68%, Arista 4.52%, Vertiv 3.61% and Micron 2.52%. Nvidia fell 2.01% to $162.85. It had been up about 1.8% before the open, and reports put it down more than 4% at its worst; CNBC said its market value slipped below $4 trillion. The VanEck semiconductor fund (SMH) lost 3.02% and the technology SPDR 2.03%. Server and network makers also fell: Cisco 6.24% and CoreWeave 6.13%. Nebius dropped another 9.92%.

The rest of the market did not join in. The equal-weight S&P 500 fund rose 0.19% while the cap-weighted SPY fell 0.38%. Consumer staples (+1.42%), communication services (+1.05%, led by Meta’s 2.9% gain), energy and financials all rose. The Dow, which has less exposure to chips, finished 0.10% higher. Apple rose 0.39% to $356.27. A CNBC movers list described an intraday record of $360.80 and a market value above $5.2 trillion. Single source We found the record in only one report.

The news on Nvidia’s leadership was incremental. CNBC reported that the board has not yet chosen a search firm and will look at internal and external candidates. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said his department supported blacklisting Megaspeed, the Singapore company at the centre of the reports about Jensen Huang’s personal relationships, but that the Commerce Department “has the pen” (Bloomberg, The Hill). In Congress, Speaker Mike Johnson spoke of a growing consensus on limiting chip exports to China, and Republicans are discussing attaching restrictions to the defense bill (CBS). China’s foreign ministry warned against the “misuse of export controls.”

Why it matters Thursday looked like a vote that Nvidia had lost some edge while AI demand was intact. Friday looks more like a repricing of policy risk for the whole sector. The leadership story has become an export-control story, and that hits every company that sells advanced chips to China, not only Nvidia. That is our reading of the price action and the Washington coverage; no single report explains the move. A strong reading on the broad market (the Dow up, equal weight up) says this was a sector event, not a market panic.
What’s next Nvidia reports after the close on Tuesday, Kress’s first call as interim CEO. Watch for guidance on China and for any word on a search firm or candidate list. Senate Banking Democrats have reportedly asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to appear (TechCrunch; single source, no date set).

Go deeper

Two days of selling have cost Nvidia about 6.3% since Wednesday’s $173.73 close. Its peers had a split two days: AMD is down about 3.4% from Wednesday after its Thursday rally, and Broadcom about 0.5% up. SMH is down about 4% over the two days. Supplier stocks were relatively steady today. Taiwan Semiconductor fell 1.67% and ASML only 0.13%, so the hardest-hit names were those that sell finished chips and systems into AI data centres, not the companies that make the machines. Analyst moves were reported too, including a halved Bernstein price target and an HSBC downgrade, but we found each in only one search snippet and one of them conflicts with the share price, so we are not repeating them as fact. A Samsung–Modine deal story (below) pulled in the other direction for one part of the AI-cooling trade.

Producer prices rise less than expected in October, but annual wholesale inflation reaches 6.5%

The headline gave stocks a brief lift at the open. The detail kept a December rate hike close to certain.

The Producer Price Index for final demand rose 0.3% in October, against 0.4% expected, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics release as reported by CNBC, Babypips and Haver Analytics. That was the smallest monthly increase since July. Over twelve months the index is up 6.5%, the most since November 2022 (BLS summary via Stocktitan and others). Goods prices rose 0.5% and services 0.2%. Trade-services margins fell 0.2%, and that is what held the headline down. Prices excluding food and energy rose 0.3%, in line with forecasts. The narrower core, which also excludes trade services, rose 0.5%, the most since May, against about 0.3% expected. September’s headline was revised up to 1.4% from 1.3%, and August’s down to 0.4% from 0.5%. These figures are preliminary, as every PPI is.

Analysts split on how to read it. Haver called it “better than expected, but still firmer than desired”; MNI said the momentum was waning but inflation in the pipeline was “uncomfortably high”; Yahoo Finance’s headline spoke of inflation “broadening.” CME FedWatch snapshots after the release put the chance of a December hike near 100%, about 87–90% for a quarter point and 10–13% for a half point, little changed from Thursday. Snapshots differ by source and time.

Why it matters The headline fell because wholesalers and retailers squeezed their margins, not because input prices cooled. Goods prices still rose 0.5%. For the Fed, which watches the PPI components that feed its preferred PCE measure, this supports another hike in December rather than a pause. It does not add much to the case for a half point. October CPI on Tuesday the 10th was the hotter report.

The 10-year yield ends the week at 5.15% after the biggest two-year rise in more than two years

Every point on the curve from two years out rose again. The Fed’s hawks kept talking, and the Treasury Secretary asked them not to overreact.

The Treasury par curve closed at 4.88% for the 2-year, 5.15% for the 10-year and 5.47% for the 30-year, up 2, 5 and 4 basis points from Thursday (U.S. Treasury, EOD official). The 20-year reached 5.52%. Over the week, measured against the Nov 6 close on FRED, the 2-year rose 37 basis points and the 10-year 29. Reuters and others describe the 2-year move as its largest weekly jump in more than two years. The 3-month bill held at 4.32%, and the gap between the 10-year and 3-month widened to 83 basis points. Intraday, the 10-year reached about 5.17%, its highest since October, according to press reports.

San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said rates need to go up because firms are trying to pass on costs. Chicago’s Austan Goolsbee warned that the Fed would move more aggressively if inflation spreads (FXStreet). Single source We found both of today’s remarks only in FXStreet headlines and could not read the full reports. Bessent, in a CNBC interview, urged the Fed not to overreact to the energy-supply shock from the Iran war and said he expects inflation to ease as gasoline falls (Investing.com). The Fed sets rates independently of the Treasury.

Why it matters Real yields did most of the work this week. The 10-year inflation-protected yield was 2.86% on Thursday (FRED), up from 2.63% a week earlier, and breakeven inflation barely moved. Higher real rates tighten financial conditions directly. They show up in housing (Thursday’s home sales were the lowest since 2010), in gold, and in the valuations of long-duration growth stocks.

The yen hits 160 per dollar, and Tokyo says it is coordinating with Washington

There was no intervention today. The last time the yen was this weak, in the summer, Japan and the U.S. Treasury both bought yen.

The European Central Bank’s reference rate put the dollar at exactly 160.00 yen on Friday (Frankfurter, ECB reference). Intraday, one report had the pair at 160.03, the first move above 160 since early September, before it eased to about 159.9 (FXStreet, Investing.com). Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama called the moves “one-sided and rapid,” said Japan would “respond appropriately” and said the U.S. and Japan share a stance on currencies. Vice Finance Minister Atsushi Mimura said it was “too early to forget” the summer intervention. Japan sold about ¥15.4 trillion of dollars between July 30 and August 3, and the New York Fed has said the U.S. Treasury also bought yen on July 31, its first such purchase since 1998. The ICE dollar index was about 103.2 late in the day, near its highest since April 2025 and headed for its best week in about two months (Reuters via Investing.com). The euro was near $1.105.

Why it matters The yen’s weakness comes from the gap between U.S. and Japanese rates, and that gap grew again this week. Intervention can slow a move, but it cannot change the rate gap. A coordinated U.S.–Japan action would be a market event in its own right, because it would show Washington willing to lean against its own strong dollar.

Bitcoin falls below $70,000 for the first time since August, and ether below $2,000

More than $1 billion of leveraged bets were wiped out. Crypto has not joined this week’s equity rebound.

Bitcoin was $68,178 at about 4:45 PM ET, down 4.6% over 24 hours, and ether $1,925.63, down 4.5% (CoinGecko, live). The total crypto market was worth about $2.33 trillion, down 5.9% in a day. Press reports put the day’s low near $68,800 and described about $1.2–1.6 billion of liquidations, mostly long positions; a widely used sentiment gauge fell to 20, “extreme fear” (Forbes, Cointelegraph, FXStreet). LSEG data show bitcoin heading for a sixth straight weekly loss. The Grayscale bitcoin mini trust fell 3.49% and Coinbase 2.32%; Robinhood fell 4.29%.

Senator Van Hollen challenges Schumer for the job of Senate majority leader

Democrats will hold 55 seats in January. The leadership vote is by secret ballot in December.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland said late Thursday that he will run against Sen. Chuck Schumer of New York for Democratic leader, which in the new Congress means majority leader (CNN, Axios, CBS, ABC, Fox, the Washington Post). Van Hollen accuses Schumer of working behind the scenes against progressive candidates in primaries and named Sen.-elect Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota. Some outlets report he also criticised Schumer’s ties to the crypto industry and to AIPAC. Schumer said in a statement, “I have the votes to continue serving as Democratic leader.” Sen. Bernie Sanders endorsed Van Hollen. CNN’s analysis says the contest exposes the party’s divide over Israel; that is commentary.

Why it matters The majority leader controls the Senate’s floor, which matters for the December 11 funding deadline, the chip-export push and confirmations next year. A contested race this soon after an election win is unusual.

CNN, Axios, CBS News, ABC News, Fox News, the Washington Post.

Europe names a five-country group for the next round of Ukraine talks

The Kremlin had demanded a single European representative. Leaders in Dublin chose a team instead.

At an informal EU summit near Dublin on Friday, leaders agreed that France, Germany, Britain, Italy and Poland, together with EU institutions and NATO, will represent Europe at the next round of talks, through their national security advisers (France 24, Bloomberg). President Zelenskyy and Jared Kushner, who met him in Ireland after seeing President Putin in Moscow on Thursday, both said Europe would take part. No date for the round has been announced. Germany hosts a “Coalition of the Willing” summit on November 19, and Chancellor Friedrich Merz expects more than 20 leaders. Separately, Finland said it detected a strike on a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland on Wednesday. Ukraine’s SBU says it hit the missile corvette Naro-Fominsk during a drone attack on the Ust-Luga oil port, which would be the first Ukrainian drone attack on a ship in the open Baltic (Reuters, Defense News, Yle). Finland says it is not under threat.

France 24, Bloomberg, Reuters, Defense News, Yle, the Moscow Times.

What Moved Markets

The open: PPI relief. Likely contributor Stocks opened higher after the 8:30 AM producer-price headline came in a tenth below forecasts (Seeking Alpha, Proactive Investors). Treasury yields eased briefly. The relief lasted less than an hour.

Morning to midday: the chip sell-off. Market narrative Selling in Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom turned the S&P negative in the morning. By midday the Nasdaq was down about 1.5–1.9% and the S&P about 1%, and the S&P had swung more than 140 basis points from its early high (FXEmpire, 247wallst, TradingNews). Reporters tied the move to Nvidia’s leadership turmoil and the export-control debate in Washington. We found no single new fact that explains the timing.

Rates. Likely contributor The 10-year rose back above 5.15% intraday as the market looked past the PPI headline to the core figures. Higher real yields weigh most on the richest-valued stocks, which is where today’s selling was.

Afternoon: the recovery. Unexplained The indexes recovered about half their losses into the close: the S&P finished down 0.40% and the Nasdaq 0.91%. We found no catalyst for the late bounce. Weekly rebalancing and end-of-week short covering are possible explanations, not confirmed ones.

Defensives and a deal. Confirmed catalyst Modine Manufacturing rose about 28–31% after reports that Samsung Electronics is in talks to buy it (CNBC, Benzinga; multiple sources, no company confirmation that we saw). Market narrative Staples rose 1.4% with Walmart (+1.51%) and Costco (+1.28%), and drugmakers Eli Lilly (+1.14%) and Novo Nordisk (+2.41%) gained. That is consistent with a rotation into defensives, which the price action shows but no one confirmed.

The Economy

Today’s data. Producer prices, covered above, were the only major U.S. release. The 6.5% annual rate and the 0.5% narrow core say pressure in the supply chain has not eased, even as margins absorbed some of it in October. No other federal release came out today.

The Fed. This week’s speakers, from Williams and Bowman to Daly, Hammack, Logan and Goolsbee, all leaned toward more tightening. The questions for December 8–9 are the size of the move and the signal for 2027. FedWatch snapshots put about 90% on a further hike by March. The minutes of the October 27–28 meeting come out Wednesday at 2 PM. The Fed Board also published a revised tentative schedule of 2027 meetings on Thursday (federalreserve.gov), and on Friday the bank regulators extended a comment period on third-party risk-management guidance.

Treasury. No new auction results today. Next week’s bills, the 6-, 13- and 26-week ($100 billion, $95 billion and $82 billion), settle November 19. A $16 billion 20-year bond and a $19 billion 10-year reopening settle November 30 (TreasuryDirect). Bessent, besides his comments on the Fed, said he had moved to a military base on Secret Service advice after Iranian threats, and a Treasury spokesperson said the sanctions campaign against Iran is “collapsing” its economy (Times of Israel, Military.com, Reuters). SOFR was 3.90% on November 12 (FRED).

Rates and credit, in real terms. The 10-year nominal yield of 5.15% compares with a 10-year TIPS yield of 2.86% as of Thursday; breakeven inflation was 2.24% (FRED, Nov 12). The curve steepened slightly: 2s10s widened to 27 basis points from 24, and 5s30s is 49 basis points. High-yield credit tightened: the ICE BofA spread fell to 3.20 percentage points on Thursday from 3.30 (FRED). Credit markets are not echoing the equity sector’s worries.

Global. European stocks closed at a third straight record. The Stoxx 600 rose 0.02% to 664.14 and gained about 2.4% on the week, its best week since June, as insurers and oil majors offset chips (mlq.ai and others). London’s FTSE 100 rose 0.36% to 10,902.56, a three-month high, while Germany’s DAX slipped 0.08%. In Asia, the Nikkei rose 0.53% to 61,142.35, Korea’s Kospi fell 0.98% after topping 5,500 intraday, and Hong Kong’s Hang Seng fell about 1.5%; we could not confirm its final close. Beyond Japan’s currency warnings, we found no major central-bank action today. Next week brings the RBA minutes, China’s loan prime rate, and UK inflation and jobs.

Business & Earnings

Cisco’s paradox. Cisco beat on Thursday, with revenue of $18.52 billion and adjusted earnings of $1.40 a share, and raised its full-year outlook to $74.8–75.8 billion. Yet the stock fell 6.24% to $95.64. CNBC described profit-taking after the stock’s run; it was still up about 2% on the week. Applied Materials, which also beat and guided above consensus, fell 2.74% to $370.50, and one research firm, Summit Insights, cut it to Hold on valuation. When strong results lead to lower prices, it usually means the good news was already priced in. Investing.com’s scorecard put it as beats being rewarded “selectively.”

Samsung and Modine. Reports that Samsung Electronics is in talks to acquire Modine, a maker of thermal-management systems used to cool data centres, sent Modine up roughly 30% (CNBC, Benzinga). Unconfirmed We saw no statement from either company, and talks are not a deal.

Microsoft. Microsoft closed up 0.17% at $523.88 after a global outage of Outlook, Teams and other Microsoft 365 services that began on Thursday. The company now says the cause was “an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade,” not a cyberattack, and that service is near normal (CNBC, NBC, TechRadar). It has promised a full post-incident report.

Next week’s reports. Home Depot and Disney report before the open on Tuesday and Nvidia after the close. Target and Lowe’s report Wednesday morning and Walmart on Thursday (CNBC, Morningstar, Saxo). Anthropic’s initial public offering is expected to price on Wednesday at $70–78 a share and begin trading on Nasdaq as “ANT” on Thursday, according to secondary reports; the company has not confirmed the dates. Anthropic remains private until then, so we give no market price.

The World

Iran and the Gulf. The Revolutionary Guards say they destroyed a U.S. early-warning radar in Bahrain in retaliation for a U.S. strike on a telecommunications tower at the port of Sirik. U.S. Central Command says it shot down two Iranian drones aimed at Bahrain and that nothing was damaged and no one was hurt (The New Arab, Al Jazeera, Middle East Eye). Disputed The two accounts conflict, and we found no independent confirmation of either. President Trump said Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Kuwait had urged him to hold off on new strikes and keep up economic pressure. Science

Quantum simulation. This week’s issue of Nature (Nov 12) carries a study using a neutral-atom quantum simulator with up to 180 qubits to observe “prethermal” phase transitions, states that persist for a long time before a system settles into equilibrium. Simulators of this kind study physics that ordinary computers cannot model at this size. They are not general-purpose quantum computers.

Psilocybin in mice. A five-laboratory replication in Nature Neuroscience found psilocybin changed fear and avoidance behaviour in mice in the short term, but lasting effects were modest or inconsistent. Multi-lab replications like this test how robust widely cited single-lab findings are. Both items come from the journals’ own listings; we could not read the full papers.

Space

Vulcan waits a day. ULA called off Friday’s launch at 8:41 AM, after it had begun loading liquid oxygen, because a power interruption at Air Liquide’s gaseous-nitrogen plant knocked out its high-pressure pumps. ULA said the rocket and payload are healthy. The new target is 12:32 PM Saturday, with a 29-minute window (Spaceflight Now, Space.com, Fox 35). It is Vulcan’s first flight since a booster-nozzle problem in February and the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon has bought for its Leo broadband network.

Starship tonight. SpaceX’s window for Starship Flight 15 opens at 5:45 PM, after this edition. The plan is to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the ship and deploy 26 Starlink satellites (CNN, Space.com). Saturday’s edition will carry the result. NASA’s Amit Kshatriya told CNN that Starship’s engine and reuse problems do not put it off track for Artemis III in 2027. SPCX closed down 0.59% at $139.11.

Space stocks. AST SpaceMobile fell another 5.51% and Rocket Lab 3.96%, ahead of the Electron’s 100th launch early Saturday.
