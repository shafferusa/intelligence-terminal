Today’s News

The SEC’s look at Nvidia is at an early stage and may end without charges, the New York Times reports

A third outlet now reports the inquiry. It still has no confirmed focus, and Nvidia has said nothing before a week that includes the board’s deadline from the Treasury.

The New York Times reported on Saturday, citing people briefed on the matter, that the Securities and Exchange Commission’s inquiry into Nvidia is at an early stage and could end without any charges. That makes three outlets reporting the inquiry, after Reuters (potential accounting issues) and the Wall Street Journal (a civil probe that includes whether disclosures misled investors). None of them has established exactly what the SEC is examining. One open question, as wire coverage on Sunday put it, is whether the inquiry concerns Nvidia’s 2025 change to reporting revenue by customer headquarters, which removed Singapore from its geographic breakdown, or the affair around former chief executive Jensen Huang and the Singapore distributor Megaspeed. The SEC says it does not comment on whether an investigation exists. Nvidia has not responded (wire reports via EWN, The Star and Malay Mail; PYMNTS, InvestmentNews).

The wire coverage also reports that President Trump on Friday called for Huang to be reinstated. He had said on November 16 that he “wouldn’t mind” Huang returning. Board chairman Harvey Jones has ruled it out, saying directors unanimously accepted the resignation. The board has opened its own investigation and set up a national-security and export-control oversight committee chaired by Suzanne Nora Johnson (ABC News, The Next Web). Separately, California Attorney General Rob Bonta has said his office is looking at aspects of the matter.

The Times account cools the most alarming reading of Friday’s reports without settling anything. Most SEC inquiries are fact-gathering, and many close without action. But “early stage” also means the uncertainty will last, and the stock fell 14% on Friday on the report alone. Nvidia closed at $125.53, 48% below its 52-week high.

What’s next Three dates. Bessent asked the board on Sunday, November 15, for answers within ten days, which runs out about Wednesday. The quarterly 10-Q, delayed past results day, has no filing date. The Senate Banking Committee has asked Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick to appear on December 3. Any of the three could move the whole AI group on a thin holiday tape.

A drone sets a Russian military cargo ship on fire off Finland, and Helsinki says the war is “spilling over”

Finland says Ukraine was likely responsible. The ship drifted into Finnish waters and was still burning on Sunday.

The Sparta II, a 121-metre Russian-flagged cargo ship owned by Oboronlogistika, which handles logistics for Russia’s defence ministry, was hit by drones in international waters in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday and caught fire. Its 16 crew were taken off by another Russian vessel. The ship drifted into Finnish waters and anchored there, and the Finnish coastguard said the fire burned through the night. Finland informed NATO, imposed temporary air and sea restrictions in the area, and said the attack was likely carried out by Ukraine. Its prime minister was reported as saying Kyiv has the right to strike its aggressor. The governor of Russia’s Leningrad region said two drones attacked a cargo ship near the border. Finland also suspects a Russian helicopter of violating its airspace hours later (CNN, Al Jazeera, France 24, ABC Australia, SBS). Ukraine has not claimed the strike.

Finland’s military had reported a strike on a Russian warship in the same waters last week. Estonia’s navy commander says more attacks there cannot be ruled out.

Why it matters The Gulf of Finland carries Russia’s Baltic oil and fuel exports through Primorsk and Ust-Luga. Attacks in waters bordered by two NATO members raise the chance of an incident involving them, and can raise shipping and insurance costs on that route. Oil showed no reaction to similar news last week, and there has been no trading since.

Russian strikes hit an apartment block near Kyiv and Zelensky’s hometown

The attacks follow Putin’s promise of “painful” retaliation for Ukraine’s strikes on the Kerch Bridge.

A strike on Sunday hit a five-storey apartment block in Dymer, north of Kyiv. AP reported at least two dead and four wounded, and Ukraine’s emergency service said 11 people were believed trapped; later coverage put the figure at 14. On Saturday morning, drone and missile debris damaged homes in three Kyiv districts and injured two men. A ballistic missile attack on Kryvyi Rih wounded 60 people, including six children, regional officials said, and strikes also hit Zaporizhzhia and Kharkiv (Kyiv Post, NBC, France 24, AP as reported). Zelensky said Russia launched about 2,700 drones and 1,460 glide bombs this week. Those are casualty figures from the first hours of rescue work, and they usually change.

Iran says Hormuz stays shut until Washington accepts its conditions

Foreign Minister Araghchi spoke at Oman’s National Day. Oman says it is working with Iran to restore normal shipping.

Speaking in Muscat, Araghchi said reopening the strait “would not be difficult” if the United States accepted what he called Iran’s reasonable and lawful conditions, and he called the U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports illegal. He said Iran and Oman are discussing new security arrangements for the Gulf. Oman’s ambassador to Tehran said his country is working in good faith to restore shipping (Press TV and Nour News, both Iranian state-aligned outlets, and OilPrice). There was no U.S. response by Sunday morning. We found no confirmed new strike over the weekend; Friday’s reported U.S. strike on Qeshm Island remains disputed. CBS reported on Saturday that Washington and Israel are discussing a new assault, and the president told Israeli television he is in no hurry.

SpaceX flies its second spy-satellite mission from Vandenberg in 32 hours

NROL-136 lifted off before dawn on Sunday, and the booster landed back at the base.

A Falcon 9 launched the National Reconnaissance Office’s NROL-136 at about 4:23 AM Pacific (7:23 AM ET) from Space Launch Complex 4 East, about 32 hours after the classified USSF-317 left the same pad. Top Themes

1. Does the fall stop, and what would stop it? Last week broke a pattern: four times the market looked ready to rebound and four times it closed lower. Some conditions now look like the end of a sell-off: a VIX in the high 30s with an inverted curve, a 3.7% fall on the last day, and gold falling with stocks, which suggests forced selling. A holiday week with light volume can produce a sharp rebound. It can also make a further fall worse, since there are fewer buyers. Watch Asia tonight. Taiwan, Korea and Japan led the fall into both Monday and Friday last week.

2. The Fed against the bond market. Officials spent last week arguing for more rate rises, and a quarter point on December 9 is priced at about 97%. Bonds rallied anyway, taking a quarter point off the 2-year yield. Wednesday’s PCE report is the last big inflation number before the meeting, apart from the November jobs and CPI reports in early December. A firm reading strengthens the case for a half point. A soft one backs the market’s view that the cycle ends sooner. The Fed’s pre-meeting quiet period starts Saturday, so this week is the last chance for officials to steer expectations.

3. Was it the AI build-out or just Nvidia? Dell on Tuesday is the first large AI-hardware company to report since Nvidia, followed by HP Enterprise on Monday. Strong server orders with weak shares would repeat Nvidia’s week: the numbers are fine, but investors doubt how long the spending lasts. Weak orders would be worse.

4. Governance and Washington as market risks. Nvidia’s regulators, the Commerce chip rule, a possible order on overseas data centres, Medicare audits and a December 11 funding deadline. Washington moved individual stocks repeatedly last week, and this week the board reply to Bessent and anything on the data-centre remark could do the same.

5. The consumer into Black Friday. Retail sales and the big retailers held up last week. Best Buy, Dick’s, Kohl’s, Burlington and Dollar Tree report, and Friday is Black Friday. The household is the main reason this correction does not yet look like a recession call.

The Economy Ahead

PCE inflation, Wednesday 8:30 AM. This is the Fed’s preferred measure. September’s report put headline PCE at 3.6% on the year and core at 3.1%. For October, published forecasts put core up 0.3% on the month, which would take the yearly rate to 3.2% (most calendars) or 3.3% (RBC). The Cleveland Fed’s nowcast, as reported, has headline PCE near 3.8%, matching Governor Cook’s estimate on Friday. Most of the PCE inputs are already known: October core CPI rose 0.30% and core PPI 0.3%. What a surprise would mean: core at 0.4% or more would strengthen the half-point case that Waller raised, and the 2-year yield, which fell a quarter point last week, would be most exposed. A 0.2% would support the bond market’s bet that the Fed stops soon, and with the S&P in a correction it could be the catalyst for a relief rally. Personal spending matters too. It shows whether the strong October retail sales carried into services.

Q3 GDP, second estimate, Wednesday 8:30 AM. The advance estimate was 3.4% annualised, above the 3.1% expected and up from 2.2% in the second quarter. We found no firm consensus for the revision. Since the quarter ended in September, this is a check on the past, not on where the economy is now. The softer November data (factory surveys at 7.8 and 6.3, building permits at a five-year low) matter more for that.

Jobless claims, Wednesday 8:30 AM. Moved up a day for the holiday. Initial claims were 203,000 last week and continuing claims 1.763 million, up 70,000. A second large rise in continuing claims would be the clearest sign yet that hiring is slowing.

Consumer confidence and the Beige Book. The Conference Board’s survey on Tuesday will be the first big sentiment reading taken partly during the sell-off. Michigan’s final November reading of 51.5 closed its interviews on November 16. The Beige Book on Wednesday at 2 PM collects anecdotes from the twelve district banks. Watch for any mention of AI spending pulling back or of consumers trading down.

The Fed. The policy rate is 3.75–4.00%, and the next decision is December 9. CME FedWatch had a quarter-point rise at about 97% on Friday (FXStreet). Williams on Monday is the only confirmed speaker. He was the most measured voice last week, saying no “dramatic action” was needed. The Fed Board has no other public events listed for the week, according to one secondhand report. The quiet period begins Saturday, November 28.

Treasury. $183 billion of coupons in three days: $69 billion of 2-year notes Monday, $70 billion of 5-year notes Tuesday and $44 billion of 7-year notes Wednesday, plus a $28 billion 2-year FRN, and 6-, 13-, 26- and 52-week bills. Last week’s 20-year auction went well after three weak sales in the November refunding. These sales come after a quarter-point rally. Strong demand would say investors want Treasuries at these prices. Weak demand would say last week’s buying was a flight to safety that is already fading. The 2-, 5- and 7-year notes settle November 30.

Abroad. Tokyo’s November CPI arrives late in the week; core was 2.6% in October. It feeds the Bank of Japan’s December decision, where a rise to 1.5% is the base case in coverage. The yen is near 158.5 to the dollar. Euro-area November inflation is due the following week; October was 4.0% against a 2.5% ECB deposit rate. China’s official November PMIs are due around the end of the month, after October’s broad misses.

What we expect. We put the chance that the S&P 500 closes Friday above 6,910.39 at 50–62%, and the chance it finishes the week within 3% of that level at 45–55%. Basis: oversold conditions and an inverted VIX curve have usually been followed by rebounds within one or two weeks, holiday weeks have historically leaned slightly positive, and Wednesday’s data and Dell could swing it either way. Last week we gave a fall of more than 6% less than one chance in ten, and it happened. Scenarios

Base case: a nervous holding pattern (45–55%). The S&P ends the week within about 3% of 6,910. Conditions: core PCE at 0.3%, Dell broadly in line, no Nvidia surprise. Behaviour: large daily swings that partly cancel, the VIX easing toward the high 20s, Treasuries steady. Confirmers: Asia stable tonight, an orderly 2-year auction. Invalidators: a 3% day in either direction before Wednesday.

Bull case: a relief rally (15–25%). The S&P gains more than 4% on the week. Conditions: core PCE at 0.2% or a soft GDP revision, Dell raises its AI outlook, Nvidia’s board answers Bessent in a way that calms investors. Behaviour: chips and software lead, the VIX falls below 25, the 2-year yield slips further. Confirmers: breadth above two to one on rising days, high-yield spreads narrowing. Invalidators: a rally that fades by the close again, as all four did last week.

Bear case: another leg down (15–25%). The S&P falls more than 4%. Conditions: weak Asia, Dell cuts server guidance, core PCE at 0.4% or more revives half-point talk. Behaviour: the Nasdaq enters a bear market, the VIX holds above 35, the 2-year yield rises while longer bonds rally. Confirmers: defensives start to fall too, high-yield spreads near 4 points. Invalidators: a firm close on Monday after a weak open.

Shock (5–10%). A fall of 8% or more, or disorder in credit or funding markets. Conditions: a formal SEC action or restatement at Nvidia, a forced seller in leveraged AI or crypto positions, or a military event in the Gulf or the Baltic involving a NATO state. Indicators: high-yield spreads through 4 points, a disorderly Treasury auction, the dollar and the yen spiking together.

What would change the outlook. A core PCE print away from 0.3% would change it most, in either direction. After that, Dell’s backlog, and any formal step from Nvidia’s board or regulators. A confirmed escalation in the Gulf matters less than it did a month ago, because oil is pricing a glut.

The United States

The funding deadline. Government funding runs out after December 11, and Congress is away for Thanksgiving until November 30. That leaves less than two weeks of session. The dispute is over money for the Iran war. Sen. Tim Kaine wants language in any stopgap that ends war funding. Majority Leader Thune calls that “not a realistic ask” and says it would mean a shutdown. A stopgap needs 60 Senate votes, so Democrats can block one (NPR, Scripps, Punchbowl). Punchbowl reports a shutdown is increasingly possible. Others still see a deal as somewhat likelier. Flood insurance and surface-transportation authority lapse the same day.

Leadership. Senate Democrats are expected to vote around December 1 on Chuck Schumer’s leadership, which Sen. Chris Van Hollen is challenging. Thune has tied progress on spending to that contest. The House will change hands in January. Several outlets reported on November 4 that Speaker Johnson will not seek to lead House Republicans in the new Congress.

Oversight. The Senate Banking Committee has asked Jensen Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick to appear on December 3. Articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Hegseth remain in the House Judiciary Committee. The Supreme Court returns November 30.

Before the holiday. Thanksgiving travel starts after the FAA’s nationwide ground stop on November 13, which was traced to a network router; there is no sign of a cyberattack. Watch for weather delays from the system that flooded Florida’s Atlantic coast last week.

Reports that Washington and Israel are weighing a new assault, and the president’s comment that he is in no hurry, leave the near-term risk two-sided. China. After Shenzhen, the U.S.–China tariff truce runs to January 10, and the extra 24% levy is suspended until about January 10. Watch Beijing’s promised “necessary measures” against the Commerce chip rule; none have been named. China’s November PMIs come at month-end.

Measles. The CDC normally updates its national case count on Wednesdays. After last week’s loss of U.S. elimination status, the count stood at 5,412 cases this year, with Pennsylvania at 1,529, more than any other state. Holiday travel is the risk public-health officials will be watching.

Floods and the St. Johns River. Florida’s St. Johns River passed its 1953 record last week and remained in major flood stage, after more than 14 inches of rain fell on parts of South Florida. Forecasters were watching an offshore system that could become Tropical Storm Kyle (Nature World News, Fox Weather, Scripps). A late-November tropical storm off Florida would be unusual, though not unprecedented.

Space Ahead

Amazon’s return to launch. An Ariane 64 is due to lift off from Kourou at 6:09 AM ET Monday with 36 Amazon Leo satellites. It is Amazon’s first launch since ULA’s Vulcan lost 40 of them on November 15. Amazon had 398 satellites in orbit, about 12% of its planned 3,230. The Vulcan investigation continues under ULA’s lead with FAA oversight, and the Space Force has paused national-security missions on the rocket.

The rest of the week. Progress MS-36 to the space station from Baikonur just after midnight Tuesday. Falcon 9 with USSF-266 from Vandenberg at 10:55 AM Tuesday. China’s Shenzhou 24 crew to the Tiangong station from Jiuquan at 7:18 PM Tuesday. Rocket Lab’s Electron with a StriX radar satellite for Synspective at about 7:05 PM Thursday. Several Long March launches. All times Eastern; launch times slip often.

Starship. After the first tower catch of a Starship upper stage on November 14, Musk says the next flight will try to catch both stages. No date has been set.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Council works through O’Connor’s first budget

Mayor Corey O’Connor’s 2027 plan has a $727 million operating budget and a $145 million capital budget. It draws about $28 million from the rainy-day fund, raises no taxes, makes no layoffs, and ends transfers to the housing and anti-violence trust funds, which residents have protested at council (TribLive, PublicSource, CBS Pittsburgh, New Pittsburgh Courier). Accounts differ on the deadline: one has council voting by December 17, and others say it has until December 31 to amend and adopt. TribLive reports council has moved its vote earlier; we have not confirmed the new date.

Counties certify the election that gave Democrats Harrisburg

Counties face a Monday deadline to certify the November 3 results.
