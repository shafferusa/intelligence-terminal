Morning Edition

Democrats Win Both Houses; Stocks Jump as AI Power and Defense Shares Sink

Wednesday, November 4, 2026 · 37 min read

Democrats took the Senate and the House overnight, and after an early drop in futures the S&P 500 opened about 1.5% higher, led by Apple, Microsoft and Amazon, while the power producers and equipment makers behind the AI data-center build-out fell 8–14% and defense contractors 3–5%. Treasury left auction sizes unchanged, and this edition, late to press, includes the first hour of trading.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The tape. At 10:31 AM the S&P 500 was up 1.5% at 7,684.74, the Nasdaq Composite up 2.1% and the Dow up 2.1% (about 1,100 points). Overnight, Nasdaq 100 futures had fallen about 2–2.4% as the results came in, then recovered. The rally is narrow in an unusual way: Apple +6.5%, Amazon +7.1% and Microsoft +6.1%, while Vistra fell 14%, Vertiv 11%, Constellation 8% and Lockheed Martin 5%. The VIX dropped from 20.83 to 16.95.
  • The macro thread. Investors appear to be pricing divided government: a Democratic Congress facing a Republican president. Analysts quoted this morning expect less new fiscal legislation and more scrutiny of AI data centers, a view that coincides with the split between the companies that sell the build-out and the companies that buy it. The election did not change Friday’s jobs report or December’s Fed meeting.
  • Rates, the Fed and the Treasury. Treasury kept the quarterly refunding at $125 billion and coupon sizes unchanged, will convert the 7-year note to a quarterly new issue in March, and plans up to $95 billion of buybacks this quarter. The 10-year yield dipped overnight and is back near 4.96%, against Tuesday’s 4.95% close. ADP counted 86,000 private jobs in October, above the roughly 70,000–75,000 expected; ISM services slipped to 54.5. Markets still price a December rate increase at roughly 75% (secondary reports of CME data).
  • Biggest market risk. The sell-off in AI-power stocks is a bet on policy that does not exist yet. If it spreads from the utilities and equipment makers to the chip designers, the index-level rally will not hold. Micron is down 4% and Marvell 6% this morning; Nvidia is down 1%.
  • The country, the world and home. The AP called the Senate late Tuesday and the House early Wednesday. Democrats flipped at least seven Republican Senate seats, including Maine, North Carolina, Ohio and Texas; Alaska is uncalled. President Trump blamed gas prices. In Pennsylvania, Gov. Josh Shapiro won a second term, Democrats took four Republican-held US House seats, and Rep. Chris Deluzio was re-elected in the district that includes Bridgeville. Allegheny County voters rejected the council-funding question by about two to one (unofficial). It will reach 77 in Bridgeville; rain is likely tonight.
  • Watch today. Oil inventories at 10:30; Qualcomm, AppLovin, DoorDash and Snap after the close. Thursday: the Bank of England, which is expected to raise rates. Friday: the October jobs report.

Top Stories

Democrats win control of Congress; stocks rally after an overnight scare

Futures fell as much as 2% overnight as the Senate flipped. By the open the S&P 500 was up 1.5%, because investors appear to have concluded that divided government means fewer surprises.

The Associated Press called the Senate for Democrats shortly before midnight on Tuesday and the House early on Wednesday (Forbes, CNN, NBC and Time, citing AP). Republicans went into the election holding the Senate 53–47. AP-based reporting lists at least seven Republican-held seats that flipped: Maine, North Carolina, Ohio, Texas, Iowa, Kansas and South Carolina. Counts published this morning put Democrats at 53 or more Senate seats, with several races, Alaska among them, still uncalled. Rep. Hakeem Jeffries is in line to become Speaker in January. Published House tallies differ widely because many races are uncalled: one snapshot shows 218 Democratic seats with 91 uncalled, NBC’s page shows 231 with 60 uncalled.

Markets moved twice. As results came in, Nasdaq 100 futures fell as much as about 2.4% and S&P 500 futures about 1.4% (Reuters, via Yahoo Finance). Asia sold off with them: Japan’s Nikkei was down as much as about 4.4% before closing down 1.4%. Futures recovered through the European morning, and several reports tie the turn to President Trump’s remarks that he would accept the results; we could not find his exact words. At 10:31 AM the S&P 500 was at 7,684.74 (+1.5%), the Nasdaq Composite at 25,891 (+2.1%), the Dow at 52,886 (+2.1%) and the Russell 2000 up 1.3% (Yahoo Finance, delayed).

The gains are uneven by sector. Consumer discretionary (+3.7%), communications (+3.5%), staples (+2.7%) and financials (+1.9%) lead. Energy (−3.4%), industrials (−2.0%) and utilities (−1.3%) trail. Treasuries rallied overnight and then gave the move back: the 10-year yield traded around 4.90–4.93% early and was 4.96% at 10:15. The dollar index is down 0.3% at 101.52, gold is up 2.6% at $4,042 and bitcoin is up 4% at about $80,500.

Why it matters Through two years of a Republican Congress, markets traded on what Washington might pass: tariffs, tax changes, emergency spending. A Democratic Congress facing a Republican president makes large new laws less likely in either direction. Investors appear to be treating that as lower risk. Two exceptions show what they now expect from Congress: oversight and limits on AI data centers, and scrutiny of defense spending. Our call: the S&P 500 closes today above Tuesday’s 7,568.92 (65–80%), given a 1.5% gain at 10:30 and a falling VIX.

The AI build-out sells off: Vistra −14%, Vertiv −11%, Constellation −8%, while Apple, Amazon and Microsoft rise 6–7%

The market split the AI trade in two: the companies that build and power data centers fell, and the companies that buy them rose. Defense contractors and Trump Media also fell.

At 10:31 AM Vistra was down 14.2% at $119.81, Vertiv 10.5% at $211.08, Constellation Energy 8.2%, Micron 4.1% and Marvell 5.9% (Yahoo Finance, delayed). One market recap also listed Quanta Services and CoreWeave down more than 5%, and reported First Solar up about 14% and Sunrun about 12%; we did not verify those quotes. Morningstar wrote that AI-linked stocks came under pressure “as investors weigh reduced investment and greater regulatory scrutiny, particularly around data centers.” One analyst quoted by Yahoo Finance said a Democratic Congress would create “major hurdles” for the build-out over the next two years. Not all of Vistra’s fall is about the election: it reports on Friday, and Investing.com says several analysts cut price targets this week.

The buyers of AI capacity moved the other way. Apple rose 6.5% to $353.42, a record high according to Yahoo Finance, a day after falling on its margin forecast. Amazon rose 7.1%, Microsoft 6.1%, Alphabet 4.7% and Meta 1.5%. One reading is that these companies gain if the price of power and data-center capacity rises more slowly. That is the narrative in this morning’s coverage, not something the companies have said.

Defense contractors fell: Lockheed Martin −5.1%, RTX −3.6%, Northrop Grumman −3.2%. Trump Media & Technology fell 10.6% to $5.32; Palantir was flat and SpaceX (SPCX) was down 2.4%. Health insurers were mixed. Centene rose 6.8% and Elevance 1.9%; UnitedHealth fell 1.5% and Humana 1.6%.

What’s next No bill limiting data centers exists, and the new Congress does not sit until January. What can change sooner is state policy: one report says Texas elected a governor who campaigned on tighter limits on data-center grid connections (single report, not verified). Vistra reports on Friday, before the open, and will be asked about it. Our call: Vistra closes today at least 8% below Tuesday’s $139.59 (55–70%), given a 14% fall at 10:30 and a recovering broad market.

Treasury keeps auction sizes unchanged, moves the 7-year note to a quarterly cycle and plans up to $95 billion of buybacks

The quarterly refunding contained no surprise on size. The changes are to the 7-year note’s schedule, the buyback plan, and a small edit to the forward guidance.

Treasury will sell $125 billion of notes and bonds next week: $58 billion of 3-year notes on Monday, $42 billion of 10-year notes on Tuesday and $25 billion of 30-year bonds on Thursday, Nov. 12. That refunds about $97 billion of maturing debt and raises about $28 billion of new cash. Every coupon and floating-rate auction size for November through January is unchanged from the last quarter (Treasury refunding statement).

There are three changes. First, from March 2027 the 7-year note becomes a quarterly new issue with two monthly reopenings, the same pattern as the 10-year. Second, Treasury plans to buy back up to $70 billion of older securities for liquidity support and up to $25 billion of 1-month to 2-year debt for cash management this quarter. Third, the guidance wording changed. In August Treasury said it expected to keep coupon sizes unchanged “for at least the next several quarters.” Today it says “for the next several quarters,” and that it continues to evaluate potential changes “with a focus on trends in structural demand.” The word “at least” is gone. Dealers have pointed to early 2027 as the earliest likely increase. Treasury also expects its cash balance to peak around $1.025 trillion (±$50 billion) in late November and late January, against an $850 billion end-December assumption.

Why it matters The bond market feared more long-term supply, and today brings none. The dropped “at least” is the first hint that larger auctions may come once the next fiscal outlook is set, and that outlook now depends on a Congress that will change hands in January.

US Treasury, Quarterly Refunding Statement of Assistant Secretary Hunter McMaster (Nov. 4, 2026) and August statement; Newsquawk (dealer expectations).

AMD beats and guides to $17 billion, but most of the premarket gain is gone; Arista and Block jump

AMD’s data-center revenue nearly doubled. The stock was up 6–7% before the open and only 1.7% after it, as the AI-infrastructure trade sold off.

AMD reported record third-quarter revenue of $13.4 billion, above its guidance of about $13.0 billion ±$0.3 billion and the roughly $13.1 billion analysts expected. Adjusted earnings were $2.09 a share, against about $1.95 expected. Data-center revenue rose 97% to $8.5 billion; gaming fell 40%. GAAP earnings of $1.46 include a $1.1 billion non-cash charge tied to warrants (8-K). The fourth-quarter forecast is about $17 billion ±3%, against roughly $16.2 billion expected, at a 55% adjusted gross margin, down from 56%. Lisa Su said growth should accelerate as the Helios rack systems and “Venice” server chips ramp. Shares were indicated about $518 premarket (Benzinga) and were $492.18 at 10:31, up 1.7%.

Super Micro reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $19.95 billion, up 298%, against its own guidance of $17.35 billion. It is up 1.7%, having traded 6–9% higher after hours. Arista Networks (revenue $3.66 billion, +58%, forecast above estimates) is up 9.7%. Block raised its full-year gross-profit forecast to $12.6 billion and is up 14.8%. Booking beat on earnings at $4.66 a share; reports differ on its revenue and how it compared with estimates, and the shares are up 9.5%. Amgen’s adjusted earnings of $6.45 beat estimates, revenue was $10.4 billion and Repatha sales rose 37%; it is up 1%.

CVS will split its pharmacies from its insurer, and the stock falls 12% despite a beat and a raised forecast

Investors appear more concerned about an 18-month breakup than encouraged by a strong quarter. Novo Nordisk, by contrast, is up after raising its outlook.

CVS Health reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of $2.09 a share, against about $1.65–1.66 expected, on revenue of $108.4 billion, up 5.4%. It raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to at least $8.20, from $7.90–8.10, and its operating cash-flow forecast to at least $15.5 billion, from at least $11.5 billion (company release). It also announced plans to separate the retail pharmacy business from Aetna, the Caremark pharmacy-benefit manager and its health-services businesses, with completion expected in the first half of 2028 (CNBC; Investing.com). The shares were down 11.7% at $80.71 at 10:31. BofA and Barclays reiterated buy ratings.

Novo Nordisk’s US-listed shares are up 5.6%. Third-quarter adjusted operating profit rose 27% at constant exchange rates to DKK 31.72 billion, and the company raised its 2026 forecast to between −2% and +2% for both sales and operating profit at constant currency. Its Wegovy pill sold DKK 5.39 billion in the quarter (company release; CNBC).

Private hiring beat forecasts in October; the services sector slowed slightly

ADP’s 86,000 is the strongest private-payroll count in months and argues against a cooling labor market before Friday’s official report.

ADP said private employers added 86,000 jobs in October. Forecasts were 69,000 to 75,000 depending on the survey (Fox Business; CNBC, citing Dow Jones). September was revised down to 86,000 from 90,000. Trade, transportation and utilities led with 39,000 jobs, and education and health added 28,000. Professional and business services cut 27,000, their third straight monthly decline. Pay for workers who stayed in their jobs rose 3.2% from a year earlier on ADP’s base-pay measure; gross pay rose 4.7%. ADP’s count and the government’s often differ month to month.

The ISM services index fell to 54.5 from 54.9, against a 54.8 consensus. It is still in expansion for a 28th month, and its prices measure rose for the 113th straight month (ISM). For Friday, Dow Jones economists expect 59,000 nonfarm jobs; one other forecaster expects 74,000, with unemployment at 4.2%.

Why it matters The Fed raised rates last month and markets price another increase in December at roughly three in four. Nothing this morning undercuts that. A firm jobs report on Friday would leave it in place; a soft one, with services slowing, is the only thing in the near-term calendar that could change it.

Asked about the election, Mr. Trump told Fox News: “I don’t think it’s the war, I think it’s the gas prices.” National average gasoline was above $4 a gallon on Election Day.

The most fragile assumption. That the election sorts AI stocks cleanly into winners (buyers of compute) and losers (builders and power suppliers). Both groups depend on the same capital spending. If Microsoft, Amazon or Alphabet were to say they will slow data-center spending for political or permitting reasons, the buyers’ rally would reverse with the builders’. Without that, the split could last until the new Congress shows what it intends.

The Economy

The labor data. ADP’s 86,000 (above) and Tuesday’s 7.52 million September job openings both point to labor demand that is slowing only gradually. Weekly jobless claims were 194,000 in the latest week (FRED, week of Oct. 24). Unemployment was 4.2% in September. The official October report is Friday at 8:30.

Services. ISM services at 54.5 is a level reading: activity is still expanding, slightly more slowly than in September. Prices paid rose again. Services inflation is what the Fed is fighting: core consumer prices rose 3.1% in the year to September (PCE). That is a rate of change in prices, not a level, and it has been moving the wrong way since summer.

The Fed. The target range is 3.75–4.00% after last week’s increase, with two dissents in favor of a larger move. Futures and swaps price a December 9 increase at roughly 75% (CME FedWatch about 76% as of Nov. 2; overnight swaps about 77% on Nov. 3; both from secondary reports). No Fed officials are scheduled to speak today. On Thursday, Cleveland’s Beth Hammack and Richmond’s Tom Barkin speak in the morning and Dallas’s Lorie Logan in the evening. Last week Ms. Logan said she sees a need for at least two more increases.

The Treasury. Refunding details are above. The 3-, 10- and 30-year auctions next week (Nov. 9, 10 and 12) are the first real test of long-end demand since the election. Government funding runs to Dec. 11 under the current stopgap, so the outgoing Congress must pass another funding bill in the lame-duck session or face a shutdown.

Global central banks. The Bank of England decides Thursday at noon UK time (7 AM ET). Risks & Scenarios

Base case: the relief rally holds (about 65–80%). The S&P 500 closes above Tuesday’s 7,568.92, led by the large buyers of AI capacity and by consumer stocks, while the power and defense groups stay down. Signals: the VIX stays under 18 and the equal-weighted index holds its gain.

Downside: the AI selling spreads (about 15–25%). Chip designers follow the power names lower, the rally fades into the close, and the S&P 500 ends flat or down. Signals: SMH below $505 and Nvidia down more than 3%.

The tail. A contested or slow count in uncalled races is not what markets are pricing. Alaska’s ranked-choice count can take up to 15 days, but Senate control no longer depends on it. A bigger tail is in the Gulf: the Qeshm strikes show the ceasefire can break with little warning, and a disruption would reverse this morning’s oil decline.

Election day was largely orderly; Los Angeles election workers face a grand jury today

The feared federal confrontation at polling places did not happen. The legal fight over the Justice Department’s inquiry into California’s count continues.

Votebeat, which tracks election administration, described problems as “fairly typical”: machine faults in Westchester County, N.Y., which officials linked to a programming update; flooded polling places in the Southwest; and two bomb threats at a Toledo polling site that paused voting. It found no reports of armed federal agents at polls. Voting-rights groups said Justice Department monitors, in what the department called its largest Election Day monitoring operation, asked voters “unusual questions.”

Three Los Angeles County election employees were subpoenaed to testify before a federal grand jury today. The department has not said what it is investigating and has suggested the officials are not targets. County Registrar Dean Logan said the county will take “whatever steps necessary” to protect them. A temporary restraining order from US District Judge Trina Thompson bars the Justice Department and Homeland Security from threatening election workers with prosecution for counting ballots; a hearing on a longer order is pending. No charge has been filed against anyone. This is an investigation, at the subpoena stage.

Israel’s final count leaves Netanyahu eight seats short; President Herzog receives the results today

Neither bloc has 61 seats. The president has a week to choose who gets the first try at forming a government.

The Central Elections Committee’s final count gives Likud 27 seats and Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar 21. Mr. Netanyahu’s bloc has 53, the opposing bloc 48 and Arab parties 19, on turnout of 77.9%, the highest in decades. The committee said its review of Likud’s fraud allegations “will not affect final results.” A government needs 61 of 120 seats, so either side would need parties outside its bloc.

Iran: the strait stays restricted, and mediators are still at work

After Monday’s US strikes on Qeshm Island, Iran’s deputy foreign minister reserved the right to respond. The Revolutionary Guards claim to have shot down a US drone, which the US has not confirmed. Science

Interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS carries unusually deuterium-rich methane, pointing to a very cold birthplace

A study in Nature Astronomy reports that methane on the third known interstellar comet is strongly enriched in deuterium, the heavy form of hydrogen. Deuterium enrichment builds up in ice formed at very low temperatures, so the result suggests the comet formed in a colder region than most comets of our own solar system. It is one measurement of one object; whether it is typical of interstellar comets needs more of them.

Nature Astronomy, as reported by Phys.org (authors and DOI not retrieved).

Early humans in Arabia’s Empty Quarter settled where water and tool-quality stone coincided

A geoarchaeological survey of the Rub’ al Khali, published in Nature, finds that early human sites cluster where ancient water sources and stone suitable for tools occurred together. That suggests movement through the desert followed both resources, not water alone.

Nature (listed Nov. 2; authors and DOI not retrieved).

Space

Vulcan’s return to flight is set for Nov. 13 with 40 Amazon Leo satellites; a classified Falcon 9 launch slips to Friday

United Launch Alliance is targeting Friday, Nov. 13 from Cape Canaveral for its first Vulcan launch since the February USSF-87 booster anomaly, pending range approval. The rocket flies a redesigned solid-booster nozzle and, for the first time, six boosters. The Space Force wants two successful Amazon flights before it puts national-security payloads back on Vulcan. SpaceX’s USSF-432 mission from Vandenberg is now no earlier than 4:46 PM PT on Friday, Nov. 6; no reason was given for the slip. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman says Artemis III, now a 2027 Earth-orbit test, will not launch its crew until Blue Origin’s lander test vehicle reaches orbit and is checked out.
