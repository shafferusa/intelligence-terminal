Top Stories

The bond rout reaches a fourth day, and Williams says more hikes are coming

The 10-year yield has risen about a quarter point in a week. The Fed’s most important voice after the chair gave no sign of resisting the move, and producer prices are next.

The Treasury’s par curve for Thursday put the 2-year at 4.86%, the 10-year at 5.10% and the 30-year at 5.43%. A week earlier the 2-year was 4.51% and the 10-year 4.86%. Early Friday, CNBC showed the 10-year near 5.12–5.13% and the 30-year near 5.46%, with the 2-year a basis point lower at 4.85%. That steepening, with the long end rising and the short end steady, follows a $25 billion 30-year auction on Thursday that drew the weakest demand since June. Bloomberg’s morning headline said the surge “shatters hopes worst of bond rout is over.”

Williams, speaking in New York on Thursday evening, said “the economy is strong, labor market continues to strengthen, and inflation is persistent.” He said further increases are likely but gave no timing or size (Reuters, as carried by AOL and others). He also warned that trying to “put the genie back in the bottle” by pushing prices back down would do “huge distortions and damage.” Commentators read that as backing steady quarter-point steps rather than a half point. Reuters says markets expect hikes in December and January from the current 3.75–4.00% range. Snapshots of CME FedWatch put a December quarter point at 87–91% and a half point at 9–13%, depending on the time.

Most of this move is in real yields, not inflation expectations. The 10-year inflation-protected yield was 2.78% on November 10, up from 2.60% on November 5, while the 10-year breakeven was 2.24% on Thursday, about where it started the month (FRED). In other words, investors are pricing tighter policy, not a loss of faith that inflation will come down.

Why it matters A 10-year yield above 5% raises mortgage rates and corporate borrowing costs and lowers what investors will pay for long-dated profits. That is the macro headwind behind this week’s split between a rising S&P and weak breadth. Thursday’s home-sales figure, the lowest since 2010, shows the effect on housing.
What’s next Producer prices at 8:30 AM, then four Fed speakers: Boston’s Susan Collins, Dallas’s Lorie Logan, Governor Michelle Bowman and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson in the evening. Retail sales follow on Tuesday.

Go deeper

Over the past week the curve has moved in two phases. From November 6 to 10, after the payrolls beat and the hot CPI, the front end led: the 2-year rose 22 basis points and the 2s10s spread narrowed from 35 to 25. Since then, through the refunding’s weak finish, the long end has caught up. The 20-year par yield is 5.48%, the highest point on the curve. Supply plus a Fed that is still raising rates is the combination long-bond buyers fear most, and this week’s three refunding auctions all tailed. The counterweight is that credit is calm: high-yield spreads were 3.30 percentage points on November 11, near the tight end of their range. If spreads start widening while yields rise, the story changes from repricing to stress.

Nvidia’s first full day under an interim chief opens with a small bounce

The stock was about 1.8% higher before the bell. The Justice Department says it has reached “no conclusions” in the inquiry linked to the episode.

Nvidia closed Thursday at $166.19, down 4.34%, after falling as much as about 14% when Jensen Huang’s resignation as chief executive and director was announced. It rose about 1% after hours (CNBC) and was up 1.8–1.9% in early premarket trading (Benzinga, Reuters via Investing.com). Board chair Harvey Jones said the board has “full confidence” in interim CEO Colette Kress and that operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap continue “without interruption.” The board says it will consider internal and external candidates for the permanent job. Attorney General Todd Blanche said Thursday that the Justice Department has “no conclusions” on whether the woman at the centre of the reporting worked for Beijing, and that Justice and Commerce are investigating whether a Singapore company bought advanced Nvidia chips for China (Reuters, via U.S. News and Investing.com). Beijing calls the allegations baseless. CNN reports that Huang has been summoned to testify before Congress; we could not confirm the details.

The rest of the chip trade read the news as a rotation, not a retreat. AMD rose 5.86% to $585.30 and Broadcom 5.54% to $364.36 on Thursday, while Taiwan Semiconductor (−1.9%) and Applied Materials (−3.6%) fell. The Philadelphia semiconductor index lost only 0.54%. KeyBanc called Kress’s appointment “constructive”; Wedbush’s Dan Ives expects the interim period to last about a year (CNBC).

What’s next Nvidia reports third-quarter results on Tuesday at about 4:20 PM, with a call at 5 PM (Nvidia investor relations). Kress, who has run the numbers for years, now presents them as chief executive. That is the first real test of whether customers and investors see continuity.

Bitcoin falls below $70,000 for the first time since August

Bitcoin has lost about 7% since Wednesday while stocks held up, and the selling accelerated overnight.

Bitcoin was $69,182 at about 6:40 AM, down 5.2% in 24 hours, and ether $1,956, down 3.5% (CoinGecko). Total crypto market value fell 6.8% to $2.32 trillion. One market report puts the overnight low at $67,568. Crypto outlets cite Fed-hike expectations, outflows from bitcoin funds and $1.3–1.5 billion of forced liquidations of leveraged positions. We could not check the fund-flow figures ourselves. A widely watched sentiment gauge reads 20, “extreme fear.” Bitcoin was about $74,600 on Wednesday afternoon.

The pattern matters more than the level. Bitcoin has fallen all week while the S&P rose, which is the opposite of how it traded through most of the AI rally. Its slide fits a market where cash pays more than 4% and the dollar is at a 52-week high. Speculative assets that pay nothing look less attractive in that world. Crypto-linked stocks such as Coinbase and Robinhood are the obvious exposure this morning.

Microsoft blames unexpected server unavailability and says it sees no sign of an attack. Recovery was still partial overnight.

Outlook, Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 services were disrupted from Thursday into Friday. Australia’s cyber minister said Microsoft had told the government it was not a cyberattack. Outlets disagree on when it started. Microsoft shares rose 0.36% to $523.00 on Thursday, mostly before the worst of the outage.

The fragile assumption. The tape assumes the economy can absorb a 10-year yield above 5% because growth is strong, and Williams said as much. What would invalidate it: a producer-price print of 0.6% or more, which would push the market toward a half-point hike in December; a 10-year close above about 5.20%; or high-yield spreads starting to widen alongside yields. Any one of these would turn a rates story into an equity-valuation story. Thursday’s breadth, with four decliners for every advancer, says much of the market is already feeling it.

The Economy

Producer prices. The Bureau of Labor Statistics publishes October’s producer price index at 8:30 AM. Published previews put consensus at +0.4% for the headline and +0.3% for core, month on month. Preview sites give very different figures for September and for the year-on-year rate, and we could not check them against the BLS, so we do not print them. The read-through is to the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge. Several producer-price components, including portfolio-management fees, airfares and medical services, feed directly into the PCE price index due later this month. October consumer prices rose 0.35% in the month and core 0.30% (FRED index levels). Core has now run near 0.3% a month for three months.

The Fed. The case for a December hike is complete on the Fed’s own words. Williams called inflation “persistent.” On Thursday Daly said further tightening is needed, Hammack backed gradual hikes and Goolsbee called October’s CPI “borderline disturbing.” The labour market gives no reason to pause: claims were 200,000 last week, unemployment was 4.1% in October and payrolls rose 165,000. The open question is pace. Today’s speakers are Collins at about 9:10 AM, Logan at about 9:40 and Bowman at 10:00 (the Fed’s calendar; one schedule says 10:50), with Jefferson at 7 PM. Daly appears again; schedules conflict on whether at 11:10 or noon. Logan said on Tuesday that she expects at least two more hikes. On Thursday the Fed also published a revised tentative 2027 meeting schedule.

Real versus nominal. The 10-year real yield rose 18 basis points between November 5 and 10, while breakevens barely moved. That is why gold, which pays nothing, has broken lower even with inflation high. Rising real rates make holding it more expensive.

Risks & Scenarios

A hot PPI. This is the risk that matters today. We put 45–60% on headline producer prices rising 0.4% or more in October. That rests on a +0.4% consensus and a hot consumer-price report two days ago. A print of 0.6% or more would likely revive talk of a half-point December move and push the 10-year toward 5.2%. A 0.2% or lower print would be the first relief for bonds in a week.

Yields. We put 55–70% on the 10-year par yield closing today at or above 5.10%, Thursday’s level. It was trading at 5.12–5.13% early, but a soft PPI could reverse that within minutes.

Equities. We put 65–80% on the S&P 500 closing today above 7,550. That would take a fall of more than 0.6% from Thursday’s close, and futures are slightly positive. The weak breadth is the reason this is not higher: the index has been carried by a few large chip names.

The yen. USD/JPY above 160 is the zone where Tokyo has acted before. Intervention would most likely knock several yen off the pair within hours and could unwind carry trades. That would be a dollar-negative surprise. We are not putting a probability on its timing.

Iran. The disputed Central Command report is the headline risk for oil over the weekend. A resumption of strikes could quickly add $5–10 to Brent; confirmed talks would take it back toward $90.

Science

A cholesterol drug misses. The phase 3 HORIZON trial of Novartis and Ionis’s pelacarsen, which lowers lipoprotein(a), did not reduce heart attacks, strokes and other major cardiac events. Over a median of 4.7 years they occurred in 12.3% of treated patients and 12.1% on placebo (New England Journal of Medicine). Lp(a) is a genetic risk factor carried by about one person in five, and this was the first outcomes test of lowering it. Several other drugmakers have Lp(a) drugs in late trials.

Early Earth. A Nature paper reports rocks from northern China showing that ocean crust altered at the surface had been buried more than 50 km deep and partly melted by 3.95 billion years ago. That is evidence for crust being recycled into the mantle, a process like plate tectonics, very early in Earth’s history. The paper is peer-reviewed; we read the summary rather than the full text.

Also published. Nature Climate Change finds that coal units near data centers built since 2020 are less likely to be retired, an early measure of AI’s pull on the grid. Science lists a measurement of viscosity in two-dimensional electron fluids.

Space

Senate Democrats pick their first majority team in more than 30 years, with Allegheny County’s Jay Costa nominated for the top post

Senate Democrats elected their leaders for the 2027–28 session on Thursday. Maria Collett of Montgomery County will be majority leader, the first woman to hold the post for Pennsylvania’s Senate Democrats. Steve Santarsiero of Bucks County will be whip and Vincent Hughes appropriations chair. Jay Costa, the Allegheny County senator who has led the caucus in the minority for years, is its nominee for president pro tempore, succeeding Republican Kim Ward. The full Senate votes on that in January (Senate Democratic Caucus release, as reported by PoliticsPA, abc27 and Patch). The new Senate will be 26–24 Democratic, the party’s first majority since 1993, and 26 votes are needed to pass a bill. With Gov. Shapiro, that gives Democrats full control in Harrisburg for the first time in decades, and the party has named transit funding and the minimum wage as early priorities. House Democrats re-elected their leadership the same day.

Pennsylvania Senate Democratic Caucus (primary, as reported) · PoliticsPA, abc27, Patch · Pennsylvania House Democratic Caucus release.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Pittsburgh Regional Transit’s bus-network redesign heads to a final vote

PRT’s “Bus Line Refresh” would add 14 routes, eliminate 20 and change 56. PRT says its board decides in November and that adopted changes would start in the second half of 2027 (PRT). Single source Pittsburgh Progress reports the final vote is set for November 20. TribLive reports PRT is reconsidering keeping bus service in Shaler after public complaints. For South Hills riders, the vote decides which routes survive into 2027.
