Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · 32 min read

Nvidia gave back its post-earnings gain and closed 2.9% lower, UnitedHealth fell another 10% on a proposed Medicare audit rule, and the Fed’s minutes showed almost all officials expecting further rises, while retailers rallied and Anthropic is reported to have priced its IPO at $83.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

The equal-weight S&P fund fell less than SPY, so the selling was narrower than on Tuesday.
  • Nvidia reversed. The stock opened higher on its $130 billion guide, then closed down 2.89% at $150.96 on twice its usual volume, $2.16 above its 52-week low. AMD (+5.3%), Broadcom (+3.6%) and Arista (+4.0%) rose, so the chip group split rather than fell.
  • The Fed. The October minutes say “almost all” officials expect further rises to be appropriate, and “a couple” said a move larger than a quarter point could be considered in December if inflation data are unfavourable. The 10-year Treasury eased 3 basis points to 5.07% and the 2-year was unchanged at 4.80%. A $16 billion 20-year bond sold at 5.459%, without the tails of last week’s auctions.
  • Health care and retail. UnitedHealth fell 10.2% to $349.91, 15% in two days, after the government proposed wider Medicare Advantage audits reaching back to 2018. Retailers led the market: TJX +8.1%, Target +4.0% and Lowe’s +1.1% after their results.
  • After the bell. Bloomberg reports Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, above its $70–78 range; the company has not confirmed it, and trading starts Thursday as ANT. Palo Alto Networks beat, raised its guidance and agreed to buy Applied Compute; its shares rose about 8% in extended trading.
  • Beyond markets. Democrat Mary Peltola won Alaska’s Senate race outright, according to AP and other networks, without a ranked-choice round. Kuwait and Bahrain say they intercepted Iranian drones. Trump and Xi meet in Shenzhen on Thursday.

Top Stories

Nvidia gives back its post-earnings gain, and the S&P 500 falls for a fifth day

A $130 billion guide was not enough to hold the stock up. The rest of the market split: retailers and some chipmakers rose while health care, energy and banks fell.

Nvidia opened above Tuesday’s $155.46 close after reporting $115.8 billion of quarterly revenue and guiding to $130 billion for the January quarter. It was about 3.7% higher before the bell. By midday it was down about 2% at $152.25 (as reported), and it closed at $150.96, down 2.89%, on 340 million shares, more than twice its 50-day average. That is its fifth straight decline and leaves it 38% below its 52-week high of $243.37. Outlets pointed to the lower margin guide (71–72%, from 74.9%), the Wall Street Journal’s report of an SEC civil inquiry, and the company’s statement that it will stop selling H200 chips in China. No single confirmed catalyst explains the reversal.

The index moves were small but one-sided. The S&P 500 fell 0.23% to 7,323.63, its lowest close since July 29 and 6.3% below its 52-week closing high. It has now fallen five sessions running, losing 3.9% since November 11. The Dow lost 561.25 points, or 1.11%, to 50,198.17. The Nasdaq Composite gained 0.42% to 24,429.88 and the Russell 2000 0.46% to 2,714.31 (Yahoo Finance closes; CNBC). Consumer discretionary was the best sector (+1.8%) and technology rose 0.3%. Energy (−3.3%), health care (−2.2%) and financials (−1.1%) fell most.

The breadth picture improved from Tuesday. The equal-weight S&P fund (RSP) fell 0.15%, half as much as SPY’s 0.31%, and small caps rose. The VIX eased 3.5% to 24.56, and its 9-day measure fell to 23.95, below the 30-day index, so the sharpest part of this week’s near-term stress has relaxed. The 30-day index is still slightly above its 3-month counterpart (24.29).

Why it matters This morning the test was whether a strong Nvidia report would end the selling. It did not, but it changed its shape: Tuesday every sector fell, while Wednesday’s losses were concentrated in a few large Dow and S&P names (UnitedHealth, Nvidia, energy) while the typical stock did better. That is closer to rotation than to a broad flight from risk.
What’s next Anthropic’s first trade and Walmart’s results on Thursday. SPY closed at $730.20, just below its 200-day average of $732.55.

Go deeper

Nvidia has now fallen after three of its last four reports, and Wednesday’s pattern, a gap up that turned into a loss, matches what Cisco and Applied Materials did after beats last week. The broader chip fund (SMH) closed almost flat at $459.54 (−0.12%) because AMD, Broadcom and Arista rose while Nvidia, Micron (−1.0%) and Marvell (−2.2%) fell. Investors appear to be separating the AI suppliers rather than selling the whole group, though one session is thin evidence.

Nasdaq futures were up 0.61% at about 4:28 PM, while S&P futures were down 0.17% (Yahoo, delayed). Neither says much about Thursday’s open, which will turn on Anthropic’s debut and Walmart.

Fed minutes: almost all officials expect to raise rates again, and a couple floated more than a quarter point

A governor and a regional president dissented for a rise in October. The staff now sees inflation easing sharply only in 2027.

The minutes of the October 27–28 meeting, published at 2:00 PM, record a 10–2 vote to hold the target range at 3.75–4.00%, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting in favour of a quarter-point rise. “Almost all participants” judged that raising the range at upcoming meetings would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlooks, and some had favoured a rise in October itself. “A couple of participants” said an increase of more than 25 basis points could warrant consideration at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavourable (Federal Reserve). Some outlets described this as a half-point debate; the minutes do not use that figure.

Fed staff estimated September PCE inflation at 3.6% from a year earlier and core at 3.1%, called the pressures broad-based, with energy and AI-related demand both cited, and saw risks skewed to the upside. The staff projection has inflation falling sharply in 2027 and near 2% by 2028. Participants also flagged borrowing tied to the AI buildout as a financial-stability risk (CNBC, Benzinga). On the balance sheet, reserve-management purchases stay at zero.

Markets had expected a hawkish record. The 2-year Treasury yield rose about 1 basis point after the release, to about 4.80% (Reuters, as reported), and ended unchanged on the Treasury’s par curve. A December quarter-point rise stayed priced at about 95% (CME FedWatch, as reported by Benzinga); a half point remains a small minority bet.

Why it matters The minutes predate the hot October inflation reports, so the officials who mentioned a bigger move were talking about a condition that has since arrived. That keeps a half point alive as a December risk even though the pricing barely moved.

Anthropic is reported to have priced its IPO at $83, above its range

Bloomberg’s report cites people familiar with the matter. The company and its banks had not announced a price when this edition closed.

Bloomberg reported on Wednesday evening that Anthropic priced its offering at $83 a share and that the deal had grown, citing people familiar with the matter. The registered terms were 825 million Class A shares at $70–78; at $83 that share count alone would raise about $68.5 billion, before any increase in size. CNBC reported earlier in the day that the company was considering an upsized deal and a price well above $80. A separate $26 billion sale of non-voting shares to chip and cloud suppliers is to be priced at the IPO price. Trading on Nasdaq under ANT is expected Thursday. This newspaper is written by an Anthropic model; we report the offering on the same terms as any other.

The Nasdaq’s gain on Wednesday was tied in some live coverage to anticipation of the listing. Amazon, one of Anthropic’s largest investors, rose 3.8%. Software names that fell on Tuesday on talk of funds raising cash for the deal kept falling: Salesforce −2.1%, ServiceNow −2.5%. That is narrative, not a confirmed cause.

Why it matters A price above the range confirms demand for the largest AI listing so far. The first trade tests whether that demand carries into the open market at a valuation above $1.4 trillion, and its reception will colour the rest of the AI trade.

UnitedHealth sinks 10% after a proposed rule to widen Medicare Advantage audits

The shares have lost 15% in two days. The rule is a proposal, open for comment until January.

UnitedHealth fell 10.21% to $349.91 on about three times its usual volume. The stock was $412.02 on Monday. Reports tied Wednesday’s fall to a Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services proposal to expand audits of Medicare Advantage payments, reaching back to payment years from 2018, which could require insurers to repay money where diagnoses are not supported (Yahoo Finance, Investing.com, as reported). The proposal is open for comment until January 19, 2027. It follows Tuesday’s renewed call by Sen. Elizabeth Warren to break up UnitedHealth, CVS and Cigna. Humana was among the large-company index’s worst performers, and the health-care sector fund fell 2.2%. Eli Lilly also fell 2.3%.

Because the Dow is price-weighted, UnitedHealth’s $39.77 fall made it the index’s largest single drag; CNBC also cited Chevron and Caterpillar.

Why it matters A look-back audit turns a policy question into a possible bill for past payments, which is harder for investors to price than a future rate cut. We have not read the proposal itself, so the size of any repayment is unknown; the stock’s move suggests investors are assuming it could be large.

Retailers lead the market: TJX jumps 8% and Target 4% on holiday-quarter confidence

Off-price and discount chains beat on sales. Lowe’s rose too, though its margin outlook slipped.

TJX, owner of T.J. Maxx and Marshalls, reported comparable sales up 4% against its own 2–3% guide, revenue of about $16.0 billion and adjusted earnings of $1.34 a share, in line with forecasts. It guided fourth-quarter comparable sales up 2–3% and earnings to $1.39–1.42 a share, assuming no further tariff refunds. The shares rose 8.05% to $140.30 on more than twice their usual volume.

Target rose 4.01% to $165.93 on more than three times its usual volume. Its $2.65 a share included about $0.50 from a refund of tariffs collected under the emergency-powers law, and it raised its full-year sales growth to about 6% with comparable sales up 5.6%. Lowe’s rose 1.05% to $222.78 after comparable sales of +1.8% beat a consensus near +0.4%, online sales rose 17.4% and it raised its sales guide to about $92.5 billion, while trimming its operating-margin outlook. Ross Stores rose 3.3% with the group.

Why it matters With Tuesday’s firm retail-sales control group, these reports argue that the consumer is not the source of the market’s problems. That helps the economy and makes the Fed’s job harder.

TJX, Target and Lowe’s releases (as reported by Finimize, Seeking Alpha, PR Newswire) · Finnhub (estimates) · Yahoo Finance (prices, volume).

Palo Alto Networks beats, raises its outlook and agrees to buy Applied Compute

The stock rose 9.9% in the session and about 8% more after hours.

Palo Alto reported fiscal first-quarter adjusted earnings of $1.05 a share against about $0.97 expected, on revenue of about $3.45 billion against $3.31 billion; remaining performance obligations rose 35% to $21 billion. It raised its full-year revenue guide to $14.52–14.57 billion, from $14.10–14.20 billion and above a consensus near $14.2 billion, and its adjusted earnings guide to $4.26–4.30. It also agreed to buy Applied Compute, an AI infrastructure company, for a reported $4.5 billion in stock (Investing.com, FourWeekMBA). The shares had already risen 9.90% to $339.92 during the session as cybersecurity stocks rallied into the report, and were about $367.50 after hours, up 8% (Benzinga Pro). One outlet showed a loss after hours, which looks like a stale quote.

Housing starts jump 9.9%, but permits fall to their lowest since 2020

The two halves of the report point in opposite directions, and the forward-looking one is weaker.

October housing starts rose 9.9% to an annual rate of 1.309 million, well above forecasts of about 1.23–1.25 million; single-family starts rose 4.0% to 864,000. Building permits, which lead starts, fell 7.4% to 1.295 million, the lowest since May 2020 and far below the roughly 1.38 million expected. Single-family permits fell 8.7% to 772,000, the lowest since January 2023 (Census Bureau, as reported). Starts are a noisy series: the Census Bureau’s margin of error on the monthly change is about ±17 percentage points, so the jump may not survive revision.

Why it matters Permits are the better guide to what builders plan, and they are falling with the 10-year yield near 5% and mortgage rates following it. Housing is where the Fed’s rate rises are doing the most work.

Census Bureau new residential construction (primary, as reported by Seeking Alpha, MortgagePoint, TheStreet; consensus figures differ by source).

Gold, copper and oil keep falling as the dollar nears its high

Gold futures lost another 3.5%, copper 5% and oil about 4.5%. Energy was the worst sector.

Front-month gold futures were at $3,728.20 at about 4:28 PM, down 3.5% and at their lowest in more than a year; GLD closed at $341.19, 43 cents above its 52-week low. Silver fell 4.9% to $44.99 and copper 5.1% to $5.67 a pound. The dollar index rose 0.31% to 103.81, a tenth below its 52-week high (Yahoo Finance, delayed).

The government’s weekly report showed U.S. crude stocks up 3.4 million barrels in the week to November 13, against an expected small change, with gasoline up 5.2 million and distillates up 1.6 million; Cushing stocks fell 1.2 million (EIA, as reported by Reuters). On Yahoo’s delayed quotes, Brent was at $88.95 and WTI at $82.14 late in the day, both about 4.5% lower; Reuters had smaller losses at mid-morning, and we have not seen settlement prices. Both contracts are on course for losses of about 20% this month. The energy fund (XLE) fell 3.27%.

Why it matters Falling oil is the one part of the inflation picture moving the Fed’s way. The metals’ slide fits with a strong dollar and real yields near 2.9%, though no single confirmed catalyst explains the size of the move.

Yahoo Finance (futures, delayed; data) · EIA weekly petroleum status report (as reported by Reuters).

Kuwait and Bahrain intercept Iranian drones; mediation remains stuck on sequencing

The U.S. says the attack on its base in Kuwait failed. Gulf states condemned it.

Iranian drones targeted Ali Al Salem air base in Kuwait early Wednesday, a day after a U.S. strike on a Revolutionary Guards missile site on Iran’s Qeshm Island. Iranian state television said the targets were Patriot batteries and launch platforms. Kuwait’s army and Bahrain’s military both said they intercepted the attacks, with no damage reported (Fox, The Hill, AP via Korea Times). Stars and Stripes quotes U.S. Central Command calling the attack unsuccessful, with no U.S. injuries. Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the UAE condemned it.

Talks through Oman and Qatar have produced no breakthrough. Iran’s proposal, as reported, would have the U.S. lift its naval blockade and release some frozen funds before Iran admits inspectors and reopens the Strait of Hormuz; Washington wants simultaneous, verifiable steps. Brent fell again on Wednesday despite the attacks.

Science

Astronomy. Long-term monitoring of a giant star in the Andromeda galaxy shows, for the first time, the orbit of a companion star decaying inside the giant’s outer envelope, according to a Nature News & Views summary. Such “common-envelope” phases are thought to produce many close binary stars and the progenitors of some supernovae, but have been mostly theory until now. We read summaries, not the paper.

Ecology. An assessment of about 40,000 species in Sumatra, also in this week’s Nature, finds that intensifying agriculture drives species loss and changes which species survive, not only how many. It is an observational study across land-use types.

Space stocks fell. Intuitive Machines lost 8.4%, AST SpaceMobile 7.6% to $25.49, close to its 52-week low, and Rocket Lab 3.6%. SpaceX rose 1.3% to $143.50; the reported bond sale has not been confirmed by the company.

Vulcan. ULA still has no root cause or return-to-flight date for Sunday’s failure, which destroyed 40 Amazon Leo satellites; Blue Origin is taking part in the investigation. Amazon has 37 more Vulcan launches booked and about 398 Leo satellites in orbit.

Launches. SpaceX flies USSF-317 from Vandenberg at about 9:46 PM Thursday and NROL-136 at about 7:30 AM Saturday. The next Amazon Leo batch is set for an Ariane 64 from Kourou at about 6:09 AM on Monday, November 23 (Launch Library 2; times can slip).
