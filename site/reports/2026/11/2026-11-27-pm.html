Closing Edition

Chips lead a half-day rally as the S&P 500 books its best week since April 2025

Friday, November 27, 2026 · 31 min read

On a thin Black Friday session that ended at 1 PM, the S&P 500 rose 1.6% and the Nasdaq 2.3% as memory and networking chips rebounded after China’s pledge to support its market, giving both indexes their best week since April 2025, while Treasury yields rose 9 basis points, gold and silver held most of their surge, oil slipped before Sunday’s OPEC+ meeting and Nvidia lagged on a report that it paused shipments to Southeast Asia.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The tape. The S&P 500 closed up 1.60% at 7,297.49, the Nasdaq Composite up 2.31% at 24,129.50, the Dow up 1.18% at 50,774.94 and the Russell 2000 up 1.71% at 2,750.30. For the week the S&P rose 5.6% and the Nasdaq 6.7%. The S&P is still down about 3.7% for November with one session left.
  • Chips. The semiconductor fund rose 4.2%: Micron 7.6%, Marvell 7.3%, Dell 6.7% and AMD 4.6%. Nvidia lagged, up 0.8% after falling as much as 2.6%, as the Financial Times and Forbes Middle East reported it had paused AI-server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries.
  • Rates. Treasuries kept selling off: the 10-year par yield rose 9 basis points to 4.94% and the 2-year 8 to 4.69%. A quarter-point Fed increase on December 9 is priced at about 99%. The high-yield spread widened to 3.77 points.
  • Metals and oil. December gold futures were up 3.8% from Wednesday’s settlement at $3,668.50 and silver 9.1%, a little below this morning’s highs. WTI crude slipped 0.7% to $83.08; OPEC+ meets on Sunday.
  • Black Friday. Adobe says U.S. shoppers spent a record $7.1 billion online on Thanksgiving Day, up 10.4% and above its $6.9 billion forecast; Black Friday figures come over the weekend.
  • Biggest risk. The rally came on about 60% of normal volume, with bond yields rising and credit spreads widening underneath it. Monday is the last day of what is still the S&P’s worst month in more than a year.
  • Beyond markets. About 600,000 East Coast customers were still without power on Friday, about 150,000 of them in Pennsylvania, mostly in the east; western Pennsylvania escaped with scattered outages. Spain votes on Sunday.

Top Stories

A half-day rally caps the S&P 500’s best week since April 2025, led by the chips that sank it

The index finished within seven points of its high of the day and 5.6% above last Friday’s close, though it still needs a 3.9% gain on Monday to avoid a losing November.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,297.49, up 114.71 points or 1.60%, the Nasdaq Composite at 24,129.50, up 2.31%, and the Dow at 50,774.94, up 591.99 points or 1.18% (Yahoo Finance; Reuters reported the same figures). The S&P traded between 7,211.46 and 7,304.10, so it never fell below Wednesday’s close and ended near its high. The Nasdaq’s gain ended a three-session losing streak.

For the week, the S&P rose 5.6%, its best week since April 2025 (Reuters), the Nasdaq 6.7%, the Dow 5.5% and the Russell 2000 6.5%. The week began with a 4.7% surge on Monday, gave back ground on Tuesday and Wednesday as the AI-hardware sell-off resumed, and ended with two holiday-thinned sessions of gains. November is another matter: the S&P is down about 3.7% for the month and the Nasdaq about 5.1%, with Monday still to trade. Reuters and others report that both are on course for their worst November since 2008.

Every one of the eleven sector funds rose. Technology led (+2.80%), followed by consumer discretionary (+2.11%), communications (+1.99%) and materials (+1.84%); utilities and health care rose least, 0.28% each. The equal-weight S&P fund rose 1.22%, less than the cap-weighted index for the first time in three sessions: this was a large-cap rebound, not a broadening. The SPDR S&P 500 fund traded 39 million shares against a 65 million average, normal for a half day after Thanksgiving.

Why it matters. The week repaired a lot of damage at the index level: the S&P is back within 1% of its 200-day average. But the rebound rests on the same AI-hardware names whose valuations started the sell-off, and it came with rising bond yields and wider credit spreads, which is not how durable recoveries usually look. That is our reading, not a forecast.

What’s next. Monday closes the month. Then a week that tests the chip trade directly: Marvell reports Tuesday, Jensen Huang faces three House committees on Tuesday and Wednesday and Senate Banking on Thursday, and Salesforce and Snowflake report Wednesday.

Memory and networking chips jump 7%; Nvidia lags on a report it paused shipments to Southeast Asia

The rebound in Asia’s chip stocks carried into New York, but the market’s largest chipmaker spent most of the session below water.

Micron rose 7.60% to $646.95, Marvell 7.34% to $140.54, Dell 6.70% to $316.96, AMD 4.56% to $424.54, Broadcom 3.82% and Taiwan Semiconductor 3.21%; the VanEck semiconductor fund rose 4.17% and is up 10.0% for the week, though still 18% below its 50-day average (Yahoo Finance). Reuters reports Sandisk rose almost 16%. Nvidia closed up 0.80% at $150.49, after trading as low as $145.39, down 2.6%.

The Financial Times reported that Nvidia had halted orders or shipments to four Southeast Asian countries, as Forbes Middle East reported this morning, while it works through new U.S. licence rules; the shares turned lower after the open, down 1.66% at $146.81 at 10:35 AM (GuruFocus). We found the FT report only through summaries, and Nvidia had not commented in what we found. We label the report a likely contributor to Nvidia’s underperformance, not a confirmed cause. The ransomware group calling itself “The Gentlemen,” which listed Nvidia on its leak site on November 18 with no data samples, has a countdown that ends Saturday; Nvidia says it is investigating an incident that may have affected a third-party partner, with no sign its own systems were affected (BleepingComputer). The claim remains unverified.

Why it matters. If shipments to Southeast Asian data-centre markets are paused for licensing, the question for Nvidia’s next quarter shifts from demand to permission. Huang’s Senate Banking hearing on Thursday is about export controls; a bipartisan push to attach chip-export curbs to the year-end defense bill is under way (The Hill).

Treasury yields rise for a second session as bonds give back Wednesday’s rally

The 10-year par yield rose 9 basis points to 4.94% on a two-o’clock close, with almost all of the curve moving in step.

The Treasury’s official par curve for Friday has the 2-year at 4.69% (+8 basis points from Wednesday), the 5-year at 4.74% (+9), the 10-year at 4.94% (+9) and the 30-year at 5.33% (+9); bills barely moved, with the 3-month at 4.35%. The 2-to-10-year spread is 25 basis points. That undoes Wednesday’s 6-basis-point rally after the PCE report and a strong 7-year auction, and leaves the 10-year 2 basis points above where it began the week. The 20-year bond fund TLT fell 1.18%.

The Fed meets December 9 with its target range at 3.75–4.00%. CME FedWatch put the odds of a quarter-point rise at 98.9% on Friday, up from 97.0% on Wednesday (as relayed by Gate and Longbridge). The blackout on Fed officials’ public remarks begins Saturday, and we found no Fed speeches today. Wednesday’s data, core PCE at 3.3% a year and growth revised to 4.3%, remain the case for raising rates.

Why it matters. Stocks rose 1.6% while the 10-year rose 9 basis points and the dollar firmed: an equity rebound with no help from rates. Rising real yields are what pressured long-duration technology stocks in the first place.

Gold and silver hold most of their surge; oil slips before Sunday’s OPEC+ meeting

Metals ended well off their morning highs but still had their best day in months; crude gave up a 3% gain as Gulf attacks met a glutted market.

At 2:44 PM, December Comex gold was at $3,668.50, up 3.75% from Wednesday’s settlement, after reaching $3,712.80; silver was at $43.70, up 9.11%, and copper at $5.60 a pound, up 5.03% (Yahoo Finance, delayed). Measured against Thursday’s holiday trading, the moves were smaller: Kitco had spot gold at $3,653, up about 2.5% on the day, and spot silver at $43.62, up about 5.3%, late in the afternoon. This morning’s figures, gold up 4.8% and silver 11%, compared the futures with Wednesday’s settlement at 6:28 AM; prices have since eased. Gold is up about 4% for the week, its first weekly gain in three (Kitco, Reuters), and still a third below its 52-week high. Newmont rose 5.35% and Freeport-McMoRan 4.57%.

January WTI crude settled the session at $83.08, down 0.72% from Wednesday, after trading as high as $86.60; front-month Brent was down about 2.3% at $94.15 at 12:15 PM Eastern (Anadolu via Middle East Monitor). Thursday’s strike on Aramco’s Jazan refinery and the tanker hit off Oman pushed prices up early; an International Energy Agency note that October supply exceeded demand weighed on them later, reports say. OPEC+ meets Sunday to set 2027 policy, and Iraq and Kazakhstan are reported to be pressing for higher production baselines, which could mean larger quotas (ING via FXStreet).

Thanksgiving online spending hits a record $7.1 billion, Adobe says

The holiday beat Adobe’s own forecast; the Black Friday tally comes over the weekend, against a $12.9 billion forecast.

U.S. consumers spent $7.1 billion online on Thanksgiving Day, up 10.4% from last year and above Adobe’s $6.9 billion forecast, Adobe Analytics said (Reuters, relayed by several outlets). Adobe forecasts $12.9 billion for Black Friday, up 9.2%, $15.1 billion for Cyber Monday and $47.5 billion for the five days of Cyber Week. These are nominal dollars: with consumer prices up 3.8% from a year earlier, real growth is closer to 6%. Retail shares were mixed in the short session: Amazon rose 3.96%, Costco 1.01%, Walmart 0.90%, Home Depot 0.25% and Target fell 0.15%; the consumer discretionary fund rose 2.11%, helped by Amazon and Tesla, which rose 5.22% to $400.00.

What Moved Markets

The open. U.S. futures arrived up about 1.7% (S&P) and 2.3% (Nasdaq-100) after the State Council’s market-support pledge sent Shanghai up 3.7% and the Kospi 4.3% overnight, and stocks opened near those levels; the S&P’s low of the day, 7,211.46, was still above Wednesday’s close. Likely contributor: China’s pledge and the overseas chip rebound, which every premarket account cited. Market narrative: “AI fears fade” and buying the dip after Thanksgiving.

The morning. Nvidia turned lower after the open and was down 1.7% at 10:35 AM, as the FT report on Southeast Asia shipments circulated, while memory and networking names kept climbing; the Nasdaq traded between 23,690 and 24,149. Likely contributor: the shipment report for Nvidia’s underperformance. Altria fell as much as 2.6% early, to $74.30, then recovered to close up 0.80% at $76.89 (Yahoo Finance; Reuters reported a decline during the session); Imperial Brands rose 1.8% in London. Unexplained: the intraday swing in Altria, with no new filing from either company.

Into the close. Indexes drifted to their highs into the 1 PM bell on light volume, about 60% of a normal day’s SPY trading in a half session. The VIX fell to 23.81 at its 1:15 PM close, down 4.48 points from Wednesday and 12 points below last Friday’s 35.89; the 9-day measure (24.10) sits just above the 3-month (23.82), so the term structure is nearly flat after a week of inversion.

Rates, dollar, credit. The 10-year par yield rose 9 basis points to 4.94% and the ICE dollar index rose 0.36% to 104.05, near its 52-week high of 104.53. Market narrative: haven demand unwinding as stocks rose; we found no single catalyst. The high-yield spread was 3.77 points on November 25 and 26, wider than 3.72 on November 24 (FRED): credit has not joined the equity rebound.

Commodities. Gold, silver and copper held most of their morning gains; crude reversed from a 3% gain to a small loss. Market narrative: short covering after last week’s lows and China’s pledge for metals; the IEA surplus note and pre-OPEC+ positioning for oil. Neither is confirmed.

The Economy

No U.S. data today. The case for a December 9 hike rests on Wednesday’s numbers: October core PCE prices up 3.3% from a year earlier, headline 3.8%; third-quarter growth revised to a 4.3% annual rate; jobless claims at 189,000. The 10-year real yield was 2.73% on November 25 and the 10-year breakeven inflation rate 2.12% (FRED): the level of yields is mostly real, not inflation compensation, and today’s rise was across the curve.

Treasury. Monday brings $95 billion of 13-week and $82 billion of 26-week bills, and a $90 billion 6-week bill follows, all settling December 3 (TreasuryDirect). Europe. France’s November harmonised inflation fell to 3.2% from 3.7% and Spain’s to about 4.1%, both well below forecasts. The ECB’s consumer survey, by contrast, showed one-year-ahead inflation expectations rising to 3.5% from 3.1% (as reported). Markets price about a 93% chance of a quarter-point ECB increase on December 17, Christine Lagarde’s last meeting (CNBC). The euro’s ECB reference rate was $1.0957.

China. The State Council’s midday statement, as reported by the Seoul Economic Daily and others, promised stronger state-backed funds, policies to steer medium- and long-term money, from insurers, corporate pensions and the social-security fund, into stocks, and incentives for dividends and buybacks; it named no amount. Releasing it during trading hours was unusual. October industrial profits rose 20.1% from a year earlier on a weak base.

Dollar and Asia. The ICE dollar index ended near 104.05, up 0.36% on the day and 2.1% for November; the yen was at 159.54 per dollar on the ECB fixing, with a Bank of Japan increase on December 18 the base case.

The United States

The storm’s aftermath. Outages from the Thanksgiving nor’easter peaked near 800,000 customers on Thursday night and were about 600,000 late Friday morning (Energy News Beat, citing outage trackers; JPost). Pennsylvania had about 148,000 out at 7 AM, nearly all in the east: PECO had about 34,000 still dark after about 225,000 lost power (FOX29), and PPL says some customers may wait until Saturday afternoon. New Jersey, under a state of emergency since Wednesday evening, had about 130,000 to 140,000 out, and Baltimore Gas and Electric says repairs may run through the weekend. Gusts of 30 to 40 mph slowed crews on Friday, though the National Weather Service says the system is weakening. We found no confirmed storm deaths. About 400 flights were cancelled and more than 2,000 delayed on Thanksgiving (FlightAware via CNN, CBS).

The president on Iran. Speaking at Mar-a-Lago on Wednesday, President Trump said the United States “would absolutely hit Pickaxe Mountain,” a suspected enrichment site, if strikes resumed, “but we haven’t seen any real activity there,” and that “it would be nice if they made a deal” (AFP via Business Standard). No new strike has been announced.

Funding and leadership. Funding runs out at 11:59 PM on Friday, December 11. The disputes are over Iran-war money, a Republican defense bill of about $1.1 trillion and the administration’s further $350 billion weapons request, and Democrats are divided over whether to use the deadline as leverage (The National, NPR). On Tuesday, Senate Democrats elect a leader by secret ballot: Chuck Schumer says he has the votes and has at least 16 public endorsements to Chris Van Hollen’s 6, though Van Hollen claims about 15 private backers (The Hill). An Economist/YouGov poll of Democratic voters has Van Hollen at 40% and Schumer at 19%; senators, not voters, decide.

Birthright citizenship. Judge Christopher Cooper’s classwide preliminary injunction against the August birthright-citizenship order stands; the next stop is the D.C. Circuit, and we found no appeal filed yet. Reports differ on whether the Justice Department sought a stay.

The World

Kuwait and the Gulf. Italy’s foreign minister says the two Rheinmetall Italia technicians killed in Kuwait on Thursday died from debris at an air-defence surveillance station; Kuwait’s army says four others were wounded by shrapnel from intercepted drones. U.N. Secretary-General António Guterres condemned the attack. Iran’s embassy in Rome “categorically rejects” involvement, and its foreign minister has offered a joint investigation (Al Jazeera, The New Arab, CBS); attribution remains disputed. It was the second drone attack on Kuwait this month. Separately, the UK’s maritime agency reported an explosion near a tanker west of Dubai, with no damage and the crew safe, and U.S. Central Command said it disabled a commercial vessel while enforcing its blockade of Iranian ports (CBS, ABC). The Revolutionary Guards’ claims that two tankers hit mines north of Khasab remain unverified; Washington rejects them. We found no update on the damage at Jazan.

Ukraine. Russia launched about 155 drones overnight into Friday, of which Ukraine says 110 were downed, damaging energy, rail and logistics sites in Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr and Kirovohrad; the energy ministry reported outages in six regions (Euronews). President Volodymyr Zelensky warned that a new large strike on the power grid is imminent.

Spain votes Sunday. Friday is the last day of campaigning, with polls embargoed since Monday. The last El País/40dB survey had the Popular Party at 31.5%, the Socialists at 30.3% and Vox at 18%; poll averages give PP and Vox together roughly 190 to 200 of 350 seats, against 176 for a majority, while the state pollster CIS is an outlier showing a Socialist win. Prediction markets put Alberto Núñez Feijóo at about 83% to become prime minister. Polls are not predictions. Market read-through: Spanish bonds and banks on Monday.

Gaza. Israeli strikes killed 10 Palestinians on Thursday, hospital officials said (AP); Israel’s prime minister and defence minister said Hamas official Khaled Abu Nada was among them. Gaza’s health ministry counts 1,679 deaths since the October 2025 ceasefire.

China and Taiwan. Reuters, citing satellite imagery, reports a weeks-long amphibious drill in Dacheng Bay, Fujian, in which civilian vessels formed an 800-metre floating bridge between November 1 and 16; a security official described it as practice for seizing small islands. Taiwan’s defence ministry is assessing it; China’s did not comment. China hosts APEC leaders in Shenzhen next week.

Science

A cancer drug from a tobacco relative. Nature reports a proof of concept in which a plant related to tobacco was engineered to make paclitaxel, the chemotherapy drug now extracted from yew trees or made in cell culture. It is a proof of concept, not a production process.

The wildlife trade is bigger than thought. A study discussed in Nature counts about 107,000 species in the wildlife trade, more than double earlier estimates.

About 148,000 Pennsylvania customers were without power early Friday, down from more than 207,000 on Thursday night (PowerOutage.us via Newsweek), almost all in the east: PECO, PPL and Met-Ed account for most of it, and PPL warns some customers may wait until Saturday afternoon. In the Pittsburgh region the storm did little: Duquesne Light reported only scattered outages, about 15 customers in Allegheny County on Thursday morning (TribLive), and we could not find a Friday count.

The legislative session ends on Monday, November 30, with no transit operating money and no skill-games deal; a reported last-minute agreement fell through, and recreational marijuana and a gift ban also failed (KYW, Capital-Star).
