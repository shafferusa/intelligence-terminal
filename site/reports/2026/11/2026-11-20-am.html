Top Stories

Taiwan, Korea and Japan fall 5–6% as the AI sell-off moves through Asia

The chip-heavy markets that led the AI boom took the hardest hit. Taiwan is on course for one of its worst weeks in decades, and U.S. technology futures are following.

Taiwan’s Taiex closed down 6.23% at 36,033.29, Korea’s Kospi down 5.21% at 4,869.96 and Japan’s Nikkei 225 down 5.06% at 56,436.65 (Yahoo Finance closes). Hong Kong’s Hang Seng lost 1.89% and Shanghai 1.22%. Korean exchanges triggered a temporary halt on program selling, and SoftBank and Advantest led Tokyo lower, regional outlets reported. The Taiex has now lost 17.5% since November 13. Reuters, as reported by MarketScreener, says Asian markets are heading for their worst week since 2020, with about $1.1 trillion in value lost. Outlets disagree on how Taiwan’s week ranks historically, so we leave that comparison out.

The trigger in the outlets’ telling was Wall Street’s Thursday. The S&P 500 rose as much as 1.3% in the morning and closed down 2%, with more than 90% of its members lower, and Nvidia closed at a new 52-week low of $145.98. Reporters cited doubts about AI valuations and capital spending, Anthropic’s fade from its opening price, and a hawkish Fed. That is a narrative, not a confirmed catalyst: no single piece of news set off the reversal, and traders quoted by Gulf News said they were looking for one. Japan also has a long weekend coming, which tends to thin liquidity on the Friday before.

Europe followed at a smaller scale. At about 6:23 AM the Stoxx 600 was down 1.26%, the DAX 1.74% and the CAC 1.30%, with the FTSE 100 down 0.56% (Yahoo). CNBC reported European technology stocks down 2.7% at the open, led by ASML, ASM International and BE Semiconductor. The Stoxx 600 is on course for its worst week since March.

Why it matters Taiwan, Korea and Japan are where the chips, memory and equipment for AI data centres are made. When those markets fall 5–6% in one session, investors there are questioning the spending cycle itself, not one U.S. stock. That is a bigger worry than an IPO fading, and it is why Nasdaq futures are down while Dow futures are flat.
What’s next The U.S. open is the test: whether the Nasdaq follows Asia down or the dip-buying that appeared on Wednesday returns. Japan is closed Monday for a holiday, so Tokyo cannot react to whatever happens on Wall Street today until Tuesday.

Go deeper

The split inside the U.S. futures is the detail to watch. Nasdaq futures are down 1.45%, the S&P 0.59%, and the Dow and Russell 2000 contracts are about flat. If the selling were about the economy or the Fed, small caps would be leading it lower, as they did on Thursday. Today it is concentrated in the market’s largest and most AI-exposed companies. For the broad economy that is better news than it looks; for anyone holding an index fund it is not, because those companies are most of the index.

What would undo the worried read: Taiwan’s and Korea’s sell-offs reversing on Monday, and U.S. chip stocks closing above Thursday’s lows today despite the weak start.

Gold and silver fall with stocks as investors sell what they can

Gold is down 2.6% to about $3,590 and silver 4.1%. Treasuries are the only haven that is working.

Gold futures were at $3,590.40 at about 6:28 AM, down 2.58% on the day and 7.6% since November 13, and silver at $42.51, down 4.09% (Yahoo Finance). Copper fell 1.1%. Gold has lost about a third of its value from its 52-week high of $5,586. Bitcoin was little changed at about $61,700, 37% below its high.

In a normal scare, gold rises as stocks fall. When both fall together, the usual explanation is that investors facing losses and margin calls are selling assets that still have gains or are easy to sell. That is a reasonable reading of the pattern, not something we can confirm from flows. The dollar is also near its 52-week high, at 103.85 on the ICE index, which weighs on dollar-priced metals. Government bonds are behaving like a haven: the 2-year yield fell 4 basis points on Thursday and the 10-year 3, and one outlet reports the 10-year near 5.0% this morning.

Why it matters Assets falling together is the market signal that tends to come before forced selling and wider credit spreads. High-yield spreads widened again to 3.47 percentage points on Wednesday, the widest this month, though still far from stressed.

Anthropic is reported lower before its second session; Intuit falls about 11% on a cut outlook

Anthropic (ANT) opened its first session on Thursday at $111.50, 34% above its $83 offer, reached $115.10 and closed at $97.17, on about 852 million shares (Yahoo Finance; CNN, CBS). Investing.com reports the shares down more than 6% before the bell this morning, which would put them near $91, still about 10% above the offer. That is one report. CNN puts the fully diluted value at about $1.76 trillion.

Intuit reported fiscal first-quarter adjusted earnings of $2.91 a share, up 56%, on revenue of $4.35 billion, up 12%, and cut its full-year earnings outlook to $22.88–23.12 a share against about $23.62 expected (company results as reported by CNBC and Investing.com). It agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for about $3.68 billion, $3.31 billion in cash and the rest in Bending Spoons stock, closing early in 2027; Intuit paid $12 billion for it in 2021. Mailchimp revenue fell 1% to $313 million. The shares closed Thursday at $310.02 and are down about 10.6–11% before the open (Benzinga, Investing.com). Analysts quoted blamed weakness at Credit Karma more than the sale.

Elsewhere after the bell, Keysight beat with earnings of $3.61 a share and is about flat this morning; Gap beat on earnings, $0.82 against $0.78, with revenue slightly short, and rose about 2% after hours; Ross Stores is down about 3.5% after a fourth-quarter guide below forecasts (Benzinga, CNBC).

Two Russian missiles and a drone come down in Poland during a large attack on Ukraine

No one was hurt, Poland’s defence minister said. Warsaw will investigate and is reported to be seeking more support from NATO.

During a large overnight Russian missile and drone attack on Kyiv, Lviv and other Ukrainian cities, two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone came down on Polish territory, Poland’s defence minister said on Friday. One Kh-101 cruise missile landed near Nowy Lipowiec in Lublin province, and the others near Rudnik nad Sanem and Mołodycz (Bloomberg, Euronews, Notes from Poland, Interfax-Ukraine). Polish air defences were activated. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Poland would investigate. Casualty figures in Ukraine vary; the Kyiv Independent reports at least eight killed. Russia had not commented in what we found.

It came hours after Thursday’s talks in Berlin, where U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said Moscow had accepted a format that includes Europeans and would accept security guarantees for Ukraine equivalent to NATO’s Article 5, which he called a “big deal.” That is the American account; we found no Kremlin comment after Berlin, and a Putin aide said on November 16 that Russia did not agree to European participation. No date has been set for another round.

Why it matters Missiles landing inside a NATO member are the kind of event that can escalate quickly. So far Poland is treating it as a matter to investigate rather than an attack. A request for allied consultations would be the next step to watch.

Trump to brief on Shenzhen at noon as the White House says little about what was agreed

The four-point declaration contains nothing on tariffs or chips, the two things markets care about. Trump says he will announce trade deals with Thailand and the Philippines.

President Trump said on social media that he will hold a news conference at the White House at noon today, including trade deals with Thailand and the Philippines. The Washington Post reported on Thursday that the White House had given “little information” on the outcome of the Shenzhen summit with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, and Bloomberg reported that staff collected travelling reporters’ phones on departure, which it called unusual. A figure of $17 billion in deals circulates only on partisan sites, and we do not repeat it as fact.

The declaration released by the three governments covers U.N. reform, energy flows, the International Criminal Court and nuclear risk. It says nothing about extending the U.S.–China trade truce, which expires January 10, 2027, or about chip export controls. China’s commerce ministry has promised “necessary measures” against the U.S. rule of November 16 on chips in overseas data centres but has announced none.

The most fragile assumption. The tape assumes the selling stays inside technology. Dow and small-cap futures are flat, Treasuries are rallying and credit spreads are widening only slowly, all of which says the economy is not the worry. But gold and silver falling with stocks, and 5–6% falls in Asia’s largest chip markets, are signs of investors cutting risk across the board. If the equal-weight S&P and the Russell 2000 fall as hard as the Nasdaq today, and HYG breaks to a new low, the problem is no longer AI valuations; it is positioning. What would invalidate the worry: the Russell and RSP holding flat or better, and the VIX closing below 27.

The Fed path. Governor Christopher Waller, who has floated a half-point rise in December, is listed to speak at 1:25 PM in one calendar; we could not confirm it on the Fed’s own schedule. On Thursday, the data gave the Fed mixed signals: jobless claims stayed low at 203,000, but the Philadelphia Fed factory index fell to 6.3 from 20.9, its lowest since May, with prices paid still high at 58.2. Treasury. We found no new statement from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent or the department since Thursday. Thursday’s $19 billion 10-year TIPS reopening sold at a 2.903% real yield, reported as the highest at auction for that term since 2002. Risks & Scenarios

The S&P 500 ends its six-day losing streak today (closes above 7,175.83). Probability 30–45%, down from 45–60% in Thursday’s closing edition. Basis: futures imply a 0.6% loss and Asia fell 5–6% overnight. In its favour, Dow and small-cap futures are flat, and options expiry can produce sharp reversals in either direction.

The VIX closes above 25 today. Probability 70–85%. Basis: it is at 29.6 before the open with an inverted short end, and next week’s holiday tends to keep traders hedged into the weekend. A close below 25 would need a broad rally of 1.5% or more.

Anthropic closes above its $83 offer price today. Probability 70–85%, down from 80–92%. Basis: it closed at $97.17 and is reported down about 6% before the open, which leaves a cushion of about 10%. It would take a second day like Thursday’s for the AI group to break it.

Science

Free will, in the lab. A study in Imaging Neuroscience by researchers at the University of Sydney reports that choices people make freely and choices they are told to make use similar brain processes, with activity building before the act in both cases (ScienceDaily, single report). It is a brain-imaging study of simple laboratory choices, so it speaks to how decisions are prepared in the brain rather than settling the philosophical question.

Hantavirus. Science reported last week on an outbreak of hantavirus pulmonary syndrome linked to cruise-ship travel in April and May 2026. We found no major journal papers dated this week in our search, which was limited this morning.

Risk-off, deepening Supporting the risk-off read: the S&P 500 has fallen six straight sessions and closed 2.3% below its 200-day average (about 7,348). SPY is 4.7% below its 20-day average and 6.0% below its 50-day, and QQQ is 9.5% below its 50-day. On Thursday decliners beat advancers about five to one, and only 22.1% of liquid stocks closed above their 50-session average. The VIX is near 30 with an inverted short end. HY spreads, at 3.47, are the widest this month. Taiwan, Korea and Japan each fell 5–6% overnight, and gold and silver are falling alongside stocks. Contradicting it: Treasuries are rallying, with the 2-year down 4 basis points on Thursday; Dow and Russell futures are roughly flat; credit is wider but not disorderly, and HYG and LQD barely moved on Thursday. Six-day losing streaks have often been followed by a bounce. What changed since Thursday morning: the IPO-led bounce failed intraday, and volatility moved from easing to rising again. Confidence: moderate to high.
