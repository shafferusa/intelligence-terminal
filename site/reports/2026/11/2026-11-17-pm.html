Tuesday, November 17, 2026 · 31 min read

The S&P 500 closed at its lowest since July as software, banks and UnitedHealth fell, then Nvidia reported $115.8 billion of revenue and guided above Wall Street, lifting its shares about 2–3% after hours, though it also guided gross margin lower.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The consumer held up. October retail sales rose 0.2%, as forecast. The control group that feeds GDP rose 0.6% against 0.4% expected, and September was revised up. Industrial production fell 0.4%, though manufacturing rose 0.3%.
  • Rates and the Fed. The 10-year Treasury eased 2 basis points to 5.10% and the 2-year 3 basis points to 4.80%. Iran’s foreign minister held maritime-security talks in Oman after Tehran claimed a drone strike on a U.S. radar in Bahrain. APEC opens in Shenzhen on Wednesday, with Trump and Xi meeting Thursday.

Top Stories

Nvidia beats and guides to $130 billion in its first report since Huang’s exit

The guide cleared the roughly $125 billion Wall Street wanted. The margin guide is lower, and the company assumes no China data-centre sales.

Nvidia reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and about $7 billion above the roughly $109 billion analysts expected. Its own August guide had been $108 billion. Data-centre revenue was about $107.6 billion, up 110% from a year earlier and 21% from the second quarter, against a StreetAccount estimate near $101.9 billion. Adjusted earnings were $2.68 a share against $2.48 expected (company release, as reported by CNBC and Investing.com).

For the January quarter the company guides revenue to $130 billion, plus or minus 3%. The outlook assumes no data-centre compute revenue from China. Adjusted gross margin is guided to 71–72%, down from 74.9% in the third quarter. Coverage singled out that margin step-down as the weak spot in an otherwise strong release.

The shares closed the regular session at $155.46, down 0.44%. In extended trading they rose as high as about $161 just after the release and were near $159 at 4:40 PM, about 2.3% higher (Yahoo Finance, extended hours, delayed). Options had priced a move of about 7–9% either way, so the first reaction is smaller than the market braced for. The 5:00 PM call, Colette Kress’s first as interim chief executive, was still under way when this edition closed. The board has set up an independent committee to review the issues behind Jensen Huang’s resignation and has tightened its export-control compliance, according to live coverage. We have not seen a full company statement on the Treasury Secretary’s questions.

Why it matters This morning the bear case for Nvidia, and for the AI suppliers that have fallen all month, was a guide below $125 billion or new shipment risk from Monday’s licence rule. The guide cleared the first test, and the China assumption is already at zero, which limits how much the rule can subtract. What remains open is margin: a 3-point guide-down while revenue grows usually means costlier new products or pricing pressure, and the call will be read for which one.
What’s next The call commentary, then Wednesday’s trading in Asia (TSMC, SK Hynix, Samsung, Advantest) and the U.S. open. Watch whether SMH, which fell 17% below its 50-day average, holds the after-hours gain.

Go deeper

The size of the beat matters as much as its direction. Revenue came in about 7% above the company’s own guide, and the January-quarter guide implies another roughly 12% sequential rise at the midpoint. Against that, guiding gross margin about 3 points lower takes back part of the earnings growth: at $130 billion, each point of margin is worth about $1.3 billion of gross profit a quarter.

The stock came into the report 36% below its 52-week high of $243.37 and 24% below its 200-day average, with short-dated options pricing an unusually large move. A modest after-hours rise against that setup says the result removed the worst outcome rather than restoring the old enthusiasm. Governance is unresolved: the board review, the Treasury’s 10-day demand from Sunday and the question of a permanent chief executive are all still open.

Selling broadens: the S&P 500 hits its lowest close since July as all eleven sectors fall

Monday’s losses were concentrated in chips. Tuesday’s fell on banks, software, health care and the defensive sectors that had been holding up.

The S&P 500 closed down 1.03% at 7,340.53, 6.4% below its 52-week high. The Dow fell 848.75 points, or 1.64%, to 50,759.42, and the Russell 2000 1.50% to 2,701.87. The Nasdaq Composite lost 0.74% to 24,328.38 and the semiconductor fund only 0.39%: chips, Monday’s epicentre, were among the day’s better performers. Every one of the eleven sector funds fell. Financials were worst at −1.91%, with utilities −1.59%, real estate −1.49%, communications −1.46% and health care −1.41%. Energy (−0.52%) and industrials (−0.55%) fell least (Yahoo Finance closes).

The equal-weight S&P fund (RSP) fell 1.45%, more than SPY’s 1.01%. That reverses Monday, when the equal-weight fund lost only half as much as the index, and it means the typical stock fell harder than the giants. SPY closed at $732.44, almost exactly on its 200-day average. The futures pointed to a 0.2% loss at 6:30 AM, so most of the day’s decline came during the session.

Why it matters A sell-off confined to one crowded trade can be absorbed by rotation into everything else. When staples and utilities fall alongside banks and software, investors are cutting exposure overall rather than moving it. The S&P sitting on its 200-day line, with the VIX at 25 and its short-dated measure above its 3-month one, is where that question gets decided.

Retail sales meet forecasts and the control group beats

The GDP-relevant core rose 0.6%, and September was revised up. Factory output rose, but overall industrial production fell on utilities and mining.

The Census Bureau’s advance estimate put October retail sales up 0.2% from September, at about $748.2 billion, in line with the consensus. The control group, which excludes autos, gasoline, building materials and food services and feeds into GDP, rose 0.6% against 0.4% expected. September’s control group was revised to +0.5% from +0.1%. Furniture stores fell 2.3% and building materials rose 0.5%. Compared with October 2025, sales were up about 6.8% (Census Bureau, as reported; Newsquawk).

These are nominal figures. With consumer prices up about 0.35% in October, a 0.2% headline gain is a small decline in real terms, while a 0.6% control-group gain is a real increase. That points to steady underlying goods spending going into the holidays.

Industrial production fell 0.4% in October, against a forecast of +0.1%, after +0.7% in September. Manufacturing output rose 0.3%, helped by a 4.4% jump in motor vehicles and parts, while mining fell 0.8% and utilities 4.3%. Capacity utilisation slipped to 76.4% from 76.8% (Federal Reserve G.17, as reported). The Fed revises the series and moves the index base to 2022 on November 24.

Why it matters A firm control group supports the Fed officials arguing for more than one rise. The weak industrial headline is mostly weather-driven utility output, so it says little about demand.

Software tumbles a day before Anthropic prices; Salesforce loses 9.5%

Seat-based software makers fell across the board. Analysts point to funds raising cash for the IPO and to fears that AI agents shrink paid seats, but no single catalyst is confirmed.

Salesforce fell 9.45% to $194.52, the Dow’s worst performer, and ServiceNow 6.51% to $92.90. HubSpot fell about 7% and Workday about 5% (as reported). Microsoft lost 2.27% to $506.24 on the first day of its Ignite conference, and Oracle 3.43% to $115.00. No company-specific news explains the group move. Reports cited three explanations: funds selling software to raise cash for Anthropic’s offering, worries that AI agents will cut the number of paid user seats software firms charge for, and, for Salesforce, doubts about its $5.7 billion Braze acquisition and the pace of its Agentforce revenue. Salesforce also holds an Anthropic stake, worth about 45% of its strategic investment portfolio as of July 31.

Anthropic is offering 825 million shares at $70–78. CNBC’s sources say the book is covered across the range and that the deal is likely to price above $78; neither the company nor its banks have confirmed that. Pricing is expected Wednesday evening and trading Thursday on Nasdaq as ANT. Anthropic remains private until it lists.

Why it matters A listing of $60 billion or more draws cash from the stocks investors already own in the theme, and software is where AI is seen as a competitor rather than a customer. If the slide is mostly about funding the IPO, it should fade after Thursday. If it is about seat pricing, it will not.

UnitedHealth falls 5% as Warren renews her push to break up health conglomerates

The senator named UnitedHealth, CVS and Cigna. Her bill with Sen. Josh Hawley has not moved.

UnitedHealth fell 5.42% to $389.68, one of the largest point drags on the price-weighted Dow. The company issued no new guidance or filing that we could find. The move followed a Monday post in which Sen. Elizabeth Warren named UnitedHealth, CVS Health and Cigna, each of which owns an insurer, a pharmacy-benefit manager and pharmacies or clinics, and called for them to be broken up. Her Break Up Big Medicine Act (S. 4509), co-sponsored with Republican Sen. Josh Hawley, would bar insurers, pharmacy-benefit managers and drug wholesalers from owning healthcare providers. We found no committee action on it. Some reports also cited Medicare Advantage reimbursement and rising medical costs.

Why it matters A bipartisan sponsor list makes structural reform a real tail risk for vertically integrated insurers, even without a vote scheduled. Today’s fall coincided with Warren’s post; that is the likeliest explanation, not a confirmed cause.

Yahoo Finance (price, data) · Benzinga, 24/7 Wall St., Investing.com (reports) · Warren’s post and bill (as reported; S. 4509 on congress.gov).

Fed hawks grow louder: Musalem wants “several” rises by spring

The St. Louis president says policy is as much as a point too low. New York’s Williams backs another rise but not a sharp one.

Speaking at a Central Bank of Argentina conference in Buenos Aires, Musalem said the economy “will likely require several interest rate increases before the spring of 2027 to achieve price stability in a timely manner” (Reuters, via Investing.com). He described the 3.75–4.00% target range as up to a percentage point below where it needs to be and said inflation is running above 3%. He is not a voter this year. Williams, the New York Fed president and a permanent voter, told the Axios BFD summit that inflation is “not moving in the right direction” and that a further rise is likely, but that there is no need for “dramatic action.” We found no new remarks from Governor Christopher Waller today.

Why it matters The debate has moved from whether the Fed raises rates in December to how big that rise is and how many follow. Williams’s comments argue for a quarter point, not a half. Wednesday’s minutes of the October meeting will show how widely the half-point case was discussed.

Reuters (via Investing.com, Kitco), Quartz, Yahoo Finance (Musalem) · Reuters (Williams) · CNBC (FedWatch, morning).

Disney beats, raises its dividend 33% and targets $3 billion of savings; the shares fall

A 53-week quarter flattered the comparison. Investors sold on heavy volume.

Disney reported fiscal fourth-quarter adjusted earnings of $1.77 a share on revenue of $25.95 billion, up 16%, against forecasts near $1.68 and $25.2 billion. The quarter had an extra week. Streaming subscription revenue rose 19% to $6.05 billion, and Experiences, the parks and cruises unit, rose 15% to $10.10 billion. The board declared a $2-a-share dividend, up from $1.50, and the company is targeting $3 billion of cost savings by fiscal 2029, including job cuts (company release, as reported by CNBC and TipRanks). The shares fell 1.48% to $116.94 on about 20 million shares, more than twice their usual volume.

Disney results release (via CNBC, TipRanks, Investing.com) · Yahoo Finance (price, volume).

Ukraine says the Kerch Bridge road span is destroyed; Iran talks with Oman as attacks continue

Russia says one rail track to Crimea has reopened. In the Gulf, a tanker crew is reported unaccounted for after an attack near Khasab.

Ukraine’s SBU security service said its strike destroyed one metal arch of the Kerch Bridge and the road span beneath it, the result of a month-long operation in seven stages that began with strikes on Russian air defences. President Zelenskyy called it “unprecedented.” Russia’s Investigative Committee said only that the bridge was damaged, and its Transport Ministry said rail traffic had resumed on one track (AP, NPR, Military.com). The strike comes two days before Vladimir Putin is due to meet Trump and Xi Jinping in Shenzhen.

In the Gulf, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi discussed shipping security in Muscat and briefed Oman’s sultan on Tehran’s proposal to end the war. Qatari and Iranian officials were due to meet in Doha. Bahrain says it intercepted the drone attack that Iran’s Revolutionary Guards claimed against a U.S. radar at Sheikh Isa air base, with no casualties or damage reported. Britain’s UKMTO said the crew of a tanker attacked north of Khasab, Oman, was unaccounted for, without naming the ship, the attacker or the weapon (as reported). That is a more serious account than earlier reports of the same area. President Trump says the U.S. naval blockade of Iran stays in place while shipping is disrupted. Brent rose 0.6% to $92.97.

What Changed Today

Nvidia’s guide. This morning we put a 45–60% chance on a January-quarter guide of $125 billion or more. The company guided to $130 billion, though with a lower margin. The worst outcome for the AI suppliers, a weak guide plus new China risk, did not happen. Confidence that the chip slide has found a floor: modest, pending the call and Wednesday’s trading.

The consumer. This morning a soft reading was a real possibility after Monday’s weak Empire State survey. Instead the control group rose 0.6% and September was revised up. We had put a 20–30% chance on a control print of 0.6% or more. That keeps pressure on the Fed and makes a weaker economy a less likely reason for the stock sell-off.

The shape of the sell-off. On Monday the damage was concentrated in chips and the defensive sectors rose, which looked like rotation. On Tuesday every sector fell and the equal-weight index did worse than the cap-weighted one. That reads more like a broad reduction in risk. Confidence: moderate, one day of evidence.

Home Depot’s leadership. This morning we flagged a reported change of chief executive as unconfirmed. Home Depot’s filing confirms it: Richard McPhail, a former chief financial officer, becomes chief executive on November 23. Ted Decker, who has returned from a medical leave, stays as chair. Ann-Marie Campbell becomes president and chief operating officer, and Jordan Broggi chief financial officer. The shares fell 2.45%.

The Kerch Bridge. This morning: a road span had collapsed, per a verified image. Now Ukraine’s SBU says one arch and the road deck beneath it were destroyed, and Russia says one rail track is running again. Crimea’s road link is cut; its rail link is reduced, not severed.

The Economy

Today’s data. Retail sales +0.2% (in line), control group +0.6% (forecast +0.4%), September control revised up to +0.5%. Industrial production −0.4% (forecast +0.1%), manufacturing +0.3%, capacity use 76.4%. The two readings point in the same direction once utilities are set aside: goods demand and factory output were steady in October. We did not find the import-price or homebuilder survey results published today and do not report them.

The Fed. Musalem’s “several increases before spring” was described in coverage as one of the most hawkish public calls from an official so far. Williams, a permanent voter, put himself in the quarter-point camp. On the latest official numbers, October core CPI rose 0.30% and headline 0.35%, unemployment was 4.1% and payrolls rose 165,000. One report attributes to Musalem an estimate that October core PCE inflation will be about 3.2% from a year earlier; that figure appears in only one source and is not an official number. The FOMC minutes are due Wednesday at 2:00 PM, and the December 8–9 meeting is three weeks away.

Rates. The Treasury par curve for today: 3-month 4.33%, 2-year 4.80%, 5-year 4.90%, 10-year 5.10%, 30-year 5.46%. The 2s10s spread widened 1 basis point to +30, and the 5s30s spread to +56 as the long end held while shorter maturities rallied. The 10-year real yield was 2.92% on Monday and the 10-year breakeven 2.20% (FRED), so the 10-year’s level above 5% is still mostly real yield rather than inflation expectations. SOFR was 3.93% on Monday.

Treasury. The week’s two coupon tests are Wednesday’s $16 billion new 20-year bond at 1:00 PM and Thursday’s $19 billion 10-year TIPS reopening (TreasuryDirect). A $75 billion 17-week bill also auctions Wednesday. The last 20-year sold at 5.469%, and the 20-year par yield was 5.51% on Monday. A tail would add to the pressure on long-end supply after three tailing coupon auctions in last week’s refunding.

Credit. High-yield spreads widened to 3.34 percentage points on Monday from 3.23 on Friday (ICE BofA via FRED), wider but far from stress. HYG fell 0.13% today and LQD was flat, so credit did not echo the equity selling. That remains the strongest argument against reading this as a growth scare.

Abroad. The dollar index held at 103.53, near its 52-week high of 103.78. The yen ended at about 159.4 per dollar, just short of the 160 level Japanese officials have warned about. The ECB’s reference rate put the euro at $1.0989.

Microsoft Ignite. Satya Nadella’s keynote focused on running and governing AI agents at work rather than on new models. Copilot Workspaces lets people and their agents share context, while personal “Autopilot” agents are “coming soon.” Microsoft IQ supplies company context to agents through more than 350 third-party connectors. Agent 365 Business Analytics tracks agent use and cost, and Nadella said Agent 365 already manages more than 50 million agents. Security additions include runtime protection in Defender for local AI agents on Windows, and Windows 365 for Agents is in preview. Microsoft also introduced a family of agentic coding models, reported as Code 2 (Microsoft blog and live coverage). Several features are previews, and we have not checked availability dates against Microsoft’s Book of News.

Read-through. Microsoft is pitching itself as the system that holds the company data, identity and controls agents need. That is the same ground Salesforce and ServiceNow are defending, which helps explain why the market sold them today, though it is not a confirmed cause.

Nvidia and Anthropic lead the paper. The two biggest events of the AI trade, the results tonight and the IPO pricing Wednesday, arrive within 24 hours of each other.
