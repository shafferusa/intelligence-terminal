Closing Edition

CoreWeave soars 25% and chipmakers rally, lifting the S&P 500 1.3% on a bond-market holiday

Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 29 min read

CoreWeave’s raised outlook revived the AI trade and chip stocks led a broad gain, while the biggest technology platforms fell, the dollar firmed, oil made a round trip on Iran headlines, and Treasuries sat out the day ahead of Thursday’s 30-year auction.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The tape. The S&P 500 rose 1.31% to 7,619.38, the Nasdaq Composite 1.60% to 25,466.19 and the Dow 0.49% to 52,486.49. The S&P is up 3.3% this week and back above Monday’s close, and the gain held from the open to the bell.
  • The leadership. Chips carried it. CoreWeave jumped 24.7% to $60.77, Micron 11.9%, Marvell 9.3% and AMD 7.3%, and the VanEck semiconductor fund (SMH) gained 4.9%. The companies that write the AI cheques went the other way: Microsoft fell 2.3%, Meta 2.0% and Amazon 1.7%.
  • Rates. The cash Treasury market was closed for Veterans Day, so Tuesday’s levels stand: two-year 4.73%, 10-year 4.98%, 30-year 5.35%. Futures still price a December hike as near-certain, with about a 9% chance of a half point.
  • Dollar, oil, gold. The dollar firmed: the euro fixed at $1.1127, down from $1.1215, and the yen weakened to 159.23. Brent fell more than $3 early on the President’s comment that new strikes on Iran were “quite unlikely,” then recovered to settle at about $92.76. Gold slipped below $4,000.
  • Biggest risk. Thursday. Jobless claims at 8:30 AM, the $25 billion 30-year auction at 1 PM into 5.35% yields, then Cisco and Applied Materials after the close, with October producer prices on Friday.
  • Beyond markets. At Arlington, President Trump used his Veterans Day address to defend the Iran war. The Mississippi and Alaska Senate races are still uncalled; Democrat Scott Colom’s lead in Mississippi passed 3,000 votes as counties near Friday’s certification deadline.

Top Stories

CoreWeave’s 25% jump revives the AI trade, and chip stocks lead a broad rally

The AI cloud provider rose 24.7% after raising its outlook. Semiconductors jumped about 5%, while the hyperscalers that buy the chips fell.

CoreWeave closed at $60.77, up $12.04 or 24.71%, after trading as much as 26.8% higher in the early afternoon (CNBC). Reuters credited a smaller-than-expected quarterly loss, the raised 2026 revenue outlook of $13.0–13.3 billion and the company’s target of an adjusted profit in the first half of 2028. The stock had lost about 40% in the month before Tuesday’s report. It is still well below its summer levels, and its $37–39 billion capital-spending plan rests on nearly $47 billion of debt.

The rest of the AI supply chain followed. On our watchlist, Micron rose 11.9% to $811.30, Marvell 9.3%, AMD 7.3%, Arista 6.3%, Vertiv 6.3%, Applied Materials 6.1%, TSMC 4.3% and Broadcom 4.0%. The semiconductor fund SMH gained 4.9% and the technology sector fund XLK 3.2%; Reuters said technology was the best of the 11 S&P sectors, up nearly 4%. The Nasdaq-100 rose 2.28%, with 83 of its 100 members higher (investingLive). Reuters-syndicated reports said the Nasdaq has now recovered all of its losses since the midterm elections.

The gain was not evenly shared. The Roundhill Magnificent Seven fund rose only 0.5%: Microsoft fell 2.3% to $521.13, Meta 2.0% and Amazon 1.7%, while Apple (+2.0%), Alphabet (+2.1%), Tesla (+2.9%) and Nvidia (+2.9%) rose. The equal-weight S&P (RSP, +0.8%) lagged the cap-weighted index (SPY, +1.3%), so the rally was led by large chip names rather than spread evenly across the market.

Why it matters A week ago the market was asking whether the AI build-out could still be financed. Today it bought the suppliers on a single strong report from the most leveraged buyer in the chain. It sold the hyperscalers whose capital spending pays those suppliers. That split is a market narrative rather than a confirmed explanation, but it points to the open question: whether spending of this size earns a return when the Fed is still raising rates.
What’s next Cisco and Applied Materials report after Thursday’s close; options imply a move of about 7.5% for Applied Materials. Nvidia reports next week, and Anthropic is expected to price its IPO on November 18.

Closing quotes (Twelve Data, Finnhub) · CNBC, Reuters (via Globe and Mail, MarketScreener), investingLive, Yahoo Finance.

Go deeper

The day’s pattern matches what the morning edition flagged as the test: whether the rally was conviction or short-covering. Two signs point to more than a squeeze. The gain held through the session, since the Nasdaq was up about 1.8% at midday and finished up 1.6%. It also spread across the chip complex rather than staying in CoreWeave. On Tuesday 52% of CoreWeave’s off-exchange volume was short sales, so covering likely added to its own move. Two signs point the other way. Breadth beyond technology was ordinary, and the platforms with the biggest capital budgets fell. Tomorrow’s Cisco and Applied Materials results are the next direct read on AI networking and chip-equipment demand.

Nvidia rises 2.9% but lags its peers as two brokers turn cautious

Morgan Stanley took the stock off its top-pick list and Baird downgraded it. The Justice Department has taken over the related export inquiry.

Nvidia closed at $173.73, up 2.87%, after being up close to 5% earlier in the day (CNBC). That is well short of the 4.9% gain in the semiconductor fund. CNBC and Fox Business report that Morgan Stanley analyst Joseph Moore removed Nvidia from the bank’s top-pick list under its stop-loss rule while keeping an Overweight rating and a $300 target. Baird cut the stock to Neutral, citing poor visibility into 2027 data-center spending and uncertainty over Jensen Huang’s tenure (Globe and Mail, Fox Business).

On the investigation, NBC reports that the Justice Department’s National Security Division has taken over the inquiry into Megaspeed, the Singapore data-center firm at the centre of the board’s review, and that the Commerce Department paused its own work at the department’s request. A shareholder class action filed on Tuesday alleges Huang concealed a relationship with a Megaspeed executive. Those are allegations in a civil complaint. No one has been charged, Megaspeed denies illegally exporting chips, and Nvidia says it has no reason to believe secrets or national security were compromised. Sen. Michael Bennet wrote to four agencies asking for an update (The Hill). Single source Fox Business reports the board has hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton for its inquiry.

Closing quote (Finnhub) · CNBC, Fox Business, Globe and Mail on the analyst calls · NBC, CBS, The Hill on the investigations.

Anthropic’s IPO book is reported to be several times oversubscribed

Bankers are weighing a price above the $70–78 range, which would value the company at roughly $1.5 trillion.

Bloomberg reports the order book is “multiple times oversubscribed,” helped by large anchor-style orders. The Financial Times, and Capital Brief citing Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, report that the underwriters expect to price above the filed range for the 825 million shares on offer. The range implies a valuation of about $1.26–1.41 trillion. Single source One Bloomberg-derived account says some investors expecting allocations are already reselling slices at a 3–5% premium. Pricing is expected November 18 and the first trade, on Nasdaq as “ANT,” on November 19. Anthropic is private until then, so there is no market price, and the company has not commented on the reports.

Why it matters Strong demand for one of the largest listings on record supports today’s read that investors will still fund AI at scale. It also means tens of billions of dollars leaving other AI holdings to pay for allocations next week.

Bloomberg, Financial Times, Capital Brief, Syz Group · amended S-1 as previously reported.

Oil falls more than $3 on Trump’s Iran comments, then recovers to close higher

Brent settled around $92.76, up 1.4%, after Iran denied reports of a meeting in Qatar.

Crude dropped early after U.S. officials played down further military action. President Trump called new strikes “quite unlikely” and cited “positive momentum” in the talks. Prices then recovered after Mohsen Rezaei, a senior Iranian official, called reports of a meeting in Qatar “pure lies” intended to push oil prices down. Continued attacks on tankers near the Strait of Hormuz also supported prices (Reuters via Investing.com, OilPrice.com). Brent settled at $92.76, up $1.27, and WTI at $86.53, up $0.49. Single source We saw the settlement figures only in Reuters copy as summarized by other outlets. Qatar’s foreign ministry says its mediation is “gaining momentum.” The terms of a reported bridge deal, which would ease the blockade and release part of about $8 billion in frozen funds in exchange for inspector access and a temporary reopening of Hormuz, are unconfirmed.

Why it matters Energy stocks rose with crude (XLE +1.9%). The intraday swing shows how much of the oil price now rests on headlines about talks rather than on supply data. The government’s weekly inventory report was pushed to Thursday by the holiday.

Reuters (via Investing.com, search summaries) · OilPrice.com · Euronews (Qatar statement) · AP / Times of Israel on the reported framework.

The dollar firms and gold slips under $4,000 as hike bets hold

With no Fed speakers and no bond market, currency and metals traders kept pricing a December hike.

At today’s European Central Bank reference fix the euro was $1.1127, down from $1.1215 on Tuesday, and the dollar bought 159.23 yen, up from 158.18. That puts the yen back near the levels where Japan has intervened before; it spent a record ¥7.98 trillion on a single day in July. December gold futures were about $3,996 in the mid-afternoon, down 0.9% (Yahoo Finance), and the GLD fund fell 1.1%. Bitcoin rose about 1% to $74,600 (CoinGecko). CME FedWatch, as reported by Yahoo Finance, priced about a 91% chance of a quarter-point hike on December 9 and 9% for a half point. No Fed officials spoke on the holiday.

ECB reference rates via Frankfurter (primary) · Yahoo Finance (gold futures, FedWatch) · CoinGecko.

European stocks close at a record as Siemens Energy and the AI rebound lift the region

The Stoxx 600 rose 0.7% to 663.05. For dollar-based investors the weaker euro erased the gain.

Reuters reports that the Stoxx 600 closed at a record 663.05, up 0.72%. Germany’s DAX rose 1.12% to 26,642.06, London’s FTSE 100 0.78% to 10,835.48 and Paris’s CAC 40 0.66%. Siemens Energy’s 2027 margin outlook and the CoreWeave report led the gains, and Société Générale strategists called the rally “somewhat stretched.” Measured in dollars, the Vanguard Europe fund (VGK) fell 0.2% because the euro lost about 0.8%. Emerging markets did better: the EEM fund rose 2.4%, helped by the chip-heavy Korean and Taiwanese markets.

Reuters via MarketScreener and Yahoo Finance · fund quotes (Twelve Data).

What Moved Markets

The open. The S&P 500 opened up about 0.8%, with the Nasdaq well ahead and the Dow lagging (24/7 Wall St.). CoreWeave opened sharply higher on its results. Confirmed catalyst: CoreWeave’s report and raised outlook are documented, and its 25% move and the chip rally began with the release.

Morning and midday. The Nasdaq was up about 1.8% and the S&P about 1.3% at midday (Reuters via Globe and Mail). Oil fell more than $3 in the morning after the President’s comments on Iran, then recovered. Likely contributor: the Iranian denial and new tanker attacks for the oil rebound, which lifted energy stocks. Microsoft, Meta and Amazon traded lower all session. Unexplained: we found no company-specific news for their declines.

Afternoon and close. The indexes held most of their gains. The Nasdaq gave back about 0.2 percentage points of its midday advance and the S&P finished where it was at midday. Six of the 11 sectors rose, led by technology and energy (Reuters). Market narrative: several reports described the day as the market deciding that AI spending is intact; that is an interpretation, not a documented cause.

Cross-asset. Treasury funds barely moved with the cash market shut (TLT +0.1%, IEF −0.1%). The dollar rose and gold fell, the pattern of a market still pricing Fed hikes. High-yield bonds edged up (HYG +0.2%), in line with equities. Bitcoin added about 1%, joining the risk rally for the first time this week.

Winners & Losers

Winners. CoreWeave (+24.7%) led everything we track. Behind it came the AI hardware chain: Micron +11.9%, Marvell +9.3%, AMD +7.3% to $552.90, Arista +6.3%, Vertiv +6.3%, Applied Materials +6.1% ahead of Thursday’s report, Quanta +4.3% and TSMC +4.3%. AST SpaceMobile rose 8.7% ahead of Thursday’s unveiling of its planned reusable rocket, and Rocket Lab 5.5%. Robinhood (+4.0%) and Coinbase (+3.1%) rose with bitcoin. Costco gained 2.0% to $1,087.61. Apple rose 2.0% to $355.63; Single source one outlet called it a record close, which we could not confirm.

Losers. The losses were small and concentrated in mega-cap platforms: Microsoft −2.3%, Meta −2.0%, Amazon −1.7%. McDonald’s fell 2.6% and SpaceX (SPCX) 2.3% to $139.75; we found no news for either. Regional banks slipped (KRE −0.2%), and gold was the weakest of the defensive assets (GLD −1.1%). After the close, Corteva reported third-quarter net sales of $1.591 billion against forecasts near $1.77 billion and cut its full-year earnings outlook on weakness in Brazil; we did not see an after-hours price.

What Changed Today

The AI trade. This morning, futures pointed to a technology rebound and the question was whether it was conviction or short-covering. It held to the close and spread across the chip group, so it was more than a squeeze in one stock. But the hyperscalers fell and the equal-weight index lagged, which makes this a rally in the suppliers rather than in the whole AI story. Our confidence in that read is moderate. Cisco and Applied Materials on Thursday and Nvidia next week are the next tests.

Oil and Iran. This morning Brent was about $92.80 and Iran was denying talks. Since then the President has said new strikes are “quite unlikely,” Iran has repeated its denial, and Brent made a round trip to finish about where it started. Nothing in the underlying picture changed: the blockade continues, the strait is shut, and Qatar is still mediating.

Our calls. Most of the forecasts due today appear to have come in. The S&P closed above 7,520.93, CoreWeave closed above $52.63, Nvidia closed above $168.89, Brent settled below $95, and Alaska’s Senate race was not called. We could not get a VIX close this evening to check Tuesday’s call that it would finish below 22. All are graded on Saturday.

The Economy

A data-free day. Veterans Day closed federal agencies and the bond market, so there were no releases, no Treasury par curve and no Fed remarks. The weekly government oil inventory report moves to Thursday.

The Fed. The market holds Tuesday’s repricing. FedWatch, as reported, puts about 91% on a quarter-point hike on December 9 and 9% on a half point, after Governor Waller, Dallas’s Lorie Logan and Chicago’s Austan Goolsbee all sounded hawkish on October’s CPI. Bloomberg-syndicated reports say the odds of a further hike in January have also risen. Single source One outlet reports Goldman Sachs has added a March 2027 hike to its forecast. Cleveland’s Beth Hammack speaks Thursday at 9 AM.

Inflation. October’s CPI rose 0.4% (core 0.3%) month over month. There is still no year-over-year rate because of the 2025 shutdown gap. Core has run about 0.3% a month for three months, an annualized pace near 3.7%. October producer prices are due Friday; the reported consensus is +0.4% headline and +0.3% core.

Treasury. The refunding finishes Thursday with $25 billion of 30-year bonds at 1 PM, settling November 16. The par 30-year closed Tuesday at 5.35%, and the 3-year and 10-year sales both drew weaker demand than last month. The bond market gets no session to rebuild before the auction. TreasuryDirect lists $115 billion of 4-week, $110 billion of 8-week and $75 billion of 17-week bills settling November 17, with 6-, 13- and 26-week bills settling November 19.

Global. Europe’s record close is covered above. The euro’s 0.8% drop against the dollar at the ECB fix reflects a Fed expected to keep hiking. In Asia, Japan’s Nikkei rose 1.3% to 61,248.59 for a third straight gain, and Hong Kong and Shanghai fell.

Business & Earnings

CoreWeave and the analyst calls on Nvidia were the day’s main corporate news. Corteva’s miss and lower outlook after the close is a farm-economy story with no read-through to the AI trade. Thursday after the close brings Cisco, with consensus near $1.29–1.32 a share on about $18.1 billion of revenue after a 14% fall in the stock over the past month. Applied Materials reports at the same time, with consensus near $4.05 a share on about $10.3 billion. It has beaten estimates for eight straight quarters. Nebius also reports Thursday. SEC EDGAR was closed for the holiday and has posted no new filings since 5:30 PM Tuesday.

Tomorrow

Then Hammack at 9 AM, the delayed weekly oil inventory report, and the $25 billion 30-year bond auction at 1 PM, the day’s key event for rates. AST SpaceMobile unveils its rocket plans in New York. Cisco, Applied Materials and Nebius report after the close. In Mississippi, Hinds County commissioners are due to certify their count, and all counties must certify by Friday.

Next week brings retail sales, Nvidia’s results, Anthropic’s pricing on the 18th, Alaska’s ranked-choice tabulation on the 18th and the APEC summit in Shenzhen on the 18th and 19th.

A fade in chips is the main risk; a broad reversal would need a poor auction or a claims shock. We put 65–80% on initial claims for the week to November 7 printing below 205,000, against 189,000 last week and a three-month trend near the cycle low. We put 60–75% on Cisco’s fiscal first-quarter revenue coming in at or above $18.1 billion, given AI networking demand and the company’s history of meeting guidance. Tuesday’s call of 70–85% that the 30-year auction clears at or above 5.30% still stands.

The United States

Veterans Day. President Trump laid a wreath at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier and spoke at Arlington after Vice President JD Vance and VA Secretary Doug Collins. According to the AP, he used the address to defend the war with Iran, which polls show is unpopular, saying Tehran started it and that U.S. forces are acting to stop it building a nuclear weapon. He praised Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, promoted his planned triumphal arch near the cemetery, and cited a smaller VA claims backlog and lower veteran homelessness.

The uncalled Senate races. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom’s lead over Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith grew past 3,000 votes as Hinds County finished counting affidavit ballots. Colom won 703 of the 817 accepted, Hyde-Smith 96. Reported margins range from about 3,041 to 3,783 depending on the update (AP, Mississippi Today). Hinds rejected 1,609 of 2,426 affidavit ballots, a rate Democracy Docket and Raw Story put at about three times the state’s. Hyde-Smith’s lawyers have asked officials to preserve all affidavit ballots, a step that often comes before litigation; no suit has been filed. In Alaska, Democrat Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by 4,250 first-choice votes as of Tuesday, with tens of thousands of ballots left to count (Anchorage Daily News). The ranked-choice tabulation is set for November 18. Democrats already hold at least 54 seats.

Congress. There was no floor action on the holiday. Government funding runs through December 11, and Military.com reports that Democrats plan to use the funding deadline and the defense authorization bill to press for cuts to the administration’s $1.5 trillion defense request. The Hegseth impeachment articles referred to the Judiciary Committee on Tuesday have no hearing scheduled.

The World

Iran. The oil-market swing and the reported Qatar framework are covered above. Iran’s foreign minister says Hormuz could reopen within two days of sanctions and the blockade being lifted and fighting ending on all fronts. U.S. Central Command says the blockade had redirected 144 vessels by the end of October. Iran International reports the President calling the war “effectively over” militarily while saying the U.S. is ready to strike again. We found no new attacks on shipping reported today.

Russia and Ukraine. AP and NPR describe the prisoner exchange agreed in principle in Abu Dhabi as likely the largest since 2022. Russia’s Vladimir Medinsky said it could happen in two to three weeks, and Jared Kushner said final numbers would follow. Kushner and Steve Witkoff are due in Moscow to meet President Putin and then in Dublin to meet President Zelenskyy. NATO’s secretary general said the U.S., Europe and Ukraine are “aligned.” No territorial breakthrough has been reported.

China and APEC. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Foreign Minister Wang Yi spoke by phone to prepare the Trump–Xi meeting at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18–19, which Beijing called “positive, constructive” (South China Morning Post). A three-way meeting with Putin has been discussed but not agreed.

Gaza. At least eight Palestinians were reported killed by Israeli fire in Gaza City and Khan Younis. More than 1,600 people have been killed since the October 2025 ceasefire, by the Gaza Health Ministry’s count. Israel’s prime minister’s office said Hamas must disarm before reconstruction begins under the 20-point plan.

AI & Technology

CoreWeave, Nvidia and Anthropic are covered in Top Stories. On export controls, The Information reports that White House officials gave congressional staff feedback in late October on new China chip restrictions that could be written into the defense authorization bill, and that Commerce is drafting rules in two areas. Nothing has been finalized, and the report does not tie the proposals to the Nvidia inquiry. Several members of Congress, including Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer and House China committee chair John Moolenaar, have called for scrutiny of the Megaspeed matter (CNBC).

Science

Rings around a dead star. A paper in Nature on November 10 reports evidence of two concentric rings of gas around the white dwarf WD J1328−1450, read from emission lines with four peaks. Gas discs around white dwarfs usually come from shredded planetary material, so a double ring suggests a more complicated history of debris around the star. It is a single peer-reviewed observation and has not yet been independently confirmed.

Space

Tonight and Friday. A Falcon 9 carrying the classified USSF-627 payload for the Space Force has a window from 9:35 PM to 1:35 AM ET from Vandenberg, after two one-day slips, with Thursday as backup (Noozhawk, KSBY). On Friday, ULA’s Vulcan is due to return to flight carrying 40 Amazon Leo satellites. It will be the first Vulcan flight with six solid boosters and a redesigned nozzle after February’s booster anomaly, and a launch time has not been set. Starship Flight 15 is still targeted for Friday evening. NASASpaceflight reported it is waiting on an FAA license change for a ship catch with an overflight of populated areas, and we found no sign the license has been issued.

The stocks. AST SpaceMobile rose 8.7% to $36.62 ahead of Thursday’s event on its reusable “Blacklift” rocket, which it plans to build with Firefly engines under a $1.3 billion agreement and fly from late 2028. Rocket Lab gained 5.5% to $66.15, extending its rally after Monday’s record $267.8 million quarterly revenue and $3.57 billion backlog. SpaceX fell 2.3% to $139.75.

Also in the News

Measles status under review. The Pan American Health Organization’s elimination commission is meeting through Friday. Its decision, expected to be made public early next week, could end the measles-elimination status the United States has held since 2000. The CDC’s latest count is 5,182 cases this year as of November 5, up 330 in a week, with 562 hospitalized (CDC, Scientific American).

Pennsylvania

Shapiro and Democratic leaders prepare a 100-day agenda as electricity prices reset on December 1

Gov. Josh Shapiro and the leaders of the new Democratic majorities say they will publish a first-100-days agenda for 2027 “in the coming weeks” (City & State PA, Capital-Star). A higher minimum wage and transit funding are at the top of the list. The AP projects a House majority of at least 111–88, alongside the 26–24 Senate. Separately, utilities’ residential “price to compare” for electricity resets on December 1. The current rate of 12.61 cents per kilowatt-hour runs 11.9% above a year earlier. Shapiro has intervened in PPL’s rate case to ask for a separate rate class for large data centers, and that case is unresolved.
