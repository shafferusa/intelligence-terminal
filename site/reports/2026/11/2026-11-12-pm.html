Top Stories

Jensen Huang resigns from Nvidia, and his finance chief takes over while the board searches

The co-founder who ran the company since 1993 left both his CEO and board roles “effective immediately.” The stock swung from a 14% loss to a 4% loss as investors weighed the gap he leaves against the board’s quick action.

Nvidia announced a “CEO transition” on Thursday in a press release and a separate statement from its board. Huang has stepped down as president, chief executive and director. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer since 2013, becomes interim CEO and stays CFO. Harvey C. Jones, a long-time director and former chief executive of Synopsys, becomes the board’s first chair. The board says it will consider internal and external candidates for the permanent job. An independent committee of directors, advised by outside counsel, continues the inquiry it opened this week (CNBC, CNN, TechCrunch, Fortune, ABC).

Huang’s departure follows a week of reports about his personal conduct. These include reports of affairs, one of them, according to Variety and CNN, with a woman U.S. officials reportedly suspect of working for Chinese intelligence. Huang has denied compromising national security or company secrets. Disputed Whether he resigned or was forced out is contested: CNBC cites a source saying the board had decided the night before to remove him. The press release calls it a resignation. Huang has agreed to brief lawmakers, according to reports, but no date is set.

The shares fell as much as about 14% in the first hour, then recovered steadily, and closed down 4.34% at $166.19 on 734 million shares, about twice the recent daily average (Yahoo Finance). That leaves the stock about 11% below the $186.64 it closed at last Friday, before the board first announced its inquiry. Money moved to Nvidia’s rivals. AMD rose 5.86% to $585.30 and Broadcom 5.54% to $364.36, the S&P 500’s two biggest gains. Arista Networks rose 5.1%. Nvidia’s suppliers fell: Taiwan Semiconductor lost 1.9% and ASML 2.4%. The PHLX semiconductor index ended only 0.54% lower.

Why it matters The question of whether the board would act is settled within a day. The new question is who runs the most valuable chip company through its earnings next week, the Anthropic IPO (where Nvidia is a reported strategic buyer) and the China export debate, in which Huang was the industry’s loudest voice for sales. Kress is a finance executive, not a product architect. The market’s verdict, a modest loss and gains for AMD and Broadcom, reads as “Nvidia loses some edge, and AI demand is intact.” That is an interpretation of the price action, not a confirmed motive.
What’s next Nvidia reports its quarter next week, and the call will be Kress’s first as CEO. Watch for any change in guidance language, senior engineering departures, and the date of Huang’s congressional briefing.

Go deeper

There are three ways to read Thursday’s recovery from the lows. Investors may have been relieved that the board ended the uncertainty within a day. They may have priced a short interim period rather than a long vacuum. Or dip buyers in the biggest index weight may have been mechanical buyers, not a vote on governance. These readings are not exclusive, and none can be proven from the tape. Nvidia’s orders are booked quarters ahead, so the near-term numbers are not in question. The risk is to execution over a longer horizon, through the annual chip cadence and the CUDA software moat, both closely identified with Huang. Two of this morning’s calls resolved today. The stock closed below $166.78, and the board changed chief executive well before November 20. On the policy side, CNN’s analysis argues that Huang’s exit could weaken the industry’s lobbying for China sales. That is commentary, but it is relevant to export-control watchers.

A weak 30-year auction and a hawkish Fed chorus push the 10-year yield to 5.10%

This week’s 3-, 10- and 30-year sales all tailed, the first time since May. The new 30-year bond carries a 5.375% coupon, the highest since 2001.

The Treasury sold $25 billion of 30-year bonds at a high yield of 5.441%, about 0.4 basis point above the 5.437% when-issued level at 1 PM (Newsquawk). The bid-to-cover ratio of 2.36 was the lowest since June, against 2.54 last month (TreasuryDirect, primary). Foreign and other indirect bidders took 68.1%, down from 72.3%. Dealers were left with 11.4%, above their recent average of about 9.5%. The bond pays a 5.375% coupon. The par curve closed at 4.86% for the 2-year, 5.10% for the 10-year and 5.43% for the 30-year, up 13, 12 and 8 basis points from Tuesday. The 10-year inflation-protected yield rose to 2.86%, so nearly all of the move was in real yields.

Fed officials gave the bond market no relief. San Francisco’s Mary Daly said further tightening is needed and that inflation pressure “is up, not down” (FXStreet and others). Cleveland’s Beth Hammack, who dissented for a hike in October, backed gradual increases. Chicago’s Austan Goolsbee called October’s CPI “borderline disturbing” and said there was “plenty of room” to discuss options (Newsquawk). Vice Chair for Supervision Michael Barr spoke on AI and jobs, not policy, and Kansas City’s Jeff Schmid’s prepared text did not discuss the outlook. New York’s John Williams speaks at 6:30 PM, after this edition. CME FedWatch snapshots put a December hike near 100%, about 91% for a quarter point and 9% for a half, though snapshots differ.

Why it matters A 10-year above 5% is a rate the equity market has had to live with for weeks. Today’s new elements are the demand problem at auctions and a real-yield move of eight basis points in two sessions. Rising real yields tighten financial conditions on their own, and they are the most direct pressure on long-duration growth stocks and on small caps that borrow at floating rates. That fits a day when the Russell 2000 fell 1.7%.

Jobless claims rise to 200,000 and home sales hit their lowest level since 2010

The labour market is still tight, but higher mortgage rates are cutting housing turnover.

Initial claims for unemployment benefits rose 9,000 to 200,000 in the week to November 7, above the 195,000 Reuters poll. The prior week was revised up to 191,000 from 189,000. The four-week average is 195,250, and continuing claims fell 20,000 to 1.697 million (Labor Department via Reuters and FXStreet; FRED). By historical standards these are still very low numbers. Existing-home sales fell 5.5% in October to a 3.75 million annual rate, against roughly 3.85 million expected. That is the lowest since August 2010 and 8.8% below a year earlier, according to the National Association of Realtors. Prices have not given way: the median rose 0.4% from a year earlier to $416,700, the 40th straight annual gain. Inventory is 1.59 million, or 5.1 months’ supply.

Why it matters Neither report changes the December case. The Fed is tightening into a labour market that has not cracked, and housing is the sector that feels higher rates first. Sales are falling while prices hold, which means turnover is freezing rather than prices falling.

Oil jumps after the President says the U.S. does not “need a deal” with Iran

Brent rose more than $3 by late afternoon, the opposite of the morning’s move. Gold broke to a one-year low as real yields and the dollar rose.

In a Truth Social post on Thursday, President Trump said Washington does not “need a deal with Iran,” because it controls Iran’s money and the Strait, and told Vice President Vance to focus on crime and corruption in Democratic-run cities (Reuters via US News, SCMP, Ynet). Several outlets read it as moving Vance off the Iran file; others note that the President did not end indirect talks through mediators. Brent was $95.96 at 4:28 PM, up 3.45% on the day, and WTI $88.85, up 2.7% (Yahoo Finance, delayed; official settlements not confirmed). Single source One market summary also tied the rise to reported Houthi attacks on Saudi oil facilities, which we could not confirm. The IRGC’s claim that mines disabled two supertankers in Hormuz is still unconfirmed by UKMTO or Central Command, and no ships have been named. U.S. crude inventories rose 4.66 million barrels last week against an expected draw (EIA, via Reuters), so supply did not support the rally.

Gold futures fell about 3% to $3,879, a one-year low. GLD closed at $354.89, just above its 52-week low. The ICE dollar index rose 0.27% to 103.18 and touched a 52-week high. The yen weakened to 159.94 at its weakest, its lowest in more than two months, before ending near 159.9. Japan’s top currency diplomat, Atsushi Mimura, said it was “too early to forget” the summer intervention (Reuters via MarketScreener). Japan has not intervened.

Cisco and Applied Materials beat after the bell, and both raise the bar

On the day Nvidia changed chief executives, two of the AI build-out’s suppliers reported stronger demand.

Cisco reported fiscal first-quarter revenue of $18.52 billion, against about $18.14 billion expected, and adjusted earnings of $1.40 a share against $1.33. Networking revenue rose 43% to $11.13 billion. It raised its full-year revenue outlook to $74.8–75.8 billion and guided the current quarter to $18.0–18.2 billion (Benzinga, Seeking Alpha). The stock was up roughly 2–5% after hours, depending on the snapshot. Applied Materials reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $10.41 billion, up 53%, and adjusted earnings of $4.16 against $4.06. It guided the next quarter to $11.1 billion, plus or minus $500 million, and $4.35 a share, both above consensus of about $10.87 billion and $4.23 (MarketBeat, AP). It had closed down 3.6% at $380.95 before the report, and early after-hours readings were mixed. Both companies issue official releases, but we saw the results through press summaries.

Trump’s envoys meet Putin for about two hours, then fly to Ireland to see Zelenskyy

Moscow showed no sign of moving on its core demands, but Witkoff said another three-way round would take place.

Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met President Putin at the Kremlin on Thursday. NPR put the meeting at about two hours and RFE/RL at two and a half. Kushner said the aim was to build on the Abu Dhabi talks, which produced a large prisoner exchange but little else. The envoys pressed for European participation. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said European leaders would first have to name a single representative. Witkoff said another U.S.-Ukraine-Russia round would follow (Liga.net). Russian state media reported, without independent confirmation, that Putin proposed a humanitarian mission in Kostiantynivka and repeated that foreign troops in Ukraine would be a “legitimate target.” The envoys then flew to Ireland, where President Zelenskyy is attending the European Political Community summit near Dublin. Reports say French, German and British advisers were to join. We found no readout of that meeting by press time. Overnight, Ukraine said it shot down 94 of 123 Russian drones. Kyiv’s mayor said one woman was killed when a drone hit an apartment building.

The Economy

Today’s data. Claims rose to 200,000, against 195,000 expected and a revised 191,000 the week before. Continuing claims fell to 1.697 million. Existing-home sales fell to a 3.75 million annual rate, the lowest since 2010, while the median price held at $416,700, up 0.4% from a year earlier. The EIA’s crude build of 4.66 million barrels took inventories to 414.4 million, about 4% below the five-year average. Refineries ran at 95.2% of capacity.

The Fed. Three regional presidents sounded hawkish on the same day. Daly said tightening needs to go further. Hammack backed gradual hikes, saying the Fed has missed on inflation for years. Goolsbee, usually among the more cautious, called October’s CPI “borderline disturbing.” Barr warned that AI could cause “serious short-term disruptions” in jobs but did not discuss rates. With a hike in December treated as settled, the market’s question is its size and what comes after. A half point is priced at about 9%. Williams, the leadership’s main voice after the chair, speaks tonight.

Treasury. The refunding closed with three tails: the 3-year at 4.627% (bid-to-cover 2.53), the 10-year at 5.020% (2.46) and the 30-year at 5.441% (2.36). Today’s bills sold with bid-to-cover ratios of 2.94 (4-week, $115 billion), 2.50 (8-week, $110 billion) and 2.78 (17-week, $75 billion), all settling November 17. Next on the schedule are 6-, 13- and 26-week bills settling November 19, and a $16 billion 20-year bond and $19 billion 10-year reopening settling November 30 (TreasuryDirect). SOFR was 3.89% on November 10.

Rates in real terms. The 10-year real yield rose to 2.86% today from 2.78% on Tuesday and 2.60% a week earlier (Treasury real curve). Subtracting it from the 5.10% nominal yield gives inflation compensation of about 2.24%, little changed. The sell-off is about the price of money, not about expected inflation. The curve flattened slightly: 2s10s narrowed to 24 bp from 25, and 5s30s is 48 bp.

Global. European shares edged up, and the Stoxx 600 rose 0.14% to 664.01 near its record, as Burberry and Munich Re offset chip weakness. Germany’s DAX rose 0.41%, France’s CAC 0.34% and London’s FTSE 0.25%. Earlier, Asia sold chips: Taiwan’s Taiex fell 2.63%. We found no new remarks from the ECB, the Bank of England or the People’s Bank of China.

Space

AST SpaceMobile’s rocket. The satellite-phone company unveiled “Blacklift,” a reusable rocket in roughly the 20-ton class meant to carry up to five of its BlueBird satellites per launch, at a New York event. It targets operations in late 2028 (company webcast, TechTimes). The rocket uses Firefly’s Miranda engines under a reported $1.3 billion deal. Those engines have been test-fired but have never flown to orbit. AST’s shares fell 5.8% to $34.50.

Friday’s double header. ULA’s Vulcan returns to flight at 12:54 PM with 40 Amazon Leo broadband satellites. It is the rocket’s first flight since a February failure, and the boosters have redesigned nozzles. SpaceX targets Starship Flight 15 from Starbase in a 75-minute window from 5:45 PM, pending regulatory approval, and plans to try catching both the booster and the ship (NASASpaceflight, SpacePolicyOnline).

SpaceX. SPCX closed up 0.14% at $139.94 after an intraday rise of about 3%. That move followed speculation over Elon Musk’s comment that it would be “an honor” if Huang joined SpaceX (Benzinga, Motley Fool). That is a remark, not an offer. The company also announced an Arizona supercomputing campus as part of its $74 billion Texas and Arizona build-out.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Council begins work on a Pittsburgh budget that draws down the rainy-day fund

City Council’s budget hearings began this week on Mayor Corey O’Connor’s first spending plan. It has about $727 million of operating spending and a $145 million capital budget, with no tax increase and no layoffs. It balances by drawing about $27–28 million from reserves (WESA, WTAE, PublicSource; outlets differ by $1 million). The five-year forecast shows deficits every year, with reserves running out by the end of 2031. A companion bill would suspend the city’s 10% minimum fund-balance rule for 2029–2031; WESA reports its public hearing on December 10. Councilmember Bob Charland called the housing fund “the most challenging portion of this budget.” Department hearings run through late November, and the final vote is in December.

WESA, WTAE, PublicSource, Audacy.

A second daily Pittsburgh–New York train starts Wednesday

Amtrak’s Pennsylvanian goes to two round trips a day on November 18, making a same-day round trip to New York possible, under the expansion PennDOT announced in October (Railway Age, Mass Transit). A House panel votes 33–0 to censure Rep. Aaron Bernstine

The House Rules Committee advanced House Resolution 676 on Tuesday. The resolution would censure the Butler County Republican over alleged homophobic slurs at a Harrisburg bar; he denies making the remarks and has given up his committee assignments (Pennsylvania Capital-Star, Butler Eagle). Outlets differ on whether the full House will vote before the session ends. The House returns November 19, and both chambers adjourn for good on November 30.
