Top Stories

The Dow gains 1,569 points, its best day in 19 months, as investors price a Democratic Congress

The rally that started at the open did not fade. It spread to banks, retailers, health care and small companies, and left behind only energy, defense, and the companies selling the AI build-out.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 3.03% to 53,356.12, the S&P 500 1.58% to 7,688.62, the Nasdaq Composite 1.44% to 25,736.51 and the Russell 2000 1.58% to 2,856.95 (CNBC; CNN; Yahoo Finance). The S&P 500 was up 1.5% at 10:31 AM and finished slightly higher; the Dow was up 2.6% at midday, by the Associated Press’s count, and kept climbing. CNN, citing CFRA Research, said it was the S&P 500’s second-largest gain the day after a midterm since 1982 and the Dow’s largest. No major index set a record; the Dow is about 2.5% below its high.

Unlike Tuesday’s rally, which a handful of chip stocks carried while most stocks fell, this one was broad. The equal-weighted S&P 500 fund rose 1.9%, more than the cap-weighted fund’s 1.6%. Among sector funds, consumer discretionary rose 4.3%, financials 3.5%, communications 3.5%, staples 3.4%, real estate 3.3% and health care 3.1%; technology was flat, industrials fell 1.1% and energy 4.7% (Finnhub, EOD). Banks and private-capital firms were among the strongest large companies: JPMorgan +4.2%, Morgan Stanley +4.2%, Blackstone +6.2%, Apollo +8.1%. Treasury yields fell, gold rose 3.4%, and the VIX fell to 16.61 from 20.83.

The losers were concentrated. The semiconductor index fell 3.1% and Nvidia 3.2%, power producers and grid-equipment makers fell 9–14% (see below), and defense contractors fell: Lockheed Martin −6.9%, L3Harris −6.5%, Northrop Grumman −4.5%. Trump Media fell 24.5% to $4.49 (Finnhub). Tesla (−3.3%) and SpaceX (−3.8%) fell with them.

Strategists quoted today offered one explanation for the pattern: a Democratic Congress facing a Republican president with a veto is less likely to pass either large tax and spending bills or sweeping new restrictions, so investors bought companies sensitive to consumer spending and interest rates and sold those that depend on federal spending or on fast approval of power plants and data centers. Charu Chanana of Saxo told CNN the result “significantly” constrains the president’s tax and spending plans while his veto limits Democrats. That is a market narrative, not a documented cause. Falling oil prices, which fell on a different thread (next story), coincided with the gains in retailers and airlines.

Why it matters

The election removed one uncertainty and replaced it with two: whether a Democratic Congress can actually curb the AI power build-out, and whether the White House now seeks a deal with Iran. Today’s prices assume the answers are “partly” and “probably.” Neither is settled, and the Fed, which markets still expect to raise rates in December, has not changed. This morning’s calls that the S&P 500 would close above Tuesday’s level and that Vistra would close at least 8% lower were both right.

CNBC and CNN market coverage (CFRA Research figures as quoted); the Associated Press; Benzinga; Yahoo Finance (index closes, via proxy fetch); Finnhub and Twelve Data (stock and fund closes).

Oil falls 8% to $88 as Vance blames the Iran war for Republican losses and traders bet on a deal

A bigger fall than Monday’s 7.4% drop, and this one came on politics, not supply: the weekly government count showed inventories falling.

West Texas Intermediate settled at $88.12 a barrel, down 8.3% from Tuesday’s $96.09, according to Quartz and OilPrice.com; Brent traded near $95 after 4 PM, down about 8% from Tuesday’s $103.60 settlement (Yahoo Finance; a Brent settlement price was not confirmed). The United States Oil Fund fell 8.1% and the energy sector fund 4.7%; Exxon Mobil fell 4.6% and Chevron 3.7%.

The fall followed a political turn in Washington. Vance wrote on X that the Iran war was the reason for his party’s losses and that “any honest conversation about where we go from here must start there,” as reported by CNBC and OilPrice.com. President Trump acknowledged the war contributed to the results but said he had no regrets, according to Axios; he told NBC “No” when asked about new strikes after the election, and Axios that he is “in no rush.” An analyst quoted by OilPrice.com said traders were betting the president will now seek a deal. Iran says talks through Qatar and Pakistan are “very intensive” but that there is no agreement, and its officials continue to claim tanker strikes in the strait that the US disputes (see The World).

The supply data pointed the other way. The Energy Information Administration reported US crude inventories fell 865,000 barrels in the week to Oct. 30, to 409.8 million, against a Reuters poll for an 877,000-barrel build; gasoline fell 222,000 barrels and distillates 3.6 million, leaving distillate stocks 13% below their five-year average. Refineries ran at 94.5% of capacity (Reuters via BOE Report).

Why it matters

Oil at $88 is a large tax cut for consumers if it lasts, and gasoline was the issue the president himself blamed. But the price now assumes diplomacy that has not happened, with the strait still disrupted and distillate stocks tight. Our call: front-month WTI settles below $92 on Thursday (60–75%), given today’s settlement and no sign of a reversal after hours; a new incident in the strait is the risk.

Quartz and OilPrice.com (settlement as reported); Yahoo Finance (Brent, late trade); Energy Information Administration via Reuters/BOE Report; CNBC; Axios; NBC News.

AI power stocks fall 9–14% as voters approve local limits on data centers and a Democratic Congress looms

The companies that generate electricity for data centers and build the equipment inside them had their worst day in months; memory makers and chip-equipment stocks fell with them.

Vistra closed down 13.8% at $120.29, NRG 13.5%, Vertiv 12.9% at $205.55, Quanta Services 11.7%, Eaton 9.9% (after a 7% gain on Tuesday’s record orders), GE Vernova 9.3%, Talen 8.5% and Constellation Energy 6.7% (Finnhub, EOD). Memory fell: Micron −7.0%, and Marvell −7.1%. Nvidia lost 3.2%, ASML 3.6% and Broadcom 2.0%. Data-center landlords went the other way: Equinix +3.9%, Digital Realty +2.6%.

CNBC tied the selling to Democratic wins in Texas, where Gina Hinojosa defeated Gov. Greg Abbott according to NBC and AP projections, and to local ballot measures rejecting data centers. Axios, citing Ballotpedia, reported that about two dozen of 41 local measures in ten states that restrict or block data centers appear to have passed; one other tally put the number far higher, and we use the lower count. Granville, Ohio, approved a ban with about 82% of the vote and El Dorado, Kansas, a ten-year moratorium. In Ohio, Sherrod Brown won a Senate seat after campaigning against data centers, according to secondary reports. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer listed the cost of living, including electricity, among his priorities.

Why it matters

For two years the market has paid up for anything that supplies power to AI, on the assumption that demand is unlimited and permitting will keep up. Today investors appeared to price the second assumption as weaker. Local votes bind only their towns, and Congress has passed nothing; the hyperscalers that buy this equipment (Microsoft, Amazon) rose today, so the market is not pricing less AI spending, only a harder road to building it.

Finnhub (closes); CNBC; Axios and Ballotpedia; 10TV; NBC News and AP projections; Time News (secondary, Ohio).

Bonds rally, gold jumps 3.4% and the dollar slips; Treasury keeps auctions at $125 billion

Lower oil and the prospect of less new borrowing coincided with the biggest fall in yields in a week.

On the Treasury’s official curve the 2-year yield closed at 4.58% (−3 basis points), the 5-year at 4.69% (−5), the 10-year at 4.90% (−5) and the 30-year at 5.28% (−4); the 3-month bill was unchanged at 4.21%. The gap between 2- and 10-year yields narrowed to 32 basis points. Long-bond funds rose: TLT +0.8%, investment-grade LQD +0.8%, high-yield HYG +0.7%. Gold futures settled up 3.4% at about $4,073 an ounce and silver up 5.1% at about $52.30 (CNBC); the dollar index fell 0.3% to 101.52 (Yahoo Finance). One strategist quoted by CNBC summed up the bond market’s view: less fiscal stimulus argues for lower long yields and a softer dollar, offset by a Fed still leaning toward another increase.

At 8:30 AM the Treasury announced next week’s quarterly refunding at $125 billion — $58 billion of 3-year notes on Monday, $42 billion of 10-year notes on Tuesday and $25 billion of 30-year bonds on Thursday — refinancing about $97 billion of maturing debt. The statement, delivered by Assistant Secretary for Financial Markets Hunter McMaster, kept coupon sizes unchanged and said Treasury expects to cut bill sizes in December and raise them in January. This morning’s edition reported the remaining details: the 7-year note moving to a quarterly new-issue cycle in March and up to $95 billion of buybacks this quarter.

Why it matters

The 10-year yield is now 10 basis points below the 5% line it crossed in October. Divided government lowers the odds of a deficit-widening bill, which is what bond investors appear to be pricing. Friday’s jobs report and the Fed’s December decision matter more for the short end: futures last priced a December rate increase at roughly three chances in four (as of Tuesday, secondary reports).

After the bell: AppLovin jumps about 16%; Qualcomm and Arm beat but slip on outlooks; Snap falls

Two chip designers delivered the second read on chips after AMD. Both beat, and neither forecast was strong enough to lift its stock.

Qualcomm reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of about $10.32 billion against roughly $10.11 billion expected and adjusted earnings of $2.17 a share against $2.16. It forecast fiscal first-quarter revenue of $10.4–11.2 billion and earnings of $2.15–2.35 a share, whose midpoint is slightly below the roughly $2.27 consensus; the company pointed to the coming ramp of its data-center business. The shares, which rose 5.3% in the session to $173.19, fell 2–5% after hours, as reported (InvestingLive; TipRanks; Benzinga).

Arm reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $1.44 billion against about $1.38 billion and adjusted earnings of $0.52 against $0.47. Reports of its royalty revenue conflict ($715 million in one account, $670 million below expectations in another), and the shares, down 5.3% in the session, slipped about 2–4% after hours.

AppLovin reported third-quarter revenue of about $2.19 billion, up 55%, against $2.08 billion expected, and adjusted earnings of $4.30 a share against $4.07, and forecast fourth-quarter revenue above estimates. The stock, already up 10.7% in the session, rose about 16% after hours (Benzinga; Seeking Alpha).

Snap reported revenue of $1.722 billion, up 14% and slightly under the roughly $1.73 billion expected, with 495 million daily users against about 499 million expected; adjusted operating earnings beat. Its fourth-quarter revenue forecast of $1.95–1.99 billion brackets consensus. Chief executive Evan Spiegel said ad growth “remains below our ambitions.” The shares fell after hours (Bloomberg; Snap).

What’s next

AMD closed up 1.0% at $488.78 after its strong forecast, a much smaller gain than the 6–7% it showed last night, in a day when the chip index fell 3.1% — our call that it would close above Tuesday’s $483.90 was right, narrowly. Thursday brings more consumer and software results; see Tomorrow.

Company releases as reported by InvestingLive, Benzinga, TipRanks, Seeking Alpha, Reuters and Bloomberg; Finnhub (closes). After-hours moves are as reported and change quickly.

CVS falls 12.5% on a surprise plan to split its pharmacies from Aetna and Caremark

The stock was up 3% before the open on better-than-expected results; it lost about $15 billion of market value once investors read the plan.

CVS Health closed at $79.96, down 12.5% (Finnhub). The company plans to separate into CVS Pharmacy — stores, mail order and MinuteClinic, led by Prem Shah — and CVS Health, comprising the Aetna insurer, the Caremark pharmacy-benefit manager, specialty pharmacy, Oak Street and Signify, led by David Joyner, with completion targeted for the first half of 2028. It reported third-quarter adjusted earnings of $2.09 a share and raised its full-year outlook to at least $8.20. UBS analyst Kevin Caliendo called the move a surprise that looked defensive against regulation; Barclays’ Andrew Mok noted the absence of financial detail (Reuters; Star Advertiser).

Why it matters

Pharmacy-benefit managers are a bipartisan target in Congress, and the plan separates the regulated middleman from the retail business. Investors’ first verdict was that a breakup two years away adds cost and uncertainty before it adds value. Health care overall rose 3.1%, so this was company-specific.

CVS release and results as reported by Reuters and the Honolulu Star-Advertiser; Finnhub (close).

Democrats reach 54 Senate seats with two races uncalled; Mississippi is separated by 43 votes

The president said he accepts the results and wants deals, then posted a picture of a “veto” hat; the House speaker will not seek a leadership post.

NBC’s tally gives Democrats 54 Senate seats and Republicans 44, a net Democratic gain of seven, with Alaska and Mississippi uncalled. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom led Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith 462,821 to 462,778 with about 95.6% tabulated at 3 AM, according to WLBT and the Mississippi Free Press; an earlier Decision Desk HQ count showed a lead of about 2,000 votes. Affidavit and absentee ballots remain, the state has no automatic recount, and both campaigns say they are confident. Count changing In Alaska, Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by 626 votes of 213,873 counted; ranked-choice tabulation, if needed, comes about 15 days after Election Day (CBS; Alaska Beacon). In the House, where the AP called Democratic control early this morning, published counts differ widely because dozens of races are uncalled.

President Trump told The New York Times he is “absolutely willing to accept those results.” He said Republicans would have won with him on the ballot, blamed gasoline prices, said he had spoken with Democratic leaders and wants deals but will veto bills if needed, and about an hour later posted an image of a “veto” hat (Axios). House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, in line to be speaker, called the result “an American tsunami” and a mandate for “a new path forward.” Speaker Mike Johnson said, “I am disappointed, too. But we are never discouraged,” and NBC reports he will not seek a leadership post in the next Congress. Schumer called the result a “generational remake of the Senate map” and listed the cost of living, ending the Iran war and investigations as priorities; Senate Republican leader John Thune wrote that Republicans “will not stand idly by.”

What’s next

The new Congress takes office Jan. 3. Until then the current Republican majorities run the lame-duck session, including government funding. Our call: the AP has not called the Mississippi Senate race by 6 PM Friday, Nov. 6 (70–85%), given a margin of dozens of votes with ballots outstanding and no automatic recount.

NBC News; WLBT; Mississippi Free Press; The Hill and Decision Desk HQ; CBS News; Alaska Beacon; Axios; The New York Times as quoted; ABC7; NPR; PBS.

Sweden raises rates for the first time since 2023; the Bank of England is expected to follow on Thursday

Two more central banks are moving the way the Fed is expected to in December: up.

The Riksbank raised its policy rate by a quarter point to 2.00%, unanimously, effective Nov. 11, and said further increases are “likely” in the coming year (Riksbank; Bloomberg; RTÉ). On Tuesday the Reserve Bank of Australia raised its cash rate to 4.85%. The Bank of England decides at noon UK time on Thursday; a Reuters poll last month found 54 of 63 economists expecting an increase to 4.00% from 3.75%, and swap pricing implies near certainty. UK inflation was 3.5% in September, and the September vote was 6–3 to hold, the three dissenters favoring an increase.

Why it matters

Oil’s fall today, if it lasts, eases the energy-driven inflation behind these increases, but none of these banks set policy on one day’s price. A coordinated tightening cycle is one reason long yields worldwide had risen; today’s fall in US yields went against it.

Sveriges Riksbank; Bloomberg and RTÉ; Reuters poll as reported; Office for National Statistics; Bank of England.

The Economy

Today’s data. ISM’s services index fell to 54.5 in October from 54.9, below the 54.8 expected and the lowest since July, but still the 28th straight month of expansion. Its prices-paid index eased to 73.0 from 74.0, the second-highest reading since mid-2022; 17 of 18 industries reported paying more, and respondents cited tariffs and fuel most (ISM). ADP’s count of private payrolls rose 86,000, above the 69,000 Reuters consensus. Mortgage-application data from the Mortgage Bankers Association were not retrieved this evening.

The Fed. No Fed officials spoke publicly today, by our search. The Fed held its target at 3.75–4.00% on Oct. 28, with two officials dissenting in favor of an increase; since then Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan has said she wants at least two more increases and St. Louis’s Alberto Musalem that further tightening is needed “soon,” while Chair Kevin Warsh has said inflation is “too high” without committing to December. Futures last showed roughly a 77% chance of an increase on Dec. 9 (HousingWire, citing CME FedWatch, Tuesday). Lower oil, if it lasts, would cut headline inflation in the coming months, but services prices, by ISM’s measure, are still rising fast. Level and direction: inflation is high and not yet falling; the labor market is slow but stable.

The Treasury. Refunding sizes unchanged (see Top Stories). The Treasury’s cash balance is expected to peak around $1.025 trillion in late November and late January, per the refunding documents as reported. Next week’s 3-, 10- and 30-year auctions will test demand at lower yields than last quarter’s.

Real yields and inflation expectations. The 10-year breakeven inflation rate was 2.30% and the 10-year real yield 2.65% on Tuesday (FRED). Today’s nominal fall alongside lower oil suggests some of the move came from inflation expectations; the official real-yield curve for today was not retrieved.

The United States

Governors. Democrats now hold a majority of governorships for the first time in well over a decade, according to NBC and the AP; the two describe the span differently. Projected flips include Texas, where Gina Hinojosa defeated Gov. Greg Abbott about 52.8% to 45.7%; Georgia (Keisha Lance Bottoms over Rick Jackson); and Iowa (Rob Sand over Zach Lahn). NBC also lists Ohio and Nevada as flips, which we could not confirm independently.

The House. Rep. Max Miller (R-Ohio) lost to Brian Poindexter, CNN projects; Rep. Tom Suozzi (D-N.Y.) was re-elected. Dozens of races, including California’s 5th, remain uncalled. A Trump adviser told CNN the administration does not expect to challenge results broadly and is preparing for congressional oversight; that rests on one outlet’s sources.

Courts. We found no Supreme Court action today. On Monday the court denied, without noted dissent, the administration’s emergency request to make California set aside mail ballots from people on a federal list of suspected noncitizens.

The World

Iran and the Strait of Hormuz. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says shuttle diplomacy through Qatar and Pakistan is “very intensive,” based on Iran’s seven-step plan, with no deal yet; a Doha round ended without a breakthrough (Express Tribune; Jerusalem Post). Iran’s state broadcaster says 11 tankers have caught fire in nine days, disputing US statements that the main shipping routes are clear; the Revolutionary Guards claim to have shot down a US drone over Qeshm Island. Neither claim is independently confirmed. Iranian officials said the US results show Americans reject the war. Axios reports that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu urged strikes right after the election. Market read-through: the 8% fall in oil assumes these talks succeed.

Ukraine. US, Russian and Ukrainian officials meet in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10–11, confirmed by envoy Steve Witkoff, President Volodymyr Zelensky and Moscow; Zelensky lists security guarantees, energy infrastructure, grain and prisoner exchanges and says intelligence shows Russia preparing another large strike on energy targets. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said President Vladimir Putin “has not once refused talks.” The EU will not take part.

Israel. Final results were presented to President Isaac Herzog today: Netanyahu’s bloc 53 seats, the opposition led by Gadi Eisenkot 48, Arab parties 19, with no bloc at 61. The Knesset is sworn in on Nov. 10 and Herzog’s consultations on who forms a government come next.

China. The US and China plan reciprocal tariff cuts on about $30 billion of each other’s goods after last week’s summit, the South China Morning Post reports; analysts call the truce fragile.

Europe. The EU says cyberattacks on cold-storage systems at supermarkets and blood banks in 11 countries were “not isolated” (Al Jazeera); no attribution has been made. European stocks rose about 2% on the US results.

AI & Technology

The voters and the data centers. The day’s biggest AI story was at the ballot box: about two dozen local measures restricting data centers passed, by Axios and Ballotpedia’s count, and investors sold the companies that power and equip them (see Top Stories). The read-through is uneven: Microsoft and Amazon, the largest buyers, rose 6–7%; data-center landlords Equinix and Digital Realty rose 3–4%, perhaps because existing capacity becomes scarcer if new building slows; utilities and equipment makers fell.

Chips and capacity. Bloomberg reports, as relayed by Benzinga, that TSMC’s advanced capacity is tight and Nvidia is prioritising its top chips for OpenAI and Anthropic; this rests on one outlet’s sources. The semiconductor index fell 3.1%.

Science

All five DNA and RNA bases found in asteroid samples. Researchers report finding all five nucleobases — the letters of genetic code — in pristine samples of the asteroid Ryugu returned by Japan’s Hayabusa2 mission, as reported by ScienceDaily on Nov. 3. Because the samples were sealed from Earth’s environment, contamination is less likely than in meteorites, which strengthens the case that life’s building blocks formed in space. It does not show life existed there. We have not yet seen the journal paper; one source.

Space

Electron’s 99th flight. Rocket Lab launched a radar-imaging satellite, WADATSUMI-II, from New Zealand at 5:41 AM local time on Tuesday (11:41 AM Eastern Monday), its 20th mission of 2026, according to the company. Rocket Lab shares fell 3.8% today with the space group.

This week and next. SpaceX’s classified USSF-432 launch from Vandenberg stays listed for Friday evening, and United Launch Alliance’s Vulcan returns no earlier than Nov. 13 with Amazon Leo satellites. Blue Origin is targeting a New Glenn return to flight in November with no date set.

Pennsylvania

Democrats win the state Senate and full control of Harrisburg for the first time since 1993

Democrats flipped three state Senate seats for a 26–24 majority and expanded their state House majority from 103–100 to at least 111 seats, according to CBS Philadelphia, Spotlight PA and KDKA Radio, citing AP data. With Gov. Josh Shapiro re-elected over Treasurer Stacy Garrity by about 60% to 38% (unofficial), Democrats will control the governorship and both chambers for the first time in 33 years. Ms. Garrity conceded, saying the governor’s “success is our success.” Statewide, Democrats also flipped four US House seats: Bob Harvie over Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick in the 1st, Bob Brooks over Rep. Ryan Mackenzie in the 7th, Paige Cognetti over Rep. Rob Bresnahan in the 8th and Janelle Stelson over Rep. Scott Perry in the 10th.

What changes: a governor who has repeatedly lost budget fights in a Republican Senate gets a legislature of his own party in January. Spending, transit funding, energy siting and taxes are the fights to watch in the 2027 budget.

CBS Philadelphia; Spotlight PA; KDKA Radio; WHYY; Pennsylvania Department of State (unofficial returns); the Associated Press.
