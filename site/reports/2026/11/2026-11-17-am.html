Top Stories

Nasdaq enters a correction and Asia extends the chip slide

Technology lost another 3% on Monday, and Korea’s market fell below 5,000 overnight. U.S. futures suggest a quieter open, but no recovery.

The Nasdaq Composite closed Monday at 24,509.76, down 2.49% on the day and 11.6% below its 52-week high of 27,722.75, past the conventional 10% line for a correction. The S&P 500 fell 1.92% to 7,416.59 and the Dow 1.41% to 51,608.17. The VanEck semiconductor fund (SMH) lost 5.11%. Micron fell 7.6%, Marvell 7.3%, TSMC’s U.S. shares 6.3%, ASML 5.9%, AMD 5.6% and Nvidia 4.1% to $156.14. Only staples and utilities rose (Yahoo Finance closes; AP).

Asia followed for a second day. The Kospi fell 2.59% to 4,968.48, its fourth straight loss and its first close below 5,000 since January (AP). Samsung was down about 3% early in the session. Taiwan’s Taiex lost 2.3% to 40,092 after Monday’s 6% fall, the Nikkei 1.3% to 58,860 with SoftBank, Advantest and Kioxia among the heaviest fallers, and Hong Kong about 1%. The selling followed Monday’s Commerce Department licence rule for advanced AI chips (below) and the run-up to Nvidia’s results. No single confirmed catalyst explains Asia’s session.

At about 6:27 AM, S&P futures were 0.19% lower at 7,425, Nasdaq-100 futures flat at 27,279, Dow futures down 0.47% and Russell 2000 futures down 0.52%. The VIX was 23.30, up from 19.30 on Friday.

Why it matters The sell-off is no longer one stock’s governance story. It now covers memory, equipment, foundry and networking on three continents. The equal-weight S&P fund fell only 1.01% against 1.91% for SPY, so the damage is concentrated in the largest index weights. That makes tonight’s Nvidia guidance the next hard number for the whole group.
What’s next Retail sales at 8:30 AM, then the open. Watch SMH, NVDA, TSM, MU and AMD, and whether the S&P holds above its 200-day average: SPY closed at $739.95, 1.1% above it.

Nvidia reports tonight, with an interim chief, a Treasury deadline and a 9% implied move

Colette Kress takes her first call as interim chief executive. The board faces a 10-day demand from the Treasury Secretary and conflicting signals about Jensen Huang.

Nvidia reports fiscal third-quarter results at about 4:20 PM, with its call at 5:00 PM. The company guided in August to revenue of $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, and an adjusted gross margin of about 74%. Wall Street expects about $109.0–109.2 billion and adjusted earnings of $2.47–2.49 a share (LSEG, Zacks). Options imply a move of about 8.5% (Susquehanna, via Reuters) to 9.6% (Benzinga’s calculation) either way, more than twice the average over the past eight reports. Nvidia has beaten earnings forecasts in each of the past four quarters, yet the stock fell after three of those reports.

Huang resigned as chief executive and director last week. CNBC, citing an unnamed source, reported Monday that the board is not considering his return and is focused on its review of his conduct. President Trump said Monday he was “open to” Huang coming back. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday that the board should hire an outside chief executive. He also gave the board and its outside counsel 10 days to answer whether the company’s culture encouraged “seemingly illicit sales” through shell companies that sent chips to China. These are questions from a senior official, not findings, and Nvidia has not responded publicly that we could find.

Nvidia’s quarterly holdings filing, released Monday, showed it cut its stake in Intel by about a quarter, to 161 million shares (Reuters, as reported). Intel was down about 3% before the bell after falling 7% Monday.

Why it matters Nvidia is the single largest weight in the S&P 500 and the reference point for every AI supplier in Asia. The guide for the January quarter, its comments on China and on licences under the new rule, and anything on the governance reviews will matter more than whether the quarter beats.
What’s next The release, then the 5:00 PM call. Watch the data-center number, gross margin and any statement on the board’s review or the Treasury’s questions. The closing edition will carry the first reaction.

Commerce adds licence requirements for AI chips, and Beijing calls it coercion

Advanced chips for data centres in five countries now need U.S. licences, as do overseas data centres that serve Chinese-owned firms.

The Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on Monday. It requires licences for advanced AI chip exports to Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. It also requires licences when data centres anywhere provide large amounts of computing power to companies headquartered in, or ultimately owned from, China, Macau or other restricted destinations. The threshold is reported as roughly the capacity of 750 Nvidia H100s. The rule formally rescinds the 2025 AI Diffusion Rule and takes effect when published in the Federal Register, which had not happened as of Monday night. Existing arrangements get 90 days to obtain licences, and comments are due in 60 days (CNN, TechCrunch, Bloomberg, as reported).

China’s foreign ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun called the rule “economic coercion.” In Congress, Senate Majority Leader John Thune said last week there was a “high probability” of more chip-export restrictions being added to the defence authorisation bill (Semafor). No conference text has been published.

Why it matters Southeast Asian data centres were among the fastest-growing buyers of AI chips. The rule adds friction and paperwork to that channel, which is exactly what the Treasury is now asking Nvidia about. The rule arrives two days before President Trump meets Xi Jinping in Shenzhen.

Home Depot beats on same-store sales and narrows its outlook

Comparable sales rose 2.2% on higher average tickets. Shoppers made slightly fewer trips.

Home Depot reported fiscal third-quarter sales of $43.4 billion, up 4.9%. Comparable sales rose 2.2%, ahead of the roughly 1.8% expected (StreetAccount via CNBC). Adjusted earnings were $3.86 a share against about $3.84 expected, and GAAP earnings $3.72. The average ticket rose 3.0% while transactions fell 0.5%, and the operating margin narrowed to 12.6% from 12.9% a year earlier. For the full year the company now expects sales growth of about 4.5%, the top of its prior 2.5–4.5% range, and comparable sales of about 1.4%, inside its prior range of flat to +2% (company release).

Unconfirmed CNBC’s headline also reports a change of chief executive, naming Richard McPhail. We had not confirmed that from the company’s release at press time.

Why it matters Home Depot is a clean read on housing-linked spending: existing-home sales are at their lowest since 2010, and mortgage rates track a 5% 10-year yield. Growth from higher tickets with fewer trips points to price, not volume. Lowe’s and Target report Wednesday.

Iran’s Guards claim another strike on a U.S. radar in Bahrain, and tankers are hit near Hormuz

Bahrain says it intercepted the attack. Shipping monitors logged two vessels struck, while most of the Guards’ mine claims remain unconfirmed.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said on Tuesday that they had attacked a U.S. radar at Sheikh Isa air base in Bahrain with drones, calling it retaliation for U.S. drone flights over Iran. Bahrain said the attack was intercepted, and the U.S. Embassy issued a security alert (Reuters via US News; The National). Britain’s maritime security service, UKMTO, reported that the tanker On Phoenix was hit by an unknown projectile on Monday with no injuries. It also reported an attack north of Khasab, Oman, that caused an engine-room fire, again without casualties. The Guards’ claims that two more tankers struck mines have not been matched by UKMTO, the joint maritime centre or CENTCOM. Iran’s foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, was in Muscat on Tuesday for talks with Oman on maritime security, and Qatar warned that the Bahrain attack threatens the peace talks.

Oil has not reacted much. Brent was at $92.85 at about 6:26 AM, up 0.4% from Monday, and WTI at $86.00 (Yahoo). Monday’s 3.5% drop followed a Goldman Sachs note forecasting a supply glut in 2027 and the IEA’s view that the market returns to surplus this quarter.

What’s next Washington is awaiting Iran’s answer to its proposal to end the war and reopen the strait. Tehran reportedly wants frozen assets released and sanctions relief first. A confirmed hit on a U.S. installation is the escalation path oil is not pricing.

Risks & Scenarios

Nvidia, base case: a beat with a January-quarter guide at or above about $125 billion. Probability 45–60%. Basis: four straight beats, a guide range the company has historically cleared, and consensus already sitting above the guide. Even then the shares could fall, as they did after three of the last four reports; the guide and the China commentary decide that.

Guide below about $125 billion, or new shipment risk from the licence rule or the inquiries. Probability 25–40%. Basis: the new rule hits a growing customer base, the interim leadership may guide conservatively, and Bessent’s deadline is open. Indicators: data-centre guide, gross margin, China language. If it happens: SMH and Asian suppliers extend losses, and the Nasdaq correction deepens. Invalidated by a guide above the street and explicit reassurance on licences.

Retail sales surprise firm (control group +0.6% or more). Probability 20–30%. Basis: October card-spending data were mixed, and the consensus already assumes a solid month. If it happens: the 2-year rises, half-point pricing for December grows, and rate-sensitive stocks lag. Invalidated by a flat or negative control print.

Escalation in the Gulf. A confirmed hit on a U.S. installation or a named tanker loss would put the war premium back into oil, which has fallen from $96 to about $92 since Friday on glut forecasts. Our standing estimate from Sunday, 25–40% for a confirmed U.S. strike on Iranian territory by Friday, is unchanged.

Science

The early universe. Data from the European Space Agency’s Euclid telescope identified 31 distant quasars, including the two earliest yet seen, about 670 million years after the Big Bang (as reported last week). The finding adds to evidence that supermassive black holes grew faster than standard models predict. Separately, James Webb Space Telescope observations found the earliest known pair of quasars, two black holes about 18,100 light-years apart.

Physics. Physicists at Germany’s Forschungszentrum Jülich, publishing in Nature, used an ultrafast electron camera to film electrons in graphene making transitions across energy gaps that conventional rules forbid. It is a measurement advance, of interest for future high-speed electronics, not a device.

Pittsburgh

Controller questions the pension cut in the mayor’s 2027 budget

City Controller Rachael Heisler called Mayor Corey O’Connor’s proposed 2027 budget “honest” but objected to a change in how the city funds its pensions. PublicSource reports the plan lowers the city’s pension contribution to $105 million from $112 million. The budget totals about $873 million, roughly $727 million operating and $145 million capital. It holds city taxes flat after this year’s 20% property-tax increase and draws $26–28 million from reserves; sources differ on the exact figure. It also cuts transfers to the housing and anti-violence trust funds, which Councilmember Bobby Rucker opposes. Council must adopt a budget by year-end.

Pennsylvania

Fiscal office warns of a $7 billion structural gap as the Senate returns today

The Independent Fiscal Office says Pennsylvania’s structural deficit could exceed $7 billion by 2030 without spending cuts or new recurring revenue (Capital-Star, via WESA and PhillyVoice). That is the main constraint on the incoming Democratic majority’s first budget: its agenda includes a higher minimum wage, transit funding for systems such as Pittsburgh Regional Transit, and possibly legal marijuana.

The Senate reconvenes at 1:00 PM today and the House on Thursday, for a lame-duck stretch that reportedly ends November 30. Spotlight PA says the current session is on pace for the fewest bills passed since at least 1973–74. The new session, with Democrats controlling both chambers and the governor’s office, begins January 5. Counties must certify this month’s election results by Monday, November 23.

Independent Fiscal Office (via Capital-Star, WESA, PhillyVoice) · Spotlight PA · PA NewsMedia Association (calendar).
