China pledges market support and the world rebounds: S&P futures up 1.7% for a half day

Friday, November 27, 2026 · 32 min read

A rare midday pledge from China’s cabinet to support its stock market, strong Chinese factory profits and a rebound in battered chip shares sent Shanghai up 3.7%, Korea 4.3%, silver 11% and U.S. stock futures 1.7% overnight, while softer-than-expected French and Spanish inflation eased pressure on the ECB, ahead of a 1 PM close on Wall Street and a weekend that brings OPEC+ and Spain’s election.

Top Stories

Beijing’s promise to back its market sets off a global rebound in chips, metals and stocks

A cabinet statement with no dollar figure attached did what a week of U.S. data could not: it turned Asia, then Europe, then U.S. futures sharply higher on the last trading day of a bruising week.

China’s State Council, the cabinet, issued a statement around midday Beijing time on Friday pledging to strengthen the role of the “national team” of state-backed investors and to channel long-term funds into stocks; reports say it told Central Huijin, the sovereign investment arm, to ensure liquidity (Bloomberg, Reuters via Investing.com). Analysts noted that it set no amount, target or timetable. The market did not wait: the Shanghai Composite closed up 3.73% at 3,606.49, Hong Kong’s Hang Seng rose 2.11% to 22,694.77 and its tech index 6.45%, led by chipmaker SMIC, up about 16% (The Standard). One market-news site puts the CSI semiconductor index up 17.6%, a figure we could not check against a second source.

The same morning, the statistics bureau reported that industrial profits rose 20.1% in October from a year earlier, up from 14.4% in September and the fastest since May; profits for January to October were up 16.0%, with high-tech manufacturing up about 51% and electronics, which the bureau tied to demand for AI memory, roughly doubling (CNBC). Car makers’ profits fell.

The rebound spread through the region’s battered chip trade. Korea’s Kospi rose 4.34% to 4,915.68, with Samsung Electronics up 4.6% and SK Hynix 5.4% (KED Global), and Japan’s Nikkei 2.11% to 58,711.11, with Advantest up 7%. Taiwan’s Taiex lagged, up 0.40%, and India fell about 1%. The Stoxx 600 was up 1.0% at 6:23 AM, led by technology and miners (Yahoo Finance; ADVFN).

U.S. futures followed: at 6:27 AM, S&P 500 futures were up 1.74% from Wednesday’s settlement and Nasdaq-100 futures 2.32%, about 1.2% and 1.9% above where they stood at Thursday’s 1 PM holiday halt (Yahoo Finance, delayed). Forbes reported Nvidia up about 4% before the bell; regular premarket quotes were not yet posted when we checked. Even with today’s gain, the Nasdaq-100 is down about 8% for November, on course for its worst month since December 2022 (Forbes). Futures are not a guaranteed open.

Why it matters. The week’s sell-off was about AI-hardware valuations and the debt behind them, not about the U.S. economy, which keeps running hot enough for a Fed hike. A policy promise in Beijing changes neither. The size and speed of the moves in chips and metals look like positions being unwound on a holiday-thinned session; that is our inference, not a confirmed cause.

What’s next. Whether China puts money and a number behind the pledge, and whether the rebound survives a full U.S. session; today’s is three and a half hours. Next week brings Marvell, CrowdStrike and Salesforce results and Nvidia’s chief executive in front of Congress.

Silver jumps 11% and gold 4.8% as metals rebound from last week’s lows

The biggest one-day gains for gold and copper since January, by one count, came with no central-bank news and little move in the dollar.

At 6:28 AM, December Comex gold was at $3,704.40, up 4.77% from Wednesday’s settlement, silver at $44.49, up 11.07%, and copper at $5.68 a pound, up 6.45% (Yahoo Finance, delayed). Gold had fallen to a 15-month low near $3,425 on Wednesday, and is still about a third below its 52-week high of $5,586. Market reports cite a rebound after the sell-off, short covering, the rally in stocks, a slightly softer dollar and China’s profits data (Investing.com, ADVFN); one Canadian report called the gold and copper moves the largest one-day gains since January. The ICE dollar index was little changed at 103.81, and the yuan was firmer at 6.64 per dollar.

Why it matters. Gold rising alongside stocks and against falling bond prices is not a fear trade; it is a squeeze. That makes it more fragile than it looks. Miners, Freeport-McMoRan and Newmont, and the silver and gold funds open with the most to gain and the most to give back.

Inflation in France and Spain falls far more than expected, easing the case for an ECB hike

Lower fuel prices pulled both countries’ November rates down by more than half a point, and Spanish core inflation fell too.

France’s harmonised inflation rate fell to 3.2% in November from 3.7%, against 3.6% expected; the national measure fell to 2.9% from 3.4%, and prices fell 0.7% on the month (Bloomberg, XTB). Spain’s flash rate fell to 4.1% from 4.9%, against 4.7% expected, and core inflation to 2.6% from 3.1%, with fuel the main driver (XTB, investinglive). In Germany, seasonally adjusted unemployment rose by 9,000 to 3.025 million, more than the 4,000 expected, with the rate steady at 6.4% (Reuters).

The ECB’s October account, published Thursday, showed broad support for that month’s rate increase and a view that another “would likely prove necessary”; euro-area inflation was 4.0% in October. Friday’s numbers are national and preliminary, and the euro-area flash estimate comes next week. Commentators say they raise the bar for a December hike. The euro barely moved, at $1.0964.

Why it matters. If Europe’s inflation turns before America’s, the ECB can stop while the Fed is still raising rates, which tends to support the dollar against the euro and pressure European bank shares that have benefited from higher rates.

A projectile hits Aramco’s Jazan refinery, killing a worker, as Gulf attacks continue

Saudi Arabia’s energy ministry confirmed the damage and Saudi state media blamed the Houthis; separately, Britain’s maritime agency confirmed a tanker was struck off Oman.

An aerial projectile hit Saudi Aramco’s Jazan refinery on the Red Sea coast on Thursday, killing one worker, injuring several and temporarily disrupting operations, Saudi Arabia’s energy ministry said; Saudi state media blamed the Houthis (Al Jazeera, Gulf News). The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said an outbound tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 18 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman, at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz; all crew were accounted for and there was no pollution, and neither the ship nor the attacker was named (BOE Report, citing UKMTO). The Revolutionary Guards’ separate claims that tankers struck mines remain unconfirmed. Earlier Houthi claims of a strike on Ras Tanura are still unverified.

WTI crude was at $85.40 at 6:28 AM, up 2.1% from Wednesday’s settlement. Brent quotes vary with the contract month: the February contract was at $92.10 (Yahoo Finance), while front-month January was reported near $97.66 earlier this morning (CNBC, Saxo), on course for its first weekly gain in about four weeks. Natural gas rose 9.2%.

What’s next. OPEC+ meets on Sunday to set 2027 baselines; delegates are reported to be weighing a rollover of current quotas, with a capacity audit running late (The Private Banker, CNBC). Any confirmed loss of Saudi refining or Gulf export capacity would matter more for prices than the meeting.

Altria confirms it may bid for Imperial Brands, and Japan Tobacco is circling

A possible all-cash takeover of the maker of Winston and Blu would be one of the largest tobacco deals in years; Imperial says it has received no approach.

Altria said on Thursday it is at a “very early stage” of considering a possible offer for Imperial Brands, likely in cash, and is talking with an unnamed third party about buying part of Imperial’s business; Deutsche Bank and Perella Weinberg advise it (Reuters via Investing.com, Tobacco Journal). Under UK takeover rules Altria must make a firm offer or walk away by 5 PM London time on December 24. Imperial said it had received no approach; its shares rose as much as 17.6% to 3,562 pence on Thursday and closed up about 13%. Bloomberg reports Japan Tobacco is evaluating a bid and has considered teaming up with Altria. A reported $30 billion value has not been confirmed. Altria’s U.S. shares (MO) trade today for the first time since the news; they closed Wednesday at $76.28.

The United States

The storm’s aftermath. The Thanksgiving nor’easter cut power to more than 900,000 customers at its Thursday-evening peak, and more than 700,000 were still without power early Friday, with Pennsylvania and New Jersey hardest hit (Washington Post, CNN). New Jersey declared a statewide emergency. Flooding and surge closed Virginia’s Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel and the Chincoteague Causeway, and Maryland’s WSSC asked about 1.9 million water customers to conserve because outages threatened its two filtration plants (CBS News, NBC News). We found no confirmed storm deaths. The Macy’s parade, postponed for the first time in its history, now steps off Saturday at 8:30 AM.

The president on Iran. In Thanksgiving calls to troops from Mar-a-Lago, President Trump said “we have a victory at hand” and that Tehran is still being offered a negotiated settlement; he said that if the United States strikes again it would target “Pickaxe Mountain,” a suspected enrichment site, and that no new attack has been ordered (Washington Times, Al Jazeera). Science

The Riemann claim, a week on. The Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative said on November 21 that Levent Alpöge’s formal proof, about 17 million lines produced with Anthropic’s Claude models, compiles against Mathlib’s standard statement of the Riemann Hypothesis under several independent checkers, and MIT’s Andrew Sutherland and others have compiled it independently (Lean community blog, Nature). Scientific American reports independent mathematicians calling it “almost certainly true”; Terence Tao’s preliminary view is that it “appears to use the full power” of modern Langlands-program and arithmetic-geometry methods. What is still missing is a human-readable account of the argument, and the Clay Mathematics Institute has not changed the problem’s status.

Climate history. A paper in Science argues that the Laurentide ice sheet began to re-expand about 2,000 years ago in an incipient glaciation that later warming halted (ScienceDaily); other researchers describe the idea as contested.

Alzheimer’s. Nature reports that Cognito Therapeutics’ 40-hertz light-and-sound headset missed its primary endpoint in the 673-person HOPE trial; the company points to a prespecified subgroup with milder disease, a secondary finding that is not peer-reviewed (Nature, NeurologyLive).

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Council floats higher parking rates as the city budget heads to a December 17 vote

At Tuesday’s budget hearing with the Pittsburgh Parking Authority, Budget Chair Erika Strassburger said many council members would favour higher rates if they were targeted, and “dynamic pricing” was raised; the authority’s executive director, David Onorato, warned about how customers would react. Nothing was proposed or decided (WPXI). The background is Mayor Corey O’Connor’s roughly $872 million plan, which has no tax increase but draws on reserves and lowers the city’s pension contributions; an actuarial report suggests extending or raising the parking-tax allocation to the pension fund to compensate (TribLive, WESA). Reported figures for the reserve draw and the pension cut differ by outlet. The final vote is December 17.

Allegheny County Council must pass County Executive Sara Innamorato’s 2027 budget, about $1.25 billion in operating spending with no tax increase, by December 6 (WESA, TribLive).

WPXI, WESA, TribLive. Via search summaries.

Bridgeville · South Fayette: no new decisions this week. The borough’s proposed 2027 budget and the millage ordinance are open for inspection, and council votes on both on Monday, December 14, at 7 PM (borough notice).
