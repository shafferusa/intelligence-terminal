Today’s News

SpaceX catches a Starship upper stage with its launch tower for the first time

Ship 42 came back from orbit on Saturday afternoon and was caught by the tower arms at Starbase. The booster was lost in the Gulf the night before, so a full two-stage recovery is still to come.

About 22.5 hours after Friday’s 5:47 PM ET launch, Ship 42 re-entered over the Gulf of Mexico and was caught by the “chopstick” arms of the tower at Starbase, Texas, at about 4:20 PM ET on Saturday (about 3:20 PM local time). It is the first time any Starship upper stage has been recovered intact, and the first catch of a stage returning from orbit (CNBC, CGTN, Teslarati and several spaceflight outlets, all through search summaries). The ship had released 26 Starlink V3 satellites on Friday.

The booster half of the flight did not go to plan. Super Heavy Booster 22 aborted its own tower return on Friday night and came down in the Gulf. Accounts of why still differ: some outlets describe an engine failure before the no-catch call, others a low-propellant alarm. SpaceX has not published its own account. So Flight 15 recovered the stage that had never been recovered before, and lost the stage SpaceX had caught several times.

Why it matters Catching the ship is the step SpaceX’s cost case has been waiting for: a fully reusable rocket needs both stages back. Reusing a caught ship, and catching both stages on one flight, are the next milestones; neither has happened. NASA’s Artemis III lander is a Starship. SpaceX (SPCX) closed Friday at $139.11, up 3.6% on the week, before the catch; Monday’s open is the first market read. A booster lost on a flight that was supposed to show the V3 design returning is the unresolved part.

Bitcoin dips below $63,000 overnight, its lowest since August, before recovering to about $65,000

The only market trading this weekend kept falling into the Fed and IPO week. Liquidations of leveraged long positions passed $1 billion again.

Bitcoin was $65,186 at about 9:20 AM ET on Sunday, down 1.9% over 24 hours, and ether $1,822, down 2.7% (CoinGecko, live). Several crypto outlets report that bitcoin traded as low as roughly $62,000–63,400 between Saturday and early Sunday, the lowest since August, before bouncing. The whole crypto market was worth about $2.23 trillion, down 4.9% in a day, with bitcoin 58.7% of it. Reported 24-hour liquidations range from $1.2 billion to $2 billion depending on the window, most of them long positions (CoinGlass data as cited). Spot bitcoin funds had their seventh straight day of outflows on Friday, according to SoSoValue figures cited in the reports.

The explanations on offer are the same as last week: rising odds of Fed hikes and real yields near 2.9%. One outlet adds that investors are raising cash for Anthropic’s IPO. None of these is proven, and we found no network or regulatory event behind the fall. Bitcoin has fallen more than 20% since late October; its weekly close tonight decides whether it is a sixth straight weekly loss.

Iran claims a second drone attack on U.S. forces in Bahrain in three days; the U.S. says nothing was hit

Both sides’ accounts conflict again. The naval blockade of Iranian ports continues, and the White House is still signalling restraint.

Disputed The Revolutionary Guards said on Sunday that six drones targeted a U.S. air-defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base in Bahrain, calling it retaliation for U.S. actions. U.S. Central Command says it intercepted two drones with no injuries or damage, and Bahrain’s military says its air defences intercepted several Iranian attacks. It follows Friday’s claim that Iran destroyed a U.S. radar in Bahrain, which CENTCOM called false (Al Jazeera, CNN, Latin Times). Iran’s foreign ministry calls the strikes “purely defensive.” CENTCOM says it is still enforcing the blockade.

The President said over the weekend that he is considering holding off on further attacks unless there are new provocations, the Japan Times reports. Mediators are working on a framework under which Iran would stop attacks in the strait and the U.S. would lift the blockade (AP via PBS). None of the mine claims of the past two days, five ships by Tehran’s count, has been independently confirmed.

What’s next Oil opens tonight with Brent at $95.84. Last week it looked through most mine claims. An attack the U.S. confirms, or a U.S. response, would test that; our scorecard says we have been pricing small strikes too low.

Vulcan tries again at 12:11 PM today after two days of nitrogen-plant trouble

The return to flight carries Amazon’s first 40 Leo satellites on a United Launch Alliance rocket.

ULA moved the launch from Saturday to Sunday after a power interruption at Air Liquide’s nitrogen plant knocked out the high-pressure pumps that supply the pad, the same problem that scrubbed Friday’s attempt. ULA says the rocket and the 40 satellites are healthy. The window is 12:11–12:40 PM ET from Space Launch Complex 41 at Cape Canaveral (ULA mission page, Spaceflight Now). It is Vulcan’s first flight since a solid-booster nozzle problem in February, and the start of a 38-launch Amazon contract. Monday’s paper carries the result.

Top Themes

1. Rates do the tightening. Last week the 10-year real yield rose 28 basis points to 2.91% while inflation expectations stayed flat at 2.24%. That is tighter money, not an inflation scare, and it came before the Fed has moved at all. This week tests whether the economy is feeling it. Retail sales on Tuesday and housing starts on Wednesday are the first October readings on spending and building since yields went above 5%; Home Depot, Lowe’s, Target and Walmart report around them. Existing-home sales already fell to their lowest annual rate since 2010.

2. Nvidia without its founder. The company is the market’s single largest weight and has just lost the chief executive who built it. Tuesday’s numbers matter less than Kress’s guidance, what she says about China and the Justice Department inquiry into Megaspeed, and whether other executives are leaving. Last week the rest of the chip industry rose while Nvidia fell. A good Nvidia report could reverse that rotation; a bad one tests whether it spreads.

3. How fast the Fed hikes. A December 9 hike is close to fully priced, with about a 10% chance of half a point. Wednesday’s minutes cover the October meeting, when Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland’s Beth Hammack voted to hike. Any sign that more members favoured moving then, or discussed a 50 basis-point step, would push the half-point odds higher. Warsh speaks on Monday; he has said he dislikes forward guidance, so markets will read data more than speeches.

4. The biggest AI listing. Anthropic plans to price 825 million shares at $70–78 on Wednesday and trade on Thursday as ANT, at a valuation of up to about $1.4 trillion. The order book is reported to be several times covered. A strong debut would say public investors will still pay for AI at scale; a weak one, coming two days after Nvidia, would say the opposite. (This newspaper is written by an Anthropic model and holds the company to the same standard as any other.)

5. Shenzhen. Trump and Xi meet on Thursday, and Trump, Xi and Putin meet together. The White House says the subject is nuclear arms and that chip export controls are not on the table. Congress, after Nvidia, wants tighter curbs. Rare earths and the tariff truce are the trade items. Any line on chips moves semiconductors; any line on Iran moves oil.

The Economy Ahead

Retail sales (Tuesday, 8:30 AM). September sales rose 0.8% and 0.9% excluding cars. For October, published forecasts cluster around +0.2–0.3% for the headline, with the “control group” that feeds GDP at about +0.4% (Newsquawk survey and economic calendars, as reported). These are nominal figures. With consumer prices up 0.4% in October, a 0.3% gain would mean real spending fell slightly. A headline at or below zero would be the first clear sign that higher rates and $90-plus oil are reaching the shopper, and would likely pull Treasury yields down from last week’s highs. A gain of 0.6% or more would back the Fed’s hawks. Watch the gasoline-station line, which inflates the headline when fuel is expensive, and revisions to September.

Housing starts (Wednesday, 8:30 AM). Starts ran at a 1.40 million annual rate in September. Mortgage rates rose again last week with the 10-year yield, and existing-home sales fell to 3.75 million, the lowest since 2010. Permits, which lead starts, are the number to watch.

The Fed. No meeting until December 8–9. The target range is 3.75–4.00%. Wednesday’s minutes cover the October 27–28 meeting, at which the committee held and two members dissented in favour of a hike. The questions are how many others leaned that way, whether a 50 basis-point move was discussed, and what the staff said about energy-driven inflation. Speakers: Chair Warsh opens a conference at noon Monday; Williams speaks Monday and Tuesday; Musalem, Hammack, Barr and Kashkari are listed for Monday; Waller on Tuesday and Friday per one calendar; Goolsbee and Barkin on Thursday. Schedules differ across sources. Last week every official who spoke leaned toward tightening; a dissenting dovish voice would be news. What we report here is official policy and officials’ stated views. The roughly 90% December hike odds are market pricing (CME FedWatch, as reported), not guidance.

The Treasury. The refunding’s $125 billion of notes and bonds, all of which tailed, settles on Monday. Bills this week total $277 billion across 6-, 13- and 26-week maturities, followed by the 20-year bond and the 10-year reopening. The 20-year is where demand has been weakest: its par yield, 5.52%, sits above the 30-year’s 5.47%. Treasury Secretary Bessent last week asked the Fed not to overreact to what he called an energy shock. Japan says it is coordinating with the Treasury on the yen.

Central banks abroad. Japan’s GDP tonight is expected to show annualised growth slowing to about 0.3% from 1.4%, with consumption falling for the first time in seven quarters (Reuters poll of 16 economists). The yen is at 160 per dollar. Finance Minister Katayama called the moves “one-sided and rapid” on Friday. Analysts cited by Reuters see about 163 as a likelier trigger for action. A weak GDP makes a December Bank of Japan hike harder and the yen weaker. China’s October data tonight are forecast at about 0.7% annual retail-sales growth and 5.5% for industrial output (FXStreet preview), and its loan prime rates, unchanged for 17 months, are set on Friday. UK inflation on Wednesday follows the Bank of England’s November hike to 4.00%. The Reserve Bank of Australia publishes minutes on Tuesday. One report has ECB President Lagarde saying only “significant changes” would stop a December ECB hike; we have still not been able to read the speech.

Earnings & Business

Nvidia (Tuesday after the close; call 5 PM). Fiscal third quarter, ending late October. The company guided to $108 billion of revenue, plus or minus 2%. Consensus is about $109.2 billion and $2.48 a share (Benzinga Pro, LSEG via IG); Finnhub’s figures are higher, at $111.2 billion and $2.52, a reminder that estimates vary by provider. Options imply a move of about 9.1% in either direction (Benzinga). Four things matter more than the beat: Kress’s fourth-quarter guidance; the gross margin, which previews say is under pressure from memory and packaging costs; anything on China, where Nvidia says its data-centre sales are already near zero; and leadership, including whether the board names a search timetable. On the Megaspeed inquiry, reports say U.S. officials suspect a former Megaspeed executive of working for China’s intelligence service and that the Singapore company bought about $4.8 billion of Nvidia hardware over two years. Unverified Those are allegations reported by the press; Nvidia says it found no evidence of chips diverted through Megaspeed, and the board’s inquiry has published nothing. House committee chairs have asked Huang for a closed-door interview on December 1, and the Senate Banking chair has asked him and Commerce Secretary Lutnick to testify on December 3.

Anthropic’s IPO. 825 million Class A shares at $70–78, led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley with 20 other banks. Credit. The high-yield spread was 3.20 percentage points on Thursday and the investment-grade spread 0.89, both tighter than a week earlier while Treasuries sold off (ICE BofA via FRED). Scenarios

Each is measured by where the S&P 500 closes on Friday, Nov. 20, against Friday’s 7,561.67. The ranges sum to roughly 100%. After last week’s lesson, the base case is wider than in earlier editions.

Base case — within 2.5% either way (7,373–7,751): 45–55%. Conditions: Nvidia near guidance with cautious China language; retail sales between 0% and +0.6%; minutes hawkish but no surprise; Anthropic prices at or above the range. What would change the outlook. A clear turn in the consumer: weak retail sales confirmed by Walmart and Target cutting forecasts would change the Fed debate from how fast to whether. On the upside, a Hormuz framework that reopens shipping would lower oil and inflation expectations together. The minutes matter less than the data around them this week.
