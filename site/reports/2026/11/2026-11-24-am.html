Top Stories

Futures slip after the best day for U.S. stocks since April 2025, while Asia stages a record-sized catch-up

Taiwan and Korea did overnight what Wall Street did on Monday. The question for today is whether Monday was a turn, or a rebound that sellers will use.

At 6:31 AM, December S&P 500 futures were at 7,190.75, down 0.88% from Monday’s settlement, and Nasdaq-100 futures at 26,352, down 1.70% (Yahoo Finance, delayed). Dow futures were down 0.45% and Russell 2000 futures 0.45%. Outlets reported milder figures earlier in the night, and CNBC described chip stocks as giving back part of Monday’s gains. There is no single confirmed catalyst: Monday’s move was among the largest of the decade, and a partial retracement after a jump of that size is common. Futures are not a guaranteed open.

Monday’s session was a broad rally, not only a Nvidia rally. The S&P 500 rose 4.73% to 7,237.17 and ended a seven-day losing streak; the Nasdaq Composite rose 6.51% to 24,089.56, the Dow 3.01% to 49,595.31 and the Russell 2000 3.40% to 2,669.66 (Yahoo Finance closes). In our whole-market count, 4,712 liquid U.S. stocks rose and 902 fell, more than five to one, and up volume was 4.6 times down volume. That is the mirror image of Friday, when decliners outnumbered advancers four and a half to one. The equal-weight S&P 500 fund RSP rose 2.59%, about half the cap-weighted SPY’s 4.71%, so the largest companies still did most of the index work. Only 26.8% of stocks closed above their 50-day average, up from 21.2%.

Overnight, Asia’s chip-heavy markets made up Monday in one session. Taiwan’s Taiex closed at 40,122.35, up about 9.0%, with TSMC and MediaTek up nearly 10%, close to the daily limit; Korea’s Kospi rose 6.32% to 5,006.03, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each up about 6.7% (AP; Yahoo Finance closes). Japan’s Nikkei rose 3.91% to 58,644.63 after Monday’s holiday. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng fell 0.73% to 22,219.65, near its 52-week low, and Shanghai rose 0.27%. In Europe, the Stoxx 600 was down 0.45% at 6:26 AM after rising 1.7% on Monday; Germany’s DAX was down about 0.8% and London’s FTSE 100 0.25%.

Why it matters One-day rallies of 4–5% almost always come during sell-offs, not in calm markets, which is why they say little on their own. Two things will say more: whether the VIX, still near 29, keeps falling, and whether small caps and the equal-weight index keep pace on a down day. Monday’s breadth was far stronger than any day last week; one more day of it would be a sign that selling is exhausted rather than paused.
What’s next Consumer confidence at 10 AM, the 5-year auction at 1 PM, and Dell after the close. Wednesday’s PCE inflation report at 8:30 AM is the last major data before the Fed’s December 9 decision, then markets close Thursday for Thanksgiving and at 1 PM on Friday.

Go deeper

Monday’s leaders were the stocks that led the slide: the semiconductor fund SMH rose 15.0%, Micron 19.0%, TSMC’s U.S. shares 14.1% and Tesla 11.5%. Technology was the best sector (+8.97%); energy (−1.06%) and consumer staples (−0.09%) were the only losers, as oil fell and investors left defensive shelters. Treasuries barely moved: the 2-year and 10-year par yields were unchanged at 4.63% and 4.92%, a sign the rally was about stocks’ own positioning rather than a change in the rates outlook.

The VIX term structure tells the same story from another angle. On Monday the 9-day VIX fell from 40.72 to 27.76 and the VIX to 26.62, but both remained above the 3-month VIX at 25.50: near-term protection still costs more than longer-dated cover, which is what an unsettled market looks like. A return to the usual shape, with the 3-month above the spot index, would be the signal that traders have stopped paying up for this week.

Nvidia falls about 5% before the open, a day after its record gain on Huang’s return

The board’s reinstatement settled who runs the company, not the questions about chips reaching China. Those now move to Treasury, the SEC and three congressional committees.

Nvidia’s board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Monday, eleven days after he resigned (PBS NewsHour, Fortune, The Hill). The stock rose 31.48% to $165.05, adding roughly $950 billion of market value, the largest one-day gain for any company on record by outlets’ counts. In early premarket trade it was at $156.85, down about 5% (Yahoo Finance, 6:41 AM); CNBC reported it down more than 3%. One early report put the fall at more than 8% and tied it to possible leadership changes and tighter chip controls; we could not confirm that account.

President Trump called the reinstatement the “correct and proper decision.” Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who had urged the board to look outside the company, said: “I said [Nvidia] should go to the outside, but [Huang is] on the outside now” (Fortune). Treasury’s ten-day request for answers from the board and its outside counsel on how Nvidia chips reached China through shell companies ends Wednesday, November 25 (Politico, as reported). Senate Banking Committee Chair Tim Scott has called Huang as the sole witness at a 10 AM hearing on December 3 on export-control oversight (The Hill, Investing.com), and Huang and general counsel Tim Teter are to sit for closed-door interviews with three House committees on December 1–2 (NBC News). Democrats including House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries called the board’s decision “indefensible” and say they will investigate the White House’s and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s roles; Republican Sen. Pete Ricketts said the reinstatement does not excuse Huang from answering national-security questions.

The legal track continues: the Wall Street Journal and Reuters have reported an SEC civil inquiry, which the SEC has not confirmed, and California’s attorney general served Nvidia with a subpoena on November 19. Investing.com reports that the Megaspeed export-control investigation has become a criminal one; that is one outlet’s account. On Monday the Commerce Department blacklisted six Southeast Asian companies tied to Megaspeed and an Alibaba unit in Singapore (see AI & Technology). Analysts moved quickly: Citi raised its target to $240, Melius and Bernstein upgraded the stock, and Stifel reiterated Buy (aggregators).

Why it matters As one of the S&P 500’s largest companies, Nvidia now sets the index’s direction on many days. With the leadership question settled, the risk has moved from the boardroom to Washington: Wednesday’s Treasury deadline and the hearings next week are the dates on which new facts could arrive.

Sanofi agrees to buy Insmed for about $41 billion in cash

Outlets call it the largest pharmaceutical deal in three years. It buys a newly approved lung-disease drug, and pays a 46% premium for it.

Sanofi will pay $175 a share in cash for Insmed, about $41.0 billion, a premium of about 46% to Insmed’s price on November 18, before reports of talks, and about 17% to Monday’s close (Insmed’s filing as reported; Bloomberg, CNBC, RTÉ). The deal brings Sanofi Brinsupri, Insmed’s treatment for bronchiectasis, a chronic lung disease, and Arikayce, for a rare lung infection. It needs Insmed shareholders’ and antitrust approval. Insmed shares were reported up about 14% before the open.

It is the second large takeover in two days, after CrowdStrike’s all-stock agreement on Sunday to buy Zscaler for about $43.9 billion. Zscaler closed Monday at $229.24 (+17.7%) and CrowdStrike at $208.97 (−8.9%); at CrowdStrike’s close, the offer of 1.135 CrowdStrike shares is worth about $237, so Zscaler trades about 3.5% below it, which prices in some risk the deal is blocked or changed.

HPE beats estimates but falls after hours on server margins; Dollar Tree raises its forecast

The first results after Monday’s rally show the same split as the AI trade: strong demand, and costs that eat into it.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise reported adjusted earnings of $1.43 a share for its fiscal fourth quarter, against about $1.27 expected, on revenue of about $14.5 billion against $14.35 billion, announced a $5 billion buyback and guided fiscal 2027 earnings to $5.05–5.35 a share, above the $4.77 consensus (Investing.com, TipRanks, Chartmill). But its adjusted gross margin fell 2.2 points from the prior quarter to 38.2%, and the shares, which had risen 17.4% on Monday to $48.02, fell about 7% after hours. One outlet says the company warned that memory shortages will squeeze server margins through 2028; we could not confirm that from a second source. Agilent beat on earnings and revenue and guided fiscal 2027 revenue to $8.00–8.15 billion; its shares rose about 7–8% after hours. Amentum beat on earnings, missed on revenue, which fell 8%, and announced its first buyback.

This morning, Dollar Tree reported third-quarter revenue up 6.9% to $5.1 billion, same-store sales up 4.6% with traffic up 3.8%, and adjusted earnings of $1.16 a share, including a 42-cent tariff hit, against about 90 cents expected; it raised its full-year adjusted earnings forecast to $8.05–8.25 (company release). Medtronic reported revenue of $9.36 billion, up 6.5% on an organic basis but short of the roughly $9.48 billion expected, with adjusted earnings of $1.34 a share, a cent above estimates; it reaffirmed its full-year guidance and announced the purchase of Contego Medical (company release). Best Buy, Analog Devices, Dick’s, Burlington, Kohl’s and Smucker had not reported when we went to press.

What’s next Dell reports after the close, against estimates of about $6.75 a share on $50.3 billion of revenue (Finnhub). The stock rose 20.5% on Monday to $334.91, so expectations are high. Its AI-server orders and margins are the read-through for the whole hardware trade, and HPE’s margin squeeze is the warning.

Williams says rates will likely rise “over time” as $70 billion of 5-year notes go on sale

Bond markets were calm through Monday’s stock rally. Today’s auction and Wednesday’s PCE report are the two tests before the Fed’s quiet period.

New York Fed President John Williams said on Monday that inflation remains too high and that higher rates are likely “over time,” with the pace set by the data; he did not rule out increases at consecutive meetings, and expects inflation of about 3.5% at year-end (Reuters via Kitco, CNBC). Markets price about a 95% chance of a quarter-point increase on December 9, which would take the federal funds target to 4.00–4.25% from 3.75–4.00% (CME FedWatch as cited by outlets; we did not check CME directly). The Fed’s pre-meeting blackout begins Saturday.

Treasury’s official curve barely moved on Monday: the 2-year was unchanged at 4.63%, the 10-year at 4.92% and the 30-year fell 2 basis points to 5.29%, while 3-month bills rose 5 basis points to 4.37% as the December hike was priced in (Treasury). The 10-year breakeven inflation rate eased to 2.13% (FRED). This morning, CNBC has the 10-year near 4.91%, the 2-year near 4.66% and the 30-year near 5.29%. Monday’s $69 billion 2-year auction cleared at 4.700% with a bid-to-cover of 2.65, a little below the prior 4.708% and in line with market levels (TreasuryDirect); one report puts indirect bidders at 51.5% of the sale, which we could not confirm elsewhere.

Today the Treasury sells $70 billion of 5-year notes and $28 billion of 2-year floating-rate notes at 1 PM, then $44 billion of 7-year notes on Wednesday. The 5-year par yield was 4.74% on Monday. A result well above that, a “tail,” would suggest buyers want more compensation to hold duration a day before the inflation data.

Why it matters Wednesday’s core PCE is expected to rise about 0.3% for October, or about 3.2–3.3% from a year earlier (compiled calendars). The Fed is already expected to raise rates; the question the data answer is whether it raises by more, or keeps going in January. Fed Governor Christopher Waller has floated a half-point move.

Iran says three tankers hit mines in Hormuz as Washington widens sanctions; oil keeps falling

Neither the mine claims nor a reported tanker convoy has been independently confirmed. Oil is pricing more supply, not more danger.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said three “offending” tankers struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz; CNBC reported the claim, which says the ships were under U.S. escort. We found no independent confirmation, no ship names and no comment from the U.S. Navy, so the claim stands as Iran’s. Last week a similar Guards claim of tankers hit by mines was also never confirmed. Separately, the Treasury widened its Iran sanctions on Monday to target exchange houses and overseas trading firms that handle payments for Iranian oil and petrochemicals (Iran International, Benzinga), and Secretary Bessent said Iran has “no oil on the water,” an assertion we cannot test.

Reports last week of 14 or more tankers gathering off Kharg Island to run the U.S. blockade are disputed: one report says Iran’s Supreme National Security Council halted the sailing, and Iranian state media say no convoy has left. The ship-tracking firm Windward counts at least 31 laden sanctioned tankers already outside the strait (The National, RFE/RL). On Monday Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said regional talks “must be given a chance,” that Iran sees no need to meet the U.S. now, and that Qatari and Pakistani mediators are working on Iran’s terms, which include an end to the blockade and to sanctions (Press TV, Iranian state media). Iran attacked U.S. positions in Bahrain on Saturday night; Bahrain says it intercepted several drones and missiles and that a civilian industrial site caught fire, with no casualties.

Brent crude was about $87.10 at 6:31 AM and U.S. crude about $80 (Yahoo Finance, delayed), down from about $89.40 and $80.17 on Monday; Brent is down about a quarter from its late-October peak. Analysts quoted by Gulf News cite recovering Libyan and Russian supply and surplus forecasts. OPEC+ meets on November 29 to set 2027 output policy.

Zelenskyy confirms Ukrainian sea drones sank a Russian warship in Libya

The strike on the corvette Merkury carries Ukraine’s naval-drone campaign across the Mediterranean. Overnight Russia sent 96 drones at Ukraine.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Monday that seven Ukrainian naval drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi early on Sunday and destroyed a Ka-27 helicopter; Libya’s eastern government confirmed a sea-drone strike on a docked warship, and the security firm Ambrey said some of the blasts were likely onboard ammunition (CNBC, ABC News, Defense News, RFE/RL, CBC). The Kremlin said only that it had “taken note.” Ukraine also says it hit the sanctioned cargo ship Angara; that has not been verified.

Overnight, Ukraine’s air force says Russia launched 96 drones, including 64 Shahed-type, and no missiles, and that 73 were brought down. Injuries in Zaporizhzhia have risen to about 32, including four children, by local officials’ count, and a drone damaged a restaurant near Khreshchatyk in central Kyiv (Ukrainian outlets; figures not independently confirmed). Last week’s mass strike sent two Russian missiles into Poland; NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte called it a “grave violation.” We found no new round of peace talks.

Before the Open

Futures and what they price. At 6:31 AM, S&P 500 futures were at 7,190.75 (−0.88%), Nasdaq-100 futures at 26,352 (−1.70%), Dow futures at 49,455 (−0.45%) and Russell 2000 futures at 2,662.2 (−0.45%) (Yahoo Finance, delayed). The pattern matters more than the size: the Nasdaq is giving back twice as much as the Dow and small caps, so this is profit-taking concentrated in Monday’s winners, Nvidia and the chip stocks, rather than a broad reversal. That is consistent with Monday’s breadth, which was strongest outside the megacaps. Futures are not a guaranteed open; last week a 1.3% morning gain turned into a 2% loss.

The curve. On Monday’s official Treasury curve the 3-month bill yielded 4.37%, the 2-year 4.63%, the 5-year 4.74%, the 10-year 4.92% and the 30-year 5.29%. The 2s10s spread is 29 basis points and the 3-month–10-year spread 55, both positive: the curve is upward-sloping even with a hike priced, which says markets see the Fed’s tightening as limited and inflation as persistent. Bills did the moving on Monday (+5 basis points on the 3-month), long bonds did not.

The dollar. At 104.01 the dollar index is near the top of its 52-week range (95.55–104.53), supported by a Fed that is raising rates while the ECB and others are not. A strong dollar tightens conditions for emerging markets; the EEM fund nonetheless rose 7.4% on Monday, led by Taiwan and Korea.

Volatility. The VIX was 28.76 at 6:26 AM, up from 26.62. On Monday’s close the term structure was still inverted, 9-day 27.76 above the VIX and the 3-month 25.50 below it. Inverted VIX curves usually persist for days after a sell-off, and they make large moves in either direction more likely.

Oil and gold. Brent is about $87, down from $89.40, and U.S. crude about $80, despite Iran’s claims of mined tankers. The market is reading more supply ahead, not more disruption. Gold, at $3,628, is down 35% from its 52-week high and fell with stocks during last week’s sell-off, so it has not been working as a haven.

Credit. The high-yield spread was 3.76 percentage points on November 20 (FRED), up 53 basis points in a week; Monday’s reading is not yet published. HYG rose only 0.25% on Monday against a 4.7% gain in stocks. Credit has not confirmed the equity rebound yet, and that is worth watching more than the futures.

Bitcoin. Near $59,850, close to its 52-week low of about $55,150, and falling on a day stocks rose. Crypto has not participated in the relief.

The most fragile assumption. That the sell-off was mostly about Nvidia’s governance and that, with Huang back, the AI trade can resume. What would invalidate it: Dell’s results tonight showing AI-server margins under the same pressure as HPE’s, or new facts from Treasury’s Wednesday deadline or the SEC about how Nvidia chips reached China. Either would put the market back on fundamentals and legal risk rather than personalities.

The Economy

Today’s data. The Conference Board’s November consumer-confidence index at 10 AM is the first sentiment reading taken partly during last week’s sell-off; the Richmond Fed’s manufacturing survey comes at the same time, and the S&P Case-Shiller and FHFA house-price indexes for September at 9 AM. We did not find reliable consensus figures for these.

Inflation and the Fed. October CPI rose 0.35% from September on the headline index and 0.30% on core, both in level terms well above the Fed’s 2% pace (BLS via FRED). Wednesday’s PCE index, the Fed’s preferred measure, is expected to show core prices up about 0.3% for the month and about 3.2–3.3% from a year earlier. Williams’s forecast of about 3.5% inflation at year-end is the headline rate, not core. The labor market is not the constraint: unemployment was 4.1% in October and initial jobless claims 203,000 in the week to November 14 (FRED). The Fed raised rates in September to 3.75–4.00%; the October minutes showed almost all officials expecting further increases, a couple by more than a quarter point.

Treasury. This week’s $183 billion of 2-, 5- and 7-year notes settles November 30. The 2-year sold smoothly on Monday at 4.700%. Government funding lapses after December 11; the House has passed three of the twelve full-year spending bills and the Senate none, so a stopgap is the likely outcome, but Democrats Chris Van Hollen and Tim Kaine want it to bar money for the Iran war and Majority Leader John Thune calls that “not a realistic ask.” Congress returns November 30.

Real yields and inflation expectations. The 10-year TIPS yield was 2.77% on November 20 and the 10-year breakeven 2.13% on Monday (FRED). Real yields near 2.8% are high by the standard of the past fifteen years, and they are the main reason long-duration growth stocks have been so sensitive to every Fed signal.

Global central banks. New Zealand’s central bank has no November decision; its next is December 9, the same day as the Fed’s, and UOB expects a quarter-point increase to 3.00% after a 4–3 vote to hold in October. The Bank of Japan meets December 17–18; 61 of 71 economists in a Reuters poll expect an increase to 1.50%. ECB President Christine Lagarde’s speech last week was titled “Acting in time”; the ECB publishes minutes of its last meeting this week.

President Trump signed a memorandum on Monday directing the Justice Department to seek the death penalty in federal murders of children and in murders tied to rape, child exploitation, kidnapping or trafficking, and to speed up existing federal executions (White House). Attorney General Todd Blanche said the department has sought the death penalty 49 times under this administration. Supporters say it gives the gravest crimes the gravest penalty; opponents, including death-penalty abolition groups, have long argued it does not deter and risks executing the innocent.

Contractors and Congress. Friday’s executive order requires federal contractors to tell the White House about congressional requests for information and to follow its instructions on privilege, or risk losing their contracts (CNN, Federal News Network). The White House calls it a defence against “congressional overreach”; Rep. Jamie Raskin calls it “unprecedented and blatantly unlawful,” and critics say it may conflict with whistleblower law. No lawsuit has been filed that we could find.

Courts. We found no new Supreme Court orders on Monday. An appeals court lifted a pause, so the Army plans to detonate degraded explosives at an Iowa ammunition plant on Wednesday, over environmental groups’ concerns about lead contamination (CNN).

Florida. About 500 people have been rescued in central and south Florida after storms on November 17–21, and the state has asked for another major disaster declaration (Tampa Free Press).

AI & Technology

Chip controls tighten. The Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security blacklisted an Alibaba unit in Singapore and six Southeast Asian companies tied to Megaspeed, the chip buyer at the centre of the Nvidia affair, over suspected diversion of chips to China, and added licence requirements for advanced chips sent to Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand (South China Morning Post, ChinaTechNews). Megaspeed says it will pursue “all legal avenues.” Fox News reports that Bessent clashed with Lutnick over Commerce not acting on Megaspeed sooner. A report that Samsung and TSMC paused some shipments to China rests on one outlet. Market read-through: Southeast Asian data-centre demand for Nvidia, AMD and server makers such as Dell and Supermicro faces more paperwork; the size of the hit is not known.

Models. Newsletters report OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 rolling out more widely in ChatGPT, with GPT-5.6 to be retired December 23; we have not seen that on OpenAI’s own pages, so treat it as unconfirmed.

Cyber. Ransomware groups have posted claims of breaches at Samsung (November 22) and Nvidia (November 18) on their leak sites. Neither company has confirmed a breach, and criminal claims are often exaggerated.

Science

Meaning across languages. A brain-imaging study in Nature Neuroscience scanned speakers of 21 languages and reports a shared component in how the brain represents meaning across all of them (journal listing). It is a peer-reviewed study of comprehension in the brain, not of how languages arise, and it does not settle the debate over whether thought depends on language.

AI for swollen lymph nodes. A study in Nature Communications reports an AI model that reads ultrasound images to help diagnose unexplained swollen lymph nodes. It is peer-reviewed; it has not been tested in routine clinical use.

Space

Ariane 6 and Amazon Leo. An Ariane 64 lifted off from Kourou at 6:14 AM Eastern with 36 Amazon Leo internet satellites, a day after a scrub for a ground-equipment fault (Launch Library 2, ESA). Results of the satellite deployment were not in when we went to press. It is Amazon’s first launch since ULA’s Vulcan failure on November 15 destroyed about 40 Leo satellites. Vulcan remains grounded; its preliminary review points to an inadequate fuel supply to its BE-4 engines, and the Space Force has suspended its national-security launches.

Space station. Russia’s Progress MS-36 cargo ship launched from Baikonur at 5:17 AM Eastern and is due to dock on Wednesday; it is Russia’s last station cargo flight of the year (Launch Library 2, RussianSpaceWeb).

Shenzhou-24. China launches Tang Hongbo, Wu Yi and Pakistan’s Muhammad Zeeshan Ali to its Tiangong station at about 7:18 PM Eastern tonight. Ali would be the first foreign astronaut to visit Tiangong; he is to stay about a week (AP).

Also today. A Falcon 9 launches the USSF-266 national-security mission from Vandenberg at about 11:08 AM Eastern. Rocket Lab’s Electron flies a Synspective radar satellite on Thursday evening. Rocket Lab rose 9.3% and AST SpaceMobile 10.3% on Monday.

Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania records a ninth measles death as cases pass 1,600

The state has recorded its ninth measles death this year, with 1,638 cases across 46 counties and 330 hospitalisations (Philadelphia Inquirer, Al Jazeera). The national count from the CDC was 5,838 cases as of November 19, the most since 1991; the CDC lists six deaths nationwide, fewer than the states’ own tallies, a gap that reflects reporting lags. The United States lost its measles-elimination status this month. The Allegheny County Health Department is the place to check for local exposure notices and vaccination clinics.

Separately, counties had until Monday to certify the November 3 results to the Department of State, and any recounts must finish by noon today. We found no statewide report yet on whether every county certified on time. In Lancaster County a judge approved a recount of the 41st House District, where the Democrat leads by 28 votes; it does not affect control of the House (one newsletter report).

PRT shuts the Wabash Tunnel after November 30 and plans changes at Carnegie station

Pittsburgh Regional Transit will decommission the Wabash Tunnel after service ends November 30, and plans a virtual public meeting early next month on improvements to its Carnegie station and park-and-ride on the West Busway, a few miles from Bridgeville (PRT, WPXI). The county council must pass the 2027 budget, which has no tax increase, by December 6. Pittsburgh International expects more than 300,000 passengers over the ten-day Thanksgiving period (WESA).
