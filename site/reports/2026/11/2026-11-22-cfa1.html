1 · Refresher: business cycles, money, trade and exchange rates

The thread through all four (Lesson 6 has more).

Cycles come from postponable spending. Households delay cars and houses, firms delay investment, so those swing most (pp.138–142). Unemployment lags the turn, and mild inflation is preferred because deflation raises the real weight of debts (pp.145–154).

Banks create money by lending. One divided by the reserve requirement caps deposit creation; it is a ceiling, not a forecast (pp.174–176). M × V = P × Y always holds, and only surprise inflation shifts wealth from lenders to borrowers (pp.178–183).

Trade follows opportunity cost, and the balance follows saving. A country exports what it gives up least to make (pp.194–199), and its current account equals private plus government saving minus investment (pp.204–206).

A cheaper currency helps only under two conditions. Trade volumes must respond enough (Marshall–Lerner, p.224), and income must rise relative to spending (pp.226–227). Expect a J-curve: worse first.

2 · Today: what financial reporting is for, and the four statements

Module 1 answers one question: how do outsiders who lend to or invest in a company learn what it owns, owes and earns, and why can they trust the numbers?

The information gap. Managers know far more about the business than the shareholders and lenders who fund it. Reports bridge that gap. Their objective is decision usefulness: helping capital providers decide whether to supply equity, debt or credit, and judge how well management has looked after their money (pp.4–5). Standards narrow management’s choices but never remove judgment.

Setters write, regulators enforce. Private bodies, the IASB (IFRS) and the FASB (US GAAP), write the standards. Legal authorities such as the SEC decide which standards apply, enforce them, and can overrule the setters. A standard has force only where a regulator adopts it (pp.5–7).

Everything in the framework serves decision usefulness. Two fundamental qualities: relevance (it could change a decision; materiality is part of it) and faithful representation (complete, neutral, free from error). Four enhancing qualities: comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability. Two constraints: benefits must justify the cost, and the enhancing qualities trade off against each other with no fixed ranking. An estimate can make a number more relevant yet less verifiable (pp.7–9).

Asset
a resource the company controls as a result of past events (p.10)
Liability
a present obligation to transfer a resource (p.10)
Equity
what is left for owners: assets minus liabilities (p.10)
Income, expenses
increases and decreases in economic benefits, excluding owners putting money in or taking it out (p.10)
Fair value
the price you would get to sell an asset (or pay to transfer a liability) today: an exit price, not what was paid (pp.11–12)

Two assumptions sit under every number. Accrual: record a sale when it is earned and a cost when it is incurred, not when cash moves, so profit and cash differ. Going concern: the firm will keep operating. If liquidation were expected, assets would be valued at fire-sale prices and the statements would look very different (pp.10–11).

Everyday picture

A landscaper does December work and is paid in January. Under accrual the profit belongs to December; the bank balance only catches up in January.

Measurement drives the balance sheet. Items may be carried at historical cost, amortised cost, current cost, realizable or settlement value, present value or fair value (pp.11–12). Book equity is the residual at those bases, not what the market says the company is worth.

Four statements, four lenses. The balance sheet is a snapshot at one date: resources and claims, for liquidity and solvency (pp.13–16). The income statement is a flow over a period: performance (pp.17–19). The statement of changes in equity rolls owners’ claims from opening to closing (pp.21–22). The cash flow statement sorts cash into operating, investing and financing, and is harder to manage than accruals (pp.22–24).

Consolidation. A parent that controls a subsidiary (presumed above 50% of votes) includes 100% of its lines, then shows the outside owners’ share as noncontrolling interest (p.18). Other comprehensive income holds equity changes that skip profit, such as currency translation and pension remeasurements, so net income can hide swings in equity (pp.20–21).

IFRS statements also share general features: fair presentation, going concern, accrual basis, materiality, no offsetting, comparative figures and consistent presentation. The book lists them only in the Summary.

Assets = Liabilities + Equity  ·  Comprehensive income = Net income + OCI

Ending equity
opening equity + comprehensive income + new capital − dividends and buybacks, in currency units (pp.21–22)
Ending cash
beginning cash + CFO + CFI + CFF + exchange-rate effect (pp.23–24)
Basic EPS
profit to ordinary shareholders ÷ weighted-average ordinary shares, currency per share; diluted EPS assumes options and convertibles are exercised (p.19)
Assumptions and exceptions
  • The term is faithful representation, sometimes called reliability; understandability is enhancing, not a constraint.
  • Going concern fails if liquidation is likely, and the values change with it.
Memorize
  • Fundamental: relevance, faithful representation. Enhancing: comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability
  • Constraints: cost versus benefit; trade-offs among enhancing qualities
  • Assets = Liabilities + Equity
  • Comprehensive income = Net income + OCI
  • Balance sheet is a stock at a date; the other three cover a period
