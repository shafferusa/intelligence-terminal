Nvidia soars 31% after its board brings back Jensen Huang, and the S&P 500 has its best day since April 2025

Monday, November 23, 2026 · 35 min read

Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive and the stock added about $950 billion in a day, carrying the S&P 500 up 4.7% and the Nasdaq 6.5%, ending a seven-day losing streak and taking the S&P back out of correction. Treasury yields barely moved, the VIX fell by a quarter, and oil fell to near its August lows.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The tape. The S&P 500 rose 4.73% to 7,237.17, its best day since April 2025 and the end of a seven-session losing streak. The Nasdaq Composite rose 6.51% to 24,089.56, the Dow 3.01% (1,448 points) to 49,595.31 and the Russell 2000 3.40%. The S&P has recovered about half of last week’s fall and is no longer in a correction, CNBC reports, but it is still 2.4% below its close of a week ago.
  • Nvidia. The board unanimously reinstated Huang as president and chief executive, eleven days after he resigned. The stock rose 31.48% to $165.05 on 772 million shares, which outlets say added a record of about $950 billion in market value. Micron rose 19%, Dell 20%, Marvell 18%, TSMC 14% and the chip fund SMH 15%.
  • Rates, the Fed and the Treasury. On the official curve, the 2- and 10-year yields were unchanged at 4.63% and 4.92%, while bills rose about 5 basis points. The $69 billion 2-year auction sold at 4.700%, slightly below the pre-sale market yield, a solid result. New York Fed President John Williams said the Fed must act “over time” to get inflation back to 2%. A quarter-point rise on December 9 is still priced at about 93%.
  • Biggest risk. The rebound was narrow. The equal-weight S&P rose 2.6%, little more than half as much as the S&P 500. High-yield spreads widened to 3.76 points on Friday, their widest of the sell-off, and the VIX, though down 26% to 26.62, is still far above its 50-day average of about 19. Wednesday’s inflation data now matters more, not less.
  • Tomorrow. Consumer confidence at 10 AM, $70 billion of 5-year notes at 1 PM, and Dell, HP, Workday and Autodesk after the close. Best Buy, Medtronic and Analog Devices report before the open; several calendars put Dick’s, Burlington and Kohl’s there too. After the bell today, HP Enterprise beat forecasts and raised its outlook but slipped in late trading.
  • Beyond markets. Ukraine says its sea drones sank a Russian corvette at Benghazi in Libya, and NATO held an emergency meeting after Poland concluded that all three objects that fell on its territory on Friday were Russian cruise missiles. Senators of both parties pressed for investigations of Nvidia’s handling of export controls. COP31 ended without its main climate agreement. In Pennsylvania, counties faced today’s deadline to certify the November 3 results.

Top Stories

Nvidia’s board reinstates Jensen Huang, and the stock adds about $950 billion in a day

A week ago the board said it did not intend to bring him back. Today it said it was satisfied with his “candor and ability to lead”, and the rest of the AI trade went up with Nvidia.

In a statement, Nvidia said its board had voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as president, chief executive and director, after meetings with him, and that it was “satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang’s candor and ability to lead” (Nvidia investor-relations release, as reported by PBS and Al Jazeera). Huang resigned on November 12 after reports of a relationship with a subordinate; on November 17 the board had said it did not intend to reinstate him (Global News). On Sunday night President Trump had urged the board on social media to bring him back. Today he wrote that the board had “made the correct and proper decision” (NPR).

Nvidia closed at $165.05, up $39.52 or 31.48%, on 772 million shares, more than four times its average (Yahoo Finance). It traded as high as $171.50, up about 37%. Outlets put the gain in market value at about $950 billion, the largest one-day gain for any company on record; the previous record, by NBC’s and Kiplinger’s accounts, was about $450 billion. It was Nvidia’s biggest percentage gain since March 2000, one outlet says. The stock is now 5.7% above where it closed a week ago, before the worst of the sell-off, but still 32% below its 52-week high of $243.37.

What has not changed: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday the administration still wants answers on how Nvidia chips reached China and how much the company knew, and his ten-day request runs out around Wednesday (CBS, Fortune). The Wall Street Journal and Reuters have reported an early-stage SEC inquiry, and California’s attorney general served Nvidia with a subpoena on November 19. Democrats in Congress said they would pursue the national-security questions raised by the New York Times’ report on the woman at the centre of the affair (Global News). We found no new filing or statement on any of those inquiries today.

Why it matters Nvidia is one of the largest weights in the S&P 500, and today it accounted for a large share of the index’s gain on its own. The return of its founder removes one uncertainty, the leadership vacuum, but not the regulatory and legal ones that came with his departure. The stock now trades on those headlines as much as on its record $115.8 billion in quarterly revenue.
What’s next Bessent’s deadline, any word from the SEC or California, and Dell’s results on Tuesday evening, the first big read on AI server demand since the sell-off began.

The S&P 500 ends its losing streak with a 4.7% gain, but the average stock rose about half as much

It was the best day since April 2025. Breadth was positive but far less striking than the index, and credit markets moved little.

The S&P 500 rose 326.78 points to 7,237.17, the Nasdaq Composite 1,471.82 points to 24,089.56 and the Dow 1,447.98 points to 49,595.31 (AP; CNBC; Yahoo Finance). CNBC, Yahoo and NBC all describe it as the best day since April 2025. The S&P 500 and the Dow are now less than 10% below their records, so CNBC says they are no longer in a correction; the Nasdaq Composite is still 13.1% below its high on our data. One count has 422 of the 503 S&P members up on the day, and nine of eleven sectors.

The breadth was real but uneven. Technology rose 9.0% and communications 3.6%, while energy fell 1.1% and consumer staples were flat. The equal-weight S&P fund rose 2.59% against SPY’s 4.71%, and the Russell 2000 3.4%. SPY traded 86 million shares against an average of 49 million, a heavy but not panicky day. Last week the gap ran the other way: on Friday the equal-weight fund fell half as much as the index. So the weeks’ moves have been dominated by the same handful of very large companies, in both directions.

Why it matters This morning we put a 72–85% chance on the streak ending and a 40–55% chance on a 2% gain; the market did more than double that. A single company’s news did most of it. A recovery that broadens, with small caps and the equal-weight index catching up, would be a better sign that last week’s selling is over than another day like this one.

Two-year notes sell smoothly at 4.70%, as Williams says the Fed must keep acting “over time”

The curve barely moved on a day stocks jumped. The Fed’s second-ranking official signalled more rate rises, but nothing dramatic.

Treasury sold $69 billion of 2-year notes at a high yield of 4.700%, a fraction below the 4.703% at which they traded just before the sale, with a bid-to-cover of 2.65 against a recent average of 2.63 (InvestingLive, Helious; we have not seen Treasury’s own results page). Foreign and other indirect bidders took 51.5%, below their average of about 56.5%, and direct bidders 38.1%. The last 2-year sale was at 4.708%. On Treasury’s official closing curve, the 2-year was unchanged at 4.63% and the 10-year unchanged at 4.92%; bills rose, with the 3-month up 5 basis points to 4.37% and the 6-month 7 to 4.45%. Market quotes late in the day had the 2-year a few basis points higher.

In prepared remarks in New York, Williams said: “We must take actions over time that will ensure that we get inflation back to our 2% goal in a timely manner and on a sustained basis” (Bloomberg; Reuters-based reports). He expects inflation of about 3.5% at the end of this year, close to 2% next year and at target in 2028, and said he will weigh the data before the December 8–9 meeting. Yahoo Finance read the speech as ruling out a half-point rise in December. CME FedWatch put a quarter-point rise at about 93%, from about 96% this morning, according to one aggregator.

Why it matters Stocks recovered half of last week’s fall, but bonds kept almost all of Friday’s rally. Bond investors are not treating today as a reason to expect a more aggressive Fed. The test is Wednesday’s October PCE inflation, expected at 0.3% on the month for the core measure, along with the 5- and 7-year auctions.

CrowdStrike falls 9% on its Zscaler deal as Zscaler jumps 18%

Because the deal is paid in stock, CrowdStrike’s fall cut the offer’s value. Zscaler now trades about 3% below it.

CrowdStrike closed at $208.97, down 8.92%, on 42 million shares, four times its average. Zscaler closed at $229.24, up 17.67%, after rising as much as 27% early (Yahoo Finance; Motley Fool). At 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per Zscaler share, the offer is now worth about $237, against $260 at Friday’s prices, and Zscaler trades about 3.5% below it. That gap reflects the time to closing, expected in the second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal 2028, and the risk that shareholders or antitrust authorities stop it. Benzinga estimates CrowdStrike lost about $22 billion in market value. Rivals rose: Fortinet +5.3%, Cloudflare +5.9% and Palo Alto Networks +2.9%.

The Economy

Today’s data. S&P Global’s flash November surveys showed services slowing to 54.7, a four-month low, from 56.1 and below forecasts of about 55.6; manufacturing was 54.5, as expected, from 54.9; the composite fell to 54.6 from 55.9, its weakest since July (S&P Global, as reported by Newsquawk and InvestingLive). Readings above 50 still mean growth. Chris Williamson of S&P Global said the surveys point to growth of about 3% in the fourth quarter and that price measures remain consistent with inflation well above 2%. The survey ran November 12–20, so it caught most of last week’s market fall. The Chicago Fed’s national activity index for October was −0.06, from a revised +0.05, with its three-month average at −0.05: growth slightly below trend (Chicago Fed, as reported).

The Fed. Williams’ remarks are in Top Stories. He is the most senior voice yet to endorse the path the market expects: a quarter-point rise on December 9 and more later if inflation does not slow. Governors Waller and Cook argued last week for more rises, Waller with a half-point floated. The policy range is 3.75–4.00%; the pre-meeting blackout begins after Saturday. The United States

Nvidia and Washington. Nvidia said its decision followed “input from senior levels of the United States government”, and the president said he had “convinced” the board (NBC, The Hill). The reaction in Congress crossed party lines. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Democrats would use their “full power” to investigate, Sen. Elizabeth Warren said Nvidia still owes Congress answers, and Republican Sen. John Cornyn asked publicly whether there was “no national security risk” (Axios, The Hill). Senate Banking Committee Chair Tim Scott has called Huang to a public hearing on export controls on December 3 (TNW, CoinDesk). One outlet reports a December 1 appearance before three House panels, which we could not confirm.

Spending. Appropriators met without agreement, and a stopgap into the new year looks likely before funding lapses at 12:01 AM on December 12 (Deseret News, Latin Times). Democrats want current spending levels and no new Iran-war money; Republicans want the war funded. The House has passed three of the twelve spending bills and the Senate none. Federal News Network reports the $350 billion party-line defence bill is “dead”, though a smaller package is possible. Democrats take control of both chambers on January 3.

The White House. The president told the Army to hold off on detonating unstable drums of lead azide at an Iowa ammunition plant, planned for Wednesday, until a federal appeals court and Iowa’s governor-elect, Rob Sand, agree (CNN, NBC). He was due to speak at a Justice Department event this afternoon that NBC says targets California Gov. Gavin Newsom’s plan to commute the state’s death sentences. Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence, is acting national cyber director after Sean Cairncross’s departure (NBC).

Courts and lawsuits. Oregon and Virginia sued over sexual deepfakes, including of children, made with the Grok chatbot, the first states to do so under consumer-protection law; X says users deliberately got around its safeguards (CNBC). The Supreme Court sat today; we found no notable order.

The World

Ukraine strikes a Russian warship in Libya. President Zelenskyy said Ukrainian sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury at the naval base in Benghazi early on Sunday. Two Libyan officials said it was “severely damaged,” and a commercial satellite image shows it capsized (AP, CNBC, NBC). The Kremlin referred questions to its defence ministry. The UN mission in Libya called for an independent investigation, and the Tripoli government demanded an explanation from Kyiv. It is, by these accounts, the farthest from home Ukraine has struck Russia’s navy.

NATO meets on Poland. The North Atlantic Council held an emergency session in Brussels after Polish prosecutors said all three objects that came down in Poland on Friday were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, not two missiles and a drone as first thought (Washington Times, Kyiv Post). Prime Minister Donald Tusk called it a deliberate provocation; NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte said the alliance would defend “every inch” and that intent is still being assessed. NATO is sending an AWACS surveillance aircraft to Poland. We found no outcome of the meeting at press time.

The Gulf. At least three more ships were hit by projectiles near Hormuz over the weekend, the fastest pace of attacks since the war began on February 28; the UK’s maritime agency counts 106 incidents in that time (CNN, Al Jazeera, Maritime Executive). Iran’s navy says one tanker hit a mine. Bahrain says two people were hurt when Saturday’s attack hit an industrial site; U.S. Central Command says the drones were intercepted, and neither government confirms Iran’s claim to have hit Patriot radars (CNBC, Gulf News). Iran’s foreign minister says June’s Islamabad memorandum no longer operates but Iran is open to a broader deal if the U.S. blockade is lifted. Oil fell anyway (Top Stories).

Climate talks fail. The COP31 conference in Antalya ended on Sunday without adopting its main agreement, the first U.N. climate summit without one since Copenhagen in 2009. Saudi Arabia and other oil producers opposed language on fossil fuels, and the EU-led bloc rejected a text without it (CNN, CBS, Al Jazeera). A pledge on electrification by more than 90 countries survived.

Elsewhere. The Saudi-led coalition struck Yemen’s Hodeidah port on Friday, and three Houthi missiles aimed at Riyadh were intercepted on Saturday (Al Jazeera, France24). Taiwan’s military reported ten Chinese balloons between Friday and Sunday, eight over the main island (TaiwanPlus, single source).

AI & Technology

The AI trade returns. Nvidia’s reinstatement leads the paper. Its suppliers and customers rose with it, and so did the AI-power names: Vertiv +12.1%, Quanta +8.9%, Eaton +8.0%, Constellation +6.0%. Of the large platforms, Meta (+6.6%) and Alphabet (+6.3%) rose most and Microsoft (+1.7%) least.

Chips and export rules. The Commerce Department’s November 16 rule requires a licence to ship advanced chips to any data centre, anywhere, that supplies large amounts of computing power to companies headquartered in China or other restricted countries, adds licence requirements for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and rescinds the earlier AI diffusion rule (TechCrunch; Paul Hastings). Sources differ on when it takes effect. TSMC and Samsung have paused some Chinese orders for chips at 16 nanometres and below pending licences (Reuters, FT, as reported). The president’s Friday statement that U.S. companies will not be allowed to build data centres abroad still has no mechanism or text.

Security. A ransomware group listed Nvidia on its leak site on November 18 and threatens to publish data on November 28. It has shown no proof, and Nvidia says it is investigating an incident that “may have affected a third-party partner” (BleepingComputer).

Deals. AMD and Saudi Arabia’s HUMAIN announced an AI-infrastructure partnership worth up to $10 billion, and Deutsche Telekom committed €100 million to Mistral (lightly sourced, via newsletters). We found no major model release today.

Science

Blood pressure from a watch. Google researchers report in Nature an AI model that flags signs of hypertension from a smartwatch’s standard sensors, without a cuff. It missed about one case in three, so it is a screening aid at best, not a diagnosis (as reported by Fox News).

The Amazon sheds its leaves. A University of Hong Kong team led by Jin Wu reports in Nature that leaf-shedding is widespread across the Amazon, and that modelling it improves estimates of the forest’s seasonal photosynthesis over most of the basin (Phys.org, single source). That matters for how much carbon models assume the forest absorbs in dry seasons.

Space

Ariane 6 slips a day. Today’s countdown for 36 Amazon Leo satellites stopped on a ground-equipment “technical event” before the gantry was rolled back (Arianespace, ESA). The new attempt is Tuesday, in a window opening at 5:45 AM Eastern (Space.com). It would be Amazon’s first launch since the Vulcan failure on November 15 destroyed 40 of its satellites.

This week. China’s Shenzhou 24 crew is due to launch from Jiuquan at 7:18 PM Eastern on Tuesday; a Pakistani astronaut may fly a one-week visit, and the crew is named the day before (NextSpaceflight). Vulcan remains grounded with no return date, and Blue Origin’s New Glenn is aiming to return in early January (Spaceflight Now, Satnews).

Space stocks rebounded: SpaceX (SPCX) +4.4% to $133.35, Rocket Lab +9.3%, AST SpaceMobile +10.3%, Viasat +8.5%, Redwire +13.6%.

Also in the News

Measles. The CDC counts 5,838 confirmed cases this year as of November 19, the most since 1991, and six deaths; other tallies are higher. Its next update is expected later this week (CDC via Reuters).
