Chip stocks give back Monday’s surge as Nvidia falls 6%, while the Dow climbs back above 50,000

Tuesday, November 24, 2026 · 29 min read

Money rotated out of Monday’s winners and into almost everything else: the S&P 500 slipped 0.5% and the Nasdaq 1.5% as semiconductors fell 7%, but the Dow rose 0.85%, the equal-weight S&P gained, retailers jumped on strong results, consumer confidence leapt to its highest since January 2025, and Dell’s after-hours beat sets up Wednesday’s inflation report.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The tape. The S&P 500 fell 0.50% to 7,201.09 and the Nasdaq Composite 1.48% to 23,733.54, while the Dow rose 0.85% to 50,018.70 (CNBC data). Underneath, more stocks rose than fell: the equal-weight S&P fund gained 0.54% and the Russell 2000 fund 0.53%, and eight of the ten sector funds we track rose. Only technology fell, by 3.3%.
  • Chips and Nvidia. Nvidia fell 5.86% to $155.38, having been down about 9% at its low. Micron fell 11.0%, Marvell 9.1%, AMD 8.9% and the chip fund SMH 7.3%. Analog Devices fell 17.4% despite record revenue, and HP Enterprise 16.3% on its margin outlook. Anthropic fell 6.5% to $81.91, below its $83 offering price for the first time at a close.
  • Rates, the Fed and the Treasury. The $70 billion 5-year auction sold at 4.719%, a fraction of a basis point above where it traded before the sale. Foreign and other indirect buyers took 54.3%, a low share. The 5-year par yield fell 5 basis points to 4.69%, and the 2- and 10-year were steady at 4.63% and 4.91%. A December 9 rate rise stays priced at about 95%.
  • Data and earnings. Conference Board consumer confidence jumped to 103.3 from 91.9, far above the roughly 92 expected. Kohl’s rose 19.5%, Dick’s 14.6% and Dollar Tree 11.2% on their results. After the bell Dell reported revenue of about $54.9 billion against roughly $50 billion expected and raised its outlook; its shares rose 3–4% late, after falling 11.8% in the session.
  • Biggest risk. Wednesday’s 8:30 AM data: October core PCE inflation, expected at 0.3% for the month and 3.2% from a year earlier, plus revised GDP, jobless claims and durable goods. Markets then close Thursday for Thanksgiving.
  • Beyond markets. Reuters reports the SEC’s Nvidia inquiry is looking at possible accounting issues; the company has not confirmed it. Iran’s army chief called for tightening the Hormuz closure, and the U.S. is investigating suspected cyberattacks on dozens of tankers. Ariane 6 put all 36 Amazon Leo satellites into orbit. In Pennsylvania, a recount confirmed the Democrat’s 30-vote win in House District 41.

Top Stories

Chip stocks give back a large part of Monday’s rally, and the rest of the market quietly rises

The S&P 500 fell because its biggest members fell. The average stock, small caps and the Dow all went up, the reverse of Monday’s pattern.

The S&P 500 closed down 36.08 points, or 0.50%, at 7,201.09, the Nasdaq Composite down 356.02 points, or 1.48%, at 23,733.54, and the Dow up 423.39 points, or 0.85%, at 50,018.70, according to CNBC data reported at 4:15 PM; one outlet had a slightly lower S&P close of 7,198.33. The Dow’s close is back above 50,000, still about 8.6% below its 52-week high. Stocks opened lower, as futures had signalled, and the selling in chips deepened into late morning: at about 11:30 AM the S&P was down about 1.2% and the Nasdaq about 2.5%, while the Dow was roughly flat. Both recovered from their lows into the close.

The split was stark. The chip fund SMH fell 7.26% and the technology sector fund 3.26%, giving back about half of Monday’s gains. Yet the equal-weight S&P 500 fund rose 0.54%, the Russell 2000 fund 0.53%, health care 2.95%, consumer staples 1.66%, energy 0.97% and financials 0.83% (Finnhub closes). On Monday the cap-weighted index rose nearly twice as much as the average stock; today the average stock did better than the index. Reuters described investors retreating from AI-linked shares while weighing the political and governance questions around Jensen Huang’s return at Nvidia.

Why it matters This morning the worry was that Monday was only a relief rally. Today’s pattern is a better sign than the headline suggests: selling was concentrated in the stocks that had jumped most, while the rest of the market held its gains and added to them. A rally that broadens beyond a handful of AI names is the healthier kind, though one day does not make a trend.
What’s next Wednesday’s PCE inflation report at 8:30 AM, then a 7-year auction at 1 PM and a close on Thursday for Thanksgiving. Friday is a 1 PM close.

Nvidia falls 5.9% as Reuters reports the SEC is examining possible accounting issues

The stock touched about $150.50 before steadying. Analysts called Huang’s return “not a clearing event”, and Treasury’s deadline for answers from the board lands Wednesday.

Nvidia closed at $155.38, down $9.67, after trading as low as $150.50 (Finnhub). That leaves it about 24% above where it was before the board reinstated Huang on Monday. Reuters, citing two people briefed on the matter, reports that the SEC inquiry centres on possible accounting issues; the Wall Street Journal has described it as concerning whether disclosures misled investors. Neither the SEC nor Nvidia has confirmed an investigation, and no wrongdoing has been established. Goldman Sachs analysts wrote that Huang’s return is “not a clearing event”; Raymond James said Nvidia “does not get a do-over” (Investing.com, as reported).

Nvidia has said it will create a chief compliance officer post and a board committee on national security and export controls. In Washington, Sen. Elizabeth Warren called the reinstatement “reckless”, and Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said the White House’s involvement “underscores the need for Congress to act”; Republican Sen. Pete Ricketts said it does not excuse Huang from testifying (CNBC, The Hill, NBC News). President Trump said he had assured the board that Huang was not a national-security risk. Huang is due before three House committees in closed session from December 1 and the Senate Banking Committee on December 3. We found no word on Treasury’s ten-day request to the board, which runs out around Wednesday.

Dell beats estimates by a wide margin and raises its outlook after a 12% fall in the session

Its shares had fallen with HP Enterprise and the chip stocks during the day. The results suggest AI-server demand is still running ahead of forecasts.

Dell reported third-quarter revenue of about $54.9 billion against about $49–50 billion expected, and adjusted earnings of $9.45 a share against about $6.6–6.75. It guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $56.3 billion and adjusted earnings to $8.40 a share, and raised its full-year earnings outlook to about $29.68 (Reuters via AOL; Investing.com). The shares rose about 3–4% after hours, recovering part of the 11.83% regular-session fall to $295.28. Investing.com notes concerns about shrinking gross margins, the same worry that took HPE down today.

HP Enterprise fell 16.31% to $40.19, its first full session after results that beat estimates but showed a lower gross margin. Of tonight’s other reports, only one outlet’s summary was available to us: it has HP Inc. beating on earnings and revenue, and Workday and Autodesk beating but trading about 4% lower after hours on their outlooks. We could not confirm those from a second source.

Why it matters Dell is the largest seller of AI servers outside the chipmakers. A beat of this size is evidence that the spending behind the AI trade has not slowed; HPE’s fall is the reminder that margins, not demand, are what investors are now testing.

Consumer confidence jumps to its highest since January 2025

The Conference Board’s index rose more than 11 points, the opposite of what forecasters expected after last week’s market fall.

The Conference Board’s index rose to 103.3 in November from a revised 91.9, against forecasts of about 92. The expectations measure rose 9.9 points to 83.6, its first reading above 80 this year, and the present-situation measure 13.7 points to 132.8. Consumers’ expected inflation over the next twelve months eased for a second month, to 5.7%. The Conference Board linked the improvement to lower uncertainty after the midterm elections (Conference Board release; Advisor Perspectives; TheStreet).

Other data were firmer too. The Richmond Fed’s manufacturing index rose to +3 from −2, its first positive reading since August, with new orders back above zero (Reuters table; InvestingLive). House prices are rising slowly: the S&P Case-Shiller national index was 2.1% higher in September than a year earlier, and the FHFA index was flat for the month and up 2.5% for the year (HousingWire; FHFA).

Why it matters Stronger confidence supports spending into the holidays, which is good for retailers and growth, but it also gives the Fed less reason to hold back. It is one more reading that points to a December rate rise, not a pause.

Five-year notes sell close to market levels, but foreign demand is thin

The auction cleared 0.3 basis point above its pre-sale yield. Direct bidders took a record share as indirect bidders stepped back.

Treasury sold $70 billion of 5-year notes at a high yield of 4.719%, with a bid-to-cover ratio of 2.37 (TreasuryDirect). The yield was 0.3 basis point above the 4.716% at which the notes traded just before the sale, a small “tail”; indirect bidders, a proxy for foreign demand, took 54.3%, which InvestingLive and ZeroHedge describe as the lowest since March 2020, while direct bidders took a record 36.7% and dealers 9.0%. ZeroHedge called it ugly; Helious called it in line. Treasury also sold $28 billion of 2-year floating-rate notes at a high discount margin of 0.050%, with a bid-to-cover of 2.87 (TreasuryDirect).

The market took it calmly. On Treasury’s closing curve the 5-year yield fell 5 basis points to 4.69%, the 3-month bill 1 to 4.36% and the 10-year 1 to 4.91%; the 2-year was unchanged at 4.63% and the 30-year rose 1 to 5.30%. The 2s10s spread is 28 basis points. High-yield bond spreads narrowed to 3.63 percentage points on Monday from 3.76 on Friday, the first narrowing in a week (ICE BofA via FRED).

Retailers rally on results: Kohl’s ends an 18-quarter slide in sales, Dick’s and Best Buy raise forecasts

The holiday-season reports so far point to a consumer who is still spending, helped in part by tariff refunds.

Kohl’s rose 19.48% to $25.33 after comparable sales grew 1.2%, ending 18 straight quarters of declines, and it raised its guidance; reported earnings figures differ by outlet because of one-time items (TipRanks, CNBC). Dick’s Sporting Goods rose 14.59% to $153.17 on adjusted earnings of $1.94 a share against about $1.42 and a higher full-year forecast (Investing.com, Motley Fool). Dollar Tree, which raised its outlook this morning, rose 11.18%. Best Buy rose 2.70% after adjusted earnings of $1.62 against about $1.50, comparable sales up 6.1%, its best in five years, and a higher revenue forecast of $43.0–43.3 billion; one analysis says $41 million of tariff refunds helped margins (CNBC, Investing.com, Chartmill). Burlington rose 3.90% despite trimming its earnings outlook.

Not every report was rewarded. Analog Devices fell 17.37% to $312.95 although revenue rose 42% to a record $4.37 billion and earnings of $4.06 a share beat estimates; it guided to a lower operating margin, and the stock had risen 9.5% on Monday (company release; Benzinga). Medtronic fell 4.56% after missing on revenue, and Smucker fell 1.45% despite adjusted earnings of $3.20 against about $2.59 (Finnhub).

The Economy

Today’s data. Consumer confidence, the Richmond Fed survey and house prices are in Top Stories. Together they describe an economy that is not slowing: confidence at a 22-month high, factories back to modest growth, and house prices rising at about 2% a year, well below the 3%-plus pace of inflation, so real house prices are falling.

The Fed. No Fed officials spoke publicly on policy today that we found. CME FedWatch puts a quarter-point rise on December 9 at about 95% and a half-point at about 5% (as cited by Tradingpedia). That would take the target range to 4.00–4.25%. The blackout before the meeting starts Saturday, so Wednesday’s data and the Beige Book are the last major inputs officials will discuss in public.

Treasury. The 5-year and 2-year FRN sales are in Top Stories. Wednesday brings the 7-year note sale at 1 PM, reported at $44 billion; the October sale cleared at 4.894%. Government funding lapses after December 11; the White House sent Congress a list of requested “anomalies” for any stopgap, including more money for NASA’s Artemis programme and wildfire suppression (Politico, as reported), and Republicans and Democrats remain split over money for the Iran war.

The United States

Nvidia and Congress are covered above. At least one Republican senator has called for legislation to restrict AI-chip sales to China, and Democratic Sen. Ruben Gallego accused the administration of trading national-security protections for market gains (NBC News, as summarized).

The World

Iran and the Gulf. Iran’s armed forces commander, Amir Hatami, called on Tuesday for the Strait of Hormuz to be closed “further day by day” (Euronews). The Revolutionary Guards’ claim that three tankers under U.S. escort hit mines remains unconfirmed: it named no ships, and the UK’s maritime trade office reported no new attacks (as cited in oil-market reporting). Separately, the Wall Street Journal reports that U.S. agencies are investigating suspected cyberattacks on up to 50 ships carrying oil, gas and chemicals, possibly through compromised network routers; Coast Guard teams have responded on at least four ships. Iran is the main suspect, according to the report, but an official said there is no evidence yet of Iranian involvement, so attribution is unconfirmed (WSJ via gCaptain and others). Qatar’s prime minister met Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President JD Vance in Washington on Monday and said the region “cannot bear a renewed escalation.” Iran says it will not negotiate until the U.S. lifts its naval blockade and sanctions. Oil’s modest rise today, with Brent near $90, came with no confirmed catalyst.

Ukraine. A Russian strike on Zaporizhzhia at dawn killed two people; counts of the wounded range from 32 to 48, including children (AP, Washington Post, Al Jazeera). Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha called the sinking of the Russian corvette Merkury at Benghazi legitimate self-defence and said Kyiv respects Libya’s sovereignty; Libya’s Tripoli government condemned the strike and the eastern authorities asked the UN to investigate (Reuters, Kyiv Post). President Zelenskyy told Bloomberg that U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner want a peace deal “as soon as possible,” ideally by January, and that Ukraine will not trade away its interests.

Gaza and Lebanon. An Israeli strike in Gaza killed Naim Abu Nahl, described as the head of Hamas’s internal security; Hamas called it a war crime, and we found no Israeli military statement (Al Jazeera). Lebanon’s state news agency reported Israeli strikes on southern towns. In Israel, Gadi Eisenkot holds the mandate to form a government, with a deadline in early December; talks are reported stalled (Times of Israel, Haaretz).

Europe. European Council President António Costa called a special summit for November 26–27 on the EU’s long-term budget and ECB appointments. Europe’s stock fund VGK rose 0.27%.

Science

The biology of trauma. A large post-mortem study in Nature links epigenetic changes in brain tissue, chemical marks that switch genes on and off, to post-traumatic stress disorder (journal listing, as summarized). It shows association, not cause, and is based on donated brains rather than living patients.

Faster infection diagnosis. A single-hospital study in the International Journal of Infectious Diseases found that genetic sequencing of urine samples could have changed about four in ten initial antibiotic prescriptions for urinary infections. It was retrospective, so no treatment decisions were changed.

Space

Ariane 6 delivers for Amazon. The Ariane 64 that lifted off at 6:14 AM deployed all 36 Amazon Leo satellites about two hours later, bringing to 136 the number Arianespace has launched for the network (Spaceflight Now). It was Amazon’s first launch since the Vulcan failure on November 15 destroyed about 40 of its satellites. Vulcan remains grounded, with ULA’s preliminary finding pointing to an inadequate fuel supply to its BE-4 engines; Blue Origin’s New Glenn, which uses the same engines, is also grounded.
