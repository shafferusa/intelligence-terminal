Chip Stocks Rebound and AMD Beats as the Polls Begin to Close

Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · 32 min read

Semiconductors jumped 2.6% and lifted the S&P 500 and Nasdaq off a three-day slide even though most stocks fell and Apple dragged the Dow; job openings rose to 7.5 million and pushed yields up; oil gave back its morning spike; and AMD, Super Micro and Arista all beat after the bell as election-night counting began.

Voting went relatively smoothly, the Associated Press reports, with scanner failures in Westchester County, N.Y., bomb threats in Ohio and a court-ordered extension at three Chester County, Pa., polling places. Kalshi priced a Democratic Senate at about 73% at midday and the House near 94%. Pennsylvania’s polls close at 8; more than 1.1 million mail ballots had been returned statewide.

Top Stories

AMD beats and forecasts a $17 billion quarter, extending a chip rebound that lifted the market on Election Day

After a week of selling on the question of whether AI spending pays, the most closely watched chip report of the week came in above expectations on revenue, data-center sales and the forecast. Its gross-margin forecast was slightly below estimates.

AMD’s third-quarter revenue was $13.38 billion, against its own forecast of $13.0 billion plus or minus $0.3 billion and analysts’ average of about $13.06 billion. Adjusted earnings were $2.09 a share against about $1.94. Data-center revenue was a record $8.55 billion, nearly double a year earlier. For the fourth quarter AMD forecast revenue of about $17.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, against estimates near $16.2–16.3 billion, citing its Helios rack systems and Venice server processors. Its adjusted gross-margin forecast of about 55% was slightly below the 55.5% analysts expected (AMD release; Investing.com; InvestingLive). The shares, which rose 3.7% in the regular session to $483.90, were up 6–7% in late trading, as reported.

The regular session had already turned on chips. The Philadelphia semiconductor index rose 2.6% to 10,600.40, its first gain in five sessions; Broadcom rose 3.1%, Micron 2.6% and Nvidia 2.3% to $206.69 (Yahoo Finance; Finnhub). Microsoft (+2.7%), Meta (+2.4%) and Amazon (+2.1%) rose with them, and Oracle, which fell 8.8% on Monday after its $20 billion capital raise, recovered 5.9%. Two other AI-hardware suppliers reported after the close: Super Micro’s fiscal first-quarter revenue was about $19.9 billion, above the $18.5–19.5 billion it had pre-announced, and it raised its full-year forecast to $72–82 billion; Arista’s revenue rose 58.5% to about $3.66 billion and it forecast $3.9–4.0 billion for the fourth quarter against about $3.6 billion expected. Both were up in late trading, Super Micro by about 6% in early trading that later swung (Investing.com; Benzinga; 24/7 Wall St.).

The gain was narrow. The equal-weighted S&P 500 fund fell 0.3% while the cap-weighted one rose 0.3%: Bloomberg described the S&P 500 rising “even as most of its firms fell.” Consumer staples fell 2.1% after Sysco (−9.3%) and Archer-Daniels-Midland (−6.8%) reported, and materials fell 2.0%.

Why it matters

This week’s fear was that the AI build-out had reached the point where customers balk at the cost and suppliers start issuing shares to fund it. AMD’s forecast says demand for its data-center products is still rising quickly; its margin guide says it is not getting that growth for free. One beat does not settle the debate: Qualcomm and Arm report tomorrow, and the Nasdaq 100 is still in a correction. Our call: AMD closes Wednesday above today’s $483.90 (65–80%), given late trading 6–7% higher and a forecast well above estimates.

AMD release and 8-K (SEC EDGAR); Super Micro and Arista releases as reported; Investing.com; InvestingLive; Benzinga; 24/7 Wall St.; Bloomberg via Swissinfo; Yahoo Finance and Finnhub (closes).

Job openings jump to 7.5 million, the most since May, and Treasury yields rise

September’s labor-turnover survey beat every forecast we found. Quits, which track workers’ confidence in finding a better job, had their biggest monthly rise in more than three years.

Employers advertised 7.523 million job openings at the end of September, up 333,000 from August, which was revised up to 7.19 million from 7.08 million (Bureau of Labor Statistics). The openings rate rose to 4.5% from 4.3%. Economists surveyed by Dow Jones had expected about 7.15 million. Hires rose 131,000 to 5.40 million; quits rose about 321,000 to about 3.4 million, a rate of 2.1%; layoffs fell to 1.64 million, the fewest since March 2025. One caveat: state and local government accounted for about 108,000 of the rise in openings, roughly a third, as reported.

Yields rose after the release, and the lower oil price later in the day did not reverse it. On the Treasury’s official curve the 2-year closed at 4.61% (+2 basis points), the 5-year 4.74% (+4), the 10-year 4.95% (+4) and the 30-year 5.32% (+4). The 10-year breakeven inflation rate was 2.30%, almost unchanged, so the rise was in real yields (Treasury; FRED). Long-bond funds fell: TLT −0.8%.

Why it matters

The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% last week with two members wanting an increase, and futures price a December rise at about three chances in four. A labor market that is tightening again, while inflation is running at 3.7%, supports the case for that increase. But JOLTS is a month old and Friday’s payroll report, expected to show about 63,000 jobs, carries more weight. Our morning call that openings would land between 6.8 and 7.4 million was wrong.

Bureau of Labor Statistics (JOLTS, September); US Treasury daily par yield curve; FRED; Bloomberg via Swissinfo; Dow Jones survey as reported by the Daily Caller.

Apple closes 1.9% lower on its margin forecast, a smaller loss than the morning implied; Palantir recovers most of its early fall

Both companies’ results beat. Both stocks sold off before the open on forecasts, and both closed well above their lows.

Apple fell 1.9% to $331.90, after trading as much as about 4% lower early in the day (Finnhub; Yahoo Finance). Its December-quarter forecast of a 45–46% gross margin, below estimates of about 46.3–46.9%, was attributed mainly to memory-chip costs. Because the Dow is weighted by share price, Apple’s fall was the largest single drag on that index, the only one of the major three to fall. Palantir closed down 1.7% at $175.40, after being indicated 6–8% lower before the open, on reports that the value of new US commercial contracts grew only 11%, against 153% in the second quarter.

Our call this morning that Apple would close below Monday’s $338.32 was right.

Apple and Palantir 8-Ks (SEC EDGAR); Finnhub and Yahoo Finance (closes); Reuters and Investing.com (session).

Oil’s morning jump on the Qeshm strikes fades; WTI settles at $96.09

President Trump confirmed the strikes; Iran said the next move was Washington’s and that talks through Qatar and Pakistan continue.

Front-month WTI settled 37 cents lower at $96.09 a barrel, after trading below $95 during the day, and Brent fell $1.17 to $103.60, as reported by CNBC. Both had been up about 2% before the US open. Yahoo Finance had WTI futures at $96.76 at 5 PM. The United States Oil Fund closed up 0.8%, because it tracks a later contract month.

US officials described the strikes on Iran’s Qeshm Island near the Strait of Hormuz as limited and “defensive,” a response to Iranian surface-to-air fire at US aircraft (The Wall Street Journal, as reported); President Trump confirmed them in a Fox News interview. Iran’s foreign ministry spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, said “the ball is in the US court” and that Tehran’s plan, which it says could be carried out within two days, was unchanged; Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said indirect contacts continue through Qatar and Pakistan (Euronews; Middle East Eye). Middle East Eye reports Iran called the latest US ceasefire proposal “insufficient.”

What’s next

US officials said wider strikes would not come before the election; that restraint ends tonight. Our morning call that WTI would settle above Monday’s $96.46 was wrong; Monday night’s call that it would settle below $100 was right. Our call: front-month WTI settles below $100 on Wednesday (60–75%), given today’s settlement near $96 and no sign either of a deal or of a breakdown in talks.

CNBC (settlements, as reported); Yahoo Finance (futures, delayed); The Wall Street Journal as reported by Yahoo; Euronews; Middle East Eye; Fox News as reported.

Americans vote with few serious problems so far; first polls close at 6 PM

Problems were scattered and local. The bigger question for markets is how quickly control of each chamber becomes clear.

The Associated Press described Election Day as unfolding “with relatively few problems,” some caused by weather and machines. In Westchester County, N.Y., scanners rejected paper ballots at most sites after the 6 AM opening; the state elections board blamed a programming update made after testing, and ballots went into sealed emergency bins to be counted later, which may slow results in the 17th District. Broward County, Fla., opened about 20 minutes late; Fulton County, Ga., had machine problems at two sites. In Toledo, Ohio, voting at one school paused for more than an hour after a phoned-in bomb threat; police found nothing, and there were threats at schools in Bowling Green and Defiance. In Pennsylvania, a court extended voting to 8:30 PM at three Chester County polling places that opened late; ballots cast after 8 there will be provisional (Philadelphia Inquirer).

The Justice Department sent about 1,000 monitors and observers to all 50 states, which it calls its largest effort, against 289 in 2022 and 714 in 2024. Michigan’s and Nevada’s secretaries of state reported unusual questions from monitors; the department said they were “standard questions on the forms we provided” (NBC News; PBS). Early voting reached roughly 54–59 million ballots, depending on the tracker.

On Kalshi, Democrats’ chance of winning the Senate traded at about 73% at noon, after about 78% early in the morning (Benzinga); the House traded near 93–94% across platforms. Those are prices, not forecasts. Close races may take days: Maine may need a ranked-choice tabulation, and California counts late-arriving mail ballots postmarked by today.

Why it matters

A divided government has usually meant little new fiscal legislation; a Democratic sweep would change the outlook for the tariff and tax fights due in 2027. For tonight, the market risk is less the result than a long or contested count.

AP via WSLS and the Greenfield Recorder; NBC News; PBS; The Philadelphia Inquirer; WCJB/Gray TV; Benzinga; Quartz; CBS News; Maine Public.

Eaton jumps 7% on record orders as Marathon Petroleum sells two refineries; Sysco and Shopify fall

The morning’s earnings were read mostly through two questions: AI-infrastructure demand, and how much the consumer is still spending.

Eaton, which sells electrical equipment to data centers, rose 7.0% to $391.70 after record sales of $8.9 billion (+28%, 19% organic), a record 25.3% segment margin, record orders and an agreement to buy COL Group, a European medium-voltage distributor (8-K). Marathon Petroleum earned $26.93 a share on an adjusted basis against about $24.47 expected, raised its dividend 10% and agreed to sell its St. Paul Park, Minn., and Mandan, N.D., refineries to CVR Energy for about $4.2 billion, with the trader Trafigura buying $800 million of CVR stock (8-Ks; Reuters). Its shares slipped 0.7%.

On the other side: Sysco fell 9.3% to $74.11 after its fiscal first-quarter results, even though it reiterated its full-year guidance. Shopify fell 8.7% after gross merchandise volume and subscription gross profit missed estimates, as reported. Archer-Daniels-Midland fell 6.8% despite beating on profit and raising its outlook, as revenue missed by about 5%. Pfizer fell 2.2% to $30.00 even with adjusted earnings of 87 cents against 73 cents expected; revenue was flat at $16.6 billion (8-K).

Eaton, Marathon Petroleum, CVR Energy and Pfizer 8-Ks (SEC EDGAR); Reuters via Investing.com; CNBC; Finnhub (closes).

Anthropic’s amended IPO filing shows $17 billion of revenue in a single quarter

The prospectus still has no price range. Its Nasdaq ticker would be ANT.

Anthropic filed an amended registration statement on Monday (S-1/A, SEC EDGAR). It reports revenue of $17.0 billion for the three months to Sept. 30, up 1,127% from a year earlier, while compute and infrastructure costs rose 382%. The five largest customers were about 25% of revenue in the first nine months and none exceeded 10%. The cover still leaves the price per share blank. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead a syndicate that includes J.P. Morgan, Citigroup, Barclays, Wells Fargo and BofA. Secondary reports citing Reuters say marketing could start the week of Nov. 9, with pricing before Thanksgiving; the company has not confirmed a date, and the valuation and proceeds figures in circulation are not in the filing.

Why it matters

It would be the largest listing yet of an AI developer, arriving just as investors have started to question how the industry pays for its build-out. The filing also shows how fast costs are rising alongside revenue. OpenAI has reportedly cut its own funding round to about $20 billion from $30 billion; another report still describes it as up to $30 billion.

Anthropic S-1/A (SEC EDGAR, Nov. 2); Implicator.ai citing Reuters; Dataconomy; CNBC.

Ukraine counts the damage from Russia’s overnight grid strike; aid-site death toll passes 30

Three of DTEK’s five thermal power plants went offline. US-mediated talks are still planned in Abu Dhabi next week.

Ukrenergo ordered emergency power cuts in Kyiv city and region and in Dnipropetrovsk after the attack of 289 drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles, President Zelensky said; DTEK said three of its five thermal plants were knocked offline. What Moved Markets

The open. Futures had pointed to a weak start: Dow futures were down about 0.75% at 6:30 AM with Apple indicated 4% lower and oil up 2% after the Qeshm strikes. The early trade was mixed, with Apple falling and chips firming. Confirmed catalyst for Apple’s fall: its own margin forecast.

Morning. Job openings at 10:00 came in well above forecasts, and Treasury yields rose; the 10-year traded near 4.96% by mid-morning. Likely contributor to the weakness in rate-sensitive groups: regional banks fell 1.2%, utilities 0.6%, and long-bond funds fell.

Midday. Chips took over. The semiconductor index, which had fallen in each of the previous five sessions, finished up 2.6%, with AMD rising ahead of its results and Oracle recovering 5.9%. Market narrative for the group: Bloomberg called it a rebound from a rout driven by worries that AI spending is harder to justify. There was no single chip-specific catalyst during the session; a Financial Times report, relayed by other outlets, that Nvidia is limiting Chinese exposure at its cloud partners did not obviously move the stock.

The close. Oil fell back into its 2:30 PM settlement, ending slightly lower. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq ended higher, the Dow lower; most S&P 500 stocks fell. Consumer staples (−2.1%) and materials (−2.0%) were the weakest sectors, the first on earnings from Sysco and ADM and lower prices for Walmart (−2.2%) and Costco (−2.1%). Confirmed catalyst for Sysco and ADM. Unexplained for the retailers: no company news found. Materials fell with copper (−2.4%) and gold (−0.8%).

Volatility, the dollar and crypto. The VIX eased to 20.83 but the short end of the volatility curve stayed inverted ahead of tonight. The dollar index rose 0.1% to 101.87, with the yen weaker at 158.7 per dollar. Bitcoin rose about 1.4% to roughly $77,700 and Coinbase rose 5.5%. Market narrative for the dollar: higher yields after JOLTS.

Overseas. Europe closed lower as Apple’s warning and higher yields weighed on technology and London fell most: Stoxx 600 −0.58%, DAX −0.45%, FTSE 100 −1.14%, CAC 40 −0.34%. Korea’s Kospi had closed down 3.6% earlier.

What Changed Today

Chips. This morning the most fragile assumption in the tape was that the AI-hardware sell-off was a valuation reset, not an earnings problem, with AMD the test. AMD’s numbers support that assumption for now. Confidence: moderate; one company, one quarter.

Labor. We expected job openings near August’s 7.1 million; they were 7.5 million, with August revised up. That leans against the view that the labor market is cooling, and yields rose. Confidence in the signal: moderate, because a third of the rise was government jobs.

Oil. This morning WTI was up 2% on the Qeshm strikes. By the settlement it was slightly down. What changed: nothing physical; the strikes were described by both sides as limited and talks continue. Our two oil calls split: below $100 (right), above $96.46 (wrong).

Apple. The premarket indication of about −4% halved by the close. Our call that it would close below $338.32 was right.

The Economy

Today’s data. Job openings 7.52 million in September (rate 4.5%), hires 5.40 million, quits about 3.4 million (2.1%), layoffs 1.64 million (1.0%), from the Bureau of Labor Statistics. These are levels for one month and come with a month’s lag; the change in quits is the most useful signal, because people quit when they are confident of finding another job, which tends to push wages up.

The Fed. Target range 3.75–4.00%. We found no Fed speakers today and no updated futures pricing after the JOLTS release; the last figure we have is about 73% for a December increase, from early today. The next meeting is Dec. 8–9, with October payrolls on Friday and October CPI on Nov. 10 before it.

Treasury. Tomorrow’s 8:30 refunding statement sets the sizes of next week’s three-, ten- and thirty-year auctions. It is widely expected to leave them unchanged at $58 billion, $42 billion and $25 billion, for $125 billion in total, as reported by Reuters. The words to watch are whether sizes stay unchanged for “at least the next several quarters”: Barclays expects that phrase to soften to “coming quarters,” JPMorgan expects it unchanged. Dealers broadly expect cuts in 20- and 30-year sizes during 2027, and the Treasury has promised more detail on buybacks. Our call: the Treasury keeps the 3-, 10- and 30-year auction sizes at $58, $42 and $25 billion (80–90%), given its own guidance and the dealer surveys reported.

Rates and credit. Today’s official curve: 3-month 4.21%, 2-year 4.61%, 5-year 4.74%, 10-year 4.95%, 30-year 5.32%. The 2s10s spread is 34 basis points and 3m10y 74. The 10-year breakeven is 2.30% and the 10-year real yield was 2.62% on Monday. The high-yield credit spread was 3.43 percentage points on Monday, a basis point narrower than Friday’s, the widest in our archive. HYG rose 0.1% today, so credit did not confirm the weakness in the average stock.

Other central banks. The Reserve Bank of Australia raised its cash rate to 4.85% overnight, as our Nov. 2 call expected. The Bank of England decides Thursday, with most economists in a Reuters poll expecting an increase to 4.00%.

The United States

The Supreme Court and religious preschools. The court heard St. Mary Catholic Parish v. Roy, in which two Archdiocese of Denver parishes and Catholic parents challenge their exclusion from Colorado’s universal preschool program under its nondiscrimination rules covering LGBTQ families. The solicitor general argued on the parishes’ side. NBC described the court as sympathetic to the parishes; Justices Kavanaugh and Barrett pressed Colorado’s lawyer, and Justices Sotomayor and Jackson were the sharpest critics of the parishes’ case. The case could narrow or revisit Employment Division v. Smith (1990), the precedent that generally allows neutral laws that burden religion. A decision is expected by summer 2027.

The president on Election Day. President Trump posted on Truth Social through the morning, including about Alaska’s ballot and the Michigan Senate race, and on Monday told Fox News that Republicans should “pretend I’m on the ballot.” CNN reports, citing unnamed sources, that he has privately acknowledged the night could go badly for his party; we could not confirm that independently.

AI & Technology

The AI money question. Today’s numbers pointed in two directions. AMD’s data-center revenue nearly doubled and Super Micro now expects $72–82 billion of sales this fiscal year, both signs that spending on AI servers is still rising. Anthropic’s filing shows its compute costs growing by about a third as fast as its revenue, and OpenAI’s round has reportedly shrunk. Cyber. Data from a breach of Harvard’s alumni and development systems, disclosed last November, covers about 850,500 accounts, according to the breach-notification service Have I Been Pwned (BleepingComputer). Harvard has not given a count.

Science

A five-lab test of psilocybin in mice. A study in Nature Neuroscience ran the same experiments on psilocybin, the psychedelic in “magic mushrooms,” in five laboratories. The immediate effects replicated: more avoidance behaviour and less expression of learned fear. The lasting effects that have driven interest in the drug for depression were “modest or inconsistent.” It is peer-reviewed animal work, not a human trial, and its main lesson is about reproducibility: effects reported by single labs are often weaker when others try them.

USSF-432 holds at Friday. SpaceX’s classified Falcon 9 launch for the Space Force from Vandenberg is listed for no earlier than 4:46 PM Pacific (7:46 PM Eastern) on Friday, after earlier targets of Nov. 1, 2 and 3; no reason for the slips has been given (Edhat; Next Spaceflight).

Allegheny County begins counting more than 135,000 mail ballots; a council-spending question is on the ballot

Pre-canvassing of mail ballots began at 7 AM at the county’s Pennsylvania Avenue warehouse with about 140 county employees, the earliest state law allows. More than 135,000 mail ballots had been returned by Election Day. More than 50 polling places have moved since the last election, with signs at the old sites (WESA; PublicSource). County voters also decide a home-rule charter question that would loosen limits on county council members’ use of county money for staff, district offices and benefits, according to KDKA Radio. Federal monitors were expected in Allegheny, Lehigh, Luzerne and Philadelphia counties.

Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania votes for governor, the state House and 17 congressional seats; 1.1 million mail ballots are in

Polls close at 8 PM, and mail ballots count only if they arrive by then; a postmark is not enough. By Election Day 1,114,767 mail ballots had been returned out of more than 1.2 million requested (Spotlight PA). Secretary of the Commonwealth Al Schmidt repeated that counties could not start opening them before 7 this morning and that close races will take time. Gov. Chris Deluzio (D) faces Beaver County Sheriff Tony Guy (R); the 7th, 8th and 10th districts in the east are the state’s closest House races.
