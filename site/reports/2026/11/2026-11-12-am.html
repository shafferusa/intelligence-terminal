Nvidia’s board weighs removing Jensen Huang; shares fall 7% before the bell

Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 32 min read

Reports that Nvidia’s directors are preparing to remove their founder-CEO knocked the world’s most important AI stock and pulled Nasdaq futures lower, on a morning that also brings jobless claims, six Fed speakers and a 30-year bond auction into yields near 5%.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The tape. At 6:28 AM, Nasdaq-100 futures were down 0.83% and S&P 500 futures down 0.39%, while Dow futures were flat (+0.05%). Nvidia was about 7% lower in premarket trading. Wednesday’s chip rally had lifted the S&P 1.31% to 7,619.38.
  • The story. The Wall Street Journal reported, and CBS, CNBC and CNN confirmed, that Nvidia’s board is preparing to remove CEO Jensen Huang over his conduct. The board met late Wednesday with no decision announced, and Nvidia has said nothing publicly. Earnings are due next week.
  • Rates and the Fed. The cash Treasury market reopens after the holiday, with the 10-year near 5.0% and the 30-year at 5.35% (Tuesday’s par curve). The $25 billion 30-year auction is at 1 PM, and six Fed officials speak between 9 AM and 6:30 PM. Futures still price a December hike as near-certain.
  • Dollar, oil, gold. The yen weakened to about 159.7 per dollar, close to the 160 line where Tokyo has stepped in before. Gold fell 1.2% to $3,953. Brent was $92.32 after Iran said mines had disabled two supertankers in the Strait of Hormuz; that claim has not been independently confirmed.
  • What to watch. Jobless claims at 8:30 AM (consensus about 194,000–195,000), the 30-year auction at 1 PM, and Cisco and Applied Materials after the close.
  • Beyond markets. Mississippi elected a Democratic senator for the first time since 1982: the AP called the race for Scott Colom and Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith conceded. Alaska is still counting. Trump envoys are due in Moscow today, and the G7 backed the U.S.-led Ukraine talks.

Top Stories

Nvidia’s board meets on Jensen Huang’s future, and the stock slides about 7% before the open

The Wall Street Journal reports directors are preparing to remove the co-founder who has run the company since 1993. A late-night meeting on Wednesday ended without an announced decision.

The Wall Street Journal first reported on Wednesday that Nvidia’s board is preparing to oust Huang. CBS News and CNBC, citing their own sources, confirmed that the board met into Wednesday night to decide whether he stays, and CNN and The Next Web report that the meeting ended with no decision made public. The reporting ties the review to two alleged extramarital affairs, one first reported by The New York Times. Huang acknowledged in an internal message that he was “unfaithful” and apologized to his family (as quoted by ABC and CBS). It also ties the review to the board’s separate independent investigation, opened Monday, into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort. That investigation is linked to the Singapore data-center firm Megaspeed, which the Justice Department’s National Security Division is now examining. Nvidia has made no announcement about Huang’s position.

The shares fell 8.6% to $158.78 in after-hours trading on Wednesday (Forbes) and were about 7% lower in early premarket trading on Thursday (Benzinga, Investing.com), after closing up 2.9% at $173.73. Before Monday’s board announcement the stock was at $186.64. Nvidia is the largest weight in the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq-100, and reports say it accounts for more than half of this morning’s fall in Nasdaq futures. Single source Bloomberg, as cited by Forbes, reports that the board has worked with an outside firm on possible successors and may name an interim chief.

Why it matters Huang is more than a CEO to investors. He sets the company’s product roadmap and is its main link to customers, suppliers and governments in Washington, Beijing and Taipei. Removing him would be the largest leadership change in the AI industry, and it would come a week before Nvidia reports earnings and Anthropic prices a trillion-dollar IPO. One report says Nvidia’s bylaws let a majority of directors remove the CEO with or without cause. What is confirmed is the reporting of the board’s deliberations; whether it acts, and when, is not.
What’s next A board statement could come at any time, quite possibly before the open. Nvidia reports quarterly results next week. Watch whether AMD and Broadcom gain on the news, as Investing.com reported early, or fall with the AI complex.

Go deeper

This is a governance story, not an earnings story, so the usual frame of “beat or miss” does not apply. The market has to price two things: the chance of a leadership change, and what it would do to execution. Nvidia’s numbers do not depend on one person in any given quarter, since its orders are booked quarters ahead. Over a longer horizon investors have treated Huang’s product bets, from CUDA to the annual chip cadence, as the core of its advantage. Analysts noted earlier this week that Nvidia has no public succession plan. The stock has fallen about 15% from Friday’s $186.64 to this morning’s indicated price. FINRA data show only 34% of Wednesday’s off-exchange Nvidia volume was short sales, below the market-wide 47.6%, so the selling is not obviously a short-seller push. The two-source rule is met for the board deliberations (WSJ, CBS, CNBC, CNN). It is not met for any outcome, because no outcome has been reported.

The bond market reopens into a 30-year auction and six Fed speakers

After the Veterans Day close, the Treasury market has one session to absorb jobless claims, Fed remarks and $25 billion of long bonds.

The last par curve, for Tuesday, put the 2-year at 4.73%, the 10-year at 4.98% and the 30-year at 5.35%, after hot October CPI and Governor Waller’s talk of a possible half-point move pushed front-end yields up 17 basis points that day. Early on Thursday CNBC showed the 10-year near 4.99% and the 30-year near 5.34%, about unchanged. A TradingView headline reported a small rally at the long end as Nvidia unsettled stocks. The October 30-year auction stopped at 5.618%, per a market preview we could not check against Treasury’s results. This week’s 3-year and 10-year sales both drew softer demand than last month, and the 10-year tailed by about a basis point.

Six officials are scheduled, according to one published calendar, though another preview omitted three of them. They are Cleveland’s Beth Hammack at 9 AM, Vice Chair for Supervision Michael Barr at 10, Chicago’s Austan Goolsbee at 11:30, San Francisco’s Mary Daly at 1:10 PM, Kansas City’s Jeff Schmid at 1:30 and New York’s John Williams at 6:30 PM. Hammack dissented in favour of a hike in October. Futures put the chance of a December hike near 90% for a quarter point, and 10–14% for a half point, depending on the snapshot.

Why it matters A 30-year yield above 5.3% is a test of how much long-dated debt investors will absorb while the Fed is still raising rates. Williams speaks after the auction and is the Fed’s most important voice on the outlook after the chair.

The yen nears 160 per dollar and Tokyo turns up its warnings

The dollar is at its strongest against the yen since the coordinated intervention of August, and the dollar index is a whisker from a 52-week high.

The dollar bought 159.66 yen at 6:38 AM (Yahoo Finance), up from 158.18 at Tuesday’s ECB reference fix. FXStreet reported it a few pips short of 160 earlier in the European morning, with Japanese officials issuing verbal warnings. No intervention has been confirmed. The ICE dollar index was 102.90, against a 52-week high of 103.08, and the euro was $1.1093. The move follows from the Fed outlook: U.S. two-year yields near 4.7% against Japanese rates that remain far lower make holding dollars and borrowing in yen profitable, and the U.S. hike now priced for December widens that gap.

Why it matters Japan spent a record ¥7.98 trillion on a single day of intervention in July. A sudden yen rally can force investors to unwind yen-funded positions, which in the past has spilled into global equities.

Iran says mines disabled two supertankers in the Strait of Hormuz

The claim, from the Revolutionary Guard navy, has not been independently confirmed. The President told CNN a return to full-scale war is “highly unlikely” if talks progress.

Iran’s IRGC Navy said explosions it attributed to naval mines disabled two very large crude carriers, together carrying more than 4 million barrels, on Wednesday night after they took what it called an “unauthorized” route (Mehr, as carried by MarineLink and the Canberra Times). Unconfirmed We found no independent confirmation of the ships’ identities or damage. The UK Maritime Trade Operations centre logged a separate tanker incident on Wednesday. President Trump told CNN a return to full war is “highly unlikely” and that “it depends on whether or not they sign a very strong agreement.” Talks are reported to be stuck on reopening Hormuz and on the nuclear programme, and Qatari mediators are reported to be in Tehran.

Brent was $92.32 at 6:27 AM, down 0.5% from Wednesday’s settlement, and WTI $86.06. Commerzbank said the reported mine strikes were supporting prices. Brent is still about 17% lower in November, as talk of a deal has gradually pushed the war premium out of the price.

Anthropic’s IPO arrives in a week when AI leadership is in question

The book is reported to be several times covered, and underwriters are weighing a price above the $70–78 range.

Bloomberg reports the order book is multiple times oversubscribed, and the Financial Times reports the underwriters are weighing a price above the range, which could imply a fully diluted valuation of $1.5 trillion or more. Pricing is expected on November 18 and the first trade, on Nasdaq as “ANT,” on November 19. At the top of the filed range the 825 million shares would raise about $64.4 billion (CNBC, Fortune, SiliconANGLE). Seven strategic partners, including Google and Nvidia, are reported to be buying a further $26 billion of non-voting stock at the IPO price. Anthropic is private until it lists, so there is no market price. Nvidia’s role as a placement investor ties the two stories together: a leadership crisis at the main chip supplier lands in the IPO’s final week of marketing.

Britain’s economy grew 0.7% in the third quarter, the fastest in two years

Growth matched forecasts, the second quarter was revised up, and a December rate rise from the Bank of England stays in play.

The Office for National Statistics’ first estimate shows GDP up 0.7% on the quarter and 1.9% on the year, with the second quarter revised to 0.5% from 0.4%. September alone grew 0.2%, against forecasts of no change. Services rose 0.9%, led by information and communication (+2.1%), which analysts tied to AI and cloud spending; construction fell 0.7%. The Bank of England raised its rate to 4.00% last week by an 8–1 vote, and the strong quarter keeps a further move in December in play (CNBC, RTÉ). London’s FTSE 100 was up about 0.4% in early trading.

Before the Open

Futures. At 6:28 AM, December S&P 500 futures were at 7,610.50, down 30 points or 0.39%; Nasdaq-100 futures were 28,236.75, down 0.83%; Dow futures 52,603, up 0.05%; and Russell 2000 futures 2,852, down 0.06%. Volatility. The VIX closed Wednesday at 17.49 and was 18.30 early Thursday. The term structure was upward-sloping at the close (VIX9D 16.04, VIX3M 19.24), so traders were not pricing an immediate shock. A jump in the nine-day measure above the VIX would be the first sign that the Nvidia story is spreading into a broader hedge.

Credit, oil, gold, bitcoin. High-yield spreads were 3.34 percentage points on November 9 (ICE BofA, via FRED), near the tight end of their range, and HYG has held steady this week. Credit is not confirming any stress. Brent at $92.32 sits between the war premium and the deal talk. Gold below $4,000 is consistent with higher real yields and a strong dollar. Bitcoin has not joined the equity rebound this week.

The fragile assumption. The tape assumes the Nvidia story is about one company’s governance and not about AI demand. The Dow and small-cap futures say exactly that. What would invalidate it: a board decision that triggers customer or supplier anxiety, a sharp fall in Taiwan- and Korea-linked chip names after the open, or Cisco and Applied Materials guiding cautiously after the close. Any of those would make this a story about the AI trade, not just its best-known chief executive.

The Economy

Jobless claims. At 8:30 AM the Labor Department reports initial claims for the week to November 7. The reported consensus is about 194,000–195,000, against 189,000 the week before. Claims have held between 189,000 and 200,000 for six weeks (FRED), which is very low by historical standards. Continuing claims are expected near 1.72 million. A low number keeps the December hike on track. A jump above about 210,000 would be the first sign the labour market is loosening under higher rates. Existing-home sales follow at 10 AM.

The Fed. The case for a December hike rests on inflation that has stopped falling. October CPI rose 0.4% (core 0.3%). Core has now run about 0.3% a month for three months, an annualized pace near 3.7%, and there is still no clean year-over-year rate because of the 2025 shutdown gap. Governor Waller said in London on Tuesday that he leans toward a quarter-point hike and that a half point should be considered if core keeps printing 0.3% or more. The labour market gives the Fed no reason to wait: unemployment was 4.1% in October and payrolls rose 165,000. Today’s six speakers are the last big round before Friday’s producer prices. Williams, speaking after the auction, is the one to read for the leadership’s view. Single source Goldman Sachs is reported to have added a further hike to its outlook.

Treasury. The refunding finishes today with $25 billion of 30-year bonds at 1 PM, settling November 16. The 3-year sold at 4.627% (bid-to-cover 2.53) and the 10-year at 5.020% (2.46), both with softer demand than in October. The next bills auction early next week: $115 billion of 4-week, $110 billion of 8-week and $75 billion of 17-week, settling November 17 (TreasuryDirect). A 20-year bond and a 10-year reopening settle November 30. SOFR was 3.88% on November 9.

Rates in real terms. The 10-year Treasury inflation-protected yield was 2.66% on November 9, up from 2.60% on November 5. The 10-year breakeven fell to 2.20% on November 10 from 2.30% on November 3. So the recent sell-off in bonds has been about higher real rates, that is, tighter policy, not higher expected inflation. That is the market taking the Fed at its word.

Global. Britain’s 0.7% third quarter is covered above. Japan’s officials stepped up warnings about the yen, without intervening. Europe’s companies are posting their strongest profit growth in four years. We found no new Chinese data or ECB or Bank of Japan remarks this morning.

Risks & Scenarios

Nvidia contagion. The base case is that the Nvidia sell-off stays a single-stock governance event. The Dow and small-cap futures are flat, credit is calm and the VIX term structure is normal. The risk case is that a messy outcome, such as an abrupt removal with no clear successor or departures of senior engineers, makes investors question execution across the AI supply chain just as Anthropic’s IPO and Nvidia’s results arrive. We put 65–80% on Nvidia closing today at least 4% below Wednesday’s $173.73, that is at or below $166.78, given the indicated premarket loss of about 7% and no announcement yet. We put 45–65% on Nvidia announcing a change in its chief executive, permanent or interim, by November 20. That rests on multiple reports that the board is preparing to act and has met on it, weighed against the absence of any decision after Wednesday’s meeting.

The long end. A weak 30-year auction, one with a tail of more than a basis point or a bid-to-cover under 2.3, would extend the rise in real yields. With the Fed hiking, that is the macro risk most likely to pressure equity valuations. Wednesday’s call stands at 70–85% that the auction clears at or above 5.30%.

The yen. USD/JPY at 160 is where Japan has acted before. An intervention would most likely knock the dollar down 2–4 yen within hours and could ripple through carry positions. That would be a positive surprise for bonds and a negative one for leveraged equity positions. We are not putting a probability on the timing of an intervention.

Hormuz. If the mine claims are confirmed, Brent could rebuild its war premium quickly. A signed bridge deal, on the other hand, would likely take it into the low $80s. The current price sits between those two outcomes.

Wednesday evening’s calls for today still stand: the S&P 500 closing above 7,500 (75–88%), initial claims below 205,000 (65–80%), and Cisco revenue at or above $18.1 billion (60–75%).

The United States

Mississippi elects a Democratic senator. The AP called Mississippi’s Senate race for Democrat Scott Colom on Wednesday, after CNN projected it, once Hinds County finished counting, including affidavit ballots. Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith conceded late Wednesday afternoon. In a video statement she said, “While this is not the outcome we worked for, or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters,” and congratulated Colom. The AP’s unofficial count, with 99% counted, was 473,336 to 468,667, a margin of 4,669. Counties must certify by Friday. Colom, a district attorney, will be Mississippi’s first Democratic senator elected since 1982 and its first Black senator since Reconstruction (NPR, The Hill, WAPT). With the races already called, that puts Democrats at 55 seats by our count; we have not seen an outlet state the total directly.

Alaska is still counting. Democrat Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by 4,250 first-choice votes as of Tuesday, 49.0% to 47.2%. Ballots counted Wednesday narrowed the gap slightly (Anchorage Daily News). An Alaska Beacon simulation of ranked-choice transfers gives Sullivan a net gain of about 906 votes, not enough to close the gap. Peltola’s campaign has declared victory, and Sullivan’s says “the fight is not over.” The ranked-choice tabulation is November 18 and certification November 25.

Congress. Senate Republicans re-elected John Thune as their leader on Tuesday, unopposed. The conference first rejected, 31–15, Sen. Ron Johnson’s push to delay the vote; Josh Hawley joins the leadership team (AP, The Hill). On funding, the House has passed three of the 12 fiscal 2027 spending bills and the Senate none, with the government funded through December 11. The main dispute is the administration’s $1.5 trillion defense request, and a stopgap is widely expected (CNBC). Flood insurance and surface-transportation authority expire the same day. The House’s 211–204 vote on Tuesday to send Rep. Thomas Massie’s Hegseth impeachment articles to the Judiciary Committee is widely described as ending them for this Congress.

The courts. No major Supreme Court opinions this week. In Tuesday’s argument over the Labor Department’s power to impose fines (Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards), the justices appeared divided (NPR).

Science

The black hole picture holds up. A reanalysis of the original Event Horizon Telescope data on M87, published in Nature this week, again recovers the ring around the black hole. That answers critics who argued the ring was an artefact of the imaging method (Nature; The Conversation). It is a check of the same data, not a new observation.

Also in Nature. A proposed definition of obesity that adds waist measurement to body-mass index predicted health outcomes better than BMI alone. A study finds drought and warming sharply raise extinction risk for cacti. A 180-qubit neutral-atom quantum simulator was used to study how quantum systems evolve after a sudden change. All are peer-reviewed. We read them from Nature’s listings rather than the full papers.

State Police report 99% compliance with the skill-game ban

A month after enforcement began on October 14, State Police say they have visited 12,202 establishments. Of those, 12,134 had removed the machines and 68 had not. Troopers have seized 14 machines and $1,913, and six operators and distributors have signed compliance agreements setting removal schedules (Pennsylvania Capital-Star, LevittownNow). The ban follows the state Supreme Court’s June 15 ruling that the games are slot machines under the Gaming Act; its 120-day stay ran out without a legislative fix. The Attorney General’s office had estimated up to 70,000 machines statewide, and the figures all come from the State Police. Whether the new Democratic legislature taxes and licenses the games is one of the questions for January.

Pennsylvania State Police figures, via Pennsylvania Capital-Star and LevittownNow (Nov 10).
