Apple and Palantir Slide Before the Bell as Americans Vote for Control of Congress

Tuesday, November 3, 2026 · 32 min read

Apple’s holiday margin forecast and a slowdown in Palantir’s deal growth are dragging on big technology shares before the open and Korea fell 3.6%, oil is back up 2% after US strikes on an Iranian island, and the country votes today, with AMD reporting tonight while the results come in.

Top Stories

Apple guides to a thinner holiday margin and Palantir’s deal growth slows; both slide before the open

Both companies beat on the quarter just ended. Investors are selling the forecasts, which show memory-chip costs reaching Apple and a sharp slowdown in new US commercial contracts at Palantir.

Apple reported record September-quarter revenue of $114.5 billion, up 12%, and earnings of $2.10 a share, including a 5-cent benefit from tariff refunds (8-K). On the call, Chief Executive John Ternus’s first, Apple forecast December-quarter revenue growth of 7–10% and a gross margin of 45–46%. That margin is below analysts’ estimates, which sources put between 46.3% and 46.9%, and Apple pointed to higher memory costs and supply limits on Macs and iPads (MacRumors; CNBC). The September quarter’s 47.9% margin included about one point from tariff refunds, so the underlying decline is smaller than the headline gap suggests. The shares fell 2.7% after hours and were indicated 3.8–4.5% lower this morning, after closing Monday at $338.32.

Palantir’s reported numbers were strong: third-quarter revenue of $2.313 billion, up 96%, US commercial revenue up 148% to $984 million, and a full-year forecast raised to $8.530–8.534 billion from $8.150–8.158 billion (8-K). The shares, which closed at $178.34, were indicated 6–8% lower (Reuters; CNBC). Reports cite one figure: growth in the total value of new US commercial contracts slowed to 11% from 153% in the second quarter. That number is from secondary reporting of the call, not the filing.

The rest of the tape is mixed. At about 6:30 AM, S&P 500 futures were at 7,564.75, down 0.1% from Monday’s settlement; Nasdaq 100 futures 28,219 (+0.3%); Dow futures 51,742 (−0.75%); Russell 2000 futures 2,813.9 (−0.1%) (Yahoo Finance, delayed). On Monday the S&P 500 fell 0.51% to 7,541.40, its third straight loss; the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.07% to 25,164.32; the Dow rose 0.41% to 51,983.98. The equal-weighted S&P 500 fund rose 0.8%.

Why it matters For a week the market has asked one question of technology companies: does AI spending pay, and who absorbs the cost? Apple’s answer is that memory prices, pushed up by AI data-center demand, now squeeze a consumer-device margin. Palantir’s is that even the fastest grower is judged on its next contracts, not its last quarter. Apple is one of the largest weights in the S&P 500, so its fall alone is a drag on the index. Our call: Apple closes today below Monday’s $338.32 (75–88%), given premarket indications near −4% and no offsetting news.

Americans vote today for control of Congress, 36 governors and more than 140 ballot measures

Markets price a Democratic House as near-certain and a Democratic Senate as likely. Polls in the decisive Senate races are close enough that a normal polling error would change the outcome.

Republicans hold the Senate 53–47; Democrats need a net gain of four seats to take the House (CNBC). On Monday afternoon Kalshi priced the House for Democrats at about 94% and the Senate at about 74%, the platform’s highest Senate figure; Polymarket and two smaller exchanges were within a point or two (CNBC; Kalshi). Kalshi’s combined market at 4:45 PM Monday: Democratic sweep 74%, Democratic House with a Republican Senate 21%, Republican sweep 6%. Prediction markets are prices, not forecasts, and we found no figure from this morning.

The polling average on the generic ballot is about 9 points in Democrats’ favour (RealClearPolitics, D+8.9). In the Senate races that decide control, final averages are within about 5 points: Maine (D +3.3 to +4.4 across averages), Ohio (D +2.8 to +3.9), Texas (D +2.5 to +3.5), Michigan (D +4.9, Silver Bulletin), and Iowa, which is effectively tied (D +1.1 in one average, R +0.3 in another; the final New York Times/Siena poll had the Democrat, Turek, at 48 and Rep. Ashley Hinson at 47). North Carolina’s Roy Cooper leads by about 11. Polling errors of that size are common, so these leads are not safe in either direction.

More than 57 million ballots had been cast early by Monday afternoon, about 32 million in person and 25 million by mail, against about 46 million in 2022 (University of Florida Election Lab via CBS). The first polls close at 6 PM Eastern in parts of Indiana and Kentucky; most of the East closes at 7–8 PM and New York at 9. California counts mail ballots that arrive up to seven days late if postmarked today, so House control may take days if the margin is narrow. Storms are forecast across the central US, with a low severe-weather risk in southeast Texas.

On Monday the Supreme Court denied, in a one-line unsigned order with no noted dissents, the Justice Department’s emergency request to make California and Los Angeles County set aside ballots from about 20,000 people the department alleges are not citizens. Two lower courts had refused the same request; state officials said segregating ballots this late was impossible. Gov. Gavin Newsom called the order a rebuke; the US attorney in Los Angeles, Bill Essayli, said he remains “highly confident” the people on the list are ineligible. The allegation has not been tested in court (CBS; CNN; The Washington Post; CNBC). A separate order from Judge Thompson barring the Justice Department and Homeland Security from threatening election officials runs to Nov. 16; the government’s appeal is pending.

What’s next For markets the question is less who wins than how quickly the result is known and whether it is contested. A divided Congress has usually meant less new fiscal legislation; a Democratic sweep would put the 2027 tariff and tax fights in a different light. Investors will watch Indiana’s 5th District and the Kentucky and North Carolina Senate calls as early signals.

Oil rebounds 2% after US “defensive” strikes near Hormuz, recovering part of Monday’s 7% fall

The market is caught between talks that Iran says could reopen the strait in two days and military exchanges that have not stopped.

At about 6:30 AM, January WTI was $98.40, up $1.94 (2.0%) from Monday’s settlement of $96.46, and January Brent $106.40, up 1.6% from $104.77 (Yahoo Finance, delayed). Monday’s fall was crude’s largest since July (NBC News).

A US official told the Associated Press that American forces carried out “defensive strikes” on Iranian targets in the strait on Monday, described in other reports as a response to Iranian anti-aircraft fire and aimed at radar and missile launchers on Qeshm Island (Anadolu; NewsNation). Iran’s Revolutionary Guards claim to have shot down two or three US target drones; that is unverified, and there is no confirmed casualty count. The same AP report said Washington would not launch wider strikes before the election. Iran’s foreign ministry repeats that the strait could reopen within two days if the US meets its conditions, which include ending the naval blockade, a new transit route agreed with Oman and lifting sanctions imposed since August (Euronews). Commercial traffic is still close to a halt: the IMF’s PortWatch counted two transits on Oct. 25 against about 85 a day before the crisis.

Why it matters Oil is the main channel from this war to US inflation and the Fed. Today’s bounce shows how little of Monday’s fall rests on anything signed. Our call: front-month WTI settles today above Monday’s $96.46 (60–72%), given it is nearly $2 above that level eight hours before the settlement. Our call last night, a settlement below $100, still stands.

Yahoo Finance (futures, delayed); NBC News; AP via The Washington Times; Anadolu; NewsNation; Euronews; IMF PortWatch as cited by Straits Daily Brief.

Australia raises rates for the fifth time this year, to 4.85%, on a split vote

The Bank of England is widely expected to follow on Thursday. Outside the US, the world’s central banks are still tightening against the oil shock.

The Reserve Bank of Australia raised its cash rate a quarter point to 4.85% at 2:30 PM Sydney time (10:30 PM Eastern Monday), with six board members in favour and three preferring to hold at 4.60%, after third-quarter inflation of 4.3% (ABC; SBS). The Australian share market rose 0.3% afterwards, which suggests the increase was fully expected. In a Reuters poll, 54 of 63 economists expect the Bank of England to raise its rate from 3.75% to 4.00% on Thursday.

ABC (Australia); SBS; savings.com.au; Reuters poll as reported; Yahoo Finance.

Russia launches one of its largest strikes of the war on Ukraine’s power grid as the US votes

President Zelensky had predicted the timing. US-mediated working-level talks are now planned in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10–11.

Russia fired 289 attack drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles overnight, mainly at energy targets in nine regions, according to Mr. Zelensky; emergency power cuts followed, and in Kharkiv between 62 and 73 people were injured, with reports differing (Kyiv Post; agency reports). Mr. Zelensky had warned Russia would strike “when the world will be watching the American midterms.” Separately, the toll from a Russian strike on a humanitarian aid point in the Zaporizhzhia region rose to at least 29, with 48 missing (NPR). Steve Witkoff says the US will mediate working-level Ukraine–Russia talks in Abu Dhabi on Nov. 10–11 with Jared Kushner attending (Axios; The National); reports differ on whether Moscow has confirmed the dates.

The Economy

Job openings at 10. JOLTS for September is the day’s only major US release. August showed 7.08 million openings. We found no wire-service consensus; the most popular bucket on one prediction market is 7.1–7.2 million. It is a level, not a growth rate, and comes with a month’s lag. The useful details are the quits rate, which tracks wage pressure, and layoffs. Our call: openings print between 6.8 and 7.4 million (60–75%), given August’s 7.08 million and the typical monthly move.

The Fed. Target range 3.75–4.00%; interest on reserves 3.90%. Two dissenters at the last meeting wanted an increase. We found no Fed speakers scheduled today; schedules for Thursday and Friday differ between sources. The inflation backdrop: September CPI rose 3.7% from a year earlier and 0.6% on the month, while core CPI rose 2.6%, so the pressure is concentrated in energy (FRED). PCE inflation was 3.6% and core PCE 3.1% (Oct. 29). Unemployment was 4.2% in September, and payrolls rose 29,000 (FRED). Initial jobless claims were 194,000 in the week to Oct. 24, near cycle lows.

Risks & Scenarios

Base case — a cautious, narrow day (about 50–60%). Apple and Palantir weigh on the big indexes while the equal-weighted market holds up, and trading thins out ahead of the evening. The S&P 500 closes within 1% of Monday’s 7,541.40.

Downside — the chip selling deepens (about 25–35%). Apple’s margin warning spreads to other hardware names and the S&P 500 closes more than 1% lower. Signals: SMH below Monday’s $508.31, the VIX above 23, and the high-yield spread past 3.5 points when it next prints.

Upside — relief (about 10–20%). The S&P 500 closes more than 1% higher, which would probably need lower oil and a soft JOLTS print pulling yields down further.

The overnight tail. Two events land after the close: AMD’s forecast, and an election night in which a slow count or a contested result in the Senate could keep volatility high into Friday’s jobs report. The Iran risk has not gone: US officials have said wider strikes would not come before the election, which says nothing about after it. Our Sunday range for US strikes on Iranian territory between Nov. 4 and 7 (15–30%) stands; Monday’s strikes were on Iranian positions in the strait and were described as defensive, and we do not count them.

Science

Green space and children’s attention. A Washington University study in Environment International, using the large US ABCD cohort, links more nearby green space to better directed attention and crystallized intelligence in middle childhood. It is peer-reviewed but observational: it shows an association, not that parks cause the difference, and wealthier neighbourhoods tend to have more green space (Phys.org).

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Allegheny County votes on council funding; mail ballots can be dropped only downtown today

The county question asks whether to repeal the Home Rule Charter section that bars county money for council members’ personal staff, district offices and benefits; a yes vote permits funding later but spends nothing now. A separate question to lift council’s budget cap was withdrawn in July. On Election Day, unreturned mail ballots can be dropped off only at the County Office Building’s Ross Street entrance, 7 AM to 8 PM; voters can also surrender an unused mail ballot at their polling place and vote in person. Pre-canvassing of mail ballots begins at 7 AM; provisional ballots are reviewed from Friday. About four dozen polling places have moved since the May primary, according to one report, so check your location. Justice Department civil-rights monitors will observe in Allegheny, Philadelphia, Luzerne and Lehigh counties; they may not interfere with voting.

Allegheny County Elections Division ballot-question notice and Election Updates page; Axios Pittsburgh; Penn Capital-Star; Spotlight PA.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Mayor O’Connor’s 2027 budget goes to council next week with a $26 million draw on reserves

The proposed city budget is $728 million for operations and $145 million for capital projects, with no tax increase and no layoffs. It draws about $26 million from reserves, and on the city’s own projections the rainy-day fund runs dry around 2031. It trims the Stop the Violence and Housing Opportunity trust funds, which residents objected to at a public hearing. The mayor formally introduces it to council on Nov. 9 (WESA; The Tartan).

WESA; WPXI; The Tartan (public hearing).
