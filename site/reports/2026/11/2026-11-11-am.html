CoreWeave’s beat lifts Nasdaq futures nearly 2% as Fed officials talk up more hikes

Wednesday, November 11, 2026 · 31 min read

Technology stocks are set to rebound after CoreWeave raised its outlook and Nvidia rose before the bell. Two more Fed officials sounded hawkish overnight, and stocks trade a full session today while the bond market is shut for Veterans Day.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The tape. At 6:28 AM, Nasdaq-100 futures were up 1.9% and S&P 500 futures 0.9%. Dow futures were up only 0.2%, so the move is concentrated in technology. CoreWeave was up 13–15% before the open and Nvidia about 5%.
  • The macro thread. After Tuesday’s hot CPI, Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said at least two more quarter-point hikes may be needed. Chicago’s Austan Goolsbee called the report “borderline disturbing.” Futures put a December hike at near-certainty and a half-point move at about 10%.
  • Rates. Cash Treasuries do not trade today, so Tuesday’s levels stand: two-year 4.73%, 10-year 4.98%, 30-year 5.35%. The next test is Thursday’s $25 billion 30-year auction.
  • Oil. Brent is up 1.4% to about $92.80. Attacks on shipping in Hormuz have continued, and Iran denies it is in talks with the U.S.
  • Biggest risk. Stocks rallying on AI news while the Fed signals more tightening. If Thursday’s 30-year sale tails again, equities face 5% long yields without a bond rally to cushion them.
  • Beyond markets. The House voted 211–204 to send the impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, which ends them for this Congress. In Abu Dhabi, Russia and Ukraine agreed in principle on another large prisoner exchange.

Top Stories

CoreWeave’s raised outlook puts the AI trade back in front before the open

The AI cloud provider is up 13–15% before the bell. Nasdaq futures are up nearly 2% and chip stocks in Seoul and Taipei rallied overnight.

CoreWeave reported third-quarter revenue of $3.66 billion after Tuesday’s close, against forecasts near $3.54 billion. It raised its 2026 revenue outlook to $13.0–13.3 billion, and its backlog rose to about $132.5 billion. Reuters reports the company expects to turn an adjusted profit in the first half of 2028. Its chief executive said he sees “no reason” for any change in its relationship with Nvidia, which is both a supplier and an investor. CNBC, Benzinga and Investing.com put the stock 13–15% higher before the open. It closed Tuesday at $48.73, 34% below its 50-day average, after losing about 40% in a month.

The report landed in a market that had spent a week asking whether the AI build-out could still be financed. At 6:28 AM on Yahoo Finance’s delayed feed, Nasdaq-100 futures were up 1.91% at 28,366 and S&P 500 futures up 0.89% at 7,611.25. Dow futures were up only 0.15%, so the bid is concentrated in technology. Overseas, Taiwan’s Taiex closed up 3.6% and South Korea’s Kospi 2.6%, led by chipmakers including SK Hynix. Benzinga also credited Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s Tuesday speech on China, covered below. It was the only outlet to make that link.

Why it matters CoreWeave spends heavily on Nvidia hardware and borrows to do it: capital spending is guided at $37–39 billion and debt is nearly $47 billion. A raised outlook from the most leveraged buyer in the chain tells investors that demand is holding. It does not settle whether that spending earns a return at current interest rates. The names most exposed to the answer are the chip, networking and power suppliers: NVDA, AVGO, ANET, VRT and VST.
What’s next Cisco and Applied Materials report Thursday after the close, and Nvidia reports next week. Whether the rally holds into the close will show if this is conviction or a short squeeze. On Tuesday 52% of CoreWeave’s off-exchange volume was short sales.

CoreWeave release (primary) · CNBC, Reuters, Benzinga, Investing.com (premarket) · Yahoo Finance futures and index data (delayed) · FINRA daily short volume.

Logan and Goolsbee join Waller in sounding hawkish after the CPI report

Dallas’s Lorie Logan sees at least two more hikes and says the Fed may need to move faster this winter. Chicago’s Austan Goolsbee called October’s inflation report “borderline disturbing.”

Reuters reported Logan’s view on Tuesday: “at least two more” quarter-point increases, and “it may be possible to move more rapidly this winter.” She added that she expects the PCE price index, the Fed’s preferred measure, to come in hot as well. On PBS NewsHour that evening, Goolsbee said services inflation was his main concern (Bloomberg). Those remarks follow Governor Christopher Waller’s statement in London that a half-point December hike “should be considered” if core CPI came in at 0.3% or higher, which it did.

Reported CME FedWatch readings this morning put the probability of a quarter-point hike on December 9 at about 88–91% and a half-point move at about 9–11%. That adds up to near-certainty of some hike; a week ago the market saw essentially no chance of 50 bp. Goldman Sachs has reportedly added a third quarter-point hike, for March 2027. We found that only in secondary accounts. Fed officials are not scheduled to speak today, a federal holiday. Cleveland’s Beth Hammack speaks Thursday at 9 AM.

Why it matters Two-year yields rose 17 bp on Tuesday as the market moved its hikes forward. A futures rally in long-duration tech stocks now runs against that. If the Fed delivers 50 bp, the front end (two-year notes, SGOV, SHY) and the dollar have the most to reprice, and growth stocks are the most exposed.

Reuters, Bloomberg, PBS NewsHour (via Bloomberg), CNBC · CME FedWatch as reported by Investing.com, Seeking Alpha, CNBC.

Nvidia rises about 5% before the bell after a two-day, 9.5% slide

The board’s inquiry into Jensen Huang is unchanged overnight. A report of tighter export controls is still single-sourced.

CNBC and TipRanks had Nvidia around $177 before the open, up about 5% from Tuesday’s close of $168.89. That close was the lowest since March and $4.62 above its 52-week low. We found no new statement from the company or the board since Sunday. Nvidia says the inquiry is run by independent directors with outside counsel. House committee chairs have asked Huang for a closed-door briefing on November 18. Separately, The Information reported that the administration is weighing new export controls on lithography tools and on remote access by Chinese firms to advanced chips. Single source Other outlets have only repeated that report. Morgan Stanley has reportedly removed Nvidia from its top-pick list, according to one account.

Nvidia’s off-exchange short-sale ratio on Tuesday was 34%, among the lowest in our watchlist. Much of the selling looked like holders getting out rather than new short positions.

CNBC, TipRanks (premarket) · Nvidia statement as reported · The Information via Investing.com and Yahoo Finance (single origin) · FINRA daily short volume.

Bankers are reported to be weighing an Anthropic price above the $78 top of the range

The Financial Times says the IPO book was covered on its first day. A price above the range would value the company at more than $1.5 trillion.

Citing five people familiar with the deal, the FT reports that underwriters are considering pricing above the $70–78 range filed on Monday. TipRanks and CNBC report the book was covered across the range on its first day. All of these accounts rest on the FT’s unnamed sources, and Anthropic has not commented. The filed terms are 825 million shares, raising up to $64.4 billion at the top of the range. Pricing is expected November 18 and trading on Nasdaq as “ANT” on November 19. An earlier draft circulated to banks on November 9, showing a $70–80 range on more shares, was withdrawn as inaccurate. Anthropic is private until it lists, so there is no market price. Anthropic also released new workplace tools on Tuesday, covered under AI & Technology.

Why it matters Strong demand for what would be one of the largest listings on record would support today’s futures move. It would also pull tens of billions of dollars from the same investors who own the chip names.

Financial Times via TipRanks, Benzinga, Seeking Alpha · amended S-1 as reported · City AM, Nairametrics on the withdrawn draft.

Bessent: “de-risk, not decouple” from China, with a planned channel for AI incidents

The Treasury Secretary’s New York speech set a softer tone a week before Presidents Trump and Xi are expected to meet at APEC in Shenzhen.

Bessent said Washington’s aim is to reduce specific risks rather than sever economic ties, and outlined a planned U.S.–China channel for alerting each other to serious AI incidents (South China Morning Post, Bloomberg). China’s ambassador, Xie Feng, said Beijing has “red lines.” The speech followed Beijing’s two-month extension, effective Tuesday, of its suspension of the extra 24% tariff and of rare-earth export curbs.

Why it matters Lower odds of a new trade escalation help the exporters and chip names most exposed to China: AAPL, TSM, ASML and the Hang Seng tech sector. Hong Kong did not share the relief this morning; the Hang Seng Tech index fell 1.4%.

South China Morning Post, Bloomberg, Benzinga · The Standard (Hong Kong).

House sidelines the Hegseth impeachment articles, 211–204

The referral to committee ends the articles for this Congress. Democrats say they will return to them after taking the majority in January.

On Tuesday evening the House voted 211–204 to refer Rep. Thomas Massie’s eight privileged articles, which allege War Powers violations in the Iran war, to the Judiciary Committee. Many outlets described it as tabling, but the motion was a referral. Massie was the only Republican voting against it, and no Democrat voted for it. Seven Republicans and 11 Democrats did not vote. Hegseth called the effort a “farce.” President Trump posted that it had “miserably FAILED!” Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries said, “It’s just the beginning.” Removal from office would require two-thirds of the Senate in any case.

CBS News, CNN, CNBC, Fox News, The Hill, PBS (multiple; roll call not retrieved).

Russia and Ukraine agree in principle on another large prisoner exchange as Abu Dhabi talks end

Two days of U.S.-mediated talks produced no breakthrough on territory. Russian strikes on Ukraine’s power grid continued.

Russia’s lead negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said the sides reached a “fundamental agreement on another large-scale prisoner exchange,” with details to follow (TASS, NV). Sources quoted by Interfax-Ukraine put it at 1,000 for 1,000 or more. That figure is unofficial. Rustem Umerov led Ukraine’s team, and Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner represented the United States. Reports from Reuters and AP describe no movement on the Donbas. Ukraine’s prime minister said Russia hit six large electrical substations in four central regions. Gulf News reported that U.S.–Russian military contacts resumed in Abu Dhabi for the first time in five years.

TASS, NV, Interfax-Ukraine · Reuters via NBC, AP via Boston Globe · Gulf News.

Before the Open

What futures are pricing. A 0.9% S&P futures gain points to an index open near 7,590, back above Monday’s close of 7,549.94 and within 3.5% of the 52-week high. The Nasdaq-100’s 1.9% gain would recover more than all of Tuesday’s loss. Russell 2000 futures are up 0.75% and Dow futures only 0.15%, so this is a technology rebound, not a broad move into risk. Futures are not a guaranteed open.

The curve is frozen at Tuesday’s close. The two-year sits at 4.73% after a 17 bp jump, the 10-year at 4.98% and the 30-year at 5.35%. The 2s10s spread is 25 bp, down from 38 bp on November 5, and 3m10y is 68 bp. The 10-year breakeven was 2.20% on Tuesday (FRED) and the 10-year real yield 2.66% on Monday. From November 5 through Monday, the real yield rose while breakevens edged down, so the selloff is about the Fed, not inflation expectations. No par-yield curve will be published for today.

Volatility. The VIX was quoted at 18.58 early this morning, down from Tuesday’s 19.46 close. On Tuesday the term structure stayed in normal order: nine-day 18.54, 30-day 19.46, three-month 20.64. The options market is not priced for a shock.

Dollar, oil, gold, credit, bitcoin. The ICE dollar index is 102.52, near the top of its 52-week range (95.55–102.95), as markets price a Fed that keeps hiking. Brent is $92.81 and WTI $86.67. Gold is $4,062, recovering part of Tuesday’s drop. ICE BofA high-yield spreads were 3.34 percentage points on Monday, from 3.48 on November 5, so credit shows no stress. Bitcoin is near $74,000; it has not joined the equity rebound and remains the weak spot in risk assets.

Positioning and flows. Off-exchange short-sale volume across all names was 48.5% of trading on Tuesday, up from 46.7% on Monday. Short-sale shares were high in IWM (67%) and SPY (58%), which usually reflects hedging and market-making. Breadth was poor under a mild index loss: 1,822 stocks rose and 3,561 fell, with two-thirds of volume in decliners. Only 43% of liquid stocks are above their 50-session average.

The most fragile assumption. The tape assumes AI demand can carry tech stocks through a hiking cycle. That assumption breaks if the 30-year auction tails on Thursday and long yields hold above 5.3%. It also breaks if Friday’s PPI matches forecasts of +0.4%, which would put a half-point December move back in play. Watch whether Nvidia holds above $175 and whether the equal-weight index joins the rally. Without either, this is a short-covering bounce.

The Economy

No data today. Veterans Day is a federal holiday. Federal agencies are closed and no major U.S. releases are scheduled. The weekly government oil inventory report is expected to slip to Thursday.

Inflation. All of October’s CPI figures are month-over-month: headline +0.35% and core +0.30% on FRED index levels. No year-over-year rate is available because there is no October 2025 index, a gap left by the shutdown. Core has run about 0.3% a month for three months, a 3.7% annualized pace. The Fed. The hawkish shift is covered in Top Stories. The December 8–9 meeting is treated as a near-certain hike, and the question is its size. SOFR was 3.88% on Monday.

Bill auctions next week are $115 billion of 4-week, $110 billion of 8-week and $75 billion of 17-week bills, all settling November 17.

Risks & Scenarios

Base case: the tech rebound holds (about 65–80%). The S&P 500 closes above Tuesday’s 7,520.93, led by chips and AI infrastructure. CoreWeave keeps most of its premarket gain and Nvidia closes higher. Signals: SMH holds a gain of more than 1%, and the VIX stays under 20.

Downside: the rally fades (about 20–30%). Without a bond market to anchor them, stocks drift lower as the Fed’s hawkish chorus sinks in. Nasdaq gives back more than half its premarket gain and the equal-weight index stays flat or worse, repeating Tuesday’s weak breadth. Signals: Nvidia below $172 and CoreWeave up less than 5% by midday.

The tails. In the Gulf, a strike or mine incident in Hormuz could add $5 or more to Brent quickly. Ten attacks on shipping so far this month show the strike pause has not made shipping safe. In rates, a weak 30-year auction on Thursday would reopen the duration question just as futures price several hikes.

Our calls. We put 65–80% on the S&P 500 closing above 7,520.93 today, given futures up 0.9% two and a half hours before the open. We put 60–75% on CoreWeave closing at least 8% above Tuesday’s $48.73, since it is up 13–15% premarket and stocks this volatile often give back part of such a gain. We put 70–85% on Nvidia closing above $168.89, with the stock up about 5% premarket and no new negative disclosure. We put 55–70% on Friday’s October PPI showing a headline gain of at least 0.3% month over month, given a reported consensus of 0.4% and estimates ranging from 0.2% to 0.4%.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

O’Connor’s first city budget: $872.6 million, no tax increase, and a draw on the rainy-day fund

Mayor Corey O’Connor presented a 2027 budget to City Council on Monday. It totals about $872.6 million, roughly $727 million operating and $145 million capital, with no new taxes and no layoffs. That follows the 20% property-tax increase council approved for 2026. The plan draws about $28 million from reserves (the city and WESA; PublicSource reports about $27 million) and cuts the pension contribution from $112 million to $105 million. It also ends transfers to the housing and anti-violence trust funds, redirecting about $12 million from the Stop the Violence fund. The administration says the goal is to close a projected $250 million five-year gap. A public hearing is set for December 10, with council’s vote later in December. Allegheny County’s own 2027 budget, also with no tax increase, is in council committee ahead of a December vote.

City of Pittsburgh release (primary) · WESA, WTAE, PublicSource, WPXI · Allegheny County release.
