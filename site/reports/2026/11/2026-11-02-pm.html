Top Stories

Oil falls about 7%, its biggest drop in months, as Iran and the US keep talking through Qatar

This morning’s 4% fall nearly doubled by the settlement. Nothing physical changed: the strait is still restricted and the US blockade is still in place. What changed is the market’s estimate of the odds of a deal.

January West Texas Intermediate settled at about $96.46 a barrel, down about 7.4% from Friday’s $104.12, and January Brent at about $104.77, down about 7.6% from $113.43, according to settlement figures reported by Finimize; Oilprice.com and an OilPriceAPI snapshot at 2:56 PM put both within about 50 cents of those levels. Yahoo Finance had WTI at $96.13 at 4:09 PM. WTI is back where it traded in mid-October, and the United States Oil Fund fell 7.1% on 1.6 times its normal volume.

The news was diplomatic. Iran’s foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said “very intensive” talks with the United States were going on through mediators and that the outcome depends on Washington’s answers; he repeated that the Strait of Hormuz could reopen within two days of a deal. His spokesman, Esmaeil Baghaei, said responsibility now lies “entirely with Washington” and that the strait cannot reopen while the US naval blockade continues (France 24). Iran’s delegation left Doha on Sunday after three days of indirect talks; Reuters reports a draft is circulating and a follow-up meeting is possible, while The Wall Street Journal reports the Qatar channel has stalled. President Trump said there would be no new strikes before Tuesday’s vote and that he was in “no rush,” but that the war would end “very soon, one way or the other” and the US might have to “give them the hammer.”

The Iranian plan, as reported, asks for a regional ceasefire including Lebanon, guarantees against new strikes, an end to the blockade, the lifting of oil sanctions and released assets, with nuclear questions deferred to a second phase. Mr. Trump has rejected deferring the nuclear issue. Israel’s prime minister is reported to be pressing for a return to full-scale strikes; that rests on one secondary outlet.

Why it matters

Oil above $100 has been this autumn’s main inflation pressure and the main reason futures price a December Fed increase. Today it pulled energy shares down hard: the energy sector fund fell 4.0%, Diamondback 5.8% and Williams 4.3%. But the December hike odds barely moved (about 75%, as reported), which says the bond market is not yet treating this as durable. Analysts quoted by CNBC and Raymond James warned of “false dawns,” and some see Brent back at $120 if talks fail after the election.

What’s next

Election night, then the post-vote decision Mr. Trump has signalled. Our call: front-month WTI settles below $100 on Tuesday (55–70%), given today’s ~$96.50 settlement, the stated pause in strikes through the vote and no sign of a break in talks.

Finimize, Oilprice.com and OilPriceAPI (settlements, as reported); Yahoo Finance (futures and fund closes); France 24; Reuters via CNBC; The Wall Street Journal as reported; Fox News live coverage; HNGN; Quartz.

Marvell bids $115 billion for Lumentum and Oracle raises $20 billion, and the chip sell-off resumes

The morning’s bounce in semiconductors did not survive the open. Two deals that look like confidence in AI demand were sold as signs of how expensive it has become to keep up.

Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum, which makes the lasers and optical parts that connect AI servers, in an all-stock deal: 4.5 Marvell shares for each Lumentum share, an implied enterprise value of about $115 billion and, the companies said, a 23.9% premium to Lumentum’s Oct. 28 close. It is expected to close in the first half of 2028 (Lumentum 8-K; Yahoo Finance). Because the price is paid in Marvell stock, the value fell with it: Marvell closed down 12.7% at $171.41, its third straight heavy loss (Benzinga counts −27.7% over three sessions, its worst since 2001), and Lumentum fell 18.6% to $684.40, well below the headline offer.

Oracle fell 8.8% to $119.22 after filing to raise $20 billion, $10 billion in new shares and $10 billion in senior notes, to pay for its AI build-out (MarketBeat; Investing.com). Memory makers fell after a cautious outlook from Japan’s Kioxia: Micron −5.8%, Western Digital −7.5%, Sandisk −6.6%. Intel fell another 8.8% to $59.97 on twice its normal volume, after Friday’s 28.6% loss. AMD, which reports Tuesday, fell 7.5%; Dell fell 7.4%. Nvidia (−1.3%), TSMC (−1.8%) and Broadcom (−1.2%) held up better (Yahoo Finance).

Outside technology it was a good day. The equal-weighted S&P 500 fund rose 0.8% to within 2% of its 52-week high, consumer discretionary rose 2.4%, financials 1.9% and regional banks 2.4%. Amazon rose 2.2% and Tesla 1.0%.

Why it matters

For a week the market punished the companies spending on AI; since Friday it has also marked down the companies selling to them. Oracle selling shares and Meta selling about $35 billion of stock last week are the same signal: the build-out now needs outside capital, and investors are pricing in dilution and lower returns. The semiconductor fund SMH is 11% below its 50-day average.

Lumentum 8-K (SEC EDGAR); Yahoo Finance (closes and volume); Benzinga; MarketBeat; Investing.com; 24/7 Wall St. (Kioxia read-through); Kiplinger and CNBC (market close).

Apple beats with record September-quarter revenue in John Ternus’s first report as chief executive

Revenue rose 12% and iPhone sales hit a September-quarter record. The forecast for the holiday quarter, which matters more, comes on the call after this edition went to press.

Apple reported fiscal fourth-quarter revenue of $114.5 billion, up 12% from a year earlier, and diluted earnings of $2.10 a share, up 14% (8-K, filed 4:30 PM). Analysts’ estimates compiled by LSEG were about $113.4 billion and $1.99, as reported by CNBC. iPhone revenue was $57.6 billion and services an all-time high of $31.6 billion, in line with estimates; iPad revenue fell to $6.2 billion from $7.0 billion, and Greater China revenue of $17.5 billion was below the roughly $18.8 billion expected, as reported.

Gross margin was 47.9%, including about one percentage point from tariff refunds; tariff refunds also added 5 cents to earnings. Fiscal-year revenue was a record $479 billion. Apple generated $28 billion in operating cash flow in the quarter and returned $28 billion to shareholders, and declared a dividend of 27 cents. Mr. Ternus pointed to the new iPhone Duo, the iPhone 18 Pro models, touchscreen Macs and an “all-new Siri AI” for the holiday season.

The shares fell 1.8% in regular trading to $338.32. We could not confirm a reliable after-hours move before writing.

Why it matters

Apple is the biggest test of whether expensive memory chips, which Intel blamed for weak PC demand, are squeezing device makers. The quarter’s margin was flattered by tariff refunds; the December-quarter margin guidance will show the underlying trend. Analysts expected about $2.90 a share on about $152 billion of revenue for the holiday quarter (Investing.com preview).

Apple 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (SEC EDGAR); CNBC (LSEG estimates); Yahoo Finance; Investing.com.

Palantir’s revenue nearly doubles and it raises its forecast again; the shares had fallen 3% in the day

US commercial revenue overtook US government revenue for the first time.

Palantir reported third-quarter revenue of about $2.31 billion, up 96% from a year earlier, against estimates near $2.18 billion, and adjusted earnings of 44 cents a share against 41 cents. US commercial revenue rose 148% to $984 million. It forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $2.649–2.653 billion and raised its full-year forecast to $8.530–8.534 billion from $8.150–8.158 billion, with adjusted free cash flow of $4.8–5.0 billion (company release; CNBC). The stock fell 3.1% to $178.34 in regular trading; early after-hours reports conflicted, one showing a gain of about 1.6% and a later one a loss of about 3%.

Palantir release (Business Wire/SEC 8-K); CNBC; Benzinga; 24/7 Wall St.; Yahoo Finance.

The Treasury trims this quarter’s borrowing estimate to $618 billion and sees $800 billion next quarter

Wednesday’s refunding statement, which sets auction sizes, is now the main event for bonds after the election.

The Treasury expects to borrow $618 billion in privately held net marketable debt in October–December, assuming it ends December with $850 billion in cash. That is $10 billion less than it estimated in August; the Treasury says that excluding a higher-than-assumed starting cash balance, borrowing is $24 billion more than August’s estimate. For January–March it expects $800 billion, assuming a $1 trillion cash balance at the end of March. In July–September it borrowed $742 billion, close to the $739 billion it had estimated, and ended September with $984 billion in cash against an assumed $950 billion (Treasury press release, 3 PM).

Today’s bill auctions went smoothly: $95 billion of 13-week bills sold at 4.040% and $82 billion of 26-week bills at 4.150%, with bid-to-cover ratios of 2.88 and 2.89 (TreasuryDirect).

What’s next

The refunding statement at 8:30 AM Wednesday sets note and bond sizes for November–January and updates buybacks. The numbers today give no reason to raise coupon sizes; the question is whether the Treasury keeps its promise not to for “at least the next several quarters.” Our Sunday edition logged a 5–15% chance of larger sizes or softer guidance, and we are not changing it.

US Treasury, marketable borrowing estimates (press release, Nov. 2); TreasuryDirect auction results.

Factories grow faster in October and price pressures ease, though builders pulled back sharply

The ISM index rose to 55.5, its tenth month of expansion, with production at its highest since 2021.

The Institute for Supply Management’s October manufacturing index rose to 55.5 from 54.5, above forecasts of about 54.5–54.7. Readings above 50 mean activity is growing, so this is a faster pace of growth, not a level. Production rose 2.3 points to 59.0, the highest since November 2021; new orders edged up to 55.5; employment slipped to 52.4. The prices-paid index fell 2.7 points to 75.2, its biggest monthly decline in the series’ recent run but still far above 50, so input prices are rising more slowly, not falling. ISM said two-thirds of respondents’ comments were negative, citing tariffs, the war and lead times (ISM via PR Newswire).

Construction spending fell 1.9% in September to a $2.19 trillion annual rate, against an expected 0.3% gain, with residential spending down 5.5%; August was revised (Census Bureau via Reuters/Investing.com). Spending figures are nominal, so part of any change is prices.

Institute for Supply Management (PR Newswire); US Census Bureau via Investing.com and Rock Products; TD Economics.

The country votes tomorrow; markets and forecasters lean towards a Democratic House

All 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships are on the ballot. A Michigan endorsement and court fights over election administration marked the final day.

At noon, prices on the Kalshi prediction market gave Democrats a 94% chance of winning the House and 74% for the Senate; those are trader prices, not polls or forecasts. Our morning edition’s model range was 84–95% for the House and 64–78% for the Senate. More than 57 million ballots had been cast early as of Monday afternoon, according to the University of Florida Election Lab as cited by CBS and NBC; this morning’s figure was 46 million by Friday, and we could not confirm the newer number at the source.

President Trump gave a closing speech at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, saying Republicans “gave this a very strong shot” and noting that the president’s party historically “doesn’t win the midterms”; Vice President Vance held rallies in Ohio and New York (NBC; CBS). In Michigan, Rep. Haley Stevens, who lost the Democratic Senate primary, endorsed Republican Mike Rogers over Democrat Abdul El-Sayed; Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called it a “despicable betrayal,” and the Detroit Free Press reports more than half her staff quit. El-Sayed says it will not matter to voters.

The Justice Department’s appeal of Judge Trina Thompson’s order, which bars federal pressure on election officials over noncitizen-voting claims until Nov. 16, had no Ninth Circuit ruling that we could find by the close. Los Angeles County election workers are still due before a federal grand jury on Wednesday; the department says election officials are not targets.

Why it matters

Investors are weighing what a split or Democratic Congress means for tax policy, technology regulation and the stopgap budget that runs out on Dec. 11. Polls close from 6 PM Eastern; close House races and California’s count may take days.

Kalshi; University of Florida Election Lab as cited by CBS and NBC; NBC and CBS live coverage; Detroit News; Detroit Free Press as reported; CNN; NBC (DOJ appeal); CalMatters.

Binance pushes its full proof of reserves to Tuesday and says 95% of stolen user assets are replaced

Withdrawals are running well below the first day’s pace, but a second day of outflows took bitcoin back below $77,000.

Binance says a zero-knowledge proof of its reserves is complete for ten assets, including bitcoin, ether, Tether and USDC, but the full proof slipped from Monday to Tuesday (UTC) because generating it takes 40–50 hours of computing, chief executive Richard Teng said (The Block). The company now says about 95% of the affected user assets have been replenished, up from 90% on Sunday; that is its own figure. Net withdrawals were $3.64 billion in the first 9.5 hours after the restart, about 2.1% of $171 billion in assets, and DefiLlama’s 24-hour net-outflow count has since fallen to about $3.3 billion from a peak near $7 billion (The Block; CNBC). The FBI attributes the roughly $6.3 billion theft to North Korean hackers. Bitcoin was at $76,811 at 5:05 PM, down 1.2% on the day, and Coinbase shares fell 4.0%.

The Block; CNBC; Unchained; Yahoo Finance (bitcoin, Coinbase).

What Moved Markets

The open. Stocks opened higher, extending the overnight bounce in futures, with oil already down more than 4%. Midday headlines, as Yahoo Finance put it, were of stocks rallying as oil prices tumbled on diplomatic hopes. Likely contributor Lower oil, which helped airlines, retailers and other energy users.

Morning. The ISM factory index at 10:00 came in above forecasts with prices paid lower — good news for growth and slightly good news for inflation. Yields edged lower. Likely contributor to the gains in industrials (+1.4%) and materials (+1.8%).

Midday. The chip sell-off resumed. Marvell’s all-stock bid for Lumentum, Oracle’s $20 billion capital raise and Kioxia’s cautious outlook all landed on the same group; technology finished down 3.0% and the semiconductor index 3.4%. Confirmed catalyst for Marvell, Lumentum and Oracle, each of which moved on its own filing. Market narrative for the wider group: the story investors appeared to focus on is that AI spending now needs outside money, but no single event explains Intel’s further 8.8% fall.

The close. Oil kept falling into its 2:30 PM settlement and energy stocks with it (−4.0%). The Nasdaq ended at its low end; the Dow and the equal-weighted S&P 500 ended higher. The VIX rose to 21.15 even with the Dow up, and the short-dated VIX moved above the three-month, consistent with hedging ahead of election night. Market narrative for the volatility bid: pre-election hedging is the common explanation, but we have no flow data to confirm it.

Rates and the dollar. The 10-year yield fell about 4 basis points to roughly 4.89% and the 2-year was little changed, so the curve flattened slightly. The dollar index slipped 0.1% to 101.78 and the euro was near $1.119. Gold rose about 1.5% to about $3,956 in futures after Friday’s one-year low. Likely contributor for yields: lower oil reduces inflation risk; but the December hike odds barely moved.

Overseas. Europe closed higher on lower energy prices: the Stoxx 600 rose 0.4%, Germany’s DAX 0.6% and London’s FTSE 100 0.3% (Yahoo Finance; early in the session Reuters had reported banks at three-month lows). Asia closed earlier: Kospi +2.2%, Nikkei −0.3%.

Ebola screening. From 12:00 AM on Wednesday, every passenger on an international flight to the United States must sign a statement that they have not been in the Democratic Republic of Congo in the past 21 days, the CDC says (TravelPulse). The Bundibugyo outbreak there is the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record, with more than 9,700 confirmed cases and 4,690 deaths as of late October; there are no US cases.

Russian strikes on a school used as an aid-distribution point in Komyshuvakha, in Zaporizhzhia region, killed at least 24 people and injured five, Ukrainian officials say; the site was hit several times from early Monday. Science

Psilocybin, tested five times. A study published today in Nature Neuroscience ran the same psilocybin experiment in mice in five independent laboratories. A single dose reliably reduced fear expression in the short term, but the lasting effects reported in earlier single-lab studies came out “modest or inconsistent.” Replication studies like this are rare and are how a field finds out which results hold; this one does not test people.

A blood test for dementia risk. An international study in JAMA (Oct. 29) found that the blood marker p-tau217 predicts cognitive decline: people in the highest-risk group had about a 38% chance of impairment within five years. The authors caution that their ten-year estimate (about 78%) rests on far fewer people.

Local

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Pittsburgh’s first O’Connor budget heads to council with cuts to two trust funds and a draw on reserves

Mayor Corey O’Connor’s proposed 2027 budget, $728 million in operating spending and $145 million in capital, is about $13 million smaller than this year’s, with no tax increase and no layoffs. It draws about $26 million from reserves and cuts contributions to the Stop the Violence and Housing Opportunity trust funds and to pensions, while adding money for paving, vehicles and landslides. The mayor warns the city would run out of money by about 2032 on its current path; Controller Rachael Heisler has warned against cutting pension payments or letting reserves fall below 10%. Formal introduction to council is expected this month, with votes by year-end (WESA; PublicSource; KDKA Radio).
