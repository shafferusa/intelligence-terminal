Morning Edition

AI Trade Cracks: Nasdaq Falls 5%, Chip Stocks 10%, Erasing the Post-Election Rally

Thursday, November 5, 2026 · 32 min read

A day after cheering a Democratic Congress, investors dumped the AI build-out trade (Micron and AMD down 15%, the S&P 500 down nearly 4% and the VIX at 27), while Treasuries rallied, the Bank of England hiked and oil climbed back on new tanker fires in the Strait of Hormuz.

The Brief

This edition went to press late, at about 2:40 PM ET, after the morning run failed. Market figures are intraday as of about 2:20 PM ET unless stated, and the US session was still open at press time.

If you read nothing else

  • The tape. At 2:22 PM the S&P 500 was down 3.9% at 7,387, the Nasdaq down 5.0% and the Dow down 1,652 points (3.1%), which erases Wednesday’s 1,569-point gain. The Philadelphia semiconductor index was down 9.8%. The equal-weight S&P (RSP, −2.8%) was falling less than the cap-weighted index.
  • The thread. Outlets including CNN, CNBC and Seeking Alpha tie the selloff to fears that a Democratic Congress will slow or regulate the AI data-centre build-out. Qualcomm’s soft guidance and memory-cost worries added to it. Chips, power equipment and Tesla fell hardest, and AppLovin (+30%) was the big exception.
  • Rates and the Fed. Bonds rallied: the 10-year yield was about 4.83%, down 7 basis points. Initial jobless claims fell to 189,000, and Q3 productivity rose 3.6% while unit labour costs fell 0.7%. That is strong growth with less wage pressure, and it comes a day before the October jobs report. Cleveland Fed President Hammack still argued for gradual hikes.
  • Abroad. The Bank of England raised Bank Rate to 4.00% on an 8–1 vote, its first hike in more than three years. The Kospi fell 5.3% on chip stocks, while the Hang Seng rose 3.7%.
  • Biggest risk. Short-dated volatility has jumped above longer-dated volatility (VIX9D 31.4 vs VIX3M 25.0). That inversion is the signature of forced de-risking, and Friday’s payrolls land on a market already under stress.
  • Politics. Speaker Mike Johnson will not seek a Republican leadership post in the next Congress. The Mississippi and Alaska Senate counts remain too close to call.
  • Watch. The 4:00 PM close (forced selling into the bell or a late bounce), then Airbnb, Cloudflare and Monster after the close, and October payrolls at 8:30 AM Friday.

Top Stories

The AI build-out trade breaks: chips down nearly 10%, Nasdaq down 5%

The Democratic sweep first lifted the broad market and sold off AI-power stocks. On Thursday the selling spread from power to the chipmakers themselves: Micron, AMD, Marvell and Arm each fell 12–15%.

By 2:22 PM ET the S&P 500 stood at 7,387.19, down 301 points (3.9%), near its session low of 7,362.77 and well below its early high of 7,681. The Nasdaq Composite was down 5.0% at 24,444, which several outlets note puts it in correction, more than 10% below last month’s record. The Dow was down 1,652 points (3.1%) at 51,704, wiping out Wednesday’s post-election rally. The Russell 2000 was down 4.6%. If the S&P holds near this level, it would be the index’s worst day since April 2025, as one report noted.

The damage was concentrated. The PHLX semiconductor index fell 9.8%, and the VanEck semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell 9.4%. The biggest declines among chip and AI-hardware stocks:

  • AMD −14.9% to $415.95
  • Micron −14.7% to $678.36
  • Marvell −13.4%
  • Arm −11.8%
  • Arista −9.8%
  • TSMC −9.1%
  • Broadcom −8.7%
  • Nvidia −8.4% to $183.34

The power-and-equipment names that led Wednesday’s selling kept falling: Vertiv −14.1%, Quanta −11.8%, GE Vernova −7.6%, Eaton −6.7%, Vistra −5.5%. Tesla lost 11.1% and SpaceX (SPCX) 7.7% to $125.47, its second straight decline. The large AI buyers fell less: Microsoft −3.4%, Amazon −2.5%, Alphabet −2.1%, Apple −1.7%.

The prevailing explanation in coverage from CNN (“midterm results send AI trade into a tailspin”), CNBC, Seeking Alpha and the Irish Times is that a Democratic Congress could slow, tax or regulate the data-centre build-out. Wednesday’s local votes limiting data centres and Vice President Vance’s blame of the Iran war added to that worry. That is a market narrative, not a confirmed catalyst: no bill, regulation or company warning was published today. Two likely contributors are on the record. Qualcomm guided below consensus on memory and component costs (see below). A Yahoo report also linked the memory slump to Middle East turmoil. Selling hit Asia first: the Kospi fell 5.3%, with Samsung down 7.6% and SK Hynix down 9.4%, and in Europe ASML fell 6.6% and Infineon 8.5%.

Why it matters AI hardware and its power supply chain have carried much of this year’s earnings growth and index gains. The index fell more than the average stock (SPY −4.0% vs RSP −2.8%), which shows how much of the S&P’s weight sits in a few crowded trades. Defensives held up: Costco +1.7%, Walmart and CVS flat, staples (XLP) −0.5%.
What’s next The close, then Friday’s payrolls. The open question analysts are posing is whether this is a rotation out of crowded positions or a repricing of AI growth itself. Nvidia’s results later this month are the next hard data point. Exposure (not direction): SMH, SOXX, NVDA, AMD, MU, AVGO, VRT, PWR, VST, QQQ.

Go deeper

The two-day sequence matters more than either day alone. On Wednesday the market bought what it read as a check on the administration: the Dow rose 3.0%, and equal weight beat cap weight. It sold AI power (Vistra −13.8%, Vertiv −12.9%) and treated it as a sector story. On Thursday the selling reached the chip designers and memory makers. Their link to Washington policy is more indirect, so the move looks less like a reasoned policy repricing and more like a crowded trade unwinding. Two signs support that reading. Volatility jumped from 16.6 to 27 in a day, and the short end of the VIX curve inverted. AppLovin, a non-hardware AI beneficiary with a clean beat, rose 30% on the same day, which suggests investors were still rewarding earnings, not dumping everything AI.

What would argue against an unwind: a hardware company cutting guidance on order cancellations, or a hyperscaler cutting capex. Neither happened today. What would confirm a deeper repricing: semis failing to bounce once VIX term structure normalises, or credit following equities. High-yield ETF HYG was down only 0.45%. High-yield spreads were 3.32% on Wednesday and had actually tightened 8 basis points, so credit is not yet joining in.

Qualcomm and Arm slide on outlooks; AppLovin jumps 30%

Wednesday night’s reports split along a clear line: hardware guidance squeezed by memory costs was punished, and an AI-driven software beat was rewarded.

Qualcomm was down 9.1% at $157.44. It guided fiscal first-quarter EPS to $2.15–2.35, a midpoint below the roughly $2.32 consensus. Its revenue outlook was above estimates, but reports cite memory and component costs squeezing margins and a 26% drop in handset-chip revenue. Arm beat on adjusted EPS ($0.52 vs $0.47), but its outlook disappointed, and the stock was down 11.8%. Snap fell 13.9% to $5.10: revenue was $1.72 billion, against $1.73 billion expected, and daily users were 495 million, short of about 499 million.

AppLovin was the day’s standout, up 30.1% at $353.55. Revenue was $2.18 billion vs about $2.07 billion, and fourth-quarter guidance of $2.37–2.40 billion was above the roughly $2.25 billion consensus. Its CEO called last quarter’s miss “our own foot fault.” Tripadvisor fell about 22% after announcing its CEO and CFO are leaving and guiding fourth-quarter revenue lower.

Bank of England raises rates to 4.00%, its first hike in over three years

The vote was 8–1, and the lone dissenter wanted a half-point. The bank warned that energy-driven inflation will approach 5%.

The Bank of England lifted Bank Rate by a quarter-point to 4.00% after six straight holds. Governor Andrew Bailey said there is “enough inflationary pressure in the pipeline” from energy prices, and the bank signalled further increases are possible. Markets had priced the move at more than 99%. It follows the Riksbank’s hike on Wednesday and Australia’s on Tuesday, so three central banks have now tightened in one week because of the Iran-war energy shock. The FTSE 100 gave up early gains and stood at 10,783 (−0.4%) at the last check. Sterling was steady near $1.35.

CNBC, MoneyWeek, MoneySavingExpert, Irish Times (search summaries); the Bank’s own statement is the primary record.

Jobless claims fall to 189,000 and productivity surges 3.6%

Layoffs remain at half-century lows and unit labour costs fell. That is the kind of data the Fed’s hawks and doves will each read their own way, the day before payrolls.

Initial claims for the week ended Oct. 31 fell to 189,000, below the roughly 196,000 consensus, and the prior week was revised to 195,000. That matches the mid-July reading, the lowest since 1969. The four-week average eased to 194,750. Continuing claims rose 23,000 to 1.718 million, a sign that people who lose jobs are taking longer to find new ones. Reuters notes that hiring, not firing, is what the Middle East conflict is holding back.

Preliminary third-quarter productivity rose at a 3.6% annual rate against about 2.7% expected: output grew 3.7% while hours rose just 0.1%. Unit labour costs fell 0.7%, their first decline since the second quarter of 2025, and are up 2.4% from a year earlier. That is a level well below the 3.1% core PCE inflation rate, so labour costs are not what is driving inflation. The revision is due Dec. 8.

Why it matters The Fed is weighing a December hike against energy-driven inflation. Falling unit labour costs weaken the argument that a wage-price spiral is under way. The 189,000 claims figure, though, says the labour market is not cracking. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack said on Thursday that a gradual path of hikes is best and that inflation is “too high, moving in the wrong direction.”

Oil climbs back after Iran reports tanker fires in Hormuz

Crude fell overnight for a fourth day, then reversed after Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers and a tug caught fire, one after striking a mine.

Brent was trading near $93.65 and WTI near $87 overnight. Traders were reading the Republican losses as lowering the odds of escalation, and Gulf News reported that President Trump ruled out immediate strikes. The IRGC then said two tankers and a tugboat caught fire in separate incidents on “unauthorized” routes and that the strait is “unsafe and closed” to vessels that do not coordinate with Iran. Disputed count Arab News counts five tankers across IRGC and army statements, and MarineLink reports three. No ship has been independently named.

At 2:12 PM front-month WTI was $90.32 (+2.5%) and Brent $97.95 (+3.5%). Qatar’s foreign ministry says the US and Iran are reviewing mediators’ proposals and that feedback is expected “within 36 hours”. Eurasia Group cut its odds of a deal to 10%, according to one futures broker’s note.

Johnson steps back from leadership as two Senate counts stay too close to call

House Republicans will pick a leader next week. Democrats hold the Senate either way, and Mississippi and Alaska decide its size.

Speaker Mike Johnson won his own seat but told staff he will not seek a leadership post in the next Congress (Fox News, PBS). Majority Leader Steve Scalise and Judiciary Chair Jim Jordan are vying to lead the new Republican minority, with a conference vote reported for next Tuesday. Hakeem Jeffries formally launched his bid for Speaker. In the Senate, Chuck Schumer is expected to be elected majority leader in early December, though some incoming Democrats have said they may not back him.

Two Senate races are still uncalled. In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom’s lead over Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith has been reported at anywhere from about 1,100 to 1,800 votes. Mississippi has no automatic recount. In Alaska, Mary Peltola led Sen. Dan Sullivan by about 626 votes out of roughly 214,000. Mail ballots can arrive for 15 days, and ranked-choice tabulation follows if neither reaches 50%, with a result expected around mid-November. President Trump said he is “absolutely willing to accept those results” and wants to make deals, then posted an image of a “veto” hat.

What’s next The lame-duck Congress returns to a Dec. 11 government-funding deadline: the House has passed 3 of 12 spending bills and the Senate none. Republicans are weighing a debt-limit increase before Democrats take control.

The Economy

Labour and costs. The two 8:30 AM releases are covered above: claims at 189,000 and Q3 productivity +3.6% with unit labour costs −0.7%. In rate-of-change terms, unit labour costs are now running at 2.4% a year. That is below inflation, which means real labour costs are falling. FRED shows the September unemployment rate at 4.2% (up from 4.1%) and September payrolls up only 29,000. Friday’s October report is expected to show modest growth, with forecasts we have seen ranging from about 45,000 to 100,000 and ADP at 86,000. Risks & Scenarios

The selloff holds into the close. We put 70–85% on the S&P 500 closing below 7,500 today, which would mean a loss of more than 2.4%. The basis: it was at 7,387 at 2:22 PM with less than two hours left, and inverted VIX curves rarely produce full same-day reversals. We put 60–75% on the VIX closing above 25. It invalidates if a late short-covering rally lifts the index back above 7,500.

Rates keep the rally. We put 75–85% on the 10-year par yield closing today below Wednesday’s 4.90%, on the basis of the roughly 4.83% intraday reference and a risk-off bid. A hot payrolls print on Friday is the main threat to the bond rally, not today.

Chips do not recover the loss by Friday. We put 75–90% on the semiconductor index closing Friday below its Wednesday close of 10,268.65. That would take a gain of roughly 11% from the 2:22 PM level in one session, and nothing on Friday’s calendar, payrolls aside, is aimed at chips.

Tail risk. A confirmed attack on named, non-Iranian-coordinated tankers in the main shipping channel, or US strikes, would add an oil shock to an equity market already de-risking. Brent was at $98 and has traded as high as $126 in the past year.

The United States

The new Congress. Democrats won both chambers. The World

Iran. Beyond the tanker reports (see Top Stories), Iran’s demands remain the lifting of oil sanctions, an end to the US naval blockade, the release of frozen funds and a halt to fighting on all fronts, including Lebanon. Tehran says its own Hormuz management plan could start within two days, and Oman says it is still working with Iran on a joint control system. Europe and China. EU finance ministers meet Nov. 9. Beyond the Hang Seng’s tech-led rally, we found no major Chinese policy announcement today.

AI & Technology

The market verdict. Today’s selloff (see the lead) is the main AI story: investors pulled back from the chip and power suppliers to the build-out. The buyers of AI capacity fell far less, and AppLovin, which applies AI to advertising, rose 30%. In market terms, the losers were the hardware and power supply chain, and the relative winners were the mega-cap platforms and defensives.

Science

Ancient proteins point to Denisovan ancestry. A team led by Qiaomei Fu of the Chinese Academy of Sciences recovered protein information from 400,000-year-old Homo erectus teeth found in China, Nature reports. The findings suggest interbreeding with Denisovans. Recovering proteins of that age extends molecular evidence well past the reach of ancient DNA.

A natural appetite peptide. Stanford researchers used a “Peptide Predictor” algorithm to identify a naturally occurring peptide that suppresses appetite, which coverage is calling a “natural Ozempic,” in work published in Nature. The senior author has co-founded a company to pursue trials. Single source We have only a ScienceDaily summary and have not confirmed the paper’s details. It is preclinical, and no human efficacy has been shown.

Space

New Glenn slips to 2027. Blue Origin’s New Glenn will not return to flight until January 2027, chief executive Dave Limp told staff, after a May explosion damaged the company’s only launch pad (CNBC, Bloomberg, Reuters). That leaves SpaceX and United Launch Alliance as the only US heavy-lift options into early next year. Vulcan’s own return to flight is pencilled for no earlier than Nov. 13.

Artemis. NASA’s Nov. 4 update confirms Artemis III in 2027 as a 14-day Earth-orbit test of Orion docking with SpaceX and Blue Origin lander test vehicles. The first crewed landing is Artemis IV, targeted for 2028.

Launches and the market. A Falcon 9 is set to carry USSF-432, a national-security mission, from Vandenberg no earlier than 7:46 PM ET Friday. China launched three Yaogan 51 satellites on a Long March 2C today. SpaceX shares fell 7.7% to $125.47, and Rocket Lab (−12.3%) and AST SpaceMobile (−13.9%) fell further. Space stocks are trading with the broad high-momentum selloff rather than on any company news.

Pennsylvania

Democrats take the state Senate 26–24, completing their first trifecta since 1993

Democrats flipped four state Senate seats: the 6th, 16th, 24th and 40th. They lost Lancaster’s 36th, which turns a 27–23 Republican chamber into a 26–24 Democratic one, and they grew their House majority from 103–100 to at least 111 seats, per AP data. These are AP calls, not certified results. Gov. Josh Shapiro led Treasurer Stacy Garrity 60.4% to 38.3% (about 3.01 million to 1.91 million votes), and Garrity conceded.

In the US House, Democrats flipped four Pennsylvania seats:

  • PA-1: Bob Harvie over Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick, 52.7% to 47.3%
  • PA-7: Bob Brooks over Rep. Ryan Mackenzie
  • PA-8: Paige Cognetti over Rep. Rob Bresnahan Jr.
  • PA-10: Janelle Stelson over Rep. Scott Perry

Rep. Chris Deluzio held the 17th. One-party control in Harrisburg reopens the policy fights left out of July’s $50.8 billion budget, including recreational cannabis and skill-game regulation. That is our analysis; no leader has announced a 2027 agenda yet.
