Top Stories

Chip stocks fall 5% in a broad tech sell-off the day before Nvidia reports

The selling began in Taiwan and Korea and got heavier through the New York session. Nvidia closed at its lowest level since July 2025, and the S&P 500 had its worst day since the Nvidia leadership crisis began.

The S&P 500 fell 1.92% to 7,416.59 and the Nasdaq Composite 2.49% to 24,509.76. The Dow lost 739.38 points to 51,608.17 (CNBC, Yahoo/Zacks; AP gives the same figures). It was the S&P’s third straight losing day. SPY, at $739.95, is now 5.3% below its October 6 high of $781.62. The VanEck semiconductor fund (SMH) fell 5.11% to $461.90 and the technology SPDR (XLK) 3.24% (Finnhub, EOD official). Memory and equipment makers fell hardest. Micron lost 7.62% to $719.02, Marvell 7.27%, Intel 7.02%, Vertiv 6.63%, Taiwan Semiconductor’s U.S. shares 6.34%, ASML 5.91% and Applied Materials 5.47%. AMD fell 5.58% to $504.28, Arista 5.21% and Broadcom 3.92%. Nvidia lost 4.12% to $156.14, which CNBC says is its lowest close since July 1, 2025.

This was broader than Friday’s sell-off, which stayed inside chips. Alphabet fell 4.07%, Oracle 4.99%, Amazon 3.34%, Tesla 2.69% and Meta 2.38%. CoreWeave fell 6.12% and Nebius 7.55%. Benzinga estimated that the 19 largest U.S. technology stocks lost about $960 billion of combined market value during the day. Banks fell with the market: JPMorgan 2.31% and Goldman Sachs 2.73%. Defensive stocks held up. Consumer staples (XLP, +0.27%) and utilities (+0.18%) were the only sector funds to rise, and UnitedHealth, McDonald’s, Costco and Walmart all gained.

Several things lined up. Overnight, Taiwan’s Taiex fell 6.0% and Korea’s Kospi 3.6%. During the day the Commerce Department issued its new export rule (next story). CNBC reported, citing a source, that Nvidia’s board is not considering bringing back Jensen Huang, and Nvidia touched its session low after that report. Traders were also reducing positions before tomorrow’s results. The order in which these mattered cannot be separated from the price action. In the language we use for such days, the selling coincided with all four, and there was no single confirmed catalyst.

Why it matters Since Wednesday’s close, three sessions ago, SMH has lost about 9% and Nvidia about 10%. The market is now pricing risk across the whole AI supply chain: the chip designers, the memory makers, the equipment makers and the data-centre builders. Nvidia’s guidance on Tuesday is the first hard number since the leadership crisis began. The equal-weight S&P fell only half as much as the cap-weighted index. That, together with gains in staples, says investors are rotating, not selling everything.
What’s next Nvidia reports about 4:20 PM tomorrow, with the call at 5:00 PM, led by interim chief executive Colette Kress. Before that, October retail sales at 8:30 AM will test the economy outside the AI trade. Watch NVDA, SMH, TSM, MU and QQQ, and whether RSP keeps beating SPY.

Prices: Finnhub (EOD official, 4:00 PM ET) · Index closes: CNBC, Yahoo/Zacks, AP (as reported) · CNBC (Nvidia board report, movers), Benzinga (market-value estimate), via search summaries.

Go deeper

Nvidia has now fallen 10.1% from Wednesday’s $173.73 close. CNBC and other outlets count six straight weekly declines, its longest run since 2012, and put the stock about 32% below its October peak. The options market has been pricing a move of roughly 9% either way after the report; one newsletter puts it at 8.6%. Crypto-linked stocks fell almost as hard as chips. Strategy lost 7.70%, Robinhood 7.45% and Coinbase 6.11%, and the Grayscale bitcoin mini trust fell 5.49%. In space stocks, AST SpaceMobile fell 12.33%, Intuitive Machines 9.77% and Rocket Lab 6.76%. Those are the market’s most speculative corners, and they fell with the leaders.

What would change the read: data-centre revenue above the roughly $101.8 billion consensus (Visible Alpha, via S&P Global), plus a clear answer on sales through Southeast Asia. Either would ease the two worries hanging over the group. A guide at or below the company’s own $108 billion midpoint would leave today’s drop looking early rather than overdone.

Commerce requires licences for AI-chip exports to five countries, aiming at Chinese firms renting compute abroad

The interim rule covers Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand, and formally withdraws the Biden-era AI diffusion rule.

The Commerce Department’s Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim rule on Monday requiring a licence to export, re-export or transfer advanced AI chips to Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand (Bloomberg, CNBC, Fortune, CBS, Barron’s). The rule targets Chinese companies that rent computing power in data centres outside China. Barron’s names ByteDance and Alibaba among those affected. It formally rescinds the January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule, which the administration had already said it would not enforce. Fortune reports that it takes effect when it is published in the Federal Register. We could not confirm the publication date.

The rule comes as Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick faces criticism over chips reaching China through Singapore. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has publicly asked why Megaspeed, the Singapore company at the centre of the Nvidia inquiries, is not on the Entity List. Megaspeed denies shipping chips to China. In Congress, CNBC reports that chip-sale restrictions such as the GAIN AI Act, the AI OVERWATCH Act and the Chip Security Act are unlikely to make the final defense bill and could return next year. Single source We found that report only through CNBC, and The Hill had earlier listed some of them in the bill’s manager’s amendments.

Why it matters Southeast Asian data centres have been the main route by which, U.S. officials allege, advanced chips reached Chinese users. A licence requirement there makes it harder to sell compute that ends up serving Chinese customers. The rule acts through the executive branch rather than Congress, so it can take effect quickly. How much revenue is exposed will depend on Nvidia’s disclosure tomorrow. The company already guides to zero China data-centre revenue.

Warsh says AI may be the most transformational change of his adult life, and that it could also disappoint

The Fed Chair’s opening remarks at a San Francisco Fed conference avoided rates. Bond traders noticed the downside scenario.

Speaking at a conference on productivity and jobs run by the San Francisco Fed and the Board, Warsh said AI could be the most transformational period of his adult life and might sustain above-average growth (CNBC; AP via WTOP; Axios). He said productivity has been “reasonably strong” but has not broken out “definitively and structurally to the upside.” He described three scenarios. In the downside one, AI would “disappoint”: expected returns would turn down, stock valuations would fall and the economy would slow. “In time, the economy would recover, but without the productivity gains,” he said. Axios’s headline read “Warsh sees risk of stock bust if AI boom disappoints.” That is the outlet’s framing; Warsh offered it as one of three scenarios, not a forecast. According to AP and investingLive, he said nothing on interest rates, the neutral rate, the balance sheet or AI’s effect on inflation.

Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack told Bloomberg Television that she prefers “slow, steady” rate increases in small steps. She said price pressures are broadening and that financial conditions are not yet restraining the economy (FXStreet, The Private Banker). She attributed higher government borrowing costs to strong growth and expected Fed hikes, not to AI companies’ borrowing. We found no coverage of New York Fed President John Williams’s remarks this morning, or of Governor Michael Barr’s in Milan. CNBC says a quarter-point December hike remains “largely priced in” on CME FedWatch. One widely cited split, 91% for a quarter point and 9% for a half, may be several days old.

Why it matters The Fed Chair publicly sketched a scenario in which AI stocks fall, on a day when they did. That does not mean the Fed will respond to equity prices. It does show the Fed is thinking about how an AI unwind would feed into growth. For the December decision, Hammack’s view matters more: hikes continue, but in small steps.

New York factory index drops to 7.8, far below forecasts, and Treasuries rally modestly

The Empire State survey fell from 25.5. Yields on two-year notes fell five basis points as stocks sold off.

The New York Fed’s Empire State manufacturing survey for November fell to 7.8 from 25.5 in October. Forecasts were about 17–18, and estimates vary by source (MarketScreener, Benzinga). A reading above zero still means more firms reported improving conditions than worsening ones, so activity rose, but at a much slower pace. One report puts the employment index at 18.1, up from 10.3, and future business conditions at 28.9 (GuruFocus). Single source We found those component figures in only one report.

The Treasury par curve closed with the 2-year at 4.83%, the 10-year at 5.12% and the 30-year at 5.46%, down 5, 3 and 1 basis points from Friday (U.S. Treasury, EOD official). The 3-month bill rose 2 basis points to 4.34%. The 10-year inflation-protected yield rose 1 basis point to 2.92% (Treasury real-yield curve). That puts the 10-year breakeven at about 2.20%, down from 2.24%, our own subtraction of the two Treasury curves. CNBC described the move as a small bid for Treasuries as stocks fell. The 10-year touched about 5.17% on Friday, its highest since October.

Why it matters Today’s bond move came almost entirely from lower breakeven inflation, not lower real yields. That is consistent with weaker growth data and a 3%-plus drop in oil, not with any change in Fed expectations. The front end fell most, but at 4.83% the 2-year still prices more hikes. Real yields near 2.9% remain the tightest financial condition in the market.

Oil falls more than 3% after a forecast of a glut, even as Iran claims more tanker strikes

Brent was at $92.44 by mid-afternoon, its lowest in weeks. A third U.S. carrier has reached the region.

Brent crude for January was down 3.6% at $92.44 a barrel at 3:41 PM, and WTI for December was down 3.2% at $85.72 (Rigzone; these are mid-afternoon prices, not settlements). The United States Oil Fund closed down 3.81% and the energy SPDR 1.96% (Finnhub). Rigzone tied the drop to a Goldman Sachs note warning of a glut in 2027, after the International Energy Agency said last week that supply now exceeds demand for the first time since the war began, and to tanker traffic through Hormuz returning to normal. Brent peaked near $118 in late October.

That reading of Hormuz conflicts with Iran’s. The Revolutionary Guards say six commercial tankers have been disabled or sunk by mines since Sunday, including a supertanker they say carried about 2 million barrels, and that the strait “remains closed.” Unconfirmed Reuters could not confirm the Guards’ account, and no ship has been named. Independently, the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency, citing Indian shipping authorities, reported one tanker attacked near the strait on Nov 15, and Seatrade counts two confirmed strikes. Central Command announced on Monday that the USS Theodore Roosevelt has joined the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington. It is the first time three carriers have been in the region since the fighting peaked in April (AP, ABC). Bahrain says it intercepted all of Sunday’s drones aimed at the Riffa air base.

Why it matters The oil market is treating the Guards’ claims as noise and the supply forecasts as signal. Lower oil helps the inflation outlook and, at the margin, the case for smaller Fed hikes. The risk is that one confirmed sinking of a large tanker would reverse that quickly.

Tyson Foods jumps 8.7% as its beef losses are forecast to shrink

Quarterly earnings beat. The forecast for next year’s beef losses moved the stock more than the headline numbers did.

Tyson reported fiscal fourth-quarter adjusted earnings of $0.61 a share, against estimates of about $0.58. Sales rose 5.2% to about $14.5 billion. Sources disagree on how that compared with forecasts; Finnhub’s consensus was $14.3 billion. Adjusted operating income fell 41% to $361 million, with a $171 million adjusted loss in beef (CNBC and others). For fiscal 2027 it guided to adjusted operating income of $1.7–2.15 billion, against $1.98 billion this year. Within that, beef runs from a $300 million loss to a $100 million profit, against a $654 million loss this year, and chicken $1.3–1.6 billion, against $1.9 billion. It expects sales to fall 3.5–5.5%, below forecasts, and raised its dividend 2% to $0.52. The stock rose 8.72% to $63.95 (Finnhub).

Zelenskyy calls new online talks with U.S. envoys and a full European delegation “difficult”

Moscow has not agreed to European participation. The EU added 113 names to its sanctions list.

President Zelenskyy said Ukraine held new online talks on Monday with U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, the first to include a full European delegation, and called them “difficult” (Reuters via Cyprus Mail; ua.news). He said the talks would continue at a Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin. Earlier reports put that summit on Nov 19, but we could not confirm the date today. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Moscow has not agreed to European participation in the next round. In Brussels, EU foreign ministers sanctioned 113 people and entities. EU foreign-policy chief Kaja Kallas said serious talks require Russian concessions and a ceasefire first (Euronews, RTÉ).

The Economy

Today’s data. The Empire State survey, covered above, was the only major U.S. release. It is a diffusion index: 7.8 means more firms reported improvement than deterioration, but the margin narrowed sharply. A drop of this size in one month is a change in pace, not a contraction. Tuesday’s retail sales are the week’s first hard spending number.

The Fed. Warsh avoided policy; Hammack supported continued small hikes. The policy rate is 3.75–4.00%, and markets expect it to rise to 4.00–4.25% on December 9. The minutes of the October 27–28 meeting come out Wednesday at 2 PM, and readers will look for how many officials discussed a half-point move. The Fed published no new press releases today (federalreserve.gov feed).

Treasury. The 13-week and 26-week bill auctions ($95 billion and $82 billion) took place this morning. We did not get their results. A $100 billion 6-week bill follows Tuesday. The $16 billion 20-year bond and a $19 billion 10-year reopening settle November 30, and the 2-, 5- and 7-year notes settle at month-end (TreasuryDirect). SOFR was 3.90% on Friday (FRED). Congress returned with the government funded through December 11. Appropriators met on November 10 and left without an agreed path.

Rates and credit, in real terms. The 10-year nominal yield of 5.12% splits into a 2.92% real yield and an implied breakeven of about 2.20% (Treasury curves, Nov 16). Real yields rose slightly while breakevens fell, a sign that today’s bond rally reflected weaker growth and lower oil, not easier policy. The curve steepened: 2s10s widened to 29 basis points from 27, 5s30s to 53 from 49, and 3m10y narrowed to 78 from 83. The ICE BofA high-yield spread widened to 3.23 percentage points on Friday from 3.20 (FRED). HYG fell 0.27% today and LQD was flat.

Global. China’s and Japan’s soft data (in this morning’s edition) set the tone in Asia. European stocks fell, and Vanguard’s Europe fund (VGK) lost 1.12% and emerging markets (EEM) 3.33% in U.S. trading. The dollar was firmer against the euro: the ECB’s reference rate implies about $1.1014, against $1.1052 on Friday. It was slightly weaker against the yen at 159.60. Several outlets put the dollar index at a 19-month high, but we could not get an official level. Japan’s finance ministry repeated that it is coordinating with the U.S. Treasury. We found no sign of intervention.

The FAA outage. The FAA’s contractor, L3Harris, has taken two routers, from sites in Amarillo, Texas, and El Dorado, Arkansas, to its Florida headquarters for diagnostics, with FAA, Juniper and Cisco engineers and the FBI involved (CBS, CNN, Fox). Science

A pulsing white dwarf. A paper in Nature Astronomy (ScienceDaily, Nov 14) reports a long-period radio transient, a source that flashes every 7.9 minutes, traced to a white dwarf orbiting a Sun-like star. Astronomers have found a handful of these slow radio pulsers in the last few years without agreeing on what powers them. It is the first such source tied to a Sun-like companion. That narrows the explanations to magnetic interaction between the two stars rather than an isolated neutron star.

Ryugu’s building blocks. A study in Nature Astronomy earlier this month reports all five nucleobases, the letters of DNA and RNA, in samples returned from asteroid Ryugu by Japan’s Hayabusa2. Because the material came back sealed, contamination is less likely than with meteorites that fell to Earth. It strengthens the case that the ingredients for life formed in space; it says nothing about life itself.

Space

Vulcan grounded. The Space Force has suspended national-security missions on Vulcan pending ULA’s investigation of Sunday’s failure, and the rocket is expected to be out for months (CBS, CNBC, GeekWire). ULA says it does not yet have specific data on the anomaly, which came about ten minutes after liftoff during the upper stage’s burn, and that Blue Origin, which builds Vulcan’s BE-4 engines, is helping. ULA’s chief executive said the BE-4s had been modified for the oxygen-valve problem behind May’s New Glenn explosion. It was ULA’s first mission loss since 2006 and the first of 38 Vulcan launches Amazon has bought. Amazon had 398 Leo satellites in orbit before Sunday. Amazon fell 3.34% today along with the rest of big tech, so the failure’s effect on the stock cannot be separated.

Starship. Analysts remain impressed by last week’s first tower catch of Starship’s upper stage, CNBC reported. Elon Musk says the next flight will try to catch both the booster and the ship. Wells Fargo says Ship 42 could fly again on Flight 16 if its heat shield is in good shape. No date is set. SpaceX (SPCX) fell 1.26% to $137.36, less than the market’s growth stocks.

This week. A Falcon 9 is due to launch the USSF-317 national-security mission from Vandenberg late Wednesday (The Space Devs schedule). Blue Origin’s New Glenn is not expected back until January.

Also in the News

Measles. U.S. News, NPR and Forbes report that the U.S. has lost the measles-elimination status it held for 26 years after a Pan American Health Organization committee decision. The formal announcement is expected Tuesday. Elimination is lost after 12 months of continuous spread of the same strain. The CDC count is 5,412 cases this year through November 12, the most in 35 years. CDC Director Erica Schwartz said the agency will keep working with states whatever the decision. Regaining the status requires at least 12 months without sustained transmission.
