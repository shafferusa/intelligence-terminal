Wednesday, September 23, 2026 · 26 min read

A hot flash PMI, a hawkish Fed Governor Barr and the weakest five-year Treasury auction since 2018 sent the 10-year yield to its highest since 2007 and knocked stocks lower across the board, as Xi Jinping arrived in Washington for the first Chinese state visit in a decade and a federal judge heard, but did not decide, the White House press-ban case.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The world. Xi Jinping landed at Joint Base Andrews this afternoon, opening his first Washington visit in eleven years, while Russia and Ukraine kept trading strikes on each other’s energy grids even as Zelensky proposed a three-way Putin-Trump meeting.
  • Markets.   • The thread. Six days after a hawkish Fed hike, the data is now confirming the Fed’s own hawkish dot plot rather than contradicting it — today was the first session since the FOMC decision where that alignment showed up cleanly in yields.
  • Biggest risk. A bond market that keeps repricing higher on its own, cutting off the soft landing the Fed is still trying to manage — today’s weak auction is an early data point, not yet a trend.
  • What deserves attention. Whether Judge Kelly rules on the press-ban case in the next few days; what the Kennedy Center’s sworn status report actually says, once it surfaces; and Thursday’s Trump-Xi state dinner and Costco earnings.

Top Stories

10-year Treasury yield hits its highest since 2007 as a hot PMI, a hawkish Barr and a weak auction combine

Three separate signals pointed the same direction today — and for the first time since last week’s hawkish Fed hike, the bond market believed all three at once.

S&P Global’s flash September PMIs came in far hotter than expected: the composite reading jumped to 58.4 from 56.0, manufacturing to 57.0 from 53.9, and services to 58.7 from 54.6 — the fastest growth in over five years, with backlogs rising at the sharpest pace since May 2022 and input-price pressure at its highest since October 2022. Minutes later, Fed Governor Michael Barr, speaking at a Chicago housing-affordability summit, said “further policy adjustments are likely to be needed to ensure inflation comes down to target in a timely fashion,” adding that risks to the inflation target have increased while labor-market risks have receded. Then, at 1 PM, the Treasury’s $70 billion five-year note auction priced at 5.033% with a bid-to-cover ratio of 2.212 — the weakest since December 2018 — and tailed by 3.1 basis points, one of the largest tails on record for the tenor; indirect bidders (foreign and institutional buyers) took only 54.3%, the lowest since March 2020.

The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 5.11% on the official par curve (intraday reports put it as high as 5.135%), its highest level since July 2007 and its biggest one-day move since April 2025, up 15 basis points on the day. Every part of the curve moved: the 3-month bill rose 3bp to 4.19%, the 2-year jumped roughly 14bp to 4.85%, and the 20- and 30-year both crossed 5.4%. Equities sold off broadly: the S&P 500 fell 0.75% to 7,706.03, the Nasdaq Composite 1.13% to 26,936.04, and the Dow 0.68% (352 points) to 51,511.59, with Treasury-sensitive sectors — utilities, homebuilders, regional banks — leading the decline. Long bonds sold off in tandem (TLT −1.58%), gold fell on higher real yields (GLD −1.80%) and the dollar firmed (DXY +0.68%). Oil was the exception, up more than 3% on USO, moving opposite the rest of the risk-off tape.

Why it matters This is the first session since the Sept. 16 hawkish hike where hard data, a sitting Fed governor and an actual Treasury auction all pointed the same way at once — not a single data point being over-read, but three independent signals agreeing. A 5%+ 10-year, if it holds, raises financing costs across mortgages, corporate debt and the federal deficit itself.
What’s next A 7-year note auction Thursday ($44B) is the next demand test at the long end. Watch whether tomorrow’s jobless claims and Friday’s durable-goods and Michigan sentiment data extend or complicate the hot-PMI read; the Oct. 27–28 FOMC meeting is the next point the Fed can act on it.

Xi arrives in Washington as the Trump-Xi summit opens; Trump greets him planeside in a rare gesture

Xi’s plane touched down at Joint Base Andrews this afternoon for his first Washington visit in eleven years — Trump met him on the tarmac rather than waiting at the White House, an honor he almost never extends.

Xi Jinping landed at Joint Base Andrews this afternoon, opening a three-day state visit that runs through Friday and marks his first trip to Washington since 2015 and the third Trump-Xi meeting in eleven months, after Busan in October and Beijing in May. Trump greeted him personally on the tarmac rather than at the White House — a gesture the South China Morning Post and other outlets describe as rare. Thursday brings the main bilateral meeting, a Rose Garden military review and a state dinner; Friday, a National Archives visit before Xi departs. As previously reported, China is expected to press Trump on a pending $14 billion Taiwan arms package, and Alibaba’s Tuesday unveiling of its “Zhenwu V900” AI chip continues to be read by outlets including Fortune and NBC News as a timed show of Chinese tech self-sufficiency heading into the talks. Taiwan itself said today it remains confident US arms sales will continue despite Beijing’s pressure.

What’s next The substantive bilateral meeting and state dinner are Thursday; watch for any statement on the Taiwan arms package specifically, and for the joint statement’s language (or its absence) on Taiwan.

ABC News, South China Morning Post, Washington Times, U.S. News (wire pool coverage of the arrival) · Taipei Times.

Judge Kelly hears the CNN/MS NOW/Politico press-ban case, reserves his ruling

The judge who restored Jim Acosta’s credentials in 2018 heard 90 minutes of argument this afternoon and said he would rule later — not the same-day decision some had expected.

Judge Timothy Kelly held the scheduled 3:30 PM videoconference hearing on CNN, MS NOW and Politico’s request for a temporary restraining order restoring their White House access. Attorney Ted Boutrous, for the outlets, argued the ban’s stated national-security rationale contradicts “the very essence of the First Amendment,” calling the cited articles “garden variety reporting.” DOJ continued to argue press access is a revocable privilege, not a right. Per NPR and CNN’s live coverage, Kelly said he would consider the arguments and rule at a later time — he did not issue a decision from the bench.

What’s next No ruling yet. This outlet’s morning edition estimated 55–70% odds of at least a partial TRO within days, based on Kelly’s 2018 Acosta precedent and the narrowness of DOJ’s filing; that estimate stands unchanged pending an actual order.

Pittsburgh DA Zappala says he expects charges in the City Hall probe; councilman named as Khari Mosley

For the first time since Monday’s unsealed warrant, the district attorney has put a number on his own confidence — and a council member has now been named on the record.

Allegheny County DA Stephen Zappala told WPXI’s 11 Investigates he expects criminal charges to be filed in the ongoing investigation into City of Pittsburgh finances under former Mayor Ed Gainey, covering p-card use, trust funds and the no-bid contracts described in Monday’s unsealed search warrant. Zappala was also asked about Gainey’s campaign-trail characterization of the investigation as racially motivated and dismissed it: “I’m a public figure so you can lie about me.” Separately, Councilman Khari Mosley told WPXI he had no involvement in the no-bid contract allegations, saying “there’s no connection between me and the insinuations and inaccuracies that are in the document” — the first on-record denial from a named council member since the warrant was unsealed. Gainey has repeatedly denied wrongdoing and says he welcomes the investigation. No charges have been filed as of this writing.

Why it matters “I expect charges” from the elected prosecutor running the investigation is a materially stronger statement than an open, uncharged probe — though it is not itself a charge, and Zappala did not name a timeline or a target.

SpaceX shares slide on an insider stock-sale disclosure ahead of Thursday’s lockup expiration

President Gwynne Shotwell’s own sale filing landed a day before up to 328 million more SPCX shares become eligible to trade — a supply story, not an operational one.

Zelensky proposes a three-way Putin-Trump meeting as both sides trade fresh energy strikes overnight

A day after floating a mutual energy truce with Trump in New York, Zelensky is now asking for something larger — while the strikes that undercut Monday’s truce talk kept going.

Zelensky said today Ukraine is ready for an “energy ceasefire” if Russia stops striking its infrastructure, and called on Trump to organize a trilateral meeting with Putin to move “as quickly as possible” toward ending the war — a step beyond Monday’s bilateral truce talk in New York. He said no agreement was reached to unilaterally halt Ukrainian strikes on Russia’s oil industry. Despite the diplomacy, Russia and Ukraine traded fresh strikes on each other’s energy infrastructure overnight; Al Jazeera reports at least six killed in Kyiv and Donetsk region strikes hours after the Trump-Zelensky talks, and Russia’s defense ministry has said it is planning further “massive strikes” on Ukraine’s grid ahead of winter.

Why it matters A trilateral-meeting proposal is a bigger ask than an energy truce, and it lands the same day both sides keep proving the truce talk hasn’t translated into action on the ground.

CNBC, Al Jazeera (Zelensky’s remarks, primary via wire) · CBC.

Houthi forces complete their takeover of Bab el-Mandeb; analysts warn of a second Hormuz-style chokepoint

With government forces withdrawn from Perim Island, the Houthis now control both shipping lanes through the strait — alongside Iran’s pressure on Hormuz, roughly a third of global maritime shipping now sits near one faction or another.

Houthi fighters have reached Perim Island, which divides Bab el-Mandeb into two shipping lanes, after government forces withdrew — completing a rapid fortnight-long expansion that already included the port of Mokha and the Hanish islands. Commentary from The Week and other outlets frames the combined Houthi hold on Bab el-Mandeb and Iran’s pressure on the Strait of Hormuz as putting roughly a third of global shipping at risk from one direction or another. An adviser to Iran’s supreme leader publicly congratulated the Houthis on the advance; unconfirmed reports continue to describe IRGC advisors assisting the operation.

What’s next Watch shipping-insurance rates and any new UKMTO incident reports for whether control translates into interdiction, not just territory.

Euronews · NBC News · The Week (analysis, labeled) · Times of Israel.

Anthropic’s IPO marketing confirmed slipping to November, targeting a roughly $2 trillion valuation

Bloomberg’s Tuesday report that the roadshow moved off a mid-October target now has independent confirmation the delay is real, not a single-source rumor.

Anthropic’s IPO timeline has slipped from a September target to late October at the earliest, with the company now aiming for November so its roadshow can include third-quarter results, according to multiple outlets including Forbes and Empower. Reports continue to put the ask near a $2 trillion valuation with as much as $100 billion raised — which would be the largest IPO ever, ahead of SpaceX’s roughly $86 billion June debut — on a reported Nasdaq listing. The New York Times has reported Anthropic’s annualized revenue will top $100 billion this year; Bloomberg separately notes only three companies have listed since Labor Day, citing market conditions. Anthropic remains private on every measure this outlet tracks: no self-filing has appeared on SEC EDGAR as of the most recent weekly sweep.

The Economy

The flash PMI, Barr’s remarks and the five-year auction — all covered in the lead story — were the day’s economic news. One additional detail worth separating out: the PMI’s price component hit its highest since October 2022, driven by fuel, transport and wage costs together — a rate-of-change signal on inflation pressure, not a new inflation print itself, and one more input pointing the same hawkish direction as the dot plot from last week’s FOMC meeting.

Today’s $70 billion five-year note auction is the specific data point: a 2.212 bid-to-cover (lowest since December 2018), a 3.1bp tail (one of the largest on record for the tenor), and indirect participation at 54.3% (lowest since March 2020) all point to the same story — buyers wanted a higher yield than the market was offering going in, and got one. Whether that reflects a one-off soft patch in demand or a more persistent repricing of term premium is not yet answerable from a single auction; Thursday’s 7-year note ($44B) is the next read.
