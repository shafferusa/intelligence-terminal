Top Stories

Xi arrives in Washington today, first visit in over a decade, as China presses Trump on Taiwan arms

The three-day state visit opens with a rare tarmac greeting at Joint Base Andrews; Beijing has called a pending $14 billion Taiwan arms package a possible dealbreaker, and timed a new AI chip reveal to land two days before the summit.

Xi Jinping’s state visit — his first trip to Washington since 2015 — runs today through Friday. Arrival at Joint Base Andrews is expected around 4 PM ET, with Trump greeting him personally on the tarmac, a B-2 and four F-22s flying overhead. A South Lawn arrival ceremony, a Rose Garden military review and a state dinner follow Thursday, with a National Archives visit Friday. China’s foreign ministry, spokesperson Guo Jiakun, confirmed the visit Sept. 21.

Reuters reports Xi is expected to press Trump to freeze the pending Taiwan arms package — F-16 upgrades, air-defense systems and anti-ship missiles — citing the 1982 US-China communiqué, and possibly to seek a freeze on already-approved as well as future transfers. Beijing’s foreign ministry says it “resolutely opposes” the sales and calls adherence to the three communiqués a “necessary precondition” for stable ties. A White House official said Trump will decide on a new package “in a fairly short time” and noted he has already approved more Taiwan arms sales in his first year of this term than the prior administration did in four — a signal of no US retreat. Disputed A separate report that China threatened to cancel the summit outright over the package was disputed by a senior US official; treat the cancellation claim as unresolved, not the standing position of either government.

Timed two days ahead of the summit, Alibaba unveiled the “Zhenwu V900,” which it calls China’s most powerful AI chip — roughly three times the performance of its predecessor, scalable to 500,000-unit training clusters — alongside plans for a model of five to ten trillion parameters, more than double its current largest. Bloomberg and the South China Morning Post frame it explicitly as a demonstration of Chinese AI self-sufficiency heading into talks where AI, trade and tariffs are all on the agenda.

Why it matters This is the most concrete point of friction heading into the first Xi visit to Washington in eleven years, and Alibaba’s timing turns a corporate product launch into a piece of summit leverage.
What’s next Arrival and reception today; the main bilateral meeting and state dinner Thursday; National Archives Friday. Watch for any statement on the arms package specifically, and for market reaction in Chinese and US chip names once Asian markets have digested the announcement.

China Ministry of Foreign Affairs statements (primary) · Reuters · South China Morning Post · Bloomberg · Taipei Times · White House officials, via wire.

Russia and Ukraine strike each other’s energy grids overnight; Kremlin dismisses Trump’s territorial reversal

Hours after Trump said Ukraine, with EU support, could win back all its territory, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov called Russia “a real bear,” not “a paper bear” — and both sides kept hitting each other’s power and fuel infrastructure through the night.

Trump and Zelensky met Monday on the UN General Assembly sidelines in New York, discussing a reciprocal halt on energy-infrastructure strikes. Zelensky told reporters Ukraine is “ready” to stop hitting Russian energy facilities if Moscow reciprocates, but no agreement was reached for Kyiv to stand down unilaterally. Despite that, Ukraine struck two Russian oil refineries overnight while Russia fired a missile barrage at southern Ukraine, hitting an oil-and-gas facility officials describe as damaged beyond repair — the latest exchange in a week that has already included Sunday’s bombardment of Zaporizhzhia and a more-than-1,000-drone Ukrainian barrage timed to Russia’s Duma election weekend.

Peskov dismissed Trump’s post-meeting Truth Social claim that Ukraine could “fight and win all of Ukraine back in its original form” with EU support, attributing the shift to Trump having simply heard “Ukraine’s perspective” from Zelensky and rejecting the substance outright. No confirmation has surfaced of the technical talks in Saudi Arabia that were said to follow Monday’s meeting.

Why it matters The gap between UN-week diplomacy and a night of strikes on both sides’ energy grids shows no ceasefire is actually operative, whatever language either side uses in New York.

CBC · Al Jazeera · NBC News · PBS · Kremlin statement via Peskov (primary).

Judge Kelly hears CNN, MS NOW and Politico’s press-ban suit at 3:30 PM

The judge assigned is a first-term Trump appointee who ruled for CNN’s Jim Acosta on nearly identical facts in 2018 — a precedent Trump himself predicted will go against him.

CNN, MS NOW and Politico sued after being banned from White House press access; the case was assigned to US District Judge Timothy Kelly, who set a videoconference hearing for 3:30 PM ET today on the outlets’ request for a temporary restraining order. DOJ’s late-Tuesday filing argues press access is “a privilege, not a right” revocable “at the president’s will,” citing national-security concerns tied to the outlets’ coverage of the Iran war and the White House ballroom construction project. The suit itself alleges First and Fifth/Fourteenth Amendment violations. ABC, CBS, CNN, Fox News and NBC have suspended their TV press-pool coverage in apparent solidarity; the White House has separately launched a 24/7 “Trump TV” YouTube stream. Trump posted on Truth Social that, given the Acosta precedent, he expects Kelly to rule against him and that he will have to appeal.

What’s next The hearing is this afternoon; no ruling had issued as of this edition. A same-day ruling is possible given the emergency posture of the TRO request.

NPR · CBS News · CNN · court filings.

Kennedy Center’s court-ordered sworn status report is due today

Judge Cooper wants a knowledgeable representative to explain, under oath, the building’s closure and its compliance with a standing 30-day demolition-notice order — the filing’s contents are not yet public.

The board closed the building following a Sept. 4 storm, citing ceiling and roof-terrace damage; executive director Matt Floca’s sworn declaration attributed it to “acute risks to public safety.” After an Air Force One photo showed Trump viewing a “Kennedy Center DEMOLISHED” poster, Judge Cooper separately ordered 30 days’ written notice before any major building change, including demolition, and opened discovery into the board’s actions. Today’s deadline is the next compliance checkpoint in that order. Pending No filing or wire report describing today’s report’s actual contents had surfaced as of this edition’s publication.

What’s next Watch for the filing later today; the Closing Brief will cover what it says.

PBS NewsHour · Washingtonian · Judge Cooper’s court order (primary).

Houthi forces push for high ground over Bab el-Mandeb; a Trump-Macron Hormuz pledge remains unconfirmed by Washington

Control of Yemen’s Kahboub Mountains is reported both ways within days of each other; France says it and the US agreed to protect the strait, but no US official has confirmed it on the record.

Al Jazeera reported Monday that Houthi forces are “battling for strategic heights” in the Kahboub range overlooking Bab el-Mandeb, with thousands fleeing and a senior Houthi general reportedly killed with six staff — days after other reporting described government forces retaking the same heights around Sept. 18. The two accounts have not been reconciled; treat control of the range as unsettled. The New York Times reported Trump came close to ordering strikes on Houthi targets Sunday before standing down at the last minute; the UK has agreed limited air-to-air refueling support for Saudi Arabia rather than direct involvement.

Single-reliable-source Separately, Macron posted that he and Trump agreed the US and France will work to protect Middle East energy infrastructure and support freedom of navigation through Hormuz, following their Sept. 21 New York meeting — a claim that rests on Macron’s own statement, with no confirming US statement located. No new UKMTO incident has been reported in the strait in the past day.

Why it matters Kahboub sits over one of the world’s most important shipping chokepoints; whoever holds it shapes the next phase of a war that has already drawn a G7 statement demanding a Houthi stand-down.

Al Jazeera · The New York Times, via wire · Macron’s own statement (primary, single-party) · UKMTO.

EU’s Russia-sanctions rollover draws Kyiv’s rebuke over two oligarch delistings

Ukraine’s foreign minister calls the three-year extension “shameful” after France and Luxembourg’s own interests reportedly got Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman removed from the list.

EU envoys agreed Monday to extend sanctions on roughly 3,000 Russia-linked individuals and entities for three more years — an unusually long renewal — while delisting Usmanov, reportedly tied to a French-national prisoner exchange in Azerbaijan, and Fridman, who is suing Luxembourg over frozen assets. Latvia abstained rather than block the deal. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha called the move “shameful and unjustifiable” on X, saying “Moscow is celebrating because it got what it wanted: a sense of impunity, the humiliation of the EU, and division among Europeans,” and urged member states to impose their own national sanctions on both men.

Reuters/Censor.net · Euronews · Kyiv Independent · Sybiha’s own statement (primary).

AMD’s trillion-dollar milestone holds — Tuesday’s dip was profit-taking, not a reversal

AMD closed Tuesday higher than Monday’s record close; the premarket wobble this outlet flagged Tuesday morning proved to be intraday noise, not a fade of the rally.

AMD rose roughly 10% Monday to $615.52, crossing $1 trillion in market value for the first time on a five-session, ~24% rally tied to reported price increases on some AI accelerators and Meta’s Muse app topping the App Store. Tuesday’s premarket dip of more than 1% coincided with the 30-year Treasury yield near 5.33% — its highest since 2007 — and fading hopes for a quick Middle East oil settlement, but AMD nonetheless closed Tuesday at $623.77, above Monday’s close. AMD is now the fourth US chipmaker over $1 trillion, after Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron.

Critical F5 zero-day forces an emergency federal patch window

A maximum-severity flaw in BIG-IP’s Access Policy Manager was being exploited before F5 had a fix ready; federal agencies have until Friday.

CVE-2026-94127 (CVSS 9.8) is a heap-based buffer overflow that allows unauthenticated remote code execution when BIG-IP APM is configured as an OAuth authorization server. F5 disclosed and hotfixed it Sept. 22; CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day with a Sept. 25 federal remediation deadline, alongside three lower-profile additions (two Check Point flaws and an Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator bug). Affects BIG-IP APM 21.1.0 and 17.1.x–17.5.x.

CISA KEV catalog (primary) · SecurityWeek · The Hacker News.

Paramount Skydance expects to close its $111 billion Warner Bros. Discovery merger within two weeks

A 12-state settlement removed the last legal obstacle; CEO David Ellison told employees to expect closing ahead of the merger’s Sept. 30 late-fee deadline.

The settlement commits Paramount Skydance to $300 million a year in added US film production for five years, a 30-to-32-film annual release slate, and a five-year commitment not to sell either studio’s California lots, under an independent monitor. A $7 million-a-day late fee owed to WBD shareholders past Sept. 30 explains the timing pressure behind Ellison’s roughly two-week estimate. Paramount Skydance shares rose about 4% on the settlement; WBD was little changed.

Axios · Variety · CBS News · consent decree filing (primary).

Contributing factors reported since Monday include above-average South-Central heat forecasts through early October, daily production drifting toward an 11-week low, a shrinking storage surplus versus the five-year average, and rising LNG export flows — several plausible contributors, no single confirmed cause.

Risks & Scenarios

The press-ban hearing goes against the administration. Judge Kelly ruled for CNN’s Jim Acosta on nearly identical due-process facts in 2018, and DOJ’s national-security argument for excluding three specific outlets is a thin basis to distinguish that precedent. Probability of at least a partial TRO being granted today or within days: 55–70%, on the basis of the Acosta ruling and the narrowness of DOJ’s filing. Invalidated by a materially stronger national-security showing than has been reported, or if Kelly defers to a later merits hearing instead of ruling on the TRO itself.

The Trump-Xi summit is materially disrupted over Taiwan arms. Preconditions: Trump declines to offer any concession on the pending package, or signs it during the visit itself. Probability of a public rupture, early departure, or cancelled joint statement: 20–35%, on the basis of China’s explicit “resolutely opposes” language weighed against both sides’ evident interest in the visit proceeding and a White House official’s denial of any cancellation threat. Indicators to watch: whether Thursday’s joint statement addresses Taiwan at all, and whether Trump signs or delays the arms package during the visit.

A direct US strike on Houthi targets within two weeks. Preconditions: another Houthi strike reaching Riyadh, Mecca, or a US-flagged vessel. Probability: 20–35%, on the basis that Trump has twice declined direct strikes despite Saudi requests, but Sunday’s near-order and the Kahboub escalation raise the pressure. Invalidated by a genuine Muscat-brokered stand-down or a new G7-led diplomatic track gaining traction.
