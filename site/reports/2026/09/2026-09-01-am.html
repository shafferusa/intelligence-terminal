Top Stories

Two oil tankers struck overnight in the Strait of Hormuz as the US-Iran standoff widens further

No one has claimed the attacks; both vessels sustained damage and no casualties are reported, three days into an escalating exchange that has now touched Jordan, and by Iran’s disputed account, the UAE.

The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency reported two separate strikes on oil tankers transiting the Strait of Hormuz overnight Monday into Tuesday: the Saudi-flagged VLCC Sidr, operated by Bahri, was struck northeast of Khasab, Oman, and the Liberia-flagged, Sinokor-operated Senegal Prosperity was hit by three projectiles that damaged its port side, engine room and a ballast tank and knocked out its communications. No casualties have been reported on either vessel. Neither CENTCOM nor Iran’s military has commented on the strikes as of this writing, and no group has claimed responsibility — several outlets describe the damage as consistent with anti-ship cruise missiles, but that is analysis, not attribution.

The strikes follow three days of direct escalation: the US struck Iranian minelaying forces near Larak Island over the weekend; Iran retaliated Monday with missiles at Jordan’s King Hussein and Al Azraq air bases (Jordan says all eight were intercepted, with no casualties or damage) and, by Iran’s own account, a drone strike on the UAE’s Al Minhad Air Base (the UAE confirms only that it intercepted one drone over water and flatly denies any base was struck). Iran has separately claimed “heavy damage” to the Jordanian bases, a claim Jordan directly disputes. President Trump said Monday “we’re going to hit them hard” while explicitly ruling out nuclear weapons (“I rule out. There’s no reason for it”); a single tabloid report that the Pentagon had been weighing a nuclear option is unverified and this outlet treats it skeptically. The Pentagon was separately forced to deny striking Kharg Island after an AI-generated video Trump posted claiming exactly that went viral; Iran’s oil minister called the video “laughable,” and Vice President Vance said Trump was “sending a message” — an implicit acknowledgment the video did not depict a real event.

Why it matters Roughly a fifth of global oil trade normally transits Hormuz; shipping through the strait has reportedly collapsed to about five confirmed crossings Tuesday versus 130-plus a day before the standoff began. An attack on tankers, rather than military targets, is a direct escalation against the commercial traffic the whole dispute has revolved around protecting.
What’s next Watch for a CENTCOM or IRGC statement attributing the tanker strikes, and for any further Iranian action against Gulf shipping or US-linked facilities in the next several days — the pattern since Saturday has been strike, retaliation, then a further move within 24 to 48 hours.

Go deeper

The oil market is treating this as an energy-specific shock rather than a broad risk-off event so far: WTI crude is up roughly 2% before the bell while equity-index futures are only modestly lower, a narrower reaction than a full geopolitical panic would produce. That reading depends on the tanker strikes staying unclaimed and unattributed rather than escalating into a confirmed state-on-state attack on commercial shipping, which insurers and shippers would likely price very differently. The disputed casualty and damage claims on both the Jordan and UAE strikes — Iran asserting damage the target nations directly deny — are themselves now part of the story: neither side’s account of the same events matches, and this report is not resolving that dispute, only describing it.

Supreme Court lets the White House ballroom proceed, 5-4, with Roberts in dissent

The full Court ruled Monday that the historic-preservation group challenging the project likely lacks standing to sue, superseding Roberts’s own earlier stay and letting construction continue — but leaving the underlying legality of the roughly 90,000-square-foot addition unresolved.

In an unsigned order Monday, the Supreme Court ruled 5-4 that the National Trust for Historic Preservation likely lacks standing to challenge construction of President Trump’s White House ballroom, allowing the project to continue. The order supersedes Chief Justice Roberts’s Aug. 21 administrative stay: the full Court has now acted, and the majority ruled only on standing, not on whether the roughly 90,000-square-foot project is lawful without congressional approval. Roberts joined the Court’s three liberal justices in dissent, writing that “construction of the ballroom has proceeded apace for the better part of a year” and that it “is likely unlawful.”

Why it matters This is the first full-Court disposition on either live White House-related emergency docket this report has tracked, and a standing ruling against the Trust may effectively end this specific challenge, though another plaintiff with a clearer injury could still refile.

Supreme Court order (primary) · CNN · NBC News · NPR · Fox News.

Army Secretary Dan Driscoll resigns amid reported friction with Hegseth

The White House confirmed the departure, effective Wednesday, without giving a reason; multiple outlets tie it to months of tension over the Army’s direction with Defense Secretary Hegseth.

The White House confirmed Monday that Army Secretary Dan Driscoll is stepping down after 18 months, effective Sept. 3. White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly praised his tenure; no official reason was given, and no statement from Hegseth has been found. Multiple outlets report months of friction between Driscoll and Hegseth over Army “transformation and readiness,” following Hegseth’s April removal of Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George and June exit of Gen. Christopher Donahue — both seen as Driscoll allies.

Why it matters It is the third senior Army or Pentagon departure this year tied to the same internal power struggle, and Driscoll is a Vance ally leaving the administration.

White House statement (primary) · CNN · NBC News · NPR · Axios · The Hill · Military Times.

Fed rate-hike odds near two-thirds as Warsh hardens his tone at the G20 and Bessent pushes back on the record

CME-implied odds of a September hike have climbed from roughly 40% a week ago to the high 50s and 60s, and for the first time Bessent and Warsh made competing public cases on the same day.

Fed Chair Warsh reinforced his hawkish Jackson Hole tone at the G20 on Monday, saying financial conditions “didn’t look restrictive enough” and that recent benign inflation readings had not convinced him the underlying trend has improved — a harder line than his Jackson Hole keynote, not a softer one. The same day, Treasury Secretary Bessent told CNBC on the record: “It is my belief that we’ve seen a supply shock. And traditionally, you don’t raise interest rates into a supply shock unless you see second or third order effects, and we are seeing the core inflation has remained very, very restrained” — while declining to predict the September outcome. CME FedWatch-implied odds of a 25-basis-point hike have risen in a fairly clean trajectory from roughly 40% on Aug. 21 to the high 50s and 60s as of Monday night; sources vary on the precise reading (57–66%), so treat it as “solidly above half and rising,” not a single number.

Why it matters This is the first direct, same-day public disagreement between the Treasury Secretary and the Fed Chair since the Jackson Hole split, and neither has moderated toward the other.
What’s next Today’s JOLTS and ISM Manufacturing releases (see Today’s Calendar) are the first hard data since these remarks; a soft print would hand Bessent’s framing some cover, a strong one reinforces the hike case ahead of the Sept. 16 FOMC decision.

CNBC (Bessent interview, primary) · Fed G20 remarks via CNBC, KuCoin/CME FedWatch trackers · investinglive.com.

Disputed pre-dawn incident in Gaza City as Israel says it arrested a senior Hamas operative

A compromised Israeli special-forces operation triggered intense strikes and gunfire near the Ansar compound; casualty counts and the circumstances both diverge sharply between sources.

Israeli forces carried out intense strikes and exchanged gunfire in western Gaza City’s al-Katiba area before dawn Tuesday after what Israeli sources describe as a compromised special-forces unit. Defense Minister Israel Katz said Israel arrested a senior Hamas operative and vowed to keep pursuing Hamas infrastructure until Gaza is demilitarized. Disputed, single-sourced A Hamas-aligned outlet, citing a Hamas security source, alleges an armed militia tried to assassinate a senior Hamas internal-security official and that the Israeli strikes provided cover for that attempt — a causal claim Israel has not addressed. Casualty tolls diverge too: Al-Shifa Hospital’s director reports three killed, including two children, and eight wounded, while Israel Hayom cites eight killed overall.

Why it matters It is the most serious flare-up inside Gaza City since the ceasefire mechanism’s own overseer, Mladenov, warned last week that the arrangement risks collapse, and it comes with no agreed account of what actually happened.

Haaretz · Israel Hayom · Al-Araby (via secondary reporting) · Al Jazeera.

Nepal-Tibet flood toll climbs past 950, still driven by body recovery rather than a resolved count

The combined death toll has more than doubled in a week as search teams reach areas cut off by the disaster; a UN assessment finds tens of thousands still need emergency water and sanitation support.

The combined Nepal-Tibet flash-flood death toll now stands at roughly 955 — 939 in Nepal and 16 in Tibet — with more than 4,400 people missing, up from 919 dead and roughly 4,700 missing as of Sunday. As in prior days, the climbing count appears to reflect ongoing body recovery, including bodies found up to 240 kilometers downstream in India, rather than the earlier reconciliation dispute between Nepali agencies. A UN assessment finds roughly 65,000 people need emergency water, sanitation and hygiene support and more than 22,000 children need protection services.

ABC News/AP · NPR (UN assessment) · Al Jazeera.

DRC’s Ebola outbreak becomes the country’s largest ever recorded

The case count has crossed 6,000 with the death toll approaching 3,000, and the UN Secretary-General calls it the fastest-spreading Ebola epidemic on record.

The Democratic Republic of Congo’s Ministry of Communication and Media disclosed Monday that confirmed cases have crossed 6,000 (6,041) with 2,911 deaths, up from 5,794 confirmed and 2,786 deaths as of Aug. 27 — now the largest Ebola outbreak in the country’s history. UN Secretary-General Guterres separately called it “the fastest-spreading Ebola epidemic ever recorded.” The case-fatality ratio holds near 48%. Pending WHO corroboration The newer government figure has not yet been echoed in a fresh WHO Disease Outbreak News bulletin, whose own last update (Aug. 26) showed 5,815 confirmed across DRC, Uganda and France combined; treat the 6,041 figure as government-sourced and not yet independently confirmed by WHO.

Why it matters This is the Bundibugyo strain, for which no approved vaccine or treatment exists, unlike the Zaire-strain outbreaks that current vaccines target — and the case count is still rising with response funding reported running low.

DRC Ministry of Communication and Media (primary) · WHO Disease Outbreak News (primary, last dated Aug. 26) · UN News · Al Jazeera.

Lancaster County coroner confirms a new measles-linked death report as the state-federal dispute continues

A second report of a death “with measles” arrived Monday, separate from the infant case already at the center of the fight between Pennsylvania, HHS Secretary Kennedy and the CDC.

Lancaster County Coroner Stephen Diamantoni said his office received a new report Monday of a person who died “with measles” — distinct from the infant case his office already ruled a death by lacerated spleen, not measles. Diamantoni says it is too early to determine cause and unclear whether this is the same second death Gov. Shapiro cited last week. The underlying dispute stands unresolved: Pennsylvania’s Health Secretary Bogen maintains two measles-associated deaths occurred; the CDC has declined to add Pennsylvania’s deaths to its national tally pending its own review; and HHS Secretary Kennedy has suggested on social media, without support, that the deaths “may even have been altogether fabricated” by Shapiro’s staff — reported here as a statement, not a fact. Pennsylvania has run more than 4,100 MMR vaccinations at 91 pop-up clinics since April; the outbreak's case count stands near 460 across 31 counties.

House returns for an expected floor vote on the government funding bill

H.R. 6500, already passed 90-6 in the Senate, needs a two-thirds vote under the suspension procedure Republican leadership plans to use — a higher bar than a simple majority, with the exact vote hour still unconfirmed.

The House convenes at 11:00 AM ET for a session expected to include a floor vote on H.R. 6500, the “Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027,” which would fund the government through Dec. 11 and already passed the Senate 90-6 on Aug. 8. Anthropic’s infostealer warning keeps spreading through security press as Hasbro discloses an employee data breach

No new user-impact numbers on the Claude account-hijacking campaign, but a fresh disclosure shows the cost of a slow breach-notification timeline elsewhere.

Anthropic’s Sunday disclosure — six infostealer malware families (Vidar, LummaC2, StealC, RedLine, Acreed and Atomic Stealer) hijacking Claude account sessions via stolen browser cookies — continued rippling through trade press overnight, with no updated figure yet on how many accounts were affected and no parallel disclosure from another AI lab. Separately, Hasbro has begun notifying state regulators (a Massachusetts filing lists 436 affected residents so far) that the March cyberattack that took its systems offline also exposed employee Social Security numbers, financial account and payment card data, and driver’s license numbers — a roughly five-month gap between the intrusion and notification. And CISA added two chainable PaperCut print-management vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Sunday, with a Sept. 14 federal remediation deadline, alongside new research from Sygnia describing the China-nexus “Fire Ant” actor — previously known for VMware ESXi compromise — expanding into Cisco routers and authentication servers. No US government agency has confirmed the China-nexus attribution.

Why it matters Together these show the same pattern on three fronts: AI-subscription accounts, employee records and network infrastructure are all being targeted by increasingly patient, increasingly broad campaigns, while individual companies’ and the federal government’s own disclosure and remediation timelines keep lagging behind them.

Anthropic (primary) · CISA KEV catalog (primary) · Sygnia (primary research) · Hasbro SEC filings and Massachusetts AG breach notification (primary) · BleepingComputer · The Hacker News · SecurityWeek.
