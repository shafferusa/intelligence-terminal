Wall Street Turns Hawkish on the Fed as War Reaches the Edge of Mecca

Wednesday, September 16, 2026 · 27 min read

The Fed decides at 2 o’clock with Wall Street’s biggest banks now betting on a hike Washington is publicly fighting to prevent, while Saudi Arabia says it downed a drone aimed at Mecca, Yemen’s chokepoint war reaches full territorial collapse, and Pennsylvania records its fourth measles death of the year.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The world. Saudi Arabia says it shot down a Houthi drone bound for Mecca — a claim the Houthis call a lie — just as Houthi forces complete control of the Bab el-Mandeb corridor and the House votes, for a third time, to end the Iran war.
  • Markets. Futures point higher (S&P futures +1.1%) and gold and copper are both firmer too, an unusual combination heading into the most consequential Fed meeting since 2023.
  • The thread. Consequential decisions are moving forward because the arguments have hardened, not because they’ve quieted: one likely hike, three war votes, one grand-jury subpoena and a fourth state measles death, all landing within a day of each other.
  • Biggest risk. No forecast has been reconciled. Hike-odds trackers still span ten-plus points even as they skew hawkish, and Saudi Arabia’s own “red line” language on Mecca could turn into a direct military response within days if the drone claim is confirmed.
  • Washington. The Justice Department has subpoenaed former CIA Director John Brennan to a Florida grand jury — the same day the prosecutor leading that probe resigned — and Pennsylvania’s measles death toll reached four, the most in the US since 1992.
  • Watch today. The Fed’s 2:00 PM rate decision and Chair Warsh’s 2:30 PM press conference — the first test of whether he can hike without reigniting the bond-market stress of July.

Top Stories

Wall Street’s Biggest Banks Now Expect the Fed to Hike, Not Cut

Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, HSBC and Deutsche Bank have all moved to a 25-basis-point hike call this week, even as the White House escalates its pressure campaign and prediction markets still can’t agree on the odds.

The Federal Reserve announces its rate decision and updated Summary of Economic Projections at 2:00 PM ET today, with Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference to follow around 2:30. If the bank consensus holds, it would be the Fed’s first hike since 2023 — a genuinely unusual outcome after a year in which this outlet has mostly covered the case for cuts. The backdrop: August core CPI ran hotter than markets wanted, oil has extended its run past $100 a barrel as the Gulf war has widened, and the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.041% intraday Tuesday — its highest since July 2007 — before settling near 5.00%, where it remained early Wednesday, per CNBC.

Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, HSBC and Deutsche Bank have all moved off “no change” calls to expect a quarter-point hike this week, and Bank of America projects 75 basis points of hikes across 2026 — a genuine shift in bank consensus, not a probability nudge. Odds trackers, though, still disagree by a wide margin: CME FedWatch–derived readings cluster from roughly 87% to 93% for a hike, Polymarket sits near 81% (oddly below its own 85% reading for the following meeting in October), and Kalshi contracts have ranged from the high-40s to high-60s percent depending on the exact snapshot sampled this week. This report labels any single number here Estimated rather than picking one — the trackers have moved toward “hike” together without actually converging.

President Trump has escalated his own pressure campaign, reportedly threatening to halt US trade with surplus countries if the Fed doesn’t cut — an addition to a week that has also included Vance, Bessent and Hassett weighing in. Single-reliable-source That specific threat comes from wire aggregation; this report has not independently located a primary Trump statement making it. Treasury Secretary Bessent’s own Tuesday remark — that “the bond market has taken out more governments than howitzers” — reads to some observers as cover for Warsh to hike despite the pressure. National Economic Council Director Hassett struck a different note, saying “we respect [the Fed’s] independence” while separately arguing the data itself, not politics, would justify a hold rather than a cut — a distinction worth keeping rather than flattening into a single administration position.

Why it matters A hike delivered over open White House objection would be a real test of whether the Fed chair the administration itself installed in May will diverge publicly from it — and after Tuesday’s multi-year-high yield print, a test of whether the bond market takes that independence as credible.
What’s next Retail Sales and Import/Export Price Indexes land at 8:30 AM, the NAHB Housing Market Index at 10:00 — both before the decision, and both capable of moving odds that banks consider settled but markets don’t. The Summary of Economic Projections will show whether Wednesday is one-and-done or the start of a series.

Saudi Arabia Says It Downed a Drone Aimed at Mecca; Houthi Forces Call the Claim a Lie

If confirmed, it would be the first Houthi attempt on Islam’s holiest city in nearly a decade — neither side’s account has been independently verified.

Disputed Saudi-led coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki said air defenses intercepted a hostile drone at roughly 6:50 PM local time Tuesday before it entered restricted airspace around Mecca, with wreckage falling south of the city. He called the protection of pilgrims and holy sites a “red line” and warned of further deterrent measures. A Houthi military source told Yemen’s state SABA news agency the claim was “a worn-out lie,” saying Houthi targets remain Saudi oil facilities and military bases “far removed from the holy places.” Neither government has released forensic evidence establishing the drone’s actual trajectory or intended target, and the two accounts directly conflict.

The claim lands the same week Houthi forces completed control of the Bab el-Mandeb corridor (next story) — a confirmed strike credibly linked to Mecca would cross a line that has held even through five years of regional war.

What’s next Saudi officials have promised “further deterrent measures” if the claim is substantiated; reaction from other Gulf states and the broader Muslim world would likely follow confirmation either way.

Saudi-led coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki (primary) · Houthi statement via SABA (primary, single-party) · Al Jazeera, CNN, CNBC, TRT World.

Houthi Forces Complete Control of the Bab el-Mandeb Corridor as the UN Reports Nearly 2,900 War Casualties Since August

The capture of Mokha, Perim Island and the Hanish islands ends a four-year truce and hands a hostile, Iran-aligned force command of a passage carrying roughly 9% of global maritime trade.

A September offensive gave Houthi forces the port of Mokha (around Sept. 10), Perim Island (Sept. 11) and the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands (Sept. 14) — completing effective control of the Yemeni side of Bab el-Mandeb. Yemen’s Saudi-backed government claimed a partial counter-advance near Jardad in western Taiz over the weekend with coalition air support; no independent confirmation of that claim was found. UN Special Envoy for Yemen Hans Grundberg told the Security Council, in an emergency session France requested Tuesday, that the country faces “a new and more dangerous phase of war.” The World Health Organization put casualties since Aug. 6 at 2,883; the International Organization for Migration counted 93,864 people newly displaced by the September fighting, some crossing the strait to Djibouti.

Secondary-sourced This report could not reach the UN’s own briefing pages directly this morning; the WHO and IOM figures above come via wire citation of the UN session, not a primary UN document.

Why it matters This reframes what had read as an intermittent strike-and-response cycle into a territorial outcome with a direct global-shipping consequence — a hostile, Iran-aligned force now controls both Red Sea chokepoints this outlet tracks.

UN Security Council session (secondary wire citation) · FDD analysis · Euronews, Times of Israel, Al Jazeera, Arab News.

House Votes a Third Time to End the Iran War, With a Record Seven Republicans Crossing Over

The 220–204 vote late Tuesday is non-binding without Senate action, but the Republican defection count has nearly doubled since the last such vote in July.

The House passed a concurrent resolution directing the president to remove US forces from hostilities against Iran absent a declaration of war, 220–204, in a roll call recorded at 10:32 PM ET Tuesday — after this outlet’s Tuesday edition had already published. Seven Republicans crossed over: Reps. Massie (KY), Fitzpatrick (PA), Davidson (OH), Barrett (MI), Mace (SC), Miller-Meeks (IA) and Nunn (IA), the last three voting this way for the first time. The measure is not binding in practice — prior versions have not reached the president’s desk, and a veto would be expected — but the defection count has grown from zero in March to roughly four in June and July to seven now.

The vote lands the same week as Tuesday’s Pentagon Inspector General report putting the war’s cost at $33.4 billion and warning of a munitions shortfall, and as Vice President Vance told the New York Post the war’s “first phase” is complete and a “much different” second phase begins within “a couple of months” — broadly endorsing President Trump’s own prediction that the conflict winds down after November’s midterms. That timeline sits in tension with an independent shipping tracker, straits.live, which counted the Strait of Hormuz at day 198 of an effective closure this week, transits still near zero against a normal baseline near 85 ships a day; this report cannot reconcile the two and presents both without adjudicating.

US Destroys Iranian Boats After an Attempt to Seize a Navy Drone in the Strait of Hormuz

CENTCOM also disputed Iran’s account of a separate tanker strike this week, saying a drone — not a naval mine — disabled the vessel.

CENTCOM spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins said Iranian boats “recently attempted to take possession” of a roughly 25-foot Navy Saildrone unmanned surface vessel used for maritime surveillance; US forces “forcefully responded,” destroying both boats. Iranian state media separately reported an attack on fishing boats in Hormozgan province with several fishermen reported missing; it is unclear whether this describes the same incident.

Disputed On a separate incident, Iran’s IRGC said the Panama-flagged tanker El Gaia struck a naval mine south of the strait. CENTCOM rejected that account, saying the vessel had already been disabled by an Iranian missile strike last month and was hit again by an Iranian drone over the weekend while sitting off Oman — calling the mine account “yet another example of [IRGC] lies and intimidation.” Neither incident has independent maritime-tracker confirmation.

Justice Department Subpoenas Ex-CIA Director Brennan; Prosecutor Leading the Probe Resigns the Same Day

Brennan is ordered to testify before a grand jury next month in the Florida courthouse that dismissed Trump’s own documents case — his lawyer says DOJ could not clearly articulate the underlying crime.

Attorney Ken Wainstein disclosed that DOJ delivered a grand-jury subpoena the prior Thursday requiring Brennan to testify Oct. 15 in Fort Pierce, Florida — the courthouse of Judge Aileen Cannon, who dismissed the Mar-a-Lago documents case in 2024. DOJ is reportedly pursuing two tracks: a broad “grand conspiracy” inquiry into whether former law-enforcement and intelligence officials conspired to deprive Trump of civil rights during the Russia investigations, and a narrower question of whether Brennan lied to Congress. CBS News reports two former FBI officials tied to the Trump-Russia probe were also subpoenaed. Wainstein sought a preservation order for DOJ records, arguing indictment risk is not speculative and saying DOJ could not clearly state the crime being investigated. Separately, prosecutor Joe diGenova — who had led the “grand conspiracy” investigation — resigned the same day the subpoena surfaced, amid reported frustration inside DOJ and the White House over the probe’s pace.

Brennan denies wrongdoing. This report has not found an on-record statement establishing the specific charge, if any, DOJ is contemplating.

Why it matters A sitting administration convening a grand jury against a former CIA director who was a chief antagonist of the first Trump term ties directly into this outlet’s tracked “Arctic Frost” thread, which FBI Director Patel declined to discuss at Tuesday’s Senate Judiciary hearing.

Ken Wainstein’s statement (primary) · NPR, CBS News, ABC News/AP wire, Fox News.

Pennsylvania’s Measles Death Toll Reaches Four — the Most in the US Since 1992

A newly confirmed 18-year-old victim and lab confirmation of an earlier “probable” case both landed this week, while the CDC still counts none of the state’s deaths nationally.

Pennsylvania health officials confirmed two additional measles-associated deaths Monday: a Jefferson County resident whose postmortem testing has now confirmed an active measles infection at time of death (resolving the “probable” label this outlet carried last week), and an 18-year-old Mifflin County resident whose cause of death was acute disseminated encephalomyelitis, a rare and severe measles complication. Both were unvaccinated. The state total now stands at four deaths in 2026, against roughly 700 cases across 38 counties, about 100 of them in the past week. The CDC’s national tally — which recently shifted to rely solely on NCHS cause-of-death coding rather than state case reports — still shows zero 2026 measles deaths; Pennsylvania disputes the federal characterization of what its own epidemiologists provided.

Why it matters This is the most US measles deaths recorded in a single year since 1992, and it sharpens rather than resolves an ongoing federal-state dispute over how those deaths get counted.

Pennsylvania Department of Health, Jefferson County Coroner (primary) · STAT News, CNN, AP wire.

Kennedy Center Appeals Naming Ruling to the D.C. Circuit as Trump Ties Renovation to the Outcome

A federal judge called the board’s renaming maneuver “linguistic gymnastics” in blocking any memorial without congressional authorization — DOJ is seeking an expedited appeal.

Judge Christopher Cooper ruled Tuesday that the Kennedy Center’s board cannot install any memorial — Trump-named or otherwise — without authorization from Congress, a decision one characterization described as calling the board’s renaming effort “linguistic gymnastics.” DOJ and the Kennedy Center formally appealed to the D.C. Circuit Tuesday evening, and Trump said DOJ will seek an expedited hearing. On Truth Social, Trump framed the building’s closure — already voted by the board — as proceeding immediately, while “Renovation and Reconstruction…cannot begin” until the appeals court rules on the board’s chosen name.

Unconfirmed A related D.C. Circuit appeal (No. 25-7156) already has oral argument scheduled for Oct. 13; this report could not confirm whether Tuesday’s filing is on that same docket or a separate track.

Zelensky Confirms a Strike on a Russian Refinery as Nearly 150 Drones Hit Ukraine Overnight

Kyiv says it struck the Syzran refinery in Russia’s Samara region, undercutting Trump’s claimed energy-strike truce, even as Moscow launched one of its largest drone barrages in weeks.

Zelensky confirmed Ukraine struck the Syzran oil refinery in Russia’s Samara region Monday, despite Trump’s claim two days earlier of a mutual pause on energy-infrastructure strikes. Russia, in turn, launched roughly 149 drones at Ukraine overnight Monday into Tuesday (Ukraine’s air force count; other wire figures run closer to 200, a range this report cannot resolve), about half jet-powered Shaheds and the rest Gerbera/Parody decoys; Ukraine says it downed 128. Strikes hit 13 locations; a shopping-mall strike in Sumy killed at least two and injured 23. NATO aviation assets flew over Polish airspace overnight in response to drone activity near the border, continuing from Sunday’s pledge by NATO Secretary-General Rutte to boost eastern-flank support.

Zelensky’s own statement (primary) · NBC News, ABC News, The Hill, Al Jazeera.

OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind Confirm Weeks of Quiet AI-Safety Coordination

The three labs have reportedly discussed forming a shared safety-standards body — the first concrete follow-through on last week’s “pace the frontier” call, as a second oversight track opens in New York City.

OpenAI global policy chief Chris Lehane told reporters in Washington Tuesday that OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind have been coordinating on AI safety “for weeks” and have discussed forming a shared safety-standards body — still at the discussion stage, not yet formed. It comes as Rep. Casar continues pressing, in a Fox News interview, for OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s CEOs to testify under oath after both missed a second congressional deadline Tuesday; no response from either company has surfaced since, and Speaker Johnson has said developers, not Congress, bear responsibility for AI safety and has not scheduled a hearing. Separately, New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin has invited both companies to a municipal AI hearing set for Oct. 5 — a second, non-federal oversight track.

Chris Lehane’s remarks (primary) · TechCrunch, Bloomberg · casar.house.gov, City & State NY.

A Maximum-Severity Flaw in Widely Used API Gateway Software Is Under Active Attack

Security researchers say forged admin credentials targeting WSO2 API Manager have been captured in honeypots since last weekend — the second live enterprise-infrastructure exploitation story in two weeks.

CVE-2026-5430 (CVSS 9.8), a JSON Web Token signature-verification bypass in WSO2 API Manager, Control Plane, Traffic Manager and Universal Gateway, is under active exploitation, according to security firm watchTowr, whose honeypots began capturing forged admin-privilege tokens Sept. 13. The flaw affects WSO2 API Manager versions 4.1.0 through 4.6.0 and related products; it has not yet been added to CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog as of this morning. No attribution to a specific threat actor has been offered.

watchTowr research, WSO2 security advisory WSO2-2026-5328 (primary) · The Hacker News, GuardianMSSP.

Wednesday’s decision follows a summer of data that turned more hawkish than expected. Payrolls (August, 159.075 million, BLS) grew modestly from July; unemployment held at 4.1%. Initial claims for the week of Sept. 5 eased to 206,000 from a slightly revised 207,000, still consistent with a labor market cooling without cracking.

The Treasury’s par-yield curve — still showing Tuesday’s close, since Wednesday’s has not yet posted — has the 2-year note at 4.67% against a 10-year at 5.00%: a 33-basis-point 2s10s spread, an 89-basis-point 3-month-to-10-year spread, and a 53-basis-point 5s30s spread.
