Top Stories

Trump and Xi hold their summit; the Taiwan arms question stays unresolved

The Rose Garden ceremony and bilateral meeting went ahead as planned and the overnight trade-truce extension was confirmed on the record — but the $14 billion arms package Beijing wanted addressed has no announced outcome heading into tonight’s state dinner.

The two leaders’ bilateral meeting ran a little over an hour this afternoon, opened by a Rose Garden welcome ceremony with the Old Guard Fife and Drum Corps, a military flyover, and both countries’ flags flown together — the first such Washington ceremony for a Chinese leader in more than a decade. A state dinner in the East Room follows tonight, with US business executives reportedly among the guests; it falls after this edition’s publication.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed on the record what had circulated overnight: the US and China have extended their trade truce, previously set to expire November 10, through January 10, 2027, alongside a new bilateral channel for AI-safety incidents. China’s Xinhua news agency, in its own readout of the meeting, said Xi urged the US to oppose “Taiwan independence”; no joint written statement from the two governments has been issued.

The $14 billion Taiwan arms package that China had flagged as a potential irritant going into the visit remains undecided. A senior administration official told reporters Trump would decide “in a fairly short time,” without a date. Single source One outlet reported Trump may hold off any decision until after November’s APEC summit — a single account, not corroborated elsewhere, and not confirmed by the White House.

Why it matters This is the first Chinese state visit to Washington in over a decade, and the one concrete flashpoint identified before it began — the arms package — is still open. How and when it resolves will say more about the relationship than today’s ceremony did.
What’s next Tonight’s state dinner, and any statement on the arms package before Xi departs Friday.

Costco beats estimates after the bell, again helped by a one-time tariff refund

Fiscal fourth-quarter EPS of $6.75 topped both vendor estimates analysts had split on, but revenue landed just under consensus even as comparable sales accelerated in every region.

Costco’s own 8-K, filed at 4:17 PM ET, shows fourth-quarter (16-week) net sales of $93.9 billion, up 11.2% year over year, and fiscal-year sales of $297.2 billion, up 10.1%. Diluted EPS was $6.75, against $5.87 a year ago; full-year diluted EPS was $20.76, against $18.21. Total-company comparable sales rose 9.4% (6.7% excluding gasoline-price and currency swings), with every region positive; digitally-enabled sales rose 19.5%.

The company’s own release says the quarter “was positively impacted by a non-recurring benefit of $0.15 per diluted share from IEEPA tariff refunds received in the quarter, less partial reinvestment of those refunds in increased member values” — the same dynamic an Oppenheimer analyst flagged ahead of this report as a risk to reading the underlying business off the headline number. Reported revenue of $93.9 billion came in just under the roughly $94.9 billion analysts had penciled in, even with the comp-sales strength.

No after-hours share reaction was available by this edition’s publication; the earnings call is scheduled for 2:00 PM PT today.

Why it matters Comparable-sales acceleration across every region and format is a genuine read on consumer spending heading into the holidays — but the second straight quarter leaning on a one-time refund makes the underlying trend harder to isolate.

Costco Wholesale Corp. 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (primary, SEC EDGAR) — Confirmed-primary.

This week’s Treasury auctions keep finding weak demand

Today’s seven-year note priced at its highest yield since 1993, extending Wednesday’s tailed five-year auction — a pattern equities have so far shrugged off.

The Treasury’s $44 billion seven-year note auction priced at a 5.085% high yield — the highest for the tenor since April 1993 — with a 2.42 bid-to-cover ratio, down from roughly 2.50 at the prior seven-year auction. Indirect bidders took 57.2% of the competitive award, down from about 61% last time; primary dealers were left holding 12.5% of competitive bids, a smaller share than typical, which points to genuine end-investor demand rather than dealer absorption alone easing the internals slightly even as the headline yield and bid-to-cover both weakened.

It follows Wednesday’s five-year note auction, which tailed by 3.1 basis points on a 2.212 bid-to-cover — the weakest since December 2018 — and helped push the 10-year yield to its highest since 2007. The par-yield curve’s 10-year point rose a further 7 basis points today to 5.18%.

Why it matters Two soft auctions in three days, in the same week the 10-year crossed levels last seen in 2007, is the clearest sign yet that higher financing costs are not a one-day story.

TreasuryDirect auction results (primary) — Confirmed-primary.

Court-ordered press access broke down at the gate this morning, then was restored

Judge Kelly’s overnight order restoring CNN, MS NOW and Politico’s White House access wasn’t honored when reporters showed up — by early afternoon the dispute resolved without further court intervention.

Despite the 14-day temporary restraining order Judge Kelly issued overnight, reporters from the three outlets were turned away again this morning. MS NOW, CNN and Politico filed a notice of noncompliance, and Kelly ordered the administration to respond in writing by 12:30 PM ET. A White House declaration said access had been “restored as of 9:55 a.m.”; by around noon, outlets reported their journalists were being let back onto the grounds, with MS NOW’s Secret Service-held credentials returned just before the deadline.

What’s next Judge Kelly has not yet ruled on the underlying merits of the case; today’s TRO enforcement dispute suggests that ruling may come with its own compliance questions.

Russia hits Kyiv and Odesa overnight as Zelensky floats a G20 Miami venue for talks

Zircon and Iskander missiles plus more than 280 drones struck Ukraine’s capital and south overnight, killing at least two, as Zelensky named a possible venue for the trilateral meeting he proposed Monday.

Ukraine’s Air Force said Russia fired Zircon anti-ship missiles, Iskander ballistic missiles and 282 drones overnight, concentrated on Kyiv and the Odesa region; two people were killed and eight injured in Kyiv. That is separate from the roughly 280-drone, six-dead barrage this morning’s edition reported in the Kharkiv region — two distinct strike waves in the same 24 hours, not a double count of one. Zelensky said the Kyiv strikes began while he was meeting US lawmakers on Russia sanctions.

Briefing reporters in New York, Zelensky floated the G20’s Miami summit as a possible venue for the three-way Trump-Putin-Zelensky meeting he raised earlier this week, saying Kyiv would support that format if agreed. Separately, the Trump administration is reportedly pushing three steps — an energy-infrastructure strike ceasefire, reopening the Black Sea grain corridor, and a trilateral meeting possibly in Abu Dhabi — though Zelensky voiced skepticism Putin would actually attend. The Kremlin’s Peskov again called a Putin-Zelensky meeting “premature” and a “waste of time” absent prior expert-level talks.

Why it matters A third distinct meeting format has now been floated this week with no agreement on venue, sequencing or even whether Putin would attend — while the strikes that make the case for urgency continue nightly.

Jobless claims fall, new home sales jump — but the current-account deficit widens again

Two upside labor and housing surprises landed the same morning as a wider external deficit and a bond market still pricing more Fed tightening.

Initial jobless claims for the week ending September 19 came in at 197,000, below a roughly 201,000–204,000 consensus range, with the prior week revised to 198,000 from 196,000. The four-week average sits at 202,250; continuing claims were 1.719 million, below the roughly 1.745 million expected. New home sales jumped 6.4% in August to a 684,000 seasonally adjusted annual rate, from an upwardly revised 643,000 in July, though still down 2.0% year over year; the median price was $393,700, up 0.4% month over month but down 5.8% from a year ago, with 8.5 months’ supply on the market.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis’s second-quarter current-account figures, also released today, show the deficit widening by $33.4 billion (15.7%) to $246.0 billion, or 3.0% of GDP, up from 2.7% in the first quarter — driven by an expanded goods deficit, the agency’s own release says, partly offset by smaller income-account deficits. The net international investment position stood at negative $22.42 trillion.

Why it matters A cooling-but-not-cracking labor market and a genuine housing bounce sit awkwardly next to a widening external deficit and a bond market that keeps pricing in more tightening — not a contradiction, but not a clean read either.

US Department of Labor (primary, via wire) · Census Bureau/HUD (primary) · Bureau of Economic Analysis (primary) — Confirmed-primary.
