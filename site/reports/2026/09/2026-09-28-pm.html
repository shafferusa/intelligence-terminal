Top Stories

Bond selloff resumes: 2-year jumps 11 basis points and 10-year closes at 5.24% as stocks slip

The short end rose most, so the curve flattened, and the day’s only Fed speaker kept the hike case alive. The market is now closer to pricing an October hike before PCE and payrolls than to pricing a pause.

Treasury published today’s par-yield curve this afternoon. Every tenor from 3 months to 30 years rose:

  • 2-year: 4.92%, up from 4.81% on Friday.
  • 5-year: 5.06%, up 8bp.
  • 10-year: 5.24%, up 7bp. That is above last week’s 5.17–5.18% closes. CNBC and Bloomberg put it at its highest level since 2007.
  • 30-year: 5.56%, up 7bp.
Because the front end rose most, the gap between the 2-year and 10-year yields narrowed to 32bp from 36bp. That kind of move, where short rates rise faster than long rates, usually reflects expectations for Fed policy more than worries about the long-run supply of bonds.

Governor Lisa Cook spoke in Oakland this morning. Her text is on the Fed’s website and was read directly for this edition. She said she “voted along with the rest of the FOMC to raise rates 25 basis points” on September 16. Her figures:

  • Total inflation: about 3.8% in the twelve months to August, “almost double our target.”
  • Core inflation: about 3.4%.
She said further pressure would come from “the pass-through of higher oil prices and supply chain disruptions associated with the conflict in the Middle East.” On the path ahead, she said “the number and magnitude of any future adjustments will be informed by observations of the economy’s reaction to our policy actions thus far.” That is guarded language, and she did not signal a pause. Her 3.8% and 3.4% are the Fed’s own estimates. The official August PCE figures arrive Wednesday.

Stocks fell from the open. By late morning the Nasdaq was down about 1% and the S&P about 0.8%, and neither made up much ground before the close. Likely contributor Market reports from CNBC and the Motley Fool named rising yields as the main pressure on stocks. Today’s bond and stock moves are consistent with that. It is still an interpretation, and several stock-specific losses (below) added to the decline.

Credit weakened too, but less than stocks:

  • The high-yield bond fund HYG fell 0.41%. It traded 96 million shares, about 2.7 times its 50-day average.
  • The investment-grade fund LQD fell 0.72%, to within 29 cents of its 52-week low.
  • FRED’s high-yield spread series widened to 2.93% on Friday from 2.68% a week earlier. That is the latest print; today’s value is not yet posted.
Yahoo Finance summarised a Goldman Sachs note warning that heavy high-yield supply is weighing on the market. Paramount’s roughly $44 billion secured bond sale is the largest recent example (see Business).

Why it matters For the second week running, the pressure on stocks is coming from rates rather than from earnings. A 2-year near 4.92% is pricing more Fed tightening, not less, going into Wednesday’s PCE and Friday’s payrolls. A hot print on either could push the 2-year above 5%.

Oil gives back its Hormuz jump as Saudi pipeline reports cross; gold falls about 4%

Oil jumped overnight after Trump rejected Iran’s offer, but it had faded by the settle. Brent finished up less than a dollar. Gold fell about 4% as yields and the dollar rose.

Brent crude traded as high as $108.83 during the day. It settled at $105.28 for the front month, up $0.96. WTI settled at $92.60, up $0.19, well below the roughly $96 it touched in Asian trading. Both settlements come from CNBC.

Likely contributor CNBC reported in the afternoon that Saudi Arabia is increasing flows on its East-West pipeline again. It said flows are back to about 3.5 million barrels a day and loadings have resumed at the Red Sea port of Yanbu. The pipeline carries Saudi crude around the Strait of Hormuz, so that report fits the afternoon fade. Saudi Aramco has not publicly confirmed the figure, so this edition treats it as reported, not confirmed.

On the diplomatic track, Iran’s ISNA agency, cited by Reuters, said Foreign Minister Araghchi would meet Qatari mediators in New York. A US official said Washington would meet the mediators separately today or tomorrow. The discussion is about a revised version of Iran’s seven-day proposal. No direct US–Iran meeting is planned, and no outcome had been reported by publication. Bloomberg reported that Iranian officials privately doubt a deal is possible before the US midterms.

Gold futures fell about 4.0%, to roughly $4,149. The gold fund GLD fell 3.94% on about 1.4 times its normal volume. Several outlets reported that gold hit its lowest level since early August. Silver fell below $62. Likely contributor Rising real and nominal yields and a firmer dollar (DXY up 0.22%, to 101.19) coincided with the drop. This edition found no single confirmed catalyst beyond those.

Why it matters Last week’s inflation worry rested on a Hormuz premium in oil. Today that premium was tested and partly given back. With Brent back near $105, the oil threat to inflation is smaller than it looked this morning, though Cook specifically named oil pass-through as a risk.

CNBC (settlements, Saudi pipeline report) · Reuters via JPost and Arab News (ISNA, Qatari mediation) · Bloomberg · Yahoo Finance futures data. Confirmed-multiple for prices; the pipeline flow figure is single-source.

Boeing falls about 7% after the FAA delays 737 Max 10 certification over a software glitch

The FAA will not certify the largest 737 until it has assessed a problem in the automated flight-guidance system that Boeing itself flagged last month.

Boeing closed down 6.91%, at $184.39. The FAA said it will hold off on certifying the 737 Max 10 while it evaluates a glitch in the aircraft’s automated flight-guidance software. Bloomberg and CNBC both reported this. GE Aerospace, which supplies the jet’s LEAP engines through a joint venture, fell 2.71%.

The Max 10 is the largest version of the 737. Airlines including United have large orders for it. Certification has already slipped several times. The FAA has not given a new timeline.

Why it matters The delay widens the gap between Boeing’s delivery plans and what the FAA will approve. Airlines waiting for the plane, including United (−2.2% today, a drop that also reflected higher oil), may have to change their fleet plans.

FAA statement as reported by Bloomberg and CNBC · Yahoo Finance and Finnhub closes. Confirmed-multiple.

MongoDB sinks about 18% as its CEO leaves to run Meta’s new enterprise platform; software stocks fall with it

Meta hired MongoDB’s chief executive to lead a new product that competes directly with established business-software vendors. Several of those vendors’ stocks fell 3–5%.

MongoDB’s CEO, Chirantan “CJ” Desai, resigned effective immediately to become Meta’s chief enterprise platform officer. Former CEO Dev Ittycheria returns on an interim basis, and the company kept its third-quarter and full-year guidance. MongoDB closed down 18.46%, at $334.68.

Meta also launched a “Meta Enterprise Platform.” Meta fell 4.79%. Other software stocks fell too:

  • ServiceNow: −3.07%
  • Oracle: −3.28%
  • Salesforce: −2.88%
  • Snowflake: −2.33%
  • Microsoft: −1.35%
Market narrative 24/7 Wall St and Seeking Alpha framed the decline as investors repricing software for a new large competitor. That is a reasonable reading, but it is not the only one. All of these are long-duration growth stocks, and they fell on a day of sharply higher yields.

Nvidia adds $150 billion to its buyback and rises about 1.7% against a falling market

The new authorization brings Nvidia’s remaining repurchase capacity to about $235 billion. Bloomberg, CNBC and Reuters all called it the largest buyback addition on record.

Nvidia’s board approved an additional $150 billion for share repurchases. Nvidia closed up 1.68%, at $228.86, and was one of the few large tech stocks to rise today. The semiconductor fund SMH still fell 1.08%. Micron fell 2.61% two days before its earnings report.

Earlier in the day, investing.com reported that OpenAI’s second training pause (covered in this morning’s edition) weighed on Asian memory-chip stocks: SK Hynix fell about 4.8% and Samsung about 4.6%. No link between that news and US chip stocks has been established.

Starship reaches orbit for the first time, deploys 26 Starlink satellites, and splashes down in the Pacific

Flight 14 launched 33 minutes after the window opened. Two engines shut down early, but the Ship reached orbit and released its full satellite load. SpaceX then cut the mission short.

Booster 21 and Ship 41 launched from Starbase at 8:48 AM ET. One booster Raptor shut down during ascent, and the booster did not relight all its engines for the return. It still made what reports described as a soft splashdown in the Gulf. One of the Ship’s vacuum Raptors shut down early, so the Ship fired a single engine for about 19 extra seconds to reach an orbit roughly 170 miles high. It then deployed all 26 Starlink V3 satellites, and SpaceX confirmed contact with some of them.

SpaceX shortened the planned six-orbit flight and brought the Ship down in the northern Pacific at about 11:58 AM ET. That splashdown was harder and ended in a fireball. Musk posted: “First orbital flight of Starship successful!” NPR, CNN, Space.com and NASASpaceflight all reported the flight. The FAA had not said by publication whether it will require a mishap investigation for the engine failures.

SpaceX shares (SPCX) closed down 2.16%, at $145.47, in a falling market. This edition has no basis to link that move to the flight.

SpaceX webcast and posts, as reported by NPR, CNN, Space.com, NASASpaceflight · Finnhub close. Confirmed-multiple.

Trump announces a $15 billion Iowa steel plant, backed by up to $10 billion of Export-Import Bank financing

Mesabi Metallics, part of India’s Essar Group, plans to start production in 2030 using ore from its mine on Minnesota’s Iron Range.

Trump announced the project at the White House this afternoon. Details as reported by CNBC, CBS, NBC and ABC:

  • Capacity of 7.5 million tons a year in the first phase and 10 million tons at full build.
  • About 1,750 permanent jobs and up to 6,000 construction jobs.
  • Up to $10 billion in Ex-Im Bank financing.
CBS and Newsweek reported that it was billed as the largest steel plant in US history. That is the announcers’ description. No site, permit or financing documents have been filed publicly yet.

Why it matters A government-backed loan of this size to a new, non-US-owned steel producer would be unusual for the Ex-Im Bank. The plant would add capacity in an industry already protected by tariffs, which puts pressure on existing producers’ pricing over the next several years rather than soon.

White House announcement as reported by CNBC, CBS, NBC, ABC, The Hill. Confirmed-multiple.

What Moved Markets

Overnight and the open. Stock futures fell and oil rose overnight after Trump said of Iran’s offer, “They made a proposal but I rejected it.” S&P futures fell about 0.5% and Nasdaq futures about 1%. Brent reached $106–107 in Asia. Likely contributor The overnight oil jump followed the Hormuz news directly. Several outlets also cited the OpenAI training pause in explaining weakness in chip stocks. That is plausible for memory makers in Asia and unconfirmed for US names.

Morning. Treasury yields rose from the start, led by the 2-year, and stocks fell from the opening bell. Likely contributor Most market reports named rising yields as the main drag. Governor Cook’s morning speech, which said inflation is almost double the Fed’s target, came during the move and did nothing to ease it. There is no evidence that her speech itself started the move. The Dallas Fed’s factory survey came in slightly softer than August at 9.8 but above forecasts, and there is no sign it moved markets. At 11:30, Treasury sold $95 billion of 13-week bills at 4.110% and $82 billion of 26-week bills at 4.285%. Demand was ordinary, with bid-to-cover ratios of 2.99 and 2.64.

Midday. Three company stories hit at once:

  • Boeing’s certification delay.
  • MongoDB’s CEO leaving for Meta.
  • The fall in enterprise software that followed Meta’s launch.
Confirmed catalyst Boeing and MongoDB each fell on documented, company-specific news. Market narrative The fall in the wider software group is the market’s interpretation of Meta’s move, and higher yields were also weighing on those stocks at the same time. Nvidia’s buyback lifted Nvidia but not the Nasdaq.

Afternoon and close. Oil fell back from its highs after the Saudi pipeline report. Stocks did not recover and closed near their lows for the day. Likely contributor The Saudi report fits the fade in oil. Unexplained This edition found no specific reason for gold’s size of drop (about 4%) beyond the rise in yields and the dollar. A move that large on those factors alone is unusual, and positioning or liquidation may have played a part. That cannot be confirmed from free data.

Breadth and style. The equal-weight S&P (RSP, −0.65%) fell slightly less than the cap-weighted SPY (−0.74%). Small caps (IWM, −0.69%) did no worse than the market. The Magnificent Seven fund (MAGS, −1.54%) fell twice as much as the index, so the damage was concentrated in the largest stocks, not spread across the whole market. The VIX rose 1.20, to 16.07. Its term structure is still normal: 9-day 14.39 < 30-day 16.07 < 3-month 18.23. This was a steady decline, not panic selling.

The Economy

The Fed. The Fed raised rates by 25bp on September 16, to a range of 3.75–4.00%. The effective fed funds rate (DFF) is 3.88%. Cook’s speech is the clearest sign yet that the Board’s centrist governors, not only its hawks, see inflation risk as the priority. Her 3.8% headline and 3.4% core estimates for August are ahead of Wednesday’s official figures. Single source Market pricing of an October 28 hike was about 72% today, according to a CME FedWatch reading cited by a prediction-market site. That is up from the 60s last week, and Yahoo also reported odds above 70%. This edition’s own forecast of 60–70% for an October hike stays in place.

Treasury. Today’s bill auctions were routine. From TreasuryDirect:

  • 13-week: $95 billion at a 4.110% discount rate (4.211% investment yield), bid-to-cover 2.99.
  • 26-week: $82 billion at 4.285% (4.441% yield), bid-to-cover 2.64.
There are no coupon auctions this week. The next are 3-, 10- and 30-year notes and bonds on October 6–8. So none of today’s move in longer yields came from auction supply. The federal fiscal year ends Wednesday. Funding runs to December 11 under the continuing resolution, so there is no shutdown risk this week. Treasury Secretary Bessent said on Sunday that the Fed should keep an “open mind” because of productivity gains from AI and deregulation. That puts the Treasury on the dovish side of this debate, but it has no bearing on the October decision.

The curve, in plain terms. The 3-month bill yields 4.28% and the 10-year 5.24%, a gap of 96bp. The 2-year to 10-year gap is 32bp and the 5-year to 30-year gap 50bp. All of these are nominal yields. The latest real (inflation-adjusted) 10-year yield from FRED is Friday’s 2.83%, with 10-year expected inflation (breakevens) at 2.34%. Most of the rise in yields over the past two weeks has been in real yields. That means investors are demanding more return after inflation, not expecting much more inflation. SOFR, the benchmark overnight funding rate, was 3.90% on Friday, in line with the Fed’s new rate range.

Today’s data. The Dallas Fed’s Texas manufacturing index for September was 9.8, down from 11.6. Seeking Alpha put the consensus at about 6, and other forecast figures varied. The details were stronger than the headline:

  • Production: 29.5, the highest since July 2021.
  • New orders: 30.7.
  • Employment: 15.1.
Firms’ outlook for the future fell sharply, to 20.8 from 37.2. This is a regional survey and carries little weight on its own. It shows current activity running strong while firms expect less ahead.

Global. ECB President Lagarde told the European Parliament that “long-term interest rates have risen notably” since the ECB’s last meeting, which “will slow growth and reduce pass-through by more than projected.” She said measured hikes remain appropriate. That leaves the ECB less hawkish than the Fed. The Reserve Bank of Australia decides tonight at 12:30 AM ET, and a hike to 4.60% is expected. In China, Reuters reported that the CSI 300 fell more than 2%, to a one-year low, after a bipartisan push in Washington to ban Chinese data-centre components. The dollar index closed at 101.19, near its 52-week high of 101.80. The ECB’s reference rate for the euro was $1.1378.

The United States

Alito steps aside in the climate-liability case. The Supreme Court Clerk said Justice Samuel Alito “will not continue to participate” in Suncor Energy v. Boulder County. The case asks whether state-law climate-damage suits against oil companies can proceed, and it is set for argument on October 5. The letter gave no reason. Environmental groups had pointed to his reported holdings in ConocoPhillips and Phillips 66. NBC described the move as a reversal. With eight justices, a 4–4 tie would leave the lower-court ruling in place, and that ruling allowed Boulder’s suit to proceed. For the oil industry, the recusal makes a nationwide win harder to secure. (CNN, CNBC, NBC, Forbes. Confirmed-multiple.)

FBI co-deputy director resigns. Andrew Bailey, the former Missouri attorney general, appointed co-deputy director in September 2025, is resigning to “return to my family in Missouri.” His is the latest in a series of leadership changes under Director Kash Patel. (ABC, NBC, Fox, Washington Times. Confirmed-multiple.)

Immigration enforcement. Over the weekend the Department of Homeland Security announced a nationwide surge of enforcement in jurisdictions it calls sanctuary jurisdictions. It named California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, the District of Columbia, Illinois, Minnesota, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont and Washington. DHS denied reports that it had scaled back arrests and said it would continue arresting people who have no criminal record. Officials in the named places have previously argued that local non-cooperation policies are lawful and protect public safety. The administration argues they obstruct federal law.

Science

DarkSide finds no “nuclear” dark matter. The DarkSide collaboration searched for composite dark-matter particles in seven years of data from its liquid-argon detector and found no direct evidence of them. The result rules out a range of theories in which dark matter comes as larger, nucleus-like clumps. It is a null result, which is how dark-matter searches narrow the field. (Phys.org summary of the collaboration’s release. Single release.)

A face for the oldest known Homo sapiens. Researchers published a digital facial reconstruction of the roughly 315,000-year-old Jebel Irhoud fossils from Morocco. They are the oldest known remains of our species. A reconstruction like this is an interpretation built from the skull, not a measurement, but it helps explain how a modern-looking face developed before a modern-shaped braincase.

Hurricane Polo nears Baja California. Polo is a Category 3 hurricane with sustained winds near 115 mph and is expected to make landfall in Baja California Sur this evening, then in southern Sonora early Tuesday. The National Hurricane Center forecasts 6–12 inches of rain in Baja California Sur and 4–8 inches in Sonora, with risk of life-threatening flooding and mudslides.
