Top Stories

Stocks close out the week higher as the Treasury curve twists to fresh long-end highs

The S&P, Nasdaq and Dow all gained Friday even as the 20- and 30-year Treasury yields pushed to new multi-decade highs while the rest of the curve eased — a genuine divergence, not a uniform move in either direction.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,743.41 (+0.51%), the Nasdaq Composite at 27,068.72 (+0.48%), the Dow at 51,828.62 (+0.93%), and the Russell 2000 at 2,837.55 (+0.07%, essentially flat). It was the best day of the week for the Dow and enough to end a stretch of weekly losses heading into today, per multiple outlets.

Two economic releases landed ahead of the open. Durable Goods Orders for August came in essentially flat month over month, beating a consensus that had expected a 0.3–0.4% decline; core capital-goods orders (nondefense, ex-aircraft — a proxy for business investment) rose 1.6%, also above consensus, though the broader core reading (ex-transportation) rose a more modest 0.3%. The University of Michigan’s final September consumer-sentiment index came in at 48.1, up from the 47.8 preliminary and above the roughly 47.5 consensus — though still near a four-month low. The survey’s own director, Joanne Hsu, said both current and year-ahead personal-finance measures deteriorated roughly 10% from August, and flagged that one-year inflation expectations jumped to 4.6% from August’s 4.0%, the steepest one-month rise since June. Likely contributor The modestly-better-than-feared data coincided with today’s gains; it is not the only plausible contributor, alongside company-specific strength in Microsoft and Costco below.

The Treasury’s own par-yield curve, published this afternoon, shows a genuine twist rather than a uniform move: the 2-year fell 6 basis points to 4.81%, the 5-year fell 5bp to 4.98%, and the 10-year eased 1bp to 5.17% — but the 20-year rose 1bp to 5.54% and the 30-year added 2bp to 5.49%, both fresh multi-decade highs. 2s10s widened to 36bp from Thursday’s 31bp. No Treasury auction was scheduled today, so today’s move has no auction-internals explanation the way Wednesday’s and Thursday’s did.

Among mega-caps, Microsoft rose 3.66% to $516.17, extending a rally that has carried the stock roughly 32% higher over the past month on continued Azure and Copilot strength, plus a Cantor Fitzgerald price-target raise to $608 from $522 this week. Costco and Meta both moved sharply in opposite directions; both are covered below.

Why it matters A long end that keeps pushing to new highs even on a day the rest of the curve eases doesn’t fit the clean “one more hike priced in” story that has driven the past two weeks — it complicates that narrative without resolving it either way.

Treasury par-yield curve, FRED, Census Bureau, University of Michigan Surveys of Consumers (primary) · Yahoo Finance close-to-close series · CNBC, Bloomberg — Confirmed-primary (data), Confirmed-multiple (market wrap).

Xi’s three-day Washington visit ends without a Taiwan-arms decision

Xi and the Trumps had tea in the Red Room and toured the National Archives this morning before Xi’s delegation departed Joint Base Andrews this afternoon; the $14 billion arms package China wanted addressed going in is still undecided.

After Thursday’s Rose Garden ceremony, bilateral meeting and state dinner produced no joint statement, today’s schedule was lower-key: tea with the Trumps in the Red Room, then a National Archives tour taking in the Declaration of Independence, the Constitution and the Bill of Rights, before Xi’s delegation departed from Joint Base Andrews this afternoon. Multiple outlets characterized the three-day visit overall as heavy on ceremony and light on substance.

The $14 billion Taiwan arms package remains undecided. A senior administration official repeated Thursday’s language that Trump will decide “in a fairly short time,” without a date; no formal notification to Congress has been sent. A bipartisan Senate letter from Sens. McConnell, Cornyn, Murkowski and Tillis, urging Trump to proceed with more than $15 billion in stalled Taiwan security assistance, continued to draw press attention today. Taiwan’s Defense Ministry maintains it expects the deal “to proceed as planned.”

China’s own readout, via Xinhua, said Xi pressed Trump to “adhere to the correct position of opposing Taiwan independence” — language stronger than the standard US position of not supporting independence outright. No new statement from China’s foreign ministry was found today. One confirmed, concrete outcome from Thursday still stands: the US-China trade truce, previously set to expire November 10, was extended through January 10, 2027.

Why it matters The first Chinese state visit to Washington in over a decade produced ceremony and a trade-truce extension, but left its one flagged flashpoint going in — the Taiwan arms package — exactly where it started.
What’s next Watch for formal notification to Congress of any Taiwan sale decision, or further comment, as the “fairly short time” window plays out.

Costco extends Thursday’s earnings beat into a Friday rally

Shares gained nearly 3% a day after the fiscal fourth-quarter beat, even as a separate cost headwind — a $152 million charge tied to memory-chip inflation — surfaced in Friday coverage.

COST closed at $922.77, up 2.93%, recovering from a softer premarket in which shares had dipped roughly 0.5–0.9% as investors digested Thursday’s report. Coincided with The rally tracks the earnings beat but is not asserted as its sole cause — several analysts cut price targets the same day the stock rose, a genuine reversal from premarket weakness that has no single confirmed catalyst in available reporting.

New in Friday’s coverage: a roughly $152 million charge to Costco’s electronics business, tied to rising memory-chip prices, was flagged by analysts as an additional headwind embedded in the print — on top of the previously reported $0.15-per-share, non-recurring IEEPA tariff-refund benefit this outlet covered yesterday. Bernstein maintained its Outperform rating but trimmed its price target to $1,143 from $1,144; Mizuho maintained Outperform but cut its target to $1,065 from $1,100. The average analyst price target sits in the roughly $1,073–1,089 range.

Why it matters A second consecutive quarter where real comparable-sales acceleration comes bundled with a one-time, company-flagged benefit — and now a separate memory-price cost headwind — makes the underlying trend genuinely harder to isolate, exactly the risk an Oppenheimer analyst flagged ahead of Thursday’s print.

Costco’s own 8-K (primary, filed Thursday) · Benzinga, TipRanks, TIKR, Yahoo Finance — Confirmed-multiple.

Meta gives back Thursday’s Muse rally as Goldman questions AI-capex payback

Shares fell 3.33% after a Goldman Sachs note estimated hyperscalers need roughly $300 billion a year in AI-services revenue just to break even on this year’s spending — a sector-wide caution note that hit Meta hardest of the group.

META closed at $751.66, down 3.33%, giving back most of Thursday’s 4.5% JPMorgan-upgrade-driven gain. Goldman Sachs analysts estimated AI hyperscalers — Meta among them — are on pace to spend roughly $800 billion in capital investment this year on chips and data centers, accelerating to about $1.1 trillion next year; while those companies have announced revenue backlogs exceeding $1.5 trillion combined, that revenue doesn’t arrive in a single year, and Goldman put the annual AI-services revenue needed just to break even at roughly $300 billion. Coincided with Friday coverage most often cites Goldman’s note as the reason for the reversal; it is a sector-wide caution note, not a Meta-specific downgrade, and Meta fell more than most mega-cap peers.

Unverified Multiple outlets (Techdirt, Digital Trends, Android Headlines) detailed a zero-day flaw in Muse’s authentication-token handling — any locally installed macOS app or terminal command could reportedly redirect Muse’s transcription endpoint and hijack its token, bypassing macOS’s own app-permission model. No fix or patch has been confirmed as of this edition. Amazon’s block of Muse from Amazon.com, reported earlier this week, remains in place with no further comment from either company.

Why it matters The same week one bank validated Muse’s consumer traction, a different one raised the sector-wide question of whether any of this AI spending pays for itself — and Meta, this week’s most visible AI-agent story, absorbed the larger share of that doubt.

Yahoo Finance (Goldman Sachs note) — Confirmed-multiple (price move); Techdirt, Digital Trends, Techtimes — corroborated across outlets but unconfirmed by Meta (security flaw).

Sanders and Casar formally introduce a bill to ban “artificial superintelligence”

The Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act would create a new Cabinet-level Department of Artificial Intelligence, pause development of systems capable of “recursive self-improvement,” and threaten corporate dissolution plus 20 years in prison for violations.

Sens. Sanders and Rep. Casar formally introduced the bill Wednesday, per both offices’ own press releases, and it continued to draw coverage today. It would create a new Cabinet-level Department of Artificial Intelligence; permanently ban developing or deploying “superintelligent AI,” defined as AI that exceeds human cognitive performance across most domains or is capable of disempowering humanity, including by overthrowing the federal government; and temporarily pause advanced AI development pending the new department’s safety rules. Penalties include corporate dissolution alongside up to 20 years’ imprisonment for violations — Sanders compared the penalty structure to that for unlawfully building nuclear weapons.

The bill faces long odds: the House remains in recess until after the midterms, and no floor action is scheduled in either chamber.

Why it matters It is the most sweeping of several competing congressional AI-safety proposals this session, introduced the same week multiple labs are reportedly courting an industry figure to lead a voluntary self-regulator instead of government rules (below).

Sanders.senate.gov, Casar.house.gov (primary) · NBC News — Confirmed-primary.

F5’s federal patch deadline lands with 14,700+ instances still exposed

Today was CISA’s remediation deadline for the actively-exploited BIG-IP zero-day; an internet-wide scan still counts thousands of unpatched, internet-facing instances.

CVE-2026-94127 (CVSS 9.8) is a heap-based buffer overflow in BIG-IP Access Policy Manager enabling unauthenticated remote code execution when APM is configured as an OAuth authorization server. F5 disclosed and hotfixed it Tuesday after confirming active exploitation; CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day, with today’s federal civilian remediation deadline.

No data was found on today’s actual federal compliance rate. Shadowserver’s most recent internet-wide scan counted more than 14,700 internet-facing IP addresses carrying a BIG-IP APM fingerprint — a rough exposure measure, not a confirmed-compromise count. No new exploitation or breach disclosure tied to this CVE has surfaced since Tuesday.

Why it matters Zero gap between disclosure and confirmed exploitation on perimeter network infrastructure is the highest-severity pattern this outlet tracks in cybersecurity; today’s deadline measures compliance, not risk — an exposed instance past the deadline is still exposed.

CISA KEV catalog, F5 security advisory (primary) · BleepingComputer, SC Media, SOCPrime — Confirmed-multiple.

Zelensky proposes US-brokered trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi as strikes continue

A third distinct meeting format floated this week, one step below a leader-level summit — while Russia and Ukraine kept trading energy-infrastructure strikes.

Per Zelensky himself, the US has proposed technical-level trilateral talks between Ukraine, the US and Russia, floating Abu Dhabi as a venue — a step below the full Trump-Putin-Zelensky summit Zelensky called for earlier this week, when he floated the G20’s Miami summit as a possible leader-level venue. The Kremlin’s Peskov has previously called a leaders’ meeting “premature” and “a waste of time” absent prior expert-level talks.

An energy-strike moratorium discussed by Macron and Trump, and one Zelensky said Ukraine was ready to accept, is not holding: both sides continued striking energy infrastructure through the week — Ukraine hit Russian oil refineries including one near Samara; Russia hit multiple Ukrainian regions, with casualties reported in Kyiv and Donetsk earlier this week. This outlet could not confirm strike activity specifically for the period since last night; that is noted as not found, not as evidence strikes stopped.

Why it matters A third distinct meeting format floated in a single week, still with no agreement on venue, sequencing, or even whether Putin would attend, while the strikes that make the case for urgency continue nightly.

Zelensky’s remarks (primary, via wire) · Kyiv Post, US News, Axios — Confirmed-multiple.

DRC’s Ebola outbreak diverges sharply by province

Ituri, the original epicenter, is declining; North Kivu, a newer front, is surging — WHO calls the overall fight “far from over.”

The outbreak now totals 7,773 confirmed cases and 3,759 deaths across seven provinces. Ituri — 5,966 cases and 2,737 deaths across 28 of 36 health zones — is declining roughly 26% over the past 21 days, with neighboring Haut-Uélé down about 15% over the same window. North Kivu — 1,438 cases and 872 deaths across 16 of 34 health zones — is up 73% over 21 days.

WHO’s own characterization: “encouraging signs” in Ituri, but the fight is “far from over.”

Why it matters A genuine two-track outbreak — meaningful progress in the original epicenter alongside a much newer, faster-growing front — complicates any single “outbreak improving” or “outbreak worsening” headline.

World Health Organization Disease Outbreak News (primary) · UN News, Al Jazeera — Confirmed-multiple.

Anthropic’s founders seek majority voting control ahead of its IPO; a $11.6 billion Akamai cloud deal surfaces two days late

A governance filing would give Anthropic’s seven co-founders combined 50.1% voting power on most matters, while a Thursday-evening cloud deal with Akamai — one of Anthropic’s largest disclosed compute commitments — went uncovered by this outlet until now.

Single source Per The Information, relayed via TechCrunch, Anthropic is asking shareholders to approve a structure giving its seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei, combined 50.1% voting control on most matters, contingent on at least three founders maintaining a minimum stake. Dual-class-like structures are common among tech IPOs, but this outlet found no second independent outlet confirming the specific terms; treat as reported, not confirmed.

Separately: Akamai announced an $11.6 billion, seven-year cloud-computing agreement with Anthropic Thursday evening, structured to expand to roughly $20 billion, via its own press release and an SEC 8-K, with a 5:30 PM ET investor call. That announcement predates Thursday’s Closing Brief, but this outlet’s research did not surface it in time for either that edition or this morning’s. It is disclosed here, one edition late, rather than folded in as newly broken; see the colophon.

Why it matters Both the voting-control filing and the Akamai commitment land in the run-up to a reported November listing this outlet has tracked closely; the compute deal in particular is a concrete, primary-sourced data point on Anthropic’s spending commitments ahead of that IPO.

Pittsburgh DA: “People will be charged” in Gainey-era corruption probe

Stephen Zappala’s first on-record comments since the warrant naming councilman Khari Mosley was unsealed go further than his prior remarks — though no charges have actually been filed.

In a WESA report published today, Allegheny County DA Stephen Zappala said, “We have charges, yes,” and “People will be charged” — his first public statement since a nine-page warrant affidavit was unsealed alleging Gainey-administration officials steered roughly $2 million in “Stop the Violence” grants and other no-bid contracts to organizations, including SEIU and the Working Families Party, that supported Gainey’s campaigns, and that the city’s no-bid contract rate roughly doubled under his administration. Councilman Khari Mosley, named in the affidavit, has publicly denied any connection, calling the probe “a witch hunt.” No charges have been filed as of this edition.

Why it matters Zappala’s language today is more definite than his earlier “I expect criminal charges to be filed” — a real escalation in tone, even though the case’s formal legal stage (allegation, under investigation) hasn’t moved yet.

WESA — Confirmed-primary (on-record quote); no charges filed — Confirmed-multiple.
