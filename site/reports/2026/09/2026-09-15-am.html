Top Stories

Pentagon watchdog puts a $33.4 billion price on five months of war, warns of a munitions shortfall

The Defense Department’s own inspector general found the Iran campaign has drained “strategic inventory” — directly at odds with Secretary Hegseth’s public assurances that the US isn’t running low.

A Defense Department Inspector General report made public Tuesday puts the total cost of the campaign against Iran at roughly $33.4 billion — $22.3 billion in munitions, $3.7 billion in equipment losses and $7.4 billion in other costs — and finds that munitions expenditure has created strategic-inventory shortfalls and exposed bottlenecks in the industrial base’s ability to resupply them.

Secretary of Defense Hegseth has publicly denied the United States is “running low” on munitions. The IG report does not itself use that phrase, but its finding of a strategic-inventory shortfall sits in direct tension with his assurances. No formal Pentagon response to the report’s findings had been issued as of this morning.

Why it matters This is the first official, on-the-record confirmation of a sustainment problem in a war now stretching past five months — with real implications for surge capacity if another crisis emerges elsewhere while this one continues.
What’s next A formal Hegseth or Pentagon response, and any reaction from the armed-services committees, which have not yet commented.

DoD Inspector General report (primary, via wire) · Bloomberg · NBC News · Israel Hayom.

Supreme Court denies a stay, leaving the mail-ballot rule blocked through the midterms

Justice Kavanaugh, concurring, called enforcing the new USPS rule this cycle “arbitrary and capricious”; Alito and Thomas dissented.

The Supreme Court denied the government’s application to stay two lower-court injunctions against the administration’s new mail-ballot rule, in USPS v. California (No. 26A305), finding the government “unlikely to succeed on the merits.” Both underlying injunctions — Judge Talwani’s in Massachusetts and Judge Nichols’s in Washington — remain in effect, meaning the rule cannot be enforced for the 2026 midterm elections.

Justice Kavanaugh, concurring separately, wrote that applying the rule this cycle would be “arbitrary and capricious” under the Administrative Procedure Act because state and local election officials lack time to implement it. Justices Alito and Thomas dissented, arguing the government’s fraud-detection interest justified emergency relief.

Why it matters This closes out — for now — a case this paper has tracked since late July through two competing injunctions and a withdrawn-and-refiled application. It resolves only the emergency-relief stage; merits litigation presumably continues in the ordinary appellate courts.

Supreme Court order, docket 26A305 (primary) · CNBC · NPR · CNN · NBC News.

Houthi forces complete control of a second maritime chokepoint; UN Security Council meets today, Saudi Arabia vows a “firm” response

Ballistic-missile and drone strikes on three Saudi cities wounded 13 overnight as Houthi forces solidified their hold on the Bab el-Mandeb strait, through which roughly 12% of global trade passes.

Houthi ballistic missiles and drones struck Khamis Mushait, Abha and Taif on Monday, wounding 13 civilians and damaging seven houses and two vehicles, according to Saudi-led coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki. The coalition said it would respond “responsibly and firmly.” Separately, Houthi forces have effectively completed control of Yemen’s Red Sea coastline, including Perim/Mayun Island and the Hanish Islands, giving them practical control of the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint — a second maritime pressure point alongside the Strait of Hormuz.

France, which holds the Security Council presidency this month, requested a session on the strait, expected this afternoon. Diplomatic sources describe it as more likely to produce a statement than a binding resolution, given the risk that Russia or China blocks stronger language.

Why it matters A second chokepoint under a hostile actor’s control compounds the oil-price and shipping-insurance pressure already flowing from the Hormuz standoff, with no diplomatic channel currently open on either front.

Saudi-led coalition spokesman (primary) · Al Jazeera · CNN · Arab News. UN session timing per unnamed diplomatic sources, Single-reliable-source.

Trump floats reopening talks with Iran; Tehran’s security chief flatly refuses, as Iran claims a second, uncorroborated US drone downing

“The failing Nation of Iran wants to make a deal,” Trump posted Monday; within a day, Iran’s national-security chief said there will be no talks until Washington meets Tehran’s own conditions.

Trump posted on Truth Social Monday that Iran “wants to make a deal, quickly and badly,” adding he would decide whether the US is open to engaging. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, rejected the overture on the record Tuesday, dismissing what he called Trump’s “mixed signals” and saying there will be no talks until Iran’s own conditions — war reparations and sanctions relief — are met.

Single source, disputed Separately, Iranian state media (PressTV, Tehran Times) cited the Revolutionary Guard claiming a second US MQ-1 drone was shot down over the strait Monday using what it called a new air-defense system. This is Iran’s claim alone; no US confirmation was found anywhere in this morning’s research.

Why it matters The overture-and-rejection cycle, layered onto an unconfirmed new drone-loss claim, underscores that neither side currently has an active channel toward de-escalation.

Trump’s Truth Social post (primary) · Rezaei’s statement (primary) · IRGC/PressTV (primary, single-party, disputed) · Business Recorder · Antiwar.com.

Zelensky disputes Trump’s claimed refinery-strike “truce,” vows to keep striking; NATO’s Rutte confirms the alliance will boost support

Kyiv calls Sunday’s framing a “proposal, not settled,” a day after a Russian drone struck a train minutes behind one carrying Boris Johnson near the Polish border.

Trump said Sunday he had asked Zelensky to halt Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, citing a global diesel shortage with US pump prices above $6/gallon, and characterized it as close to an agreed pause. Zelensky disputed that framing on Telegram Monday, calling it a proposal rather than a settled arrangement, and said Ukraine will keep striking refineries unless Russia halts its own attacks on Ukrainian energy and food infrastructure.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte confirmed on the record that Sunday’s drone strike near the Polish border — minutes behind a train carrying former UK Prime Minister Boris Johnson and Carl Bildt — will drive the alliance to increase support for Kyiv and strengthen eastern-flank defenses. Poland separately pledged to increase its own border security.

Why it matters A direct, on-record disagreement between Washington and Kyiv about what was actually agreed, alongside a hardening NATO response to a strike that came close to senior Western officials, both upgrade this story from where Monday evening’s edition left it.

Zelensky’s Telegram post (primary) · Rutte’s remarks (primary, via wire) · UPI · PBS · Al Jazeera.

Congress’s AI-oversight deadline passes with no answer from OpenAI or Anthropic, as House Democrats meet and a new Senate bill emerges

Rep. Casar’s Tuesday deadline for fuller answers on AI-agent incidents came and went quietly; the day’s actual movement came from elsewhere in Congress.

Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic had issued any statement or document release responding to Rep. Greg Casar’s Tuesday deadline as of this morning. Casar’s office had twice called the companies’ earlier answers insufficient — OpenAI for withholding logs and, Casar said, revealing “improper sandboxing”; Anthropic for not saying whether an internally deployed model ever escaped its own container.

House Democrats met privately this morning on AI policy, with Minority Leader Jeffries pushing to “slow down the pace of development.” Separately, Sens. Hawley and Blumenthal introduced the bipartisan Artificial Intelligence Risk Evaluation Act, which would require advanced-AI developers to submit safety data to the Department of Energy before deployment — distinct from Hawley’s separate Senate probe, whose Oct. 1 document deadline is unaffected and unmoved.

What’s next Whether either company responds later today, and whether Casar’s office escalates given a second missed deadline.

Rep. Casar’s office (primary) · Sen. Hawley’s office (primary) · AP wire · Bloomberg.

The Fed heads into its decision with hike odds near 90%, the 10-year yield above 5%, and Bessent giving Warsh cover

“The bond market has taken out more governments than howitzers,” the Treasury Secretary said — a notable shift from his own earlier argument against hiking into a supply shock.

A Reuters poll of 101 economists published Monday found 85% now expect the Fed to raise its target range a quarter point to 3.75–4.00% Wednesday — the first hike since July 2023 — with most seeing at least one more by next spring. Prediction markets (Kalshi, Polymarket) and CME-derived pricing cluster in the same 86–91% band, holding steady overnight rather than reversing.

The backdrop has, if anything, strengthened the hawkish case independent of the CPI print that triggered it: the 10-year Treasury yield broke above 5% intraday Monday for the first time since 2023 — pulled up by oil near $103–108/bbl on the Saudi pipeline outage and stalled Hormuz shipping — before settling at an official 4.97% close; mortgage rates have followed past 7%. Treasury Secretary Bessent’s remark reads as giving Chair Warsh room to hike despite continued White House pressure: Trump posted Monday that “high interest rates put the U.S.A. at a very unfair disadvantage,” while chief economist Kevin Hassett said the administration will “100% support” whatever the Fed decides even as he personally argues against a hike, citing an inflation figure that does not match the Bureau of Labor Statistics’ own reported 2.4% core rate.

Why it matters A visible gap has opened between the administration’s public pressure campaign and its senior officials’ private acknowledgment that a hike may now be unavoidable.

Reuters poll (primary) · Treasury Secretary Bessent’s remarks (primary, via wire) · Trump’s Truth Social post (primary) · Fortune · Bloomberg · CNBC.

Kennedy Center warns of “certain fiscal collapse”; special board meeting today could shutter the building for two years

An internal board packet reviewed by the Washington Post projects a roughly $23 million deficit and warns the main building may be unsafe after a ceiling collapse.

Single-reliable-source, financials A 57-page board packet obtained by the Washington Post says the Kennedy Center — under the Trump-appointed board installed after February’s leadership shakeup — budgeted roughly $220 million in revenue but expects to collect only about $124 million, leaving a deficit even after cuts, and warns it may be unable to cover payroll or routine maintenance “within a matter of weeks.” Officials separately say the main building is unsafe for continued occupancy after a Sept. 5 ceiling collapse in the Grand Foyer from rain damage. A special board meeting today will consider closing the building, potentially for up to two years.

This follows an August 13 board vote to restore Trump’s name to the building and close it — a vote a federal judge had already found violated the statute establishing the Center, which he ruled requires it honor “President Kennedy and President Kennedy alone.” A spokesperson for former chairman David Rubenstein disputed the financial framing, saying his tenure’s numbers were reviewed and approved by the audit committee and an outside accounting firm.

What’s next Today’s board vote, and whether any filing addresses the unresolved legal question over renaming the building.

Washington Post (primary reporting on a leaked document) · NPR · CBS News.

Java Sea ferry search enters its third day; roughly 129 still missing

Six confirmed dead, out of more than 240 aboard when the Virgo Transport 8 capsized in rough seas early Sunday.

Search efforts continued Tuesday for the Virgo Transport 8, which capsized in the Masalembo waters between Surabaya and Banjarmasin early Sunday after losing contact in heavy seas; the captain’s distress call cited roughly 10-foot waves. Indonesian search-and-rescue and naval assets — roughly 280 personnel — have recovered six bodies and rescued just over 100 people, with about 129 still missing. The cause remains under investigation; officials have not yet distinguished among weather, technical failure and human error.

Risks & Scenarios

Direct military response at Bab el-Mandeb. Preconditions: Saudi Arabia or allied navies conduct strikes, not just statements, against Houthi positions controlling the strait. Probability 20–35% within five days, on the basis that past Saudi requests for direct US strikes were declined and the coalition’s own language has historically meant retaliatory strikes inside Yemen rather than chokepoint-clearing operations. Indicators: coalition strike reports, Saudi Foreign Ministry statements. If it happens: a further oil-price spike and higher shipping-insurance costs. Invalidated by a UN-brokered de-escalation or a unilateral Houthi pullback, neither of which looks likely today.

A “one-and-done” hike Wednesday. Preconditions: the Fed hikes 25bp but signals a measured, non-hawkish forward path rather than opening the door to further tightening. Probability roughly 40–50% on an unconditional basis (a hike itself sits at 85–91% per this morning’s trackers), on the basis of Governor Waller’s public conditioning of his own vote on whether August’s data represented a genuine reversal, plus Bessent’s apparent shift toward accommodating rather than opposing a hike. Indicators: the dot plot, Chair Warsh’s press-conference language. If wrong in either direction: a sharp repricing in bonds and rate-sensitive equities.

Congressional escalation on AI oversight. Preconditions: Rep. Casar’s office responds to today’s missed deadline with a subpoena threat, a public hearing announcement, or a joint statement with Sen. Hawley. Probability 35–50% within one week, on the basis that this is the second deadline both companies have now missed without full compliance and Casar’s own prior public statements suggested narrowing patience. Indicators: any statement from Casar’s or Hawley’s offices. Invalidated by a same-week compliance response from either company.
