Today’s News

Bahrain refuses to attend Monday’s Iran-GCC talks in Oman; Iran itself says not to expect a deal

Manama says it won’t sit with Tehran until relations severed in 2016 are restored, narrowing Monday’s roster just as a senior Iranian official downplays any signed agreement on the strait.

Bahrain’s foreign ministry said Saturday it will not attend Monday’s Iran-Gulf Cooperation Council talks in Salalah, Oman, on the future of the Strait of Hormuz, saying regional security “cannot be preserved through a policy of appeasement” and that it will not deal with Tehran until diplomatic relations broken in 2016 are restored. The refusal was reported independently by Al Jazeera, Dawn and regional outlets, narrowing Monday’s roster to Iran, Iraq and the remaining Gulf states. Separately, a senior Iranian official told reporters Saturday that the session — called at Oman’s initiative — will be a discussion forum rather than a signing event: Iran still wants transit fees from ships passing through the strait, a position Oman rejects. The talks are the first direct Iran-Gulf dialogue since the war began, but arrive against a considerably harder backdrop than when Iran agreed to them Friday: Houthi forces hold every position commanding the strait’s Yemeni side, and Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline remains shut.

Why it matters The one diplomatic opening in a war that has run for weeks without one is missing a Gulf state and has been explicitly downgraded by one of its own participants before it even starts.
What’s next Monday’s session itself, and whether Iran’s low expectations turn out to be an accurate forecast or simply a negotiating posture.

Al Jazeera · Dawn · Al-Monitor · Jerusalem Post · Yahoo/AP wire.

A disputed strike hits an Iranian vessel near Qeshm Island; Iran blames the US, Trump says Iran was “probably” responsible

One person was killed and several wounded early Sunday. The attack itself is well corroborated; who carried it out is not.

An Iranian commercial vessel was struck near Qeshm Island, inside the Strait of Hormuz, in the early hours of Sunday local time, killing one person and wounding three to four others. Iranian state media and the Qeshm governor blamed “the US terrorist enemy”; the incident itself was independently reported by CNN and Gulf and wire-fed outlets, so the event is confirmed. Responsibility is not: the US military has issued no statement, and President Trump, traveling in Ireland, told reporters “Iran was probably to blame” — a remark reading as skepticism of Iran’s own account rather than a claim of US involvement. Disputed attribution No independent forensic account exists on either side as of this morning.

Why it matters Whichever account holds, a lethal strike inside the strait itself — not on a tanker in open water, this war’s more familiar pattern — is a new category of incident, arriving the day before the one scheduled diplomatic session meant to address exactly this stretch of water.

IRIB, Iranian state media (primary, disputed) · CNN · Oman Observer · wire-fed US outlets.

Russia’s heaviest barrage in weeks kills at least eleven across Ukraine; Zelensky demands tougher sanctions

Odesa was hit hardest for the second weekend running. Ukraine’s military says Russia fired 129 drones and a mix of cruise and ballistic missiles in a single night.

Russian strikes killed at least eleven people and injured roughly 96 across Ukraine overnight into Saturday, with Odesa again the hardest-hit city. Ukraine’s General Staff said Russia fired 129 drones (mostly Shahed-type, some jet-powered), eight Kalibr cruise missiles, six Kh-59/69 guided missiles and Iskander-M/KN-23 ballistic missiles in the barrage — a one-sided, primary account this report cannot independently verify, the standard caveat for wartime weapons counts. President Zelensky called for “strong decisions” and tougher US sanctions on Russia, saying “personal decisions from President Trump are also very much desired.” Separately, in an interview broadcast Saturday, Zelensky said he would be willing to meet Putin at December’s G20 summit in Miami; the Kremlin’s Peskov, speaking from the BRICS summit in New Delhi, immediately called that “impossible,” saying any Putin-Zelensky meeting must happen in Moscow. That is a separate, far more speculative track from the previously reported “preliminary agreement” for a Trump-Zelensky meeting in New York later this month, most likely tied to the UN General Assembly’s session opening Sept. 22 — neither meeting is confirmed, and this report is treating both as expectations, not scheduled events.

What’s next Whether either meeting — Trump-Zelensky in New York, or the Putin-Zelensky idea for Miami — advances beyond the discussion stage this week.

Ukraine’s General Staff (primary, one-sided) · Kyiv Independent · The Moscow Times · AFP · Kyiv Post · Euronews.

BRICS closes in New Delhi after leaders adopt a Gulf-war declaration that names no one

Saturday’s marathon overnight session produced 140 paragraphs of “deep concern” and calls for “maximum restraint” — deliberately vague after reported friction between Iran and the UAE over how far to go.

The BRICS summit in New Delhi closed its second day Sunday with a partner-country outreach round and sideline bilateral meetings, a day after its eleven members adopted a joint declaration following marathon overnight negotiations Saturday. The 140-paragraph New Delhi Declaration expresses “deep concern” over Middle East escalation and calls for “maximum restraint” without naming the United States, Israel or Iran — a deliberately vague compromise after reported drafting friction, with Iran’s foreign minister Araghchi pushing to condemn “US-Israeli aggression” and the UAE resisting language that might implicate Gulf states. This report found no further, more specific outcome from Sunday’s closing session beyond Saturday’s declaration and the standing bilateral meetings already reported.

Why it matters Three heads of state with direct stakes in the Gulf war spent two days in the same room; a text calibrated to offend no one is itself a measure of how far apart Iran and the Gulf states remain, even inside a bloc built to present a unified front.

Al Jazeera · The Wire · Outlook India · Tribune India.

The disputed US destroyer-strike claim: still unresolved, four days on

The IRGC’s claim of hitting two US Navy warships has neither been confirmed nor withdrawn since Wednesday; CENTCOM’s denial stands unchanged.

An accountability note rather than a new development: the IRGC’s claim of striking the USS Delbert D. Black and USS John Paul Jones, first reported Wednesday, remains exactly where it stood then. CENTCOM’s denial — that no US Navy warship has been struck and all IRGC attempted attacks failed — is unchanged, and no independent evidence has surfaced on either side through Sunday morning. This report is carrying the claim as unresolved rather than confirmed or dismissed.

CENTCOM statements (primary) · prior wire reporting.

Two days from a congressional deadline, OpenAI and Anthropic have said nothing new

Rep. Casar’s Tuesday deadline for fuller answers on AI-agent incidents stands unchanged; a separate Senate probe’s Oct. 1 document demand is untouched by any weekend movement.

Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic issued any new statement over the weekend ahead of Tuesday’s deadline from Rep. Greg Casar, who called both companies’ earlier responses on AI-agent incidents insufficient and set Sept. 15 for fuller answers. Sen. Josh Hawley’s separate Senate investigation into OpenAI’s July Hugging Face incident also saw no new movement; its Oct. 1 document deadline stands unchanged. This is a gap in available reporting, not a “no comment” — this report found no indication of what either company plans to submit.

Rep. Casar’s office (primary) · Sen. Hawley’s office (primary) · prior reporting.

The Economy Ahead

The Federal Reserve’s Open Market Committee meets Tuesday and Wednesday, Sept. 15–16 — a two-day meeting associated with a fresh Summary of Economic Projections, per the Fed’s own published calendar. The rate decision and projections post at 2:00 PM ET Wednesday; Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference follows roughly 30 minutes later. Recent press accounts (this report could not pull a live CME FedWatch snapshot this morning, so treat the figure as indicative rather than a confirmed real-time reading) put the market-implied probability of a 25-basis-point hike, to 3.75–4.00% from the current 3.50–3.75% range, at roughly 85–90% — up from readings in the 55–66% range in the days immediately after Jackson Hole, as the hot August CPI print and Warsh’s own rhetoric reinforced hawkish pricing. Multiple outlets report the president, vice president, Treasury secretary and other senior officials pressing against a hike, setting up open tension between the administration and a Fed chair it nominated. Economist reaction is split: Citigroup’s Andrew Hollenhorst has called Warsh’s tone “more hawkish than usual but only marginally so,” arguing the data don’t show an urgent need to tighten; other commentators argue a hike now would work against the Fed’s own employment mandate. The standard blackout period (Sept. 5–17) means no Fed speakers before Wednesday’s decision and none confirmed for Thursday or Friday either.

Around the decision, a cluster of real data lands: August retail sales and import/export prices Wednesday morning, the NAHB housing index the same day, then housing starts, building permits and weekly jobless claims Thursday morning, and industrial production, capacity utilization and the Conference Board’s Leading Economic Index Friday. A hot retail-sales print ahead of the 2:00 PM decision would reinforce the case for a hike already priced in; a soft one would be the week’s clearest test of whether the Fed moves on data or on the momentum of its own signaling. The Bank of Japan’s own two-day meeting concludes around the same point in the week, landing either Thursday or Friday Tokyo time by available reporting — this report could not confirm which day carries the decision.

Earnings & Business

No mega-cap tech, AI-infrastructure, defense, space, financial or big-box consumer name on this report’s watchlist reports earnings this week — a structurally quiet week between the Q2 and Q3 reporting seasons. The one notable print is homebuilder Lennar, reporting fiscal third-quarter results after Wednesday’s close with an earnings call Thursday at 11:00 AM ET — useful context on a day already carrying the NAHB index and, the next morning, housing starts.

The Treasury’s auction calendar, confirmed directly against TreasuryDirect’s own upcoming-securities data: Monday brings 13-week ($92B) and 26-week ($79B) bill auctions (both settling Thursday); Tuesday brings a 6-week bill ($75B) and a reopened 19-year, 11-month bond ($13B, settling Friday); Thursday brings a reopened 9-year, 10-month note ($19B, settling Sept. 30) plus routine 4-week and 8-week bills. None of these are new-issue coupon auctions of the kind that draws the market’s closest attention (2-, 5-, 7- or 10-year notes), so auction demand this week is a secondary story to the FOMC decision itself, not a competing one.

Science & Space Ahead

SpaceX is targeting Friday, Sept. 18, for Starship Flight 14, with an 8:15–10:14 AM ET window from Starbase, Texas, and Saturday as a backup date. This would be the first orbital flight of the redesigned Starship V3 vehicle, carrying roughly twenty operational Starlink V3 satellites — a genuine milestone if it flies clean, given the vehicle’s redesign. A handful of other launches (a ULA Vulcan mission, a Rocket Lab Electron flight, a Chinese Long March 12A mission) are listed only as “no earlier than September” by available trackers, with no specific date inside this week confirmed — this report is not asserting any of them for a particular day.

Market Setup

Crypto, the only market that traded this weekend, drifted lower again: Bitcoin was near $76,700 as of this morning (CoinGecko, live), down roughly 0.9% over 24 hours and a further step down from both Friday’s $76,555 close and Saturday morning’s roughly $77,366 reading — a continuing, gentle slide rather than a reversal. Ether and Solana showed similar modest weekend declines. Equities enter the week off Friday’s sharp reversal (the S&P 500 closed at 7,656.98, up 0.86% on the day, after four straight losing sessions); the Treasury curve sits near cycle highs (10-year 4.96%, 2-year 4.63%, both per Friday’s official par curve) already pricing a hike; oil remains elevated (WTI near $100, Brent near $104.6 at Friday’s close) with Gulf risk unresolved heading into Monday’s diminished Salalah talks; and VIX closed Friday at 15.84, well off Thursday’s intraweek high of 18.17. As Saturday’s edition noted, this market has not yet resolved the tension between a hot inflation print, a widening war, and its own rally — Wednesday’s FOMC decision is this week’s clearest test of which reading holds. A hike delivered as priced would be this cycle’s first real test of whether a market that has repeatedly rallied through bad news treats a fully-priced hike as a non-event or a reason to sell; a surprise hold, given how heavily a hike is priced, would likely be the bigger shock.

Risk Register

The FOMC hikes 25bp Wednesday as priced. Probability 65–80%, on the basis of recent press-reported CME FedWatch-style readings near 85–90% (not independently confirmed this morning) and the hot August CPI print. Impact: front-end yields, the dollar and high-multiple equities reprice around the new rate path. Horizon: Wednesday’s 2:00 PM ET decision. Trigger: the statement and vote count. Indicators: any dissents, and the dot plot’s signal on further moves. Affected: SHY/IEF/SGOV, XLK and other growth-heavy sectors, DXY. Mitigant: a dot plot read as “one and done” would soften the market reaction even if the hike itself lands as expected.

The FOMC surprises with a hold. Probability 15–25%, the inverse case. Impact: a sharp relief rally in duration and growth equities, a weaker dollar. Horizon: Wednesday. Trigger: the statement itself. Indicators: none visible yet. Affected: TLT/IEF, QQQ. Mitigant/note: Warsh’s own hawkish rhetoric argues against this outcome, which is why it is priced as the less likely case.

Scenarios

Base (35–45%). The FOMC hikes 25bp as priced with a measured, “one-and-done” tone from Warsh; Monday’s Salalah talks produce no deal but no collapse either; equities trade choppy but directionless, the S&P closing Friday within roughly 2% of last Friday’s 7,656.98. Indicators: dot-plot language, oil holding roughly $95–105. Confirmed by: a muted market reaction to the hike itself. Invalidated by: a surprise hold, or a Gulf incident with casualties.

Bull (15–20%). The FOMC hikes but pairs it with dovish forward guidance read as the cycle’s last hike; retail sales and housing data hold up; the Salalah talks, though thin, produce an agreement to keep talking; oil eases back under $95; equities rally through the week and VIX stays under 15.

Bear (20–30%). The FOMC hikes with hawkish guidance for further tightening; hot retail sales reinforce the inflation narrative; Gulf risk stays elevated with no de-escalation; oil holds above $100; equities give back a meaningful share of Friday’s rally and yields push toward new cycle highs.

Shock (10–15%). A Gulf incident with confirmed US or allied casualties, the Qeshm attribution resolving into a direct US-Iran accusation exchange, or a genuine FOMC surprise (a hold against odds priced near 85–90% for a hike) reprices risk sharply within the week: oil spikes, equities sell off, VIX moves back above 20, and Treasuries see a flight-to-quality bid.

What would change the outlook. A confirmed date for a Trump-Zelensky meeting; a Supreme Court order on the mail-ballot case; independent confirmation or refutation of either the destroyer claim or the Qeshm Island attribution.
