Iran Strikes a Base Hosting US Troops as the Gulf War Widens

Wednesday, September 9, 2026 · 25 min read

The US destroyed ten Iranian tankers in a week and Iran hit a base hosting American forces in Jordan, Brent crude crossed $100 as Gulf states finally spoke up after a Houthi strike on Saudi Aramco, and Russia’s resumed strikes on Kyiv left a disputed Trump-Zelensky meeting as the only thing resembling progress.

Top Stories

Iran strikes a base hosting US troops in Jordan as CENTCOM destroys ten tankers in a week

The IRGC fired on a US Navy warship twice in two days; the US answered by destroying five more Iranian tankers, and Iran hit back at Al Azraq air base — a strike on facilities used by American forces, not just a shipping skirmish.

CENTCOM said Tuesday it destroyed five Iranian Revolutionary Guard-linked crude tankers — the Kaviz, Charminar, Horizon 1 and Riesco in the Gulf of Oman, and the Derya near Kharg Island — after the IRGC fired ballistic missiles at a US Navy warship on two separate occasions over Sept 7–8. That brings the total to ten Iranian tankers destroyed by US forces in the past week alone, under the administration’s declared “tanker for tanker” retaliation policy. Iran responded by striking Al Azraq air base in Jordan, which hosts US forces, with what the IRGC’s own statement called “fierce missile strikes,” claiming to have destroyed hangars used by American fighter jets. Jordan’s Armed Forces said in a primary statement that its air-defense systems intercepted 18 of 20 incoming ballistic missiles; the remaining two landed in remote areas, with no casualties reported.

Disputed The IRGC separately claimed to have struck US Navy destroyers directly; CENTCOM has denied this, and no independent confirmation of any hit on a US vessel has surfaced. A US official described Iran’s Jordan strikes as largely ineffective given the interception rate. Iran has formally declared a Hormuz-area “exclusion zone” — a step beyond the vague threat this outlet reported earlier this week — and says it will file an alternative Iran-Oman shipping corridor with the International Maritime Organization, with maps to follow in the coming days. Iran also claims to have targeted roughly ten ships near the Strait of Hormuz, including two it describes as US-linked; that claim has not been independently verified.

After days of Gulf-state silence on the conflict’s naval exchanges, several governments are now on record. Saudi Arabia publicly called for de-escalation and a return to negotiations. Qatar condemned Iran’s strikes on Jordan. A UAE diplomatic adviser said Gulf states had failed to respond to Iran as a bloc and have “no alternative to engagement” with Tehran — language that reads as a hedge away from full alignment with Washington, though it is one adviser’s characterization, not a formal policy statement. Oman continues to avoid aligning explicitly with either side.

Why it matters This is a materially different order of escalation than the tanker-for-tanker exchanges of the past two weeks: a strike on a base hosting American forces raises the stakes toward a direct US-Iran confrontation on land, not just at sea, and puts the declared exclusion zone in a context where an actual test of it looks more likely, not less.
What’s next Whether the US responds directly to the Jordan strike, whether Iran attempts to enforce its declared exclusion zone against a real tanker transit, and whether Gulf states’ new public statements translate into any diplomatic mechanism.

Go deeper

The past week’s cadence has been genuinely unusual even against this conflict’s own escalatory pattern: three tankers destroyed Sept 5, two Iranian missile attempts on a US warship over Sept 7–8, five more tankers destroyed Sept 8, and now a direct strike on US-linked territory in Jordan within hours. Every prior round of this standoff since late August has drawn measured, proportionate-framed responses from CENTCOM; whether that framing survives a strike specifically targeting a base hosting American forces — as opposed to shipping in international waters — is the open question. Iran’s own domestic framing (Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf’s declaration weeks ago that the era of “proportionate responses” was over) now has a concrete action behind it for the first time.

Brent crude tops $100 as Houthis hit Saudi Aramco and Gulf states finally speak

Two compounding shocks on the same day — a Houthi strike on Saudi oil infrastructure and the Iran-Jordan escalation — pushed Brent above $100 for the first time since July.

Brent crude settled Tuesday at $100.73, up 2.87% and its first close above $100 since July; WTI settled at $95.38, up 2.53%. The move compounds two shocks landing the same day: the Iran-Jordan-Hormuz escalation above, and Monday’s Houthi drone-and-missile attack on Saudi Aramco facilities at Najran, Abha and the roughly 400,000-barrel-per-day Jizan refinery, plus King Khalid Air Base, which wounded 73 people. The Saudi-led coalition called the Houthi attack a “dangerous escalation” and vowed “all necessary measures,” but no confirmed large-scale Saudi retaliatory strike campaign against Houthi positions had followed as of this morning. Separately and unconfirmed, the Houthis accuse Saudi Arabia of killing seven people in a Sept 7 strike on a Yemen prison — a disputed claim this outlet has not independently verified.

Why it matters Oil’s move now reflects two distinct Gulf shocks landing on the same day rather than one escalating thread — a broader-based risk premium than this outlet had been tracking through Monday.

Russia resumes Kyiv strikes; a possible Trump-Zelensky meeting is already disputed

At least five killed as the capitals-only pause lapsed for good — and Kyiv-aligned reports of a tentative Trump-Zelensky meeting are met with a Kremlin-aligned denial that one was ever agreed.

Russia struck Kyiv again overnight into Wednesday, killing at least five and injuring roughly 30, with Ukraine’s air force reporting it downed 142 drones, 31 of 32 cruise missiles and two of an unspecified number of ballistic missiles; damage was reported across Darnytskyi, Holosiivskyi, Podilskyi, Shevchenkivskyi and Solomianskyi districts, including more than a dozen high-rises, a dormitory, a school and a clinic. The strikes followed Monday’s roughly hour-long Trump-Putin call, which the Kremlin described only as “constructive and frank” — no US-side readout has been published as of this writing.

Disputed Kyiv-aligned reporting now points to a tentative Trump-Zelensky meeting in the second half of September, possibly around Sept 20 and possibly on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly. But Kremlin-aligned sources dispute that Trump and Putin ever agreed such a meeting would happen — a genuine, unresolved conflict between the two sides’ accounts, not a confirmed date. This outlet’s Monday forecast that the Trump-Zelensky-Putin call sequence would produce a publicly announced concrete step within 48 hours did not hold up: no extended pause, no summit date either side has confirmed, and no new written proposal has surfaced. Missouri’s redistricting fight gets murkier, not clearer, after SCOTUS denies a stay

The Supreme Court turned down Missouri’s emergency stay request, but a federal judge’s separate order the same day has left it unclear which map actually governs the state’s November election.

The Supreme Court denied Missouri’s emergency application to stay the Missouri Supreme Court’s ruling, which had ordered the state to use its old 2022 congressional map rather than the newly drawn one for November. That denial leaves the state court’s injunction in place. But hours later, a federal judge separately and briefly blocked use of the old map too — producing what multiple outlets are now calling “competing court orders.” Which map actually governs Missouri’s election is not clear as of this morning; this outlet could not independently confirm the operative map in time for this edition and will follow up as soon as it is resolved. Missouri had asked the Court for a merits ruling by roughly Sept 13–14, which remains the next scheduled forcing point if this confusion isn’t resolved sooner.

Mail-ballot Supreme Court emergency: states’ response due at 4 PM today

Four amicus briefs landed Tuesday in the case this outlet corrected twice this week; no ruling is possible before this afternoon’s deadline at the earliest.

Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson’s 4:00 PM EDT deadline for California-led states and voting-rights groups to respond to the government’s stay application — USPS v. California, No. 26A305, targeting Judge Talwani’s Sept 4 preliminary injunction against the USPS mail-ballot rule — has not yet passed as this edition goes to press. Four amicus briefs were filed Tuesday: a bipartisan group of current and former members of Congress, the Society for The Rule of Law, Whistleblower Aid, and election-law professors Foley, Hasen, Karlan and Laycock. No respondents’ brief or Court order has appeared on the docket yet. Separately, this outlet’s research this week found no evidence of a second, distinct “25-state mail-ballot lawsuit” apart from this litigation thread — it appears to be the same case this outlet has tracked under two different labels across editions, a bookkeeping issue being cleaned up in the forecast ledger rather than a substantive error.

Twelve countries announce import bans on Israeli West Bank settlement goods

The UK, France, Canada and nine others cite settler violence and settlement expansion; Israel says it will close the British consulate in East Jerusalem in response.

The UK, France, Canada, Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Ireland, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Spain and Sweden announced Tuesday they will ban imports of goods produced in Israeli West Bank settlements, citing roughly 80 settler attacks between Aug 11 and Aug 24 and continued settlement expansion. It is a coordinated, twelve-country economic step — larger than the individual-country statements this outlet has previously logged on the settlements question. Israel “strongly condemned” the move; Israeli press reporting says the government has announced retaliatory steps including closing the British consulate in East Jerusalem, though this outlet has not yet seen an official Israeli government statement confirming the consulate closure independently of press reporting.

Israel-Lebanon toll rises again as two irreconcilable running counts diverge further

Lebanon’s Health Ministry reports at least 13 killed in Sept 7–8 strikes; separately, two cumulative tallies of the broader conflict since March now differ by roughly a factor of two.

Disputed Lebanon’s Health Ministry reported at least 13 killed, including women and children, in Israeli strikes across Sept 7–8 — one of the larger single-day tolls this outlet has logged in recent weeks. Israel says it struck in response to Hezbollah drone launches at its forces and continues accusing Hezbollah of ceasefire violations dating to the June truce. Separately, this outlet found two irreconcilable cumulative tolls circulating for the broader conflict since Hezbollah rejoined fighting in March: one figure near 4,300 dead, another near 8,900, reflecting different counting windows or methodologies that no outlet has reconciled. This outlet is not adopting either figure and flags the gap as widening, not narrowing, since first noted.

Congress rejects OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s AI-security responses, sets a new deadline

Both companies answered before this week’s original deadline; Rep. Casar called the answers insufficient and gave them until September 15 — specifically over Anthropic’s unanswered question about container escapes.

OpenAI and Anthropic both responded to House Democrats’ Aug 24 AI-security deadline by roughly Sept 2 — OpenAI telling Congress it is building “automated shutdown” capability, without sharing internal incident logs. Rep. Greg Casar publicly rejected both responses as insufficient, saying Anthropic’s reply in particular did not address his specific question of how many times, in the past year, an internally deployed Anthropic model has taken action outside its authorized container. Casar set a new deadline of September 15 for both companies. No further on-record response from either company specifically addressing the container-escape question has surfaced since. This outlet’s own forecast that either company would issue any public response within two weeks graded correct on the letter, but the immediate re-escalation to a tighter follow-up deadline is the more important fact for readers than the technical pass.

Microsoft patches a record ~970 flaws as two zero-days land on CISA’s exploited list

September’s Patch Tuesday is the largest on record; both actively exploited zero-days also appear on Tuesday’s separate Magento-linked KEV batch.

Microsoft’s September Patch Tuesday fixed roughly 966–974 vulnerabilities — a record volume — including two actively exploited zero-days: CVE-2026-81963 (a Windows Update Stack link-following flaw) and CVE-2026-85880 (a Windows ALPC heap overflow). CISA added both to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Sept 8, in the same four-CVE batch as CVE-2026-75650 (“StyleSmuggler,” the perfect-10 Magento/Adobe Commerce flaw this outlet covered Monday) and CVE-2026-86218 (an N-able N-central static code injection flaw). No authoritative update to StyleSmuggler’s confirmed victim count has emerged beyond the handful of compromised stores security firm Disrex Group has documented; a widely circulated “100,000-plus stores” figure remains unconfirmed by any primary or established security-research source and this outlet is not reporting it as fact.
