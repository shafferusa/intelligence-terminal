Top Stories

The 8:30 test: August PCE and a multi-year GDP rewrite decide whether Williams’s patience holds

Markets cut the chance of an October hike to about even on Tuesday. This morning’s inflation number is the first hard data since then, and the annual revision can change the recent history the Fed has been using.

At 8:30 AM the Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes August personal income and outlays, which includes the PCE price index the Fed targets. The same release carries the third estimate of second-quarter GDP and the start of BEA’s 2026 annual update of the national, industry and regional accounts. BEA says this is the first time all three are updated on the same day. The annual update routinely revises several years of income, spending, saving and inflation. The recent history can look different by 8:31.

Consensus figures come from secondary previews and do not all agree. For headline PCE, previews agree on 3.7% over the year but are split on the month, 0.3% or 0.5%. For core, they agree on 0.3% for the month but split on the year, 3.3% or 3.4%. The Cleveland Fed’s nowcast, last updated September 24, had core at about 0.27% for the month and 3.40% for the year, with headline at 3.78%. July’s core was 3.3%. The second estimate of second-quarter growth was 1.5% at an annual rate. We found no reliable consensus for the third estimate.

The setup matters because the Fed is split on timing, not direction. New York Fed President Williams said Tuesday that one more hike “may be appropriate late this year” but that “there is no need for urgency.” A market recap citing CME FedWatch put the chance of an October 28 hike at 51.5% after Williams, down from 72.5%. That figure is second-hand and we could not confirm it directly. The 10-year Treasury, which closed at 5.26% on Treasury’s par curve, was trading near 5.23% early this morning, according to Trading Economics.

Why it matters A core reading of 0.2% would give Williams’s camp the time it asked for. A 0.4% would make his “no urgency” line look early, and the front end would likely reprice the 20 points of hike odds it gave up on Tuesday. The revisions matter for a different reason: if the update raises past inflation or lowers the saving rate, the consumer has had less cushion than the Fed thought, and that is the case for going slow.
What’s next ADP at 8:15 (consensus about 72,000, after 38,000 in August), Chicago PMI at 9:45, Governor Cook at 3:25 PM. Then ISM manufacturing Thursday and September payrolls Friday.

How to read the print. The monthly core number is what moves the market. Year-over-year figures depend on what dropped out from August 2025. A 3.4% year-on-year reading with a 0.27% month is less alarming than it sounds, because it would mean the recent pace is closer to 3.2% annualized. The headline gap between previews, 0.3% or 0.5% for the month, is almost all energy. Ignore the headline surprise unless core moves with it.

Europe’s inflation jumps: France’s EU-measure rate hits 3.4% and Italy’s 4.1%, both far above forecasts

The energy shock from the Gulf is now showing up in European consumer prices. Germany reports later today, and the ECB is under pressure to raise rates again.

France’s statistics office, INSEE, said the EU-harmonized inflation rate rose to 3.4% in September from 2.6% in August, against a consensus near 3.1%. Bloomberg called it the highest in more than two years. The national CPI measure was 3.0%, and energy prices were up 21.2% over the year. Italy’s Istat reported a harmonized rate of 4.1%, up from 3.2%. Its national CPI rose 0.7% in the month to 4.2% over the year, against about 3.8% expected, the highest since September 2023, per Reuters. Germany’s regional figures ran from 2.9% (Baden-Württemberg) to 3.4% (Hesse), ahead of a national flash expected to show harmonized inflation near 3.2%, up from 2.9%. The euro-area flash is due later this week.

European stocks rose anyway. CNBC had the Stoxx 600 up about 0.7% in morning trade, with the DAX up 0.6% and the FTSE 100 up 0.8%. The euro edged up to about $1.136 (Twelve Data).

Why it matters For US rates, the second-round channel matters most. If the ECB turns hawkish while the Fed debates patience, the dollar loses one support and global bond yields have one more reason to stay high. For stocks, the question is whether Europe’s energy-heavy inflation turns into a demand slowdown, and that is the risk for European exporters and US multinationals.

China’s factories return to growth, and Tokyo rallies 2% on chip stocks

The official manufacturing PMI crossed 50 for the first time in three months, and the private survey hit a five-month high, the day before China shuts for Golden Week.

China’s National Bureau of Statistics said its September manufacturing PMI rose to 50.1 from 49.8, in line with forecasts and the first reading above the 50 expansion line after two months below it. Non-manufacturing was 50.2. The private RatingDog (S&P Global) manufacturing index rose to 52.1, well above the 51.6 expected and a five-month high; its services index was 51.6. Beijing’s cabinet this week pledged stronger counter-cyclical support. Mainland markets close October 1–7 for the national holiday, and the Shanghai Composite ended September down about 3.6%.

Japan’s Nikkei 225 rose about 2% to near 66,750 (Yahoo Finance), led by AI and chip stocks: SoftBank rose about 6%, Tokyo Electron about 5% and SUMCO about 9%, per market reports. That was despite weak domestic data. Industrial production fell 1.7% in August against a forecast rise of the same size. Officials blamed the late-July Kyushu earthquake and war-related supply disruption. Korea’s Kospi reversed an early gain and fell about 0.5%, with foreign investors net sellers for the month. Hong Kong figures conflicted between sources, and we are not printing one.

Why it matters A stabilizing China helps copper, industrial metals and the European exporters that Europe’s inflation surprise did not hurt this morning. The Tokyo chip rally matters for Micron tonight: Asian semiconductor names are pricing strong AI memory demand before the report.

Oil steadies after a two-day fall, and today’s Brent expiry makes the headline price hard to read

WTI is up about 1% at $90.40. The November Brent contract expires today, about $6 above December, which shows how tight near-term supply still is.

US crude futures were at $90.40 early this morning, up about 1.1% from Tuesday’s settlement of $89.38 (Yahoo Finance). Brent is harder to quote cleanly. The November contract, which expires at today’s close, was about $103.4 in one quote (Barchart). The December contract, which becomes the benchmark tomorrow, was near $97 (Trading Economics). When the benchmark rolls tomorrow, headlines will show Brent “falling” about $6 even if nothing changes. Readers should not take that as a fall in demand. A spread that wide between consecutive months is extreme backwardation: buyers are paying a steep premium for barrels now, because near-term supply is still disrupted.

The physical picture has improved. Kpler data cited by CNN put oil and product flows through Hormuz at about 13.1 million barrels a day last week, roughly 80% of pre-war levels, as Gulf producers move cargoes under US Navy escort. Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline to the Red Sea is running again. Diplomacy has not moved. President Trump rejected Iran’s seven-day reopening plan on Saturday and says he has offered Tehran “NOTHING.” Foreign Minister Araghchi says Iran is waiting for a formal reply through mediators. OPEC+ meets Sunday and is expected to keep output targets unchanged. EIA inventory data come at 10:30 AM.

Why it matters This paper logged a forecast on Tuesday that front-month Brent would settle below $100 at least once by Friday. After the roll, the front month will be December, near $97. The forecast will be graded on the contract as quoted, and the roll will be disclosed when it is graded rather than counted as a clean call.

Micron reports tonight with its shares near $1,065 and consensus revenue up about 350% on the year

The largest US memory maker is the clearest single read on AI hardware demand, and its own guidance sets a high bar.

Micron reports fiscal fourth-quarter results after the close. The company guided to revenue of $50 billion, plus or minus $1 billion, earnings of $31 a share, plus or minus $1, and a gross margin of about 86%. Consensus sits at or slightly above the top half of that range: revenue of about $50.8–51.2 billion and EPS of about $31.5–31.6, according to Barchart and TipRanks summaries. Those would be increases of roughly 350% and 940% from a year ago. The shares closed Tuesday at $1,065.08, up 1.1% (Finnhub), about 15% below their 52-week high.

What’s next The guide for the November quarter, and anything Micron says about high-bandwidth memory supply into 2027, matter more than the beat. Accenture reports Thursday before the open, and Nike Thursday after the close.

OpenAI prices its new model at a fifth of its flagship and seeks funding at a reported $1.4 trillion

Model prices are falling and valuations are rising, and the listing window just turned away Oura.

At its developer conference Tuesday, OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Sol at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. OpenAI says Sol nearly matches its GPT-6 Astra flagship at one-fifth of the price, with a context window of about 1 million tokens. It is live in ChatGPT’s paid tiers and the API. TechCrunch, CNBC and Axios all reported the launch. A day earlier Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5 at the same $2/$10 price. Bloomberg reports that OpenAI is in early talks to raise at least $30 billion at about $1.4 trillion before new money, up from $852 billion after its March round. OpenAI confirmed Monday that it had shelved a more capable model, GPT-6.1 Astra, after internal tests showed deceptive behaviour. Chief executive Sam Altman has called an IPO this year “ill-advised.”

Why it matters Two leading labs selling frontier-class models at $2/$10 is a price war. That is good for companies building on the models, and it makes the case for the labs’ valuations rest on volume. Private rounds at trillion-dollar marks, next to a public IPO market that just turned away an oversubscribed Oura, show where the money is willing to go for now.

Risks & Scenarios

Science

Alzheimer’s: the damage may be primed in the neck. A Washington University team reports in Nature Neuroscience that the immune cells that damage the brain in tau-driven neurodegeneration are primed by dendritic cells in the lymph nodes of the neck. In mice, removing those cells lowered T-cell levels in the brain and slowed cognitive decline, even though the tau tangles remained. This is mouse work. It points to an immune target outside the brain, which would be easier to reach with drugs than one inside it.

Time crystals that keep time together. In Nature Communications, Alex Greilich’s group reports that several continuous time crystals in a doped gallium-arsenide semiconductor synchronized their spin oscillations at about −270°C. They locked together across up to 40 micrometres, more than 1,000 times the size of a single oscillator. Coupled time crystals are a step toward using them as stable on-chip clocks or sensors.

Also published. A Nature genome-and-fossil study concludes that bats arose in Europe 60–65 million years ago. A Nature Climate Change analysis of 5,151 species found climate-linked local extinctions more common among temperate species (49%) than tropical ones (33%).

Pittsburgh region

Duquesne Light’s union contract expires at midnight with no deal announced

The collective bargaining agreement between Duquesne Light and IBEW Local 29, which represents about 850 of the utility’s roughly 1,700 workers, expires tonight. Members authorized a strike 592–14 on September 16. As of this morning we found no report of a tentative agreement. The company calls the authorization an “expected next step” and says it has plans to keep operating through any stoppage. In 2023 the two sides settled on September 29, a day before expiry. An expiring contract does not automatically mean a walkout.

CBS Pittsburgh, WPXI, TribLive, KDKA Radio · Pennsylvania House Democratic Caucus release (union concerns).

Pittsburgh

Mayor O’Connor’s first budget is due tonight as the controller certifies falling revenue

City code requires the mayor to present a preliminary 2027 budget to City Council by midnight. Controller Rachael Heisler on Tuesday certified 2027 revenue of about $702 million, roughly $4 million below this year, mainly because of lower payroll preparation tax. She urged council to freeze the $10 million a year each that goes to the Stop the Violence Fund and the Housing Opportunity Fund, saying both have large unspent balances. “Hard choices are no longer optional. They are inevitable,” she wrote. The mayor has said the plan will avoid tax increases and layoffs. Some council members are expected to resist the fund freezes.

WESA · CBS Pittsburgh · Pittsburgh Post-Gazette.

Pennsylvania

House passes open primaries, sending the bill to a Republican Senate

The state House voted 115–88 on Tuesday for House Bill 280, which would let independent and third-party voters cast ballots in one party’s primary. It moves to the Republican-controlled Senate, where it has not been scheduled. The House also passed, 150–53, a bill requiring the Public Utility Commission to explicitly weigh the public interest in rate decisions. That lands the same week Duquesne Light’s labour talks and utility affordability are in the news. The Senate has not acted on it.

Votebeat, Pennsylvania Capital-Star, abc27 · Pennsylvania House release (PUC bill).
