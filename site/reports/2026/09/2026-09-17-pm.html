A Kennedy Center Court Fight Escalates After an Air Force One Photo Read “Demolished”

Judge Cooper declined an emergency hearing but ordered 30 days’ notice before any major change to the building, after Rep. Beatty’s lawyers flagged a photo of Trump viewing a “DEMOLISHED” poster aboard Air Force One.

The Justice Department filed its court-ordered response ahead of today’s 10 a.m. deadline, arguing the Kennedy Center’s closure does not violate Judge Christopher Cooper’s standing order to keep the building open, because it addresses “acute risks to public safety resulting from continued structural deterioration.” Executive Director Matt Floca’s declaration cited soffit panels, electrical infrastructure and damage from a September 4 storm, and said the closure — now in its second day — is expected to last about seven days pending a safety assessment.

Cooper denied Rep. Joyce Beatty’s request for an emergency hearing but ordered the defendants, including Trump in his capacity as board chair, to file a detailed status report on the closure and repairs, supported by a sworn declaration, due by September 23. That leaves the core question in Beatty’s underlying lawsuit — whether the closure itself violates the order requiring the building stay open — deferred rather than resolved today.

Separately, and more pointedly, Cooper ordered the Kennedy Center to give 30 days’ notice before any major physical change to the building, “including but not limited to any ‘demolition’ of the Center’s main building,” saying the new requirement was meant “to avoid any confusion…given more recent events.” Those events: an AFP photograph showing Trump aboard Air Force One late Wednesday looking at a large printout reading “Kennedy Center DEMOLISHED,” which Beatty’s team flagged to the court this morning ahead of the deadline. A Kennedy Center spokesperson told reporters the “demolished” language in earlier court filings described “a hypothetical scenario,” not an actual plan, and said flatly that “nothing is being demolished.” Trump has separately said reconstruction cannot proceed until the D.C. Circuit rules on the board’s plan to rename the center after him, and that if the appeals court rules against the board, “the renovation will not take place” at all — language some outlets read as an implicit demolition threat, which the Kennedy Center disputes.

Why it matters This tests how far a president-chaired board can act under an existing court order while litigation over the center’s name and control continues, and the new 30-day notice requirement is now the only concrete legal check on what happens to the physical building itself.
What’s next The status report and sworn declaration are due September 23. The compliance question at the heart of Beatty’s suit remains open, as does the D.C. Circuit’s ruling on the naming dispute.

The White House and Pentagon Respond to the UN’s War-Crimes Finding, as Trump Claims “Direct” Contact With Iran

The White House called the UN body’s report “unserious nonsense” and said only Iran has committed war crimes; Trump said he’s optimistic the war is “nearing the end,” a claim Iran’s security council rejected hours later.

Tuesday’s UN Human Rights Council fact-finding mission report — which found reasonable grounds the US committed a war crime in the February strike on a Minab school and that Iran’s own crackdown on last year’s protests amounted to crimes against humanity — drew its first government responses today. White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly called the council a body that has “accomplished nothing for human rights while spouting unserious nonsense for decades,” and said “the only party in this conflict that has committed war crimes is the Iranian regime,” citing Iran’s treatment of its own people, alleged killings of Americans abroad and “acts of terrorism in the Strait of Hormuz.” The Pentagon declined to comment on “a report conducted by an outside organization,” saying its own investigation into the Minab strike “remains ongoing.” The State Department did not respond to a request for comment.

Separately today, Trump told reporters “I hope we’re nearing the end of the war,” said Iran “wants to make a deal,” and claimed he had been in “direct” contact with Tehran, without elaborating. Unverified Iran has not confirmed any direct contact. Within hours, Iran’s Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei publicly rejected the framing, telling Iranians not to “get distracted by the US president’s mixed signals” and reiterating that Tehran wants the US to honor the terms of the collapsed June deal before any new talks.

Why it matters This is the first substantive government reaction, on either side, to a UN body naming likely war crimes against the country still prosecuting the war — and it lands the same day the president claims private outreach that the other side denies.

White House and Pentagon statements via wire (primary quotes) · NPR, The Hill, Axios · Iranian SNSC statement via CNBC, Al Jazeera, IranWire.

Wall Street Rallies and the 10-Year Slips Back Below 5% as the Post-Fed Volatility Crush Continues

The S&P rose 1.14%, chipmakers and small-cap space names led a broad advance, and the VIX cratered 12.8% to its lowest close in weeks — a sharp reversal from Wednesday’s hawkish-hike selloff.

The S&P 500 closed at 7,637.76, up 85.95 points or 1.14%; the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.69% to 26,418.30; the Dow gained 0.61% to 51,778.04; the Russell 2000 added 0.55%. Semiconductors (SMH +2.76%) and technology (XLK +2.25%) led sector gains, while financials (XLF −0.09%) and communication services (XLC −0.58%) lagged — a genuine divergence from a clean, across-the-board risk-on read.

The Treasury’s par-yield curve fell 7 basis points at the 10-year to 4.94%, its first close back under 5% since Tuesday’s intraday spike to a post-2007 high; the 3-month bill fell 2 basis points to 4.12%. The curve shifted lower roughly in parallel rather than steepening or flattening: 2s10s +27bp, 3m10y +82bp, 5s30s +51bp, each essentially unchanged in shape from yesterday. The VIX closed at 15.44, down 12.82% — its sharpest one-day drop in weeks — as the specific event risk around Wednesday’s FOMC decision cleared; the VIX9D fell even further to 13.39, and the term structure returned to normal contango after Wednesday’s front-month inversion.

Market narrative, not confirmed catalyst. Financial-press coverage today converges on two linked threads rather than one hard catalyst: oil fell for a third session as Saudi Arabia rerouted more crude through Oman’s Sohar port following last week’s pipeline drone strike, feeding optimism that Gulf-driven inflation risk is easing a day after the Fed’s hawkish hike; separately, weekly jobless claims fell to 196,000 against a 208,000 estimate and single-family housing starts jumped, giving the tape a data-driven reason to extend Wednesday’s late recovery. No single outlet or release ties the full move to one cause, so this report labels it a market narrative rather than a confirmed catalyst.

Why it matters Today’s reversal suggests markets are, for now, treating Wednesday’s hawkish-hike selloff as an overreaction to unwind rather than the start of a durable repricing — though one session does not settle that question.
What’s next The Bank of Japan’s decision lands before Friday’s US open; a second global central-bank surprise so soon after the Fed would be the first real test of whether today’s calm holds.

The Saudi-Houthi War Widens: New Casualties, a New Hormuz Incident, and an Unverified Strike Report

Air defenses and strikes traded across the Yemen-Saudi border killed people on both sides today, a fresh UKMTO security incident was reported off Oman, and a single-sourced account of a drone-and-missile strike on a US-contracted vessel has not been corroborated.

Casualties were reported on both sides in the last day: Saudi civil defense says one Yemeni resident in Saudi Arabia was killed and two injured by debris from an intercepted Houthi drone near Taif; separately, Houthi-aligned media claim Saudi-led coalition strikes killed at least three children in Yemen’s Taiz province. Single-reliable-source Each casualty figure comes from only one side’s own media and is not independently confirmed; this report treats them as claims, not established facts.

AP reports, citing regional officials, that Saudi Arabia is running low on missile interceptors after six months of the Iran war and has asked France, the UK, Pakistan and Egypt to deploy air-defense support — a request Turkish and Pakistani officials told AP they had not received. Disputed That is a direct factual conflict this report cannot resolve. Militarily today, the Saudi-led coalition struck Houthi positions near Mocha and conducted drone strikes on Marib; the Houthis say they hit Khamis Mushait airbase and an oil facility at Yanbu, and claim to have downed a Saudi fighter jet over Marib with a “locally made” missile — a Houthi claim only, with no Saudi confirmation.

Washington has reaffirmed support for Yemen’s internationally recognized government but declined a Saudi request last week for a direct US military role. Separately, UKMTO reported a security incident today 16 nautical miles northeast of Khasab, Oman, in the Strait of Hormuz; the crew was reported safe, with no further detail released. Single-reliable-source/Unverified A separate Fox News report, citing unnamed sources, says a US-contracted vessel was struck earlier this week by four Iranian drones and at least one missile near the Strait, with minor injuries among crew including some described as US personnel. Neither the US government nor Iran has confirmed this, and every other outlet carrying it is repeating Fox’s reporting rather than corroborating it independently — it does not meet this paper’s two-source bar for a high-risk military claim and is flagged accordingly.

A Maximum-Severity Cisco Flaw Is Already Under Active Exploitation, With a Saturday Federal Deadline

CVE-2026-76460, an authentication-bypass bug in Cisco’s Identity Services Engine, lets an unauthenticated attacker gain root — CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the day it was disclosed.

Cisco disclosed CVE-2026-76460 on September 16: a maximum-severity, CVSS 10.0 authentication-bypass flaw in Identity Services Engine and ISE-PIC that can give an attacker unauthenticated, root-level command execution. Active exploitation was already confirmed at the time of disclosure. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day, setting a federal remediation deadline of Saturday, September 19. The disclosure is part of a larger September hardening release — more than 20 CVEs across ISE, Secure Firewall Management Center, Nexus Dashboard and ASA/FTD — separate from the Pixel-modem flaw (CVE-2026-58704) this paper covered yesterday morning; the two do not overlap.

No threat actor has been named in connection with the exploitation; this is reported as exploitation-in-the-wild of a disclosed flaw, not an attributed campaign, and this report makes no attribution claim.

Why it matters A root-level, unauthenticated bypass in identity-management infrastructure is about as severe as a network vulnerability gets, and the gap between disclosure and confirmed exploitation was effectively zero.

Cisco security advisory and CISA KEV catalog (primary) · The Hacker News, SecurityWeek, Infosecurity Magazine, TechRadar.

Rep. Meeks Holds Up a $2.8 Billion Arms Sale to Israel, Citing Gaza and Lebanon Concerns

The House Foreign Affairs Committee’s ranking Democrat is withholding informal clearance for a package reportedly including 40,000 2,000-pound bombs, citing “grave, unresolved concerns” about compliance with US law.

Rep. Gregory Meeks (D-NY) is withholding informal congressional clearance for a proposed $2.8 billion arms package to Israel, reportedly including 40,000 2,000-pound bombs, citing what his office calls “grave, unresolved concerns” about compliance with US law and civilian-protection requirements in Gaza and Lebanon. Meeks frames the hold as a demand for accountability rather than a withdrawal of support for Israel’s defense. Informal committee review of this kind can delay or effectively block a sale even without a floor vote; no response from the State Department or the Israeli government had surfaced as of this writing.

Lennar Cuts Its Full-Year Delivery Guidance as August Housing Data Comes In Mixed

Deliveries fell 3% year over year and full-year guidance was trimmed to 80,000–81,000 homes, a day after permits missed and starts split between a single-family rebound and a broader pullback.

Lennar’s fiscal third-quarter earnings call today confirmed and extended Wednesday’s print: deliveries fell 3% year over year to 20,840 homes, with a backlog of 16,857 homes valued at $6.3 billion. The company cut full-year delivery guidance to 80,000–81,000 homes, down from a prior 82,000–83,000. Shares fell roughly 2.1% in reaction, trading near 52-week lows; the Street’s average analyst price target still implies meaningful upside from Wednesday’s close, a matter of analyst opinion, not fact.

Separately, August building permits fell 2.7% month over month to a 1.394 million annual rate, below the 1.41 million estimate and led by a 15.8% Northeast decline. Housing starts fell 2.6% overall as multifamily construction dropped, but single-family starts rose 7.6% to 918,000, up 5.2% year over year, even as completions fell 11.9% — a resilient single-family read against a weaker permitting pipeline.
