Top Stories

Trump and Xi’s summit ends without a joint statement; the Taiwan arms question is still open

The two governments extended their trade truce to January and opened an AI-safety channel, but $14 billion in pending Taiwan arms sales — and what either side actually agreed on it — remains unresolved.

Xi departed the White House around 1:30 PM Thursday after the bilateral meeting, Rose Garden ceremony and state dinner closed out his three-day visit, his first to Washington in more than a decade. Neither government issued a joint statement; each published its own separate readout. China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs described the visit as leaving “greater scope for cooperation,” singling out artificial intelligence — consistent with the AI-safety communications channel the two sides opened overnight Wednesday alongside an extension of their trade truce to January 10, 2027, from a previous November 10 expiration.

The $14 billion Taiwan arms package China wants shelved was, by administration officials’ own account, the dominant subject of the private conversation. A senior official told reporters Thursday that Trump would decide “in a fairly short time” — no decision has been announced as of this edition. Taiwan’s Defense Ministry said Friday it still expects the package to “proceed as planned.”

Why it matters Congress split along predictable lines reacting to the same meeting: Rep. Gregory Meeks (D) said Xi “has leverage over the president” but not over Congress; Rep. Michael McCaul (R) called Xi “very aggressive” and said Taiwan dominated the conversation; Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick (R) compared the stakes to Ukraine. None of that reaction changes what the administration actually decides.
What’s next The Taiwan arms decision itself, on no fixed timeline, and any detail on how the new AI-safety channel gets used.

Treasury yields extend their climb to fresh multi-decade highs as two auctions tail in one week

The 10-year closed at 5.18%, the 30-year at 5.47% — both up again Thursday — while the two long-duration Treasury funds on the board hit new 52-week lows.

The Treasury’s official par curve for Thursday shows a broad-based move higher across every tenor from one month (+2bp) to 30 years (+7bp): the 10-year closed at 5.18%, the 30-year at 5.47%, the 2s10s spread widening to +31 basis points. It’s the fourth straight session of higher long yields, a run that began Tuesday with a hot flash PMI, Fed Governor Michael Barr’s hawkish remarks and a weak five-year auction, then continued Thursday with a soft seven-year auction — a 5.085% high yield, the highest for that tenor since April 1993, with a bid-to-cover of 2.42 and indirect bidders taking a reduced 57.2% of the award.

On the board, the effect is now concrete rather than abstract: TLT (20+ Year Treasury) and IEF (7-10 Year Treasury) both closed Thursday at new 52-week lows, and the investment-grade and high-yield credit funds, LQD and HYG, both closed within a few cents of theirs. Two Fed officials reinforced the hawkish read this week — Barr said further hikes are “likely to be needed”; NY Fed President John Williams, speaking Thursday in London, called another hike “reasonable” by year-end without committing to a month. Odds trackers for an October hike have not converged on a single figure this outlet is prepared to print as consensus; treat any specific percentage reported elsewhere with the same caution this outlet has applied all week.

Why it matters This is the fourth consecutive session of higher long yields, and it is now visible in fund-level price action — new 52-week lows — not just in headline Treasury levels.

Costco’s tariff-refund benefit isn’t a one-quarter story — the company says it will recur

Thursday’s earnings beat again leaned on a one-time refund; Costco now says it will disclose the net effect every quarter through fiscal 2027.

Costco’s fiscal Q4 numbers, filed via 8-K after Thursday’s close: adjusted EPS $6.75 against roughly $6.48–$6.67 in vendor estimates, net sales $93.9 billion (+11.2%), comparable sales +9.4% (digitally-enabled sales +19.5%), total revenue including membership fees $95.7 billion. The $0.15-per-share benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds, first flagged as a one-time item last quarter, is not behind the company: Costco’s own materials say the dynamic will keep affecting quarterly comparisons into fiscal 2027, and it has committed to disclosing the net impact every quarter going forward — an unusual level of standing disclosure for what started out described as one-time.

Analyst reaction was incremental rather than enthusiastic: Truist held at Hold but raised its price target to $909 from $873; BMO reiterated Outperform, lifting its target to $980 from $950; DA Davidson stayed at Neutral, raising to $880 from $780 — all modest upward revisions consistent with a confirmation-not-surprise quarter. This outlet could not obtain a reliable Friday premarket quote for Costco at gather time and is not printing one; see Before the Open.

Why it matters A company voluntarily committing to disclose a one-time benefit’s lingering effect, every quarter, is Costco itself signaling that its headline growth rate is currently running hotter than its underlying business — worth watching whether analysts start backing it out of their own models.

Meta’s Muse rally keeps running as the Amazon standoff goes unresolved

Shares are up more than 20% in two weeks and Muse is reported atop Apple’s App Store, but Amazon still won’t let the AI agent onto its own site.

Amazon continues to block Meta’s Muse AI agent from Amazon.com over unauthorized access under its Conditions of Service; Meta has separately unveiled “Muse Charm,” a roughly two-inch, AirPods-case-sized hardware device with its own 5G connection for voice-first interaction with the agent, shipping for the holidays in December. Neither company has said anything further about resolving the access dispute. Meta shares are now up more than 20% over the two weeks since Muse’s introduction, closing Thursday at $777.59 (+4.50%), just under the stock’s 52-week high of $779.82.

Today is the federal deadline to patch an actively exploited firewall flaw affecting 14,700+ systems

CISA gave federal agencies until today to fix a critical F5 BIG-IP flaw; a security-research group’s own internet scan counts nearly 15,000 exposed instances worldwide.

CVE-2026-94127 (CVSS 9.8), a heap-based buffer overflow in BIG-IP Access Policy Manager enabling unauthenticated remote code execution when APM is configured as an OAuth authorization server, was disclosed and hotfixed by F5 on September 22; CISA added it to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day, setting today as the federal civilian remediation deadline. Shadowserver’s own internet-wide scan counts more than 14,700 internet-facing IP addresses carrying a BIG-IP APM fingerprint — a rough measure of how much exposed surface may still be unpatched, not a count of actual compromises. No new confirmed exploitation or breach disclosure tied to this specific flaw has surfaced since Tuesday.

CISA KEV catalog, F5 security advisory (primary) · Shadowserver scan data via BleepingComputer, The Register, Help Net Security (secondary).

DRC’s Ebola outbreak splits in two directions: falling at its epicenter, surging in the north

The fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record has passed 7,770 cases, but the trend is no longer uniform — Ituri is improving while North Kivu’s case count is up 73% in three weeks.

WHO’s latest figures, for data through September 22: 7,773 confirmed cases, 3,759 deaths, 839 hospitalized in isolation — up 40 cases and 27 deaths from the prior day’s report. What has genuinely changed since this outlet’s last update is the trajectory itself: Ituri, the epicenter, is down roughly 26% and Haut-Uélé down about 15% over the trailing 21 days, both provinces improving, while North Kivu is up roughly 73% over the same window — the outbreak’s center of gravity may be shifting north even as the epicenter cools.

Russian drones hit Odesa again overnight as Zelensky’s three-way-meeting push goes nowhere

Ukraine says it intercepted 267 of 295 drones launched at it overnight; the Kremlin still calls a Trump-Putin-Zelensky sit-down premature.

Single-sourced casualty count Russian drones struck Odesa overnight into Friday, killing at least one person and injuring two, with fires reported at warehouses; Ukraine’s air force said it intercepted 267 of 295 drones launched nationwide. The casualty figures come from Ukrainian officials only; this outlet has not found independent corroboration and reports them as single-sourced, per its standard for war-casualty claims. This is a separate, smaller barrage from Wednesday night’s larger strike on Kyiv and Odesa (16 killed, 54 injured), already reported.

Diplomatically, nothing has moved: Zelensky continues pushing for a three-way meeting with Trump and Putin, first raised at Tuesday’s UN General Assembly session; the Kremlin’s Peskov continues to call it premature, saying expert-level talks should come first. No trilateral meeting has been scheduled.

SpaceX’s lockup opened calmly Thursday, but a Shotwell share sale and two more unlock dates lie ahead

President Gwynne Shotwell disclosed a $52 million share sale ahead of this week’s unlock; the next tranches come October 9 and 24.

SPCX fell about 4% Wednesday — described by several outlets as its sharpest single-day drop in nearly two months — after President and COO Gwynne Shotwell filed a Form 4 disclosing plans to sell 342,170 shares, worth roughly $52 million, ahead of Thursday’s lockup expiration (roughly 328.4 million additional shares becoming eligible to trade for the first time since the June IPO). Thursday’s actual unlock session was far calmer: SPCX closed at $148.03, down 0.22%, with no major dislocation observed. Further lockup tranches are scheduled for October 9 and October 24, then a larger release tied to third-quarter earnings — Thursday’s calm session doesn’t resolve what happens at those larger unlocks.

The Economy

Two releases land before this edition is far behind Logan: Durable Goods Orders for August at 8:30 AM ET, with the best available consensus estimates — not refreshed same-morning — clustered around −0.3% headline and +0.5% ex-transport, against July’s actual +1.1% headline gain (its fifteenth straight ex-transport increase). The final University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment read for September follows at 10:00 AM; the preliminary print was 47.8, down 7.5% from August, with year-ahead inflation expectations up to 4.6% from 4.0%. A final that simply confirms 47.8 would be a non-event; any revision away from it would be the actual story. The BLS also publishes its Employee Benefits in the United States survey at 10:00 AM, routine and not market-moving.

Two market-implied FRED series did roll to fresher observations overnight: the 2s10s Treasury spread (T10Y2Y) widened to 0.31 percentage points from 0.26, while 10-year breakeven inflation expectations (T10YIE) eased slightly to 2.33% from 2.35% — nominal yields rising while inflation expectations tick down implies real yields rose even more, consistent with a market pricing tighter policy rather than more inflation. CPI, core CPI, payrolls and the unemployment rate are unchanged from Thursday, still reflecting August data.

Pennsylvania

Garrity and Shapiro’s tax-cut divide sharpens ahead of next year’s governor’s race

State Treasurer Stacy Garrity, a likely 2026 gubernatorial contender, continues campaigning on cutting Pennsylvania’s flat income-tax rate below its current 3.07% — a state House bill would take it to 2.8% — along with eliminating the inheritance tax and rolling back the 5.9% gross-receipts tax on utilities. Governor Shapiro has not proposed cutting the income-tax rate itself; his platform centers on the targeted Working Pennsylvanians Tax Credit, worth up to $805 on top of the federal Earned Income Tax Credit. Fresh coverage this week confirms the divide is sharpening, not narrowing, as both sides position ahead of next year’s race.
