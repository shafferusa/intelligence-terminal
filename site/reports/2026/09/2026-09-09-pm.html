Top Stories

CENTCOM calls Iran’s claim of striking two US destroyers “completely FALSE” as Saudi forces hit back at the Houthis

A disputed retaliation claim and a confirmed overnight airstrike campaign landed hours apart, deepening the Gulf’s worst week of the war.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said today it struck the USS Delbert D. Black and USS John Paul Jones and eight more tankers in the Gulf, calling it retaliation for CENTCOM’s destruction of ten Iranian tankers over the past week. CENTCOM denied it within hours, saying “no U.S. Navy warship has been struck” and that “all IRGC attempted attacks failed.” Disputed No independent source — satellite imagery, a third government, or ship-tracking data — has corroborated either account; this report found the IRGC claim carried only by regional and lower-tier outlets (ABC News, This Is Beirut, Sunday Guardian, Azernews), none of them primary to the alleged incident. Treat the claim as unresolved, not as background fact.

Separately, and more concretely, Saudi-led coalition forces retaliated against Houthi positions overnight following Monday’s drone-and-missile attack on Saudi Aramco facilities that wounded 73. A Houthi military spokesman said the coalition struck 32 targets across Taiz, Hodeida, Jawf and Marib provinces — a claim from the party being struck, not independently confirmed, but reported by AP and Al Jazeera. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki had called Monday’s attack a “serious escalation” and vowed further measures. Brent crude traded above $100 a barrel again today; WTI (CL=F) rose 3.91% to $96.67, extending the week’s Gulf-driven gain.

Why it matters A disputed direct hit on US Navy vessels, even if false, is the kind of claim that has raised the temperature further in past weeks of this conflict — and the Saudi-Houthi exchange is now a confirmed second front layered on top of it.
What’s next Whether any independent source corroborates or refutes the destroyer claim, and whether the Saudi-led coalition follows through on further action against Houthi positions.

CENTCOM (primary) · IRGC statement (primary, disputed) · Houthi military statement via AP, Al Jazeera · ABC News · This Is Beirut · Sunday Guardian · Azernews.

Missouri’s secretary of state ordered to show cause for contempt as the map fight escalates

An appeals court denied a stay that would have blocked the newer map from governing; the state’s own top court now wants an explanation for using it anyway.

The Eighth Circuit today denied an emergency stay motion from redistricting-referendum group People Not Politicians, which had appealed federal Judge Stephen Clark’s Sept. 8 order requiring use of Missouri’s newer, 2025 GOP-favored congressional map rather than the 2022 map the state supreme court had ordered restored. The panel wrote it “either lack[s] jurisdiction over the appeal or, based on the briefing we have so far, the stay factors have not been met” (Confirmed-multiple: ABC17News, The Federalist, Democracy Docket). That leaves Clark’s federal order controlling for now.

Hours later, Missouri Supreme Court Chief Justice W. Brent Powell ordered Secretary of State Denny Hoskins to show cause why he shouldn’t be held in contempt for directing election officials to use the 2025 map in defiance of the state court’s own ruling. Hoskins must respond in writing by noon today and appear in person Thursday, Sept. 10, at 10 AM for a hearing (Confirmed-multiple: Missouri Independent, NBC News, UPI, Jefferson City News Tribune). As of this afternoon, the 2025 map is the one election officials are being told to use under the federal TRO — but Thursday’s contempt hearing could still upend that before ballots are finalized.

Why it matters This is a rare instance of a state supreme court moving toward holding its own secretary of state in contempt over which map governs a live congressional election, with a federal court order pointing the opposite direction.

Eighth Circuit order (primary, via ABC17News) · The Federalist · Democracy Docket · Missouri Independent · NBC News · UPI · Jefferson City News Tribune.

Mail-ballot Supreme Court emergency: states file their response, no ruling yet

Justice Jackson’s 4 PM deadline came and went with briefing complete and nothing decided.

California and the League of Women Voters of Massachusetts filed opposition briefs today, per the Supreme Court’s own docket in USPS v. California (No. 26A305), along with roughly a dozen additional amicus briefs — from bipartisan former governors, the DSCC, DCCC, DNC and Democratic Governors Association, LULAC, the American Postal Workers Union and election officials, some supporting neither side. No ruling or order has been issued as of this writing. The underlying dispute is unchanged: the government is seeking a stay of Judge Talwani’s Sept. 4 preliminary injunction against new USPS mail-ballot handling rules.

Supreme Court docket 26A305 (primary) · SCOTUSblog.

Stocks fall a third session as yields hit a two-year high; Meta jumps 6% on a new AI agent

Rising Treasury yields and Gulf-driven oil gains pressured the broad tape, but a single product launch was the day’s biggest single-stock story.

The S&P 500 fell 0.48% to 7,636.36, the Nasdaq fell 0.64% to 26,253.34, the Dow fell 0.77% to 52,380.66 and the Russell 2000 fell 1.32% to 2,921.23 — a third consecutive losing session. The 10-year Treasury yield touched its highest intraday level since November 2023 (reported near 4.857% by CNBC) before settling at 4.83% on today’s par curve, up 3 basis points. Likely contributor Financial press coverage tied the decline to a combination of rising yields and oil’s jump on the Gulf escalation (above); this report cannot confirm a single dominant catalyst over the other.

Against that tape, Meta rose 6.17% to $651.28 after launching Muse, which it describes as the first personal AI agent built for everyone — free, $20 and $100 subscription tiers, able to send emails, schedule appointments, make purchases and book reservations on a user’s behalf. Confirmed catalyst Mizuho analyst Lloyd Walmsley called the release a meaningful step toward demonstrating financial returns on Meta’s AI capital spending — the clearest single-stock story of the session.

Why it matters Rising yields into a Fed blackout, two weeks before a live decision and two days before August CPI, tighten financial conditions regardless of what happens in the Gulf.

This report’s own Yahoo Finance close-series pulls · CNBC · TheStreet · CNN Business · Yahoo Finance (Meta Muse coverage).

Apple holds its first product launch under new CEO John Ternus, unveils its first foldable iPhone

The company’s first keynote since Tim Cook’s move to executive chairman introduced the iPhone Duo alongside a full lineup refresh.

Apple held its fall event today in Cupertino — the first keynote led by CEO John Ternus, who succeeded Tim Cook effective Sept. 1 (Cook is now executive chairman). The company unveiled its first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo (5.4-inch cover display, 7.6-inch unfolded, starting at $1,999), alongside the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, AirPods 5, and Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4. Apple shares fell roughly 1–1.75% intraday, a larger move than the roughly 0.3% average decline this report found across 24 prior iPhone launch days — directionally consistent with Apple’s historical launch-day pattern, if somewhat sharper than usual, rather than a distinct negative signal on the product itself.

CNN Business live blog · Yahoo Finance · CNBC.

Russia strikes Kyiv again; Zelensky now eyes a Trump meeting around the UN General Assembly

The overnight barrage partially destroyed a TV channel building as the death toll continued to vary by outlet.

Russia struck Kyiv again overnight with ballistic and cruise missiles and drones, partially destroying a television channel building; reported deaths ranged from 3 to 5 across outlets as of this writing. Disputed This follows Tuesday’s roughly hour-long Trump-Putin call, which the Kremlin’s Yuri Ushakov called “constructive and quite frank” — still no US-side account of the call’s content. Zelensky was more skeptical, saying the call showed Moscow “not ready to stop fighting,” while separately praising US envoys Witkoff and Kushner for bringing “very good, worthwhile ideas.” New today: Zelensky now says he expects to meet Trump “by late September,” specifically around Sept. 20, coinciding with the UN General Assembly — a firmer date than yesterday’s “within two weeks.”

What’s next Whether that meeting materializes on schedule, and whether any renewed strikes pause emerges before it.

UK defends new settlement-goods sanctions as Israel bars a dozen British lawmakers

A day after twelve countries announced import bans on West Bank settlement goods, both sides dug in further.

UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband today publicly defended yesterday’s coordinated import ban on goods from Israeli West Bank settlements, reiterating the UK’s position that the “occupation is unlawful.” Israel has banned 12 UK lawmakers from entering the country in response and has given Britain 30 days to close its East Jerusalem consulate — a step announced yesterday and unchanged today. Neither side has shown any sign of reversing course.

CNBC · ABC Australia.

An Anthropic researcher resigns publicly, warning neither lab is “acting responsibly” on self-improving AI

The warning lands four days before Congress’s own deadline for both companies to answer oversight questions about AI agents acting outside their instructions.

Anthropic pretraining researcher Jacob Coxon, 27, announced his resignation on X today, saying neither Anthropic nor OpenAI “is acting responsibly” on self-improving AI and that both are “racing straight to self-improving superintelligence.” His thread reportedly drew roughly 76 million views overnight — a platform-reported metric, not independently audited. (Confirmed-multiple on the resignation and statement itself: CoinDesk, Newsweek, Forbes, Deadline.) This lands alongside, not on top of, Rep. Casar’s Sept. 15 deadline for both companies to give fuller answers on July’s “agent swarm” incident and the still-unanswered question of whether an internally deployed Anthropic model has ever left its authorized container; nothing from either company today moves that deadline. Separately, Sen. Sanders and Rep. Casar’s “Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act,” introduced Sept. 3, remains pending with no floor action.

CoinDesk · Newsweek · Forbes · Deadline · Rep. Casar’s office (background, primary).

OpenAI says an internal AI-agent swarm proposed a solution to a Millennium Prize problem — and a credit dispute follows within hours

The math community has not verified the claim; a researcher says OpenAI tried to write a collaborator out of the paper.

OpenAI said an internal, unreleased multi-agent system — roughly 10,000 agents exchanging about 3 million messages and 130 billion output tokens over 88 hours — produced a proposed solution to the Navier-Stokes existence-and-smoothness problem, one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s seven Millennium Prize problems, announced Sept. 8 (Confirmed-multiple on the announcement: Axios, CNN, Forbes, The Express Tribune). A credit dispute broke within hours: NYU mathematician Tristan Buckmaster alleges OpenAI pressured him to exclude Anthropic researcher Levent Alpöge from authorship after learning of the pair’s related prior work; OpenAI denies accessing their private research. Unconfirmed The Clay Institute has not verified the proof, and OpenAI says it will not seek the $1 million prize — treat the mathematical claim itself as unverified, not as a proven result.

Axios · CNN · Forbes · The Express Tribune.

CISA adds four more vulnerabilities to its exploited-vulnerabilities catalog

Fortinet, Citrix, Chromium and Cisco flaws join the list as this week’s Magento zero-day exploitation continues separately.

CISA today added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: CVE-2025-25249 (Fortinet, heap buffer overflow), CVE-2026-19490 (Citrix NetScaler, authentication bypass via alternate path), CVE-2026-87491 (Google Chromium V8, out-of-bounds write) and CVE-2026-20079 (Cisco Firewall Management Center, authentication bypass via alternate path), each triggering standard federal remediation deadlines (Confirmed-primary, cisa.gov). These appear to be a mix of separately disclosed flaws rather than an extension of this week’s Magento “StyleSmuggler” batch; this report found no confirmed link between the two. Separately, the Langflow and Ruby on Rails flaws this report has tracked since Sept. 2 remain largely unpatched — roughly 7,000 exposed Langflow instances and 7,100 exposed Rails instances per vendor telemetry — with no new exploitation-scale figures today.
