Top Stories

Houthi drone-and-missile attack hits Saudi oil infrastructure, wounds 73

A distinct escalation from Saturday’s US-Iran naval clash: an Iran-aligned force struck a US Gulf ally’s energy facilities directly, and crude jumped toward $99 a barrel.

Yemen’s Houthi movement launched a large-scale drone-and-missile attack on Saudi Arabia today, hitting Saudi Aramco facilities in Najran and Abha, the roughly 400,000-barrel-per-day Jizan refinery, a fuel terminal and King Khalid Air Base in Khamis Mushait. Saudi officials reported 73 wounded and fires at multiple sites, with some facility operations temporarily suspended. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed responsibility, framing it as retaliation for Saudi strikes on Yemen; Saudi Arabia said it would “finish the job” against the group. The Washington Post, NPR, CNN and other outlets independently reported the casualty figure and targets (Confirmed-multiple); no US government statement on this specific attack has surfaced as of this writing.

This is a separate front from the story this report has tracked since Saturday’s CENTCOM-confirmed US-Iran naval exchange near Kharg Island — no Gulf state has commented on that incident specifically, five days on — and from Iran security council chief Mohsen Rezaei’s still-unspecified threat of a Hormuz “exclusion zone,” which remains Single-reliable-source, sourced only to Iranian state broadcasting, with no new detail today. WTI crude (CL=F) rose 3.04% to $94.26; Brent, which this report does not price directly, was reported by multiple outlets trading near $98–99, a three-month high and roughly 9% higher for the month.

Why it matters An Iran-aligned proxy striking a US ally’s oil infrastructure directly widens the Gulf conflict onto energy supply itself, a different and more immediate transmission mechanism than the naval skirmishing and rhetorical threats this report has tracked for two weeks.
What’s next Any Saudi retaliatory action against Houthi positions in Yemen, and whether any Gulf state finally comments on either this attack or Saturday’s naval exchange.

Washington Post · NPR · CNN · US News · Houthi military statement (primary, via Reuters/CBC) · CENTCOM (background, primary).

Russia resumes strikes on Kyiv; Trump-Putin call ends without a deal

The toll climbed through the day as the diplomatic momentum from the weekend’s envoy trip collided with a fresh round of strikes.

Russia struck Kyiv and more than 50 sites overnight after the three-day capitals-only strikes pause lapsed Monday evening. Ukrainian officials’ reported toll climbed through the day — from an initial 2 dead to at least 5 killed and 63 injured by afternoon, per Kyiv Independent’s account of Ukrainian officials; one outlet’s combined dead-and-injured figure of 18 has not been reconciled against that count. Disputed Treat 5 dead as the most current published figure, not a final one. Ukraine’s air force said it intercepted 142 drones and all but one of 32 cruise missiles and both ballistic missiles fired — a self-reported military claim this report cannot independently verify.

Separately, Trump and Putin spoke by phone for about an hour today, following the weekend’s Witkoff-Kushner trip to Moscow and Kyiv. The Kremlin’s Yuri Ushakov called it “constructive and quite frank” and said the two agreed to stay in touch; no US-side account or concrete outcome has been published (Confirmed-primary that the call occurred; the characterization of its content is the Kremlin’s alone). Trump separately posted publicly urging Putin to stop the strikes, citing Russian and Ukrainian casualties and pushing to finish a peace deal — the exact sequence relative to the call is unclear from available reporting. Zelensky called the overnight assault deliberately designed “to cause as much harm to life as possible,” said Witkoff and Kushner brought “very good, worthwhile ideas,” and said he hopes to meet Trump within two weeks, potentially alongside Russia and European leaders.

Why it matters The pause lapsing into a worse round of strikes, on the same day as an inconclusive Trump-Putin call, undercuts the momentum the weekend’s envoy trip appeared to build.
What’s next Whether a Trump-Zelensky meeting materializes within two weeks, and whether any concrete step follows today’s call.

Kyiv Independent · Meduza · Ukrinform · Kremlin readout via Bloomberg, Al Jazeera, Moscow Times · Zelensky remarks to press (primary).

Briefing at the Supreme Court is now complete on both sides; Missouri’s own certification clock has run out first.

Missouri filed its reply brief in the shadow-docket redistricting case today, completing briefing after challengers filed their response Sunday. Missouri’s own state-law ballot-certification deadline arrived today, Sept. 8, with the congressional map still undecided; the state’s own remaining hard deadline — printing and readying overseas and military ballots — falls Sept. 19, and Missouri had asked the Court to rule by roughly Sept. 13–14 (sourcing found both dates; this report is not asserting a single one). No stay or merits order has been issued as of this writing.

Why it matters The state's own requested timeline is now running past the deadline it set for itself, with briefing complete and no ruling yet.

Correction: Meta’s Oakland youth-safety litigation is two separate cases, not one

This report’s own tracking conflated a settled $18 billion states’ case with a separate, still-live $1.4 trillion trial — both before the same Oakland judge.

Earlier editions, including Sept. 2’s, described a single ongoing “29-state” Oakland trial in which Mark Zuckerberg had just testified. That conflated two distinct lawsuits. The first — a roughly 47-state attorneys-general coalition case seeking, among other things, an $18 billion settlement (about $17 billion to the states that sued jointly in 2023, the remainder to other states and territories) — was approved by Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers on Aug. 26, before this report’s Sept. 2 edition ran. Florida did not join and is pursuing its own separate suit. The second — a trial brought by four states (California, Colorado, Kentucky and New Jersey), representing themselves and 25 other states on child-data claims, seeking as much as $1.4 trillion — opened Aug. 18 in the same Oakland courtroom before the same judge and is the case in which Zuckerberg actually testified; it continues, expected to run six to seven weeks with an advisory jury verdict and a final liability ruling by Judge Gonzalez Rogers. Nothing dated today was found on either case. This is logged to the corrections ledger.

Why it matters Clarifies which of Meta’s two Oakland cases is actually still contested ($1.4 trillion, unresolved) and which is already paid and closed ($18 billion, settled three weeks ago).

CNN · NPR · Washington Post · CNBC · KTVU · KQED · Press Democrat (all primary-adjacent court coverage).

Patrick Havas pleads not guilty in Amy Acton confrontation case

The Labor Day-delayed arraignment set bond and no-contact conditions in the Canfield Fair confrontation with Ohio’s Democratic governor nominee.

Patrick Havas, 38, appeared today in Mahoning County Area Court, Canfield — his arraignment pushed from Monday by the Labor Day holiday — before Judge Molly Johnson. He pleaded not guilty to two first-degree-misdemeanor assault counts, first-degree-misdemeanor inducing panic and fourth-degree-misdemeanor disorderly conduct, stemming from Sunday’s incident in which he allegedly forced through a crowd toward Amy Acton at her Canfield Fair appearance, knocking fairgoers down before Ohio State Highway Patrol intercepted him. Bond was set at $5,000; conditions include surrendering all firearms, no possession of guns, no contact with Acton or the two people allegedly pushed, and no speaking to media. Disputed Outlets differ on the exact weapons found on him at the time — a taser and two handguns per one account, two handguns and brass knuckles per another — a detail this report is not resolving without a primary law-enforcement statement.

What’s next No further court date has been reported yet.

CISA adds Magento “StyleSmuggler” zero-day and three more flaws to its exploited-vulnerabilities catalog

Government confirmation follows four days after the flaw was already being exploited against e-commerce stores.

CISA today added CVE-2026-75650 — “StyleSmuggler,” the CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaw in Magento Open Source and Adobe Commerce disclosed by Sansec — to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, alongside CVE-2026-81963 (Windows Link Following), CVE-2026-85880 (Windows Heap-Based Buffer Overflow) and CVE-2026-86218 (N-able N-central Static Code Injection), each triggering standard federal remediation deadlines. Confirmed exploitation of StyleSmuggler remains at a small number of named victims — at least two breached Magento stores per hosting-firm Disrex Group’s forensics, including one that was fully patched before compromise, meaning patch compliance alone did not prevent it before Adobe’s fix existed. Broader exposure — estimates run from roughly 111,000 to more than 239,000 active Magento installations — is a theoretical ceiling, not a confirmed compromise count.

CISA KEV catalog (primary) · Sansec (primary discoverer) · Disrex Group forensics · BleepingComputer · The Hacker News · TechTimes.

Mistral raises €3 billion — the largest funding round in European tech history

A Samsung-led Series D more than doubles the French AI lab’s valuation to over €21 billion in just over a year.

Mistral AI announced a €3 billion Series D at a post-money valuation above €21 billion, led by Samsung Electronics with Scaleup Europe Fund (managed by EQT) and existing investor PSG Equity as co-leads; Advent, BlackRock-managed funds and the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg joined as new investors. The company and multiple outlets describe it as the largest equity round ever completed by a European technology company. CEO Arthur Mensch told CNBC Mistral is on track to top $1 billion in annual recurring revenue before year-end. The round roughly doubles Mistral’s valuation from its €1.7 billion Series C (€11.7 billion post-money) a year ago.

Why it matters A marker of how far European “sovereign AI” ambitions have scaled on capital, even though Mistral’s valuation remains a fraction of OpenAI’s or Anthropic’s.

TechCrunch · EU-Startups · Dataconomy · CNBC (CEO interview).

DRC Ebola toll revised sharply upward: 6,604 cases, 3,175 deaths

A single day’s jump of 82 cases and 41 deaths keeps this the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record.

The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control’s Sept. 6 report, using data through Sept. 5, puts the outbreak at 6,604 confirmed cases and 3,175 deaths in the DRC plus 2 in Uganda, with 851 people currently hospitalized in isolation — up 82 cases and 41 deaths from the prior day’s report. Ituri province accounts for roughly 82% of cases. The outbreak remains complicated by a Bundibugyo strain that existing Zaire-strain vaccines and treatments do not fully cover.

ECDC, sourced from WHO and DRC Ministry of Health data (primary).

Canada’s retaliatory tariffs take effect; no sign talks are resuming

$27.6 billion of dollar-for-dollar tariffs on US goods are now operative, not just threatened.

Canada’s retaliatory tariffs — 15%, 25% and 50% brackets on roughly $27.6 billion of US goods across steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, and electronics, matching the US Section 232/338 schedule dollar-for-dollar — took effect at 12:01 AM today as scheduled. No US government response or sign of resumed bilateral talks was found today; Prime Minister Carney has said Canada is “ready to sit down” but that talks won’t resume while US officials are “publicly insulting Canada.” Separately, Canada published details today of its $1.5 billion Regional Tariff Response Initiative expansion (announced Aug. 25, bringing the program total to $3 billion) — non-repayable contributions of up to $3 million per eligible small or mid-sized business.

Canada.ca, Department of Finance (primary) · CNBC · Bloomberg · Al Jazeera.
