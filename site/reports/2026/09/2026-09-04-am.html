Top Stories

US strikes Kermanshah overnight as a wedding-strike controversy hardens the Iran war

Iran says 18 people died and 108 were hurt in Wednesday's round of strikes; a New York Times visual analysis undercuts Vice President Vance's on-record skepticism that a US strike hit a wedding, as oil holds near five-week highs.

US forces struck Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets in Kermanshah province overnight, killing four IRGC Aerospace Force personnel the Guard named as Reza Mohammadi, Shahram Jafari, Alireza Shakiba and Jafar Kahrizi, according to Iranian state media. President Trump said on Truth Social the strike answered a "failed attempt at adding sea mines" in the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian officials separately said 18 people were killed and 108 injured across Wednesday's broader round of US strikes — a toll this paper has not independently verified beyond the Iranian government's own count.

The more consequential development is a credibility fight over an earlier strike. Iran alleges a US strike hit a wedding celebration, killing four civilians and wounding dozens; Vice President Vance told reporters he is "extremely skeptical" of the claim, citing Iranian state media's record, and said the military is investigating "very fully." But a New York Times visual analysis, cross-checked against an independent weapons expert, concluded the wedding was in fact struck by a US-released bomb. A local hospital chief said most of the roughly 70 people admitted after the strike have been discharged, but 11 remain under treatment, most of them women and children. Vance separately said he "wouldn't call" the six-month exchange a war.

Why it matters A credibly documented civilian strike, contested at the vice-presidential level even as independent analysis contradicts the administration's skepticism, is a different kind of story than a rhetorical escalation — it is a verification fight this paper will keep pressing on primary imagery and DoD statements, not just competing claims.
What’s next Whether the Pentagon confirms or disputes the Times analysis on the record; whether Kermanshah draws a further Iranian response before the weekend.

CBS News, RFE/RL, Time, Middle East Eye (Vance remarks, primary via transcript) · New York Times (visual analysis) · Iranian state media via wire (Kermanshah toll, casualty figures, unverified) · Trump Truth Social post (primary).

Go deeper

This is the fourth distinct escalation track this paper has tracked inside the six-month Hormuz standoff in the past ten days alone: the widened Kuwait/UAE strikes Tuesday, Wednesday's Jordan/Bahrain/Kuwait retaliation, and now Kermanshah plus the wedding-strike dispute. Oil has not given back its gains from any of them — Brent traded near $95-96 and WTI near $90-91 through Wednesday, up more than 7% on the week per multiple financial outlets, even as US equities rallied through the same news. Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNBC that a wartime-record 17 million barrels transited Hormuz on Monday under US military escort, though Wednesday's transit count fell to six vessels versus a 10-day average near 13 — shipping has not collapsed, but it has not normalized either.

Missouri's Supreme Court unanimously kills the Trump-backed congressional map

The court ordered a November referendum and reverted the state to its 2022 district lines for this fall's election; Missouri's attorney general vows to appeal to the US Supreme Court.

The Missouri Supreme Court ruled unanimously Wednesday that the state's new, Trump-backed congressional map cannot be used for the November election, ordering a statewide referendum on whether to adopt it going forward. Missouri will instead use its 2022 map for this fall's contests. The court held that a state constitutional provision allowing a referendum on "any act" of the legislature covers congressional redistricting — the legal question at the center of a case this paper has followed since oral arguments concluded Sept. 2, one day ahead of the Sept. 8 ballot-certification deadline the ruling beat by five days.

The map reached the court after People Not Politicians Missouri gathered more than 300,000 signatures for a veto referendum; Secretary of State Denny Hoskins had refused to certify it as unconstitutional. Republican Attorney General Catherine Hanaway called the ruling "unprecedented," said no court has ever overturned a congressional map after a primary and before a general election, and said it "clearly violates federal law" — signaling an emergency appeal to the US Supreme Court.

What’s next Whether Hanaway files the promised US Supreme Court emergency application, and on what timeline given the state has already reverted to the 2022 map operationally.

NBC News, CNN, The Hill, Yahoo News, Townhall (ruling coverage) · Missouri Supreme Court opinion (primary, via reporting).

August jobs report lands at 8:30 ET — the week's real catalyst, two hours after this edition publishes

Consensus clusters near 50,000-58,000 net new jobs and a 4.1% unemployment rate, after June and July combined for essentially zero net hiring; a Haiti TPS work-permit expiration is a wildcard economists are flagging as a possible added drag.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics releases the Employment Situation for August at 8:30 AM ET. The Dow Jones consensus estimate is +53,000 nonfarm payrolls; broader surveys cluster from roughly +50,000 to +58,000, with unemployment expected to hold at 4.1%. The bar is low: June and July combined for a net loss of 3,000 jobs, and July's initial print of a 23,000 decline missed a Dow Jones consensus of +83,000 by a wide margin. Some economists are flagging the expiration of Temporary Protected Status work permits for roughly 200,000 Haitian workers as a possible one-time drag distinct from any underlying trend.

This report logged a forecast Wednesday (2026-09-03-am-2) that Friday's print would come in weak, extending the soft ADP signal from Tuesday (+38,000, the weakest since January). That forecast cannot be graded until the number is in hand.

Why it matters This is the last full jobs report before the Sept. 16 FOMC decision, and it lands into a Fed that is visibly split — see below — making today's number unusually likely to move the rate path in either direction within the hour.

BLS release schedule (primary, scheduled) · CNBC, Kiplinger, TradingKey, Seeking Alpha (consensus surveys) · this report's own forecast ledger.

Fed rate-hike odds fall 12 points in two days as Waller breaks publicly from Warsh

CME-implied September hike odds slid from roughly 66% Monday to about 55% after Governor Waller said Wednesday he would lean toward holding rates steady absent a fresh inflation surprise — a direct contrast with Chair Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole tone.

Fed Governor Christopher Waller said Wednesday he would be "inclined to support holding the target for the federal funds rate at its current setting" if incoming data over the next two weeks continues on its recent path, naming the Aug. 11 CPI report as the decisive input for his own vote. That is a direct break from Chair Kevin Warsh, whose Jackson Hole remarks — that softer inflation readings "do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved" — had pushed CME-tracked hike odds as high as roughly 66% earlier this week, per multiple trackers. Odds have since eased to around 55%, a swing large enough to satisfy — and this paper grades correct — its own Tuesday forecast that odds would fall back below 55% following that day's weak ADP print.

Treasury yields eased across the curve Thursday on the shift — the 10-year fell 2 basis points to 4.77%, the 2-year fell 5 basis points to 4.34% — helping drive Thursday's equity rally. The 2s10s spread stands at a positively sloped 43 basis points.

What’s next Today's jobs report and next Thursday's CPI print are now the two swing inputs before the Sept. 16 decision; Bessent has separately argued publicly against raising rates "into a supply shock," a third voice in what is now an open three-way Fed disagreement.

CNBC, PBS NewsHour, Washington Times, Axios (Waller remarks, primary via transcript) · CME FedWatch (odds tracking, multiple secondary aggregators, exact reading varies by source) · Treasury par-yield curve (primary, EOD official).

Downtown Minneapolis high-rise shooting kills two, wounds three officers

A 35-year-old resident facing eviction opened fire in his apartment building Wednesday afternoon, then died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound after exchanging fire with police; this report did not cover the shooting until now, one news cycle late.

Coverage gap A shooting at a downtown Minneapolis high-rise near 15 East Grant Street left two people dead Wednesday afternoon, plus the suspected gunman, who authorities say died of a self-inflicted gunshot wound after exchanging fire with responding officers. Three officers were injured, two by gunfire — one shot in the stomach, one in the leg — and are in stable condition at Hennepin County Medical Center. Police identified the suspect as Carlton Johnson II, 35, a resident of the building's ninth floor who was facing eviction and had made concerning statements about a firearm to a security officer in June. Officers responding to the building also encountered an airborne haze that affected several additional officers' visibility during the response; its source was not specified in available reporting.

Wednesday's closing edition of this paper did not run — a publishing gap, not an editorial choice — so this is the first coverage of an event now roughly 38 hours old.

Gloria Steinem, who helped build the modern women's movement, dies at 92

Steinem died Wednesday at her New York City home; no cause of death was given.

Gloria Steinem, the journalist, organizer and Ms. magazine co-founder who became one of the most visible figures of the modern American women's movement, died Wednesday at her home in New York City. She was 92. A statement posted to her Instagram account said she "passed away peacefully... surrounded by some of the many who loved her"; no cause of death was disclosed. Steinem spent more than five decades organizing around reproductive rights, workplace equality and sexual harassment, remaining a public voice on those fights into her 90s.

CNN, NPR, NBC News, The Washington Post (obituary coverage, multiple independently reporting).

Hamas warns Gaza ceasefire "could collapse" as the UN's own tally shows 29 violations in three weeks

The dispute over Tuesday's raid that Israel says captured Hamas's internal-security chief has not resolved; UNMISS-style monitoring from the ceasefire mechanism itself now documents nearly one violation a day since Aug. 19.

Hamas issued a fresh public warning that the US-backed Gaza ceasefire framework "could collapse," a direct escalation from its earlier statement disputing Israel's account of Tuesday's raid. Separately, a monitoring tally tied to the ceasefire mechanism counted 29 Israeli strikes or ceasefire violations between Aug. 19 and Sept. 3 — roughly one every three days, a cadence that has not slowed since the Board of Peace stabilization plan took effect. The specific dispute over who Israel actually captured Tuesday — Hamas's internal-security chief, per Israeli officials, versus a lower-ranking official, per a Gaza security source cited earlier this week — remains unresolved either way, as does responsibility for the covering-strike deaths of at least three Palestinians, including two children, that Israel has not disputed but also has not addressed.

Why it matters A framework built to reduce strikes that instead averages one documented violation every three days is a ceasefire in name only if the trend does not reverse; Hamas's language shift from disputing one raid to threatening the whole framework is the more significant move.

Longwarjournal.org/FDD analysis (violation count, citing ceasefire-mechanism monitoring) · Middle East Monitor, Al Jazeera (Hamas statement) · prior editions' sourcing on the raid dispute (IDF, Shin Bet statements; Gaza security source, unverified).

ChatGPT, Claude and Grok all go down within the same window; no evidence of a shared cause

OpenAI, Anthropic and xAI each confirmed Thursday outages disrupting millions of users simultaneously; each company attributes its own outage to a distinct internal cause, and no evidence has surfaced connecting the three.

OpenAI's ChatGPT and Codex, Anthropic's Claude, and xAI's Grok all experienced outages within a similar window Thursday, according to user reports and each company's own status page. OpenAI attributed its disruption to a routing error beginning around 7:43 AM PT; Anthropic said Claude's outage traced to an infrastructure issue and was resolved by 12:15 PM ET. Unconfirmed No public evidence connects the three companies' outages to a shared cause, and no party has suggested a cyberattack; speculation to that effect circulating online is not corroborated by any of the three companies' own incident statements.

Why it matters Whatever the individual causes, three of the most heavily used AI platforms failing in the same window is itself a data point on how much of the internet's day-to-day workflow now runs through a small number of AI vendors — a concentration-risk argument independent of whether the outages were actually linked.

Bloomberg, The Register, company status pages (primary, each company's own account).

DRC's Ebola toll passes 6,250 as the outbreak remains the country's largest ever recorded

WHO's most recent figures put the case count near 6,250 with more than 3,000 deaths; no vaccine is yet approved for the Bundibugyo strain driving the outbreak.

The Democratic Republic of Congo's Ebola outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo virus, has grown to roughly 6,250 confirmed cases and more than 3,000 deaths as of the most recent reporting window, continuing a rise this paper has tracked since late July. It remains the largest Ebola epidemic ever recorded in the country. Roughly 870 patients are currently hospitalized in isolation, and about 89% of identified case contacts are under active follow-up in the affected provinces — Ituri and North Kivu account for the bulk of new cases.

Risks & Scenarios

Jobs report reprices the Fed sharply in either direction. Preconditions: a print more than roughly 30,000 away from the ~53,000 consensus in either direction. Probability 35–50% of a move that size, on the basis of the past two months' large consensus misses. If weak: rate-hike odds likely fall further from today's ~55%, yields ease, small-caps (already lagging — IWM sits below both its 20- and 50-day averages) could catch a relief bid. If strong: odds could snap back toward Monday's ~66% level, pressuring the recent rally in rate-sensitive growth names. Invalidated by a print landing within a few thousand of consensus with no meaningful revision to prior months.

Iran war widens to a fifth country or a US base is hit directly. Preconditions: continued Iranian retaliatory rhetoric following Kermanshah and the wedding-strike dispute. Probability 20–30% within the next week, on the basis of the pattern of near-daily escalation since late August. If it happens: oil likely extends its move toward or past $100 Brent; equities have so far shrugged off each individual escalation, which may not hold if a US base or personnel are directly hit. Invalidated by a de-escalatory signal from either Tehran or Washington, of which there has been none this week.

Missouri's map fight reaches the US Supreme Court on an emergency basis. Preconditions: Attorney General Hanaway files the promised application. Probability 55–70% within the next two weeks, on the basis of her explicit public statement. If it happens: a national test case for how late in an election cycle a state court can redraw maps, with direct relevance to ongoing redistricting fights in other states this paper has tracked. Invalidated by Missouri instead pursuing an en banc or procedural path that avoids the emergency docket.
