Top Stories

Houthis seize a Red Sea port city as Saudi Arabia asks Trump to strike — he declines

Mocha fell Thursday near the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint; Iran’s disputed claim to have hit two US destroyers persists as oil trades at its highest since May.

Houthi fighters captured the Red Sea port city of Mocha on Thursday as Yemeni government forces withdrew — a battlefield gain with weight beyond the fighting itself. Mocha sits near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the Red Sea chokepoint tied to but distinct from the wider Gulf shipping crisis. More than 20,000 people have been displaced in two weeks of fighting around the port, and Yemen’s Presidential Leadership Council called the loss a matter of “regional and international” concern for global trade; the UN Security Council met on Yemen in response.

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman called President Trump twice Thursday asking for direct US airstrikes on Houthi positions. Trump declined. The US will provide intelligence and targeting data and keep roughly 200 personnel in the country in a non-kinetic support role, with a senior US official telling Axios the priority is keeping the Red Sea open while leaving the fight itself to regional partners.

The dispute over Iran’s claimed strike on two US Navy destroyers, first raised Tuesday, remains unresolved: Iran continues to name the USS Delbert D. Black and USS John Paul Jones as targets of an operation it calls “Ya Haidar Karrar,” and CENTCOM continues to call the claim “completely false,” saying no US ship was hit. Disputed No independent imagery or tracking data has surfaced to confirm either account, and Iran’s Supreme National Security Council has still not activated the Hormuz “exclusion zone” it threatened days ago.

Oil moved on the accumulated risk regardless of which claims hold up: Brent crude traded above $108 a barrel Thursday, its highest since May, and US retail diesel hit a record $6.05 a gallon, up from $5.85 a week earlier — a likely contributor to disrupted regional fuel flows rather than a single confirmed cause. West Texas Intermediate, the US benchmark, actually settled lower on the day at $98.98 (Previous close, EOD official), a pullback from Wednesday’s spike that still leaves it near the year’s highs and widens its usual discount to Brent.

Why it matters A chokepoint city changing hands and a great-power patron declining direct involvement both push against outright war without ending the crisis. The more dangerous near-term scenario is an incident — real or claimed — that removes the option of staying on the sidelines.
What’s next Iran’s foreign minister has spoken with Pakistan’s army chief about restoring de-escalation talks, and Iran’s president is in New Delhi for the BRICS summit, where the conflict is expected to dominate the agenda.

Israel destroys a major Hezbollah tunnel network, felt as a magnitude-4.1 quake

The IDF says it used roughly 1,100 tons of explosives to collapse eight tunnels spanning 5.4 kilometers beneath the Ali Taher Ridge — registered on independent seismic monitors as far as Sidon and Tyre.

The IDF said Thursday it destroyed a Hezbollah tunnel network beneath the Ali Taher Ridge near Beaufort, describing it as the “nerve center” of the group’s Iran-financed Badr regional division. The demolition — reported at roughly 1,100 tons of explosives across eight tunnels spanning 5.4 km — registered as a magnitude-4.1 seismic event on independent monitors, including the USGS and the Euro-Med Seismological Centre.

Lebanon’s army separately accused Israel Thursday of “deadly escalation” in the south, saying continued strikes threaten the June ceasefire; the IDF said a related strike followed a Hezbollah drone launch it called a “blatant violation” of the truce. The competing cumulative death-toll counts this outlet has flagged since early September remain unreconciled and, this run, inconsistent even within Lebanese sources alone — the Lebanese Health Ministry has been cited at both 4,358 and 4,381 killed since March 2 by different outlets on the same day, against a separate roughly-8,900-since-2023 figure with no accounting bridging the two.

Why it matters A tunnel network of this scale, if the IDF’s description holds up, is a materially different kind of operation than the strike-and-counter-strike pattern that has otherwise defined the ceasefire period.

Times of Israel, Al Bawaba (IDF statement, primary) · USGS, Euro-Med Seismological Centre (seismic data) · Lebanese army statement (primary).

Trump’s $5,000 convention pledge draws bipartisan pushback

The first-ever Republican “midterm convention” closed in Dallas with a promise to send every adult American a check if the party holds Congress — an idea Democrats and fiscal analysts call unfunded.

The two-night Republican Midterm Convention closed Thursday at Dallas’s American Airlines Center. Vice President JD Vance delivered a roughly 40-minute keynote leaning into cultural and family-policy themes, deflecting questions about his own political future (“We got to take care of business this November. Then we’ll worry about what comes next”) as the crowd chanted “48.” President Trump closed the convention leading a spoken voting pledge and telling the crowd, on the subject of not voting, “You know what happens if you don’t vote? You go to hell” — a quote corroborated across multiple independent outlets.

The convention’s dominant policy news came Wednesday night, when Trump promised: “If the Republicans win the House of Representatives and the United States Senate, both of them, I will issue a dividend to every adult citizen in the United States of America for $5,000.” Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA), the top Democrat on House Oversight, called it undeliverable, citing Trump’s earlier unfulfilled pledges of a tariff dividend and a DOGE-savings dividend; House Democratic Whip Katherine Clark called it an attempt to “bribe voters.” The Bipartisan Policy Center estimates 2026 tariff revenue — one funding source Trump has cited for such payments before — would cover roughly $857 per person, far short of $5,000, and notes more than half of this year’s tariff revenue has already gone to company refunds.

Why it matters A specific, large, election-conditioned cash pledge from a sitting president is a new category of midterm messaging, regardless of whether it is ever implemented.

NPR, CNN, The Hill, CNBC, Time, ABC News, PBS (convention coverage) · Bipartisan Policy Center (fiscal analysis).

Missouri’s map fight ends as Hoskins purges himself of contempt

The secretary of state reversed course Thursday and ordered the 2022 congressional map used after the state supreme court held him in contempt — closing a five-week standoff between competing state and federal orders.

The Missouri Supreme Court held Secretary of State Denny Hoskins in contempt Thursday for earlier directing election authorities to use the new, GOP-favored 2025 congressional map in defiance of the state court’s own ruling. Hoskins reversed his position hours later, telling the court “the only governing court order in effect is from the Missouri Supreme Court” after the US Supreme Court stayed a separate federal order that had favored the 2025 map. Chief Justice W. Brent Powell said from the bench that Hoskins had “purged himself of contempt” by complying — no fine or further sanction was imposed, and the state court’s own 2022-map ruling now stands as the sole governing order. Whether the court will separately sanction Hoskins for his pre-compliance conduct remains unaddressed.

UPI, NBC News, The Hill, ABC17, Missouri Independent.

Hot PPI and a widening oil shock reset the market’s Fed bet ahead of today’s CPI

Wholesale prices ran hotter than expected Thursday on an energy-driven surge, and stocks fell a fourth straight session, hours before the release Fed Governor Waller has called decisive for his September 16 vote.

The Producer Price Index for August rose 0.4% on the month and 5.4% year-over-year (unadjusted), with the increase concentrated in energy — diesel fuel alone rose 24.1% on the month, echoing the same Hormuz-driven fuel disruption behind this week’s oil move. Core PPI (ex food, energy and trade services) rose a more moderate 0.3% m/m, 4.7% y/y; services inflation was nearly flat, with trade services actually falling 0.2%. Weekly jobless claims, released the same morning, came in at 206,000 against a 207,000 prior — unremarkable on its own, and read by commentators as a labor market slowing in places without cracking broadly.

Markets fell a fourth straight session Thursday on the combination: the S&P 500 lost 0.58%, the Nasdaq 0.65%, the Dow 0.60%, and the Russell 2000 1.04%, small caps again underperforming on rate sensitivity. The 10-year Treasury yield closed at 4.95%, a fresh high dating to November 2023. Fed-funds futures continue to price roughly a coin flip on a 25-basis-point hike at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting, up sharply from pre-Jackson-Hole levels after Chair Kevin Warsh’s hawkish late-August remarks; Governor Christopher Waller has said he would support a hold if disinflation continues but that “it may not take much” to change his mind, naming this week’s CPI and PPI as the decisive inputs. No FOMC official has commented publicly since the blackout period began September 5, and none should before the meeting.

Consensus estimates for this morning’s CPI vary meaningfully by source — this report found figures ranging from roughly +0.3%/+2.9% y/y headline to +0.4%/+3.4% y/y in different aggregations, an unusually wide spread this close to a release. Estimated, wide dispersion Given that spread, and the energy impulse visible in Thursday’s PPI, treat any single pre-release figure as indicative only.

Why it matters A hot CPI print stacked on Thursday’s hot PPI and the oil spike would harden the case for a hike days before the vote; a cool print leaves the Fed weighing an energy-driven inflation impulse against an otherwise moderating core trend.

Congress calls the AI labs’ incident answers inadequate as a second Anthropic researcher backs a resignation warning

Rep. Greg Casar says neither OpenAI nor Anthropic met his transparency demands, four days before his own deadline — as a second senior Anthropic researcher publicly agrees with a departing colleague on self-improving AI.

Rep. Greg Casar (D-TX) said this week that neither OpenAI’s nor Anthropic’s written responses to his oversight letter adequately answered his questions. On Anthropic specifically, he said the company still had not disclosed how many times an internally deployed model has acted outside its authorized container in the past year, whether such events were reported to any government body, or which internal systems a compromised model could reach. “Your unwillingness to provide Members of Congress with the information we requested is deeply concerning,” Casar said. Both companies’ formal deadline to respond remains September 15.

Separately, Anthropic alignment researcher Samuel Marks has joined colleague Evan Hubinger in publicly backing departing researcher Jacob Coxon’s warning that neither major AI lab is acting responsibly on self-improving AI; Hubinger told CNBC he personally puts the odds of AI catastrophe within the next decade above 10%. Neither company has issued an official corporate response to Coxon’s original thread, which has drawn roughly 76 million views.

Why it matters Public disagreement from inside a lab’s own alignment team, arriving the same week as a congressional transparency deadline, is a harder signal for either company to wave off than outside criticism.

Rep. Casar’s House office (primary) · CNBC, TechCrunch, TIME. Coxon’s own risk assessment remains personal opinion, not an independently verified finding.

USPS v. California: fully briefed, still no Supreme Court ruling

The mail-ballot emergency application has sat before Justice Jackson since Wednesday’s 4 PM briefing deadline, with numerous amicus filings and no order.

The Supreme Court emergency application over the Postal Service’s mail-ballot rule — now docketed as USPS v. California, No. 26A305, after the government withdrew and refiled its original application — remains before Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson with no ruling. The government’s reply brief was filed September 10; California and the League of Women Voters of Massachusetts’s opposition was filed September 9, alongside amicus briefs from members of Congress and law professors. Judge Indira Talwani’s September 4 preliminary injunction blocking the rule remains in effect in the meantime, and a First Circuit panel separately declined to let the rule take effect pending the Supreme Court’s review.

Supreme Court docket 26A305 (primary) · SCOTUSblog, NPR, NBC News.

OpenAI publicly rebuts the Navier-Stokes credit-theft allegation

The company says it “did not see any of” two mathematicians’ unpublished work before its own agents produced a partial proof — while hedging that de-identified usage data can’t be entirely ruled out.

OpenAI posted a direct statement responding to the authorship dispute over its claimed Navier-Stokes finite-time-singularity result: “We congratulate Levent Alpöge and Tristan Buckmaster on their remarkable mathematical work. We (the researchers and the agents) did not see any of their work through any means until they released it publicly — in particular, no specific user data was accessed in order to solve this problem.” The company added it “cannot rule out” that de-identified data derived from the mathematicians’ product usage could have indirectly informed model training — a partial hedge, not a flat denial on every front. NYU’s Buckmaster and Anthropic’s Alpöge, who allege OpenAI used their unpublished work without credit, have since released their own Lean-verified proofs of finite-time blowup for three fluid systems, a related but distinct claim. Neither the Clay Institute nor peer review has certified OpenAI’s original claim.

OpenAI (primary, via its official account) · TechCrunch, Scientific American, Fortune. Disputed

CISA adds MikroTik router flaws to its exploited-vulnerabilities catalog amid active “MikroTrick” attacks

One flaw lets an unauthenticated user read kernel memory; a second, chained with it, grants full administrative control via a crafted SSH username.

CISA added two MikroTik RouterOS vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog this week based on confirmed active exploitation: CVE-2026-67277, an unauthenticated memory-disclosure and denial-of-service flaw in RouterOS’s bandwidth-test service, and CVE-2026-86060 (CVSS 9.2), an SSH login flaw that lets a crafted username achieve full administrative control. Poland’s national CERT has bundled these with a third, related flaw into an exploit chain it calls “MikroTrick,” warning that compromised routers can become durable footholds for credential theft, network interception and botnet recruitment. Fixes ship in RouterOS 6.49.21, 7.23.4 and 7.24.2.

CERT Polska (primary advisory) · BleepingComputer, Help Net Security. This outlet could not reach CISA’s own site directly this run; corroborated via independent secondary sources.

CPI prints hot. Preconditions: headline at or above 3.0% y/y. Probability 35–45%, on the basis of the unusually elevated and dispersed consensus estimates found this run plus Thursday’s energy-driven hot PPI.
