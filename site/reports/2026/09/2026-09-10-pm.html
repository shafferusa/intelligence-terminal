Top Stories

Missouri’s secretary of state purges his contempt after the Supreme Court blocks the federal map order

The Court granted a political action committee’s emergency request to stay a district judge’s order, and within hours a defiant standoff ended in compliance.

The Supreme Court today stayed the temporary restraining order that Chief U.S. District Judge Stephen Clark had issued this week requiring Missouri to use its newer, 2025 Republican-favored congressional map. The order came on an emergency application from the redistricting group People Not Politicians and its executive director, Richard von Glahn — the second time this week the Court has ruled against the map’s backers. Justice Kavanaugh, who handles emergency matters from Missouri, referred the request to the full Court rather than ruling alone, as he had Tuesday when he turned down a separate, earlier request from Missouri officials to revive the map (Confirmed-multiple: SCOTUSblog, Missouri Independent, Newsweek, CNN).

With Judge Clark’s order stayed, the only order left standing is the state supreme court’s own ruling that the 2025 map is not in effect — meaning Missouri reverts to its 2022 congressional map for the midterm cycle. That resolved, hours later, the contempt hearing Chief Justice W. Brent Powell had convened against Secretary of State Denny Hoskins for having told election officials to use the 2025 map in defiance of the state court. Hoskins agreed at the hearing to use the 2022 map; Powell said Hoskins had “purged himself of contempt” by complying (Confirmed-multiple: STLPR, ABC17News, KRCU). Hoskins still faces the separate question of whether his earlier conduct — directing officials to use the 2025 map before today’s ruling — draws any further sanction from the state court; that question was not resolved today.

Why it matters A five-week standoff between a state’s own supreme court and its secretary of state, layered on competing state and federal court orders, ended today with the map settled for November — a genuine first in this dispute, not another round of competing orders.
What’s next Whether the Missouri Supreme Court still imposes any sanction on Hoskins for his conduct before today’s compliance, and whether Missouri Republicans, who had threatened impeachment of the court’s justices if Hoskins were held in contempt, pursue that now that he was not.

SCOTUSblog · Missouri Independent · STLPR · ABC17News · KRCU · Newsweek · CNN.

Oil settles above $103 as Iran’s Hormuz threat deepens; stocks fall a fourth session

WTI jumped nearly 8% on the day even as a cooler-than-expected core inflation reading gave the market little comfort.

WTI crude (CL=F) settled at $103.58, up 7.8% on the day from Wednesday’s $96.05 close and roughly 21% above where it traded a week ago, as Iran’s threatened Hormuz “exclusion zone” — reported this morning, not yet formally activated as of this writing — continued to price into the futures market rather than fade. Brent crude, not priced directly by this report, was reported by multiple outlets trading in the same range, its highest since before the current round of Gulf strikes began. The S&P 500 fell 0.58% to 7,591.70, the Nasdaq fell 0.65% to 26,081.72, the Dow fell 0.60% (316.56 points) to 52,064.10 and the Russell 2000 fell 1.04% — a fourth consecutive losing session, the longest streak this report has tracked since early summer.

August producer prices complicated the picture rather than clarifying it. The Producer Price Index for final demand rose 0.4% month-over-month, matching consensus, but the core reading (ex food and energy) rose only 0.2%, undershooting the 0.3% consensus. Energy prices drove more than three-quarters of the headline increase, up 4.2% on the month, with diesel alone jumping 24.1%. Year-over-year, headline PPI ran 5.4% and core 4.6%. Market narrative The 10-year Treasury yield rose 12 basis points to 4.95% on today’s par curve anyway — a cooler core print would ordinarily ease yields, and this report cannot confirm why it did not, though the tape’s attention to oil’s advance and the energy-driven headline number, rather than the softer core figure, is one plausible read among several. Initial jobless claims for the week ended September 5 came in at 206,000, essentially in line with the 205,000 consensus and down from a prior week revised up to 207,000; continuing claims ran 1.774 million. A $22 billion 29-year-11-month Treasury bond reopening drew a 5.308% high yield and a 2.61 bid-to-cover ratio — indirect bidders (mostly foreign and international accounts) took 79.4% of the issue and direct bidders 18.3%, a solid, unremarkable result showing no sign of buyer's strike at the long end.

Why it matters A fifth of the world’s seaborne oil passes through the strait Iran is now threatening to control, and the market is pricing real, rising odds of disruption rather than treating the threat as noise.
What’s next Whether Iran takes any concrete step to enforce the threatened zone, and Friday’s August CPI report, which Fed Governor Waller has called decisive for his own vote at next week’s meeting.

Reviews of Apple’s iPhone Duo turn notably more positive, with demand reportedly outrunning supply

Bloomberg’s hands-on account called it Apple’s most exciting phone since the iPhone X; other outlets report Apple expects to sell every unit it can build for six months.

A day after Tuesday’s genuinely mixed early reactions to Apple’s first foldable phone, hands-on coverage published today read more favorably. Bloomberg News, after time with the device at Apple Park, said the iPhone Duo “already feels like the most exciting new iPhone since the iPhone X in 2017,” while the Irish Times and other outlets continued to flag the $1,999 starting price as the most expensive iPhone to date. Separate reporting on demand — not an Apple statement — says the company expects to sell as many units as it can manufacture through the first six months of availability, a stronger demand signal than the “extremely limited” early-supply framing this report carried this morning.

Apple shares rose 3.56% today, the clearest outperformer among mega-cap technology names on a broadly negative day for the sector — consistent with, though not proof of, the market treating today’s more positive coverage as informative. Preorders remain scheduled for October 16, with shipping beginning October 23.

AeroVironment’s record quarter and a first-of-its-kind directed-energy contract drive the session’s biggest single-stock gain

Record backlog and a nearly $465 million Army contract for a laser-weapon system briefly pushed shares up as much as 10% before they settled for a smaller, still-solid gain.

AeroVironment reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of $480.5 million, a record for the company, up 5.7% year-over-year and roughly 5% above analyst consensus. Funded backlog climbed 37% year-over-year to a record $1.5 billion, with combined funded and unfunded backlog near $2.8 billion. The quarter’s contract wins included a $117 million P550 long-range reconnaissance-drone award, a $30 million Puma drone order for Germany, a $51 million Switchblade 600 munition award, and — the standout — a nearly $465 million US Army contract for its LOCUST directed-energy system, which the company described as the US military’s first production contract for a directed-energy weapon.

Shares traded as much as roughly 10% higher intraday, per secondary reporting, before settling up 4.45% at the close — the largest single-stock gain on this report’s watchlists today. AeroVironment remains down sharply for the year (roughly 40% by one secondary account) despite today’s move, and closed well below its own 52-week high.

Why it matters A production-scale contract for a directed-energy weapon, if the “first of its kind” characterization holds up, marks a genuine capability step for a defense-technology category that has mostly stayed in testing and demonstration contracts until now.

JPMorgan upgrades Meta on its new AI agent, but shares slip anyway with the broader market

The bank cited Muse as evidence of a stronger competitive position in consumer AI — a vote of confidence that today’s broad selloff outweighed.

JPMorgan upgraded Meta to overweight today, citing Tuesday’s launch of Muse — Meta’s personal AI agent — as evidence the company is better positioned competitively in consumer AI than the market had priced. That follows Mizuho and KeyBanc turning more bullish on Meta earlier this week, KeyBanc setting a $780 price target. Despite the upgrade, Meta shares fell 1.42% today, in line with the broader technology-sector decline rather than diverging on the news — a reminder that an analyst upgrade competes with the day’s macro tape, not just with a stock’s own fundamentals.
