Trump Reverses on Ukraine, Tells the UN He’ll Deal With Iran or “Annihilate” It — Then Talks to Tehran Anyway

Tuesday, September 22, 2026 · 22 min read

On one UN General Assembly day, Trump met Zelensky and said Ukraine could win back all its territory, threatened Iran from the podium and then let Kushner and Witkoff talk to its officials anyway, signed an Arctic pact with Greenland and Denmark, and watched bank stocks slide as chipmakers rallied and oil fell on the same diplomatic optimism.

The Brief

If you read nothing else

  • The world. Trump met Zelensky in New York and reversed his own prior position, saying Ukraine can win back all its territory; the two sides agreed to pursue a truce limited to energy-infrastructure strikes, with technical talks to follow in Saudi Arabia. Hours earlier at the UN podium, Trump told Iran he would either cut a deal or “annihilate” it — then US officials met Iranian counterparts anyway.
  • Markets. Chipmakers pushed the Nasdaq toward a record on Micron and Nvidia strength; banks sold off on flat-curve margin worries; oil fell hardest in weeks, loosely coinciding with the Trump-Iran meeting; the 10-year Treasury sits at 4.96%.
  • The thread. A single UN General Assembly day produced three separate diplomatic moves — on Ukraine, Iran and Greenland — each cutting in a different direction, with markets reading the Iran contact as the most tradeable signal.
  • Biggest risk. Trump’s rhetorical shift on Ukraine’s territory could complicate the narrower energy-truce he and Zelensky just agreed to pursue, if Moscow reads it as the US abandoning any settlement built on current lines; no Russian reaction yet.
  • Watch tomorrow. Xi arrives in Washington for a state visit running through Thursday, and a federal judge hears CNN, MS NOW and Politico’s press-ban suit by videoconference.

Top Stories

Trump reverses on Ukraine’s territory after meeting Zelensky; both sides pursue an energy-strike truce

“Ukraine, with the support of the European Union, is in a position to fight and WIN all of Ukraine back,” Trump posted after the two met in New York — a public about-face from his prior line that Kyiv would need to cede territory.

Trump and Zelensky met Tuesday on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, their first sit-down since the new Russia sanctions law Trump signed last week. The stated agenda was narrower than Trump’s post-meeting rhetoric: a possible truce limited to energy-infrastructure strikes, after weeks of Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries and diesel production that a US official told CNBC have left Russia having “lost control” of parts of its oil-processing capacity — an official characterization, not an independently verified figure. A White House readout says the two leaders agreed to pursue that partial ceasefire, with technical teams from both sides due to meet in Saudi Arabia in the coming days.

Zelensky called the meeting “positive and productive,” said the two discussed “potential de-escalatory steps,” and told reporters he is “ready anytime” for a three-way meeting with Trump and Putin. Trump’s Truth Social post afterward went well beyond an energy truce, framing Ukraine as capable of retaking all its territory “in its original form” — a reversal of his own repeated prior position that a settlement would require Kyiv to give up land. An administration official described the intent as applying “maximum public pressure” on Moscow. Russia has not yet responded to either the meeting or the post.

Why it matters A truce covering only energy infrastructure is a narrower, more achievable step than a ceasefire on the front line, and both sides have incentive to protect infrastructure through the winter. But Trump’s rhetorical shift on territory sits awkwardly next to it: if Moscow reads the post as Washington abandoning any settlement built on the current line of contact, it could harden Russia’s position on the narrower energy talks before they start.
What’s next Technical-level talks in Saudi Arabia, no date yet confirmed. Watch for a Kremlin response to both the meeting and Trump’s territorial framing.

White House readout (primary) · Trump’s Truth Social post (primary) · Zelensky’s remarks (primary, via wire) · NBC News, PBS NewsHour, CNBC, National Review.

Trump tells the UN he’ll deal with Iran or “annihilate” it — then Kushner and Witkoff meet Iranian officials anyway

Iran’s delegation walked out roughly ten minutes into the speech; hours later, Trump told reporters US and Iranian officials had just held a three-hour meeting he called “very good.”

Addressing the UN General Assembly’s 81st session, Trump said he faces a “big decision” on Iran — a deal, or he will “annihilate the Islamic Republic” and “drive them into hell with no chance of survival.” He predicted any deal is unlikely before the November midterms but “right after,” and separately said the US would “encourage” AI development rather than “rein it in.” Iranian President Pezeshkian and most of his delegation left the chamber within minutes of the speech starting; Iran’s foreign ministry separately protested US visa denials to members of the delegation as “unprecedented and hostile.”

Single-reliable-source Despite the walkout, Trump told reporters afterward that senior aides Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff held a roughly three-hour meeting with Iranian officials at UN headquarters, which he called “a very good meeting.” That characterization is his own; no Iranian official has confirmed the meeting or its content on the record. World-leader reaction to the speech itself was muted: French President Macron, in his final UNGA address, urged the world not to “yield” to war, division, climate change and AI, without naming Trump.

Why it matters A public ultimatum and a private meeting on the same day is a familiar pattern in this administration’s Iran diplomacy, but the gap between Trump’s framing of the meeting and any independent confirmation is exactly the kind of claim this outlet flags rather than adopts.

Trump’s UNGA remarks and post-speech comments (primary, via wire) · Iran’s foreign ministry statement (primary) · CNN, ABC News, CBS News, NBC News, Axios, OPB, Iran International.

Trump signs a Greenland-Denmark Arctic security pact

The agreement expands US military access and plans for “two very major” new bases, but stops short of the annexation Trump has floated in the past — Greenland stays Danish.

Trump signed a trilateral agreement with Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen and Greenland Premier Jens-Frederik Nielsen on the UNGA sidelines Tuesday, expanding US military access to Greenland. The deal has no expiration date, bars non-NATO countries from basing there without all three parties’ agreement, and would survive Greenland becoming independent of Denmark. Frederiksen called it a deal that can “last forever”; Nielsen called it “win-win-win.” It follows months of tension after Trump repeatedly suggested the US should own the territory outright.

Signing ceremony (primary, via wire) · AP, Washington Post, CBS News, Al Jazeera, CNBC.

EU extends Russia sanctions through 2029 but drops two oligarchs; Kyiv calls it “shameful”

A three-year renewal — unusually long — came paired with the delisting of Mikhail Fridman and Alisher Usmanov, and Ukraine’s foreign minister said it hands Moscow a propaganda win.

EU ambassadors reached a compromise Tuesday extending sanctions on roughly 3,000 remaining individuals and entities for a full three years, rather than the usual six-to-twelve-month renewal cycle, while removing Fridman and Usmanov from the sanctions list. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha publicly criticized the delistings as “shameful and unjustifiable,” arguing they show EU division and hand Russia a public-relations win in the same week Trump is publicly pressuring Moscow.

EU Council decision (primary, via wire) · Sybiha’s statement (primary) · Kyiv Post, Reuters/Yahoo.

Trump and Macron pledge joint action on Hormuz and energy-infrastructure strikes

Meeting separately in New York, the two leaders said they would work together to protect Hormuz shipping and push a moratorium on energy-infrastructure strikes tied to the Ukraine war.

Single-reliable-source Macron said after meeting Trump that the two “decided to act together” to ease energy-market tensions, protect Middle East critical infrastructure, and safeguard freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, alongside a push for a moratorium on energy-infrastructure strikes in the Ukraine war. Trump credited Macron with helping “settle seven wars.” Most coverage of this meeting traces to wire-service re-syndication rather than an original Reuters or AP report this outlet could independently locate; treat the specifics as reported but not yet cross-confirmed by a Tier-1 wire.

Macron’s remarks (primary, via wire syndication) · Tribune India (ANI), TAG24.

The Pentagon’s own fix for the Minab strike becomes public, without the full review

Bloomberg reports CENTCOM has made “dozens of upgrades” to its AI-assisted targeting system after the strike on an Iranian school; the internal report Congress worries may stay classified is still not out.

Single-reliable-source Bloomberg reported Tuesday that CENTCOM has changed its lethal-targeting workflow following the Minab, Iran school strike this outlet has tracked since last week: refined procedures for refreshing and vetting planned targets, new open-source feeds to better track civilian movement near target sites, and what CENTCOM describes as dozens of upgrades to the Maven Smart System, the military’s AI-assisted targeting tool. This is the first public detail on what the internal review actually changed, though it comes from CENTCOM’s own characterization via a single outlet, not the review itself, which remains unreleased amid reported concern in Congress and the Pentagon that its findings could be classified.

Alibaba unveils a new AI chip two days before the Trump-Xi summit

Xi arrives in Washington Wednesday for a state visit running through Thursday; Alibaba timed what it calls China’s most powerful AI chip to land just ahead of it.

Alibaba announced Tuesday what it describes as China’s most powerful AI chip, alongside new large-model plans, days after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and other US AI executives renewed warnings about China’s AI trajectory. The announcement lands just ahead of Xi’s state visit to Washington, which runs Wednesday through Thursday — arrival and a Joint Base Andrews reception Wednesday, the main bilateral meeting, a Rose Garden ceremony and a state dinner Thursday. Tech CEOs including Bezos, Pichai, Altman, Cook and Huang are expected at the dinner. China has separately signaled the summit could be disrupted over a pending $14 billion Taiwan arms package.

What Changed Today

CENTCOM’s Minab review, from single-sourced and internal to partly public. This morning’s edition carried anonymous-official accounts that CENTCOM had completed an internal investigation into the Minab school strike, with Congress and the Pentagon reportedly worried its findings would be classified rather than released. This afternoon, Bloomberg reported CENTCOM’s own account of what it changed — refreshed target-vetting procedures, new open-source tracking of civilian movement, and dozens of upgrades to the Maven Smart System targeting tool. That is CENTCOM describing its own fix, not the review itself, which still has not been released. Business

Viking Therapeutics jumped roughly 30% after announcing positive topline results from a maintenance-phase study of its GLP-1/GIP agonist VK2735: patients retained up to 90% of induction-phase weight loss on monthly dosing and up to 97% on every-other-week dosing, with tolerability during maintenance similar to placebo. The 21-week induction phase itself had produced roughly 16–19% weight loss versus essentially none on placebo. The company says the results support starting a Phase III trial by year-end; broader obesity-drug peers did not follow the move.

AutoZone beat on profit and missed on revenue in its fiscal fourth quarter: EPS of $56.05 against a $54.54 consensus, but revenue of $6.59 billion fell short of the roughly $6.69 billion Street estimate. Same-store sales rose 2.7% (domestic +1.6%, international +10.7%); full-year profit was $2.57 billion on $20.34 billion revenue. Results included a 145-basis-point tariff-refund benefit and a 105-basis-point non-cash LIFO benefit, both one-time in nature. Shares gained on the call as management framed the results around market-share gains.

What Moved Markets

Futures were essentially flat overnight before chip names took over the tape. Semiconductors led from the open — Micron and Nvidia strength pushed the SMH sector ETF up 1.92% and the Nasdaq 100 toward its first record close since June — while bank shares sold off through the session on renewed flat-curve, margin-pressure worries (the 2s10s spread sits at a thin +25bp): JPMorgan fell 3.42%, Bank of America 3.04%, Goldman Sachs 1.03%, dragging XLF down 1.97%, the worst-performing sector. Market narrative Coverage widely tied the midday leg of the chip rally, and a matching drop in crude, to Trump’s post-UNGA description of the US-Iran sideline meeting as “very good” — the two coincided in time, but no outlet has documented a mechanical transmission from that remark to either move, so treat it as a narrative investors reached for, not a confirmed catalyst.

Crude fell sharply — USO closed down 2.75% and outside reporting puts WTI in the low $90s, down roughly 4-5% from Monday’s $95.59 close. Yahoo’s CL=F futures contract showed an implausible −6.17% for a sixth consecutive session, a pattern this outlet has flagged repeatedly as a likely contract-roll artifact rather than a real price; USO and outside reporting are used as the crude proxy instead. Unexplained Natural gas was the day’s standout mover in the other direction, up 11.6% to $3.165, continuing a rise that was already visible in this morning’s premarket data (+5.68%); this outlet found no single confirmed catalyst, though cold-weather demand and a below-average storage build reported last week are plausible contributors, not confirmed drivers.

The 10-year Treasury closed at 4.96%, essentially unchanged on the day; the 2-year fell to 4.71%, steepening 2s10s to +25bp from Monday’s +20bp. Gold rose 0.42% and the dollar index firmed slightly to 100.54. The VIX fell 4.44% to 14.21, an EOD close, with the term structure still in normal contango (VIX9D 12.13, VIX3M 17.61) — a market pricing calm despite the day’s geopolitical volume.

Unsealed warrant alleges pay-to-play no-bid contracts under former Mayor Gainey

A search warrant served on the City of Pittsburgh was unsealed Monday. It alleges the DA’s office is investigating whether the Gainey administration steered roughly $2 million in “Stop the Violence” grants and other no-bid contracts to organizations, including SEIU and the Working Families Party, that supported Gainey’s campaigns — and that the city’s no-bid contract rate roughly doubled, from about 10% to about 20%, under his administration. Information in the warrant reportedly came in part from current Councilman Anthony Coghill, former Councilwoman Theresa Kail-Smith and a former council solicitor. No charges have been filed.

Pennsylvania

State Supreme Court keeps a Green Party candidate off the PA-07 ballot

The court affirmed a Commonwealth Court ruling that Andrew Tupone, who submitted signatures for the Lehigh Valley congressional seat without the Green Party’s own endorsement, cannot appear on the November ballot — holding that nominating rights belong to the party organization, not an individual signature-gatherer, regardless of signature count (Tupone submitted 10,586 against a 4,074 requirement).

State Supreme Court order (primary, via reporting) · PoliticsPA, Ballot Access News, City & State PA.

Pennsylvania

Four lifers ask the state Supreme Court to bypass lower courts on felony-murder resentencing

Four people serving mandatory life for second-degree (felony) murder, including one tied to the Allegheny County case that triggered a March ruling opening a path to resentencing, have filed King’s Bench petitions asking the court to act directly after the legislature missed its July 24 deadline to rewrite the underlying statute. Roughly 1,100 people statewide serve these sentences; the court has granted only a handful of the roughly 288 King’s Bench petitions filed since 2021, and there is no mandated timeline for it to act.
