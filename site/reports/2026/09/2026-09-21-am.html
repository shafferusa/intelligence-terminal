Top Stories

Trump-Xi Summit Set for This Week as Beijing Threatens to Cancel Over a Taiwan Arms Sale

Xi’s first White House visit in a decade comes with a proposed US-China “AI dialogue” on the table — but a pending $14 billion Taiwan arms package could still upend it before Thursday’s state dinner.

Xi Jinping is scheduled to arrive in Washington Wednesday for his first visit to the US in a decade: an arrival ceremony at Joint Base Andrews, a White House welcome and a Rose Garden military review, followed Thursday by the main bilateral meeting and a state dinner. The publicly stated agenda spans trade, the countries’ expiring 90-day tariff truce, Taiwan and critical minerals — and now, for the first time, a formal AI-safety framework between the two governments.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng concluded roughly eight hours of talks in New York on Sunday. Bessent proposed a formal US-China channel for notifying each other of national-security-level AI incidents, to be taken up at this week’s summit, and called the talks “very successful”; China’s state Xinhua wire gave only a terse acknowledgment that AI was discussed. Multiple US outlets independently confirm the substance of the proposal with direct quotes from Bessent.

The complication is Taiwan. Beijing has warned it could still cancel or curtail the visit if Trump signs a pending $14 billion arms package for Taiwan — F-16 upgrades, air-defense platforms, anti-ship missiles and coastal surveillance infrastructure — before the two leaders meet. Trump said Friday he has “not made a decision” on the package after hearing Xi’s objections directly. Single-cluster source A tech-executive guest list for Thursday’s state dinner — reportedly including Bezos, Pichai, Altman, Cook, Musk and Huang — comes from one cluster of aggregated reporting this outlet has not independently confirmed against a primary White House source; treat it as reported, not settled. A separate claim from a single lower-reliability outlet that Xi had already arrived in Washington today does not hold up against the consistently reported Wednesday arrival across major outlets and is not repeated here as fact.

Why it matters This is the week’s central event for both markets and geopolitics at once: a formal government-to-government AI-safety mechanism would be a first, and the trade/Taiwan stakes are directly market-moving for chip and China-exposed names.
What’s next Trump is expected to address the UN General Assembly Tuesday or Wednesday; watch for any signing notification on the Taiwan package before Thursday's meeting.

Trump Calls Off Planned Strikes on the Houthis After a Disputed Omani Stand-Down Claim

Saudi Arabia’s crown prince pushed hard for direct US strikes on Yemen; Trump backed off at the last minute, and a Houthi official says a Muscat channel produced a pledge to spare US and Israeli shipping — a claim with no US or Saudi confirmation.

After Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman called Trump twice over the weekend urging direct US strikes on Houthi forces, Trump cut short his stay at Camp David — then called off the large-scale strikes that had been under consideration. Single source A senior Houthi political-bureau official, Mohamed al-Bukhaiti, told regional outlets that US and Houthi representatives met at the US embassy in Muscat, Oman, and that the Houthis “confirmed we are not targeting the United States, or any other nation, in the [Bab el-Mandeb] strait, with the sole exception of the Saudi enemy.” Neither the US government nor Saudi Arabia has confirmed any such meeting or pledge; the claim originates entirely with a combatant with an obvious interest in how it is portrayed. Houthi-affiliated media separately claim roughly 300 Saudi air raids on Yemen over five days with child casualties — figures this report is not adopting as fact, since they too come only from one side of an active war.

Separately, Trump is scheduled to meet the leaders of Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman on Tuesday, on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, to discuss a postwar plan for Iran. As of Monday morning, Trump has not announced the “big decision” on Iran he referenced in last week’s Axios interview about whether to “annihilate” the regime.

Why it matters A real Omani channel would be a genuine off-ramp from direct US involvement in the Yemen war; right now it is a claim from one interested party, not an established fact.
What’s next Tuesday’s Gulf-leaders meeting is the next concrete test of whether the stand-down, if real, holds.

Houthi official statement via Times of Israel, Euronews, The Media Line, Ynet (Single-reliable-source, Preliminary) · Axios, CNN, NPR on the Tuesday Gulf-leaders meeting (Confirmed-multiple).

Russia’s Tightly Managed Election Concludes as Leaked Documents Allege Systemic Fraud Tooling

United Russia’s landslide came with 56.66% turnout by the government’s own count; separately, leaked technical documents describe software built to alter ballots and edit electoral rolls.

Russia’s Central Election Commission reported final turnout of 56.66% for the three-day vote that concluded Sunday, with United Russia winning 57.36% of the vote (258 of 450 Duma seats), the Communist Party 13.82% (62 seats) and the LDPR 9.06% (41 seats). Those figures are the government’s own official count, not independently audited, and are reported here as such.

Single source Separately, the investigative outlet Meduza published what it says are leaked internal technical documents describing Russia’s “Vybory 2.0” election-management system, including tools to alter ballots, edit electoral rolls and upload unverified electronic-voting results. Meduza is a credible Russian-exile investigative outlet, but this report has found no second outlet independently corroborating the specific documents.

Why it matters This directly bears on whether Russia sees a post-election mobilization wave, an open question this report has been tracking; no mobilization signal has appeared yet.

Russia’s Central Election Commission (primary, government-reported) · Ukrainska Pravda, Time, Freedom House (turnout/seats, Confirmed-multiple) · Meduza (fraud-tooling claim, Single-reliable-source).

A Record Drone Barrage on Moscow Draws a Deadly Russian Strike on Zaporizhzhia

Ukraine says it launched roughly 1,000 drones at Moscow timed to the election weekend; Russia’s declared retaliation killed two people and destroyed a university building.

President Zelensky said Russian drones struck Zaporizhzhia late Sunday, hitting apartment blocks, destroying a university building and hitting a shopping mall; he reported two people killed and five wounded. Russia launched 172 drones at eight Ukrainian cities in what Moscow and state media framed explicitly as retaliation for a roughly 1,000-drone Ukrainian barrage on Moscow over the weekend, timed to coincide with Russia’s Duma election. The casualty figures come from Ukrainian officials only and have not been cross-checked against a Russian-side account.

What’s next No negotiation movement has been reported; watch for further tit-for-tat strikes on both sides.

AP wire, widely syndicated, citing Ukrainian officials and Zelensky directly (Confirmed-multiple).

The White House Bars CNN From Its Press-Pool Rotation as a Legal Fight Builds

Days after banning CNN, MS NOW and Politico from White House grounds, the administration broke decades of precedent Monday by pulling CNN from its scheduled turn as the press corps’ pool-camera representative.

Following Friday’s announced ban on CNN, MS NOW (formerly MSNBC) and Politico from White House grounds and Saturday’s enforcement of it, the White House on Monday removed CNN from its scheduled rotation as television pool representative for the full press corps — a break with decades of precedent that predates this administration. CNN and Politico are reported to be preparing First Amendment litigation, and NBC News describes “mounting legal backlash” more broadly. This report notes CNN’s own coverage of its own treatment carries an evident conflict of interest, though the pool-rotation removal itself is a discrete, independently verifiable administrative action confirmed by multiple outlets.

CNN Business, NBC News, NPR, ABC News (Confirmed-multiple).

An ICE Officer Shoots a Venezuelan Man During an Austin Vehicle Pursuit

Wilber Rafael Garces Perez, a 28-year-old DoorDash driver, was wounded in the torso; Austin’s mayor is calling for city police to help investigate.

An ICE officer shot and wounded Wilber Rafael Garces Perez, 28, in North Austin Sunday afternoon after a brief foot pursuit connected to a vehicle-pursuit call; he is in serious but stable condition. Texas state troopers arrived only after the shooting, and Austin police were not involved in the shooting itself. Austin Mayor Kirk Watson has called for city police involvement in the investigation for independent oversight, and protesters gathered at the scene into the evening.

Texas DPS and Mayor Watson, via Al Jazeera, Texas Tribune, KUT, CNN, NPR, Houston Public Media (Confirmed-multiple).

A Zero-Click Flaw in AI Coding Agents Leaves Two of Four Major Tools Unpatched

Security researchers say “Plugin4Shell” can defeat commit-pinning in AI coding assistants and inherit a developer’s own credentials; Anthropic and OpenAI have patched, Microsoft and Google reportedly have not.

Security research firm AIR Security disclosed a supply-chain flaw affecting the plugin and extension mechanisms in Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini CLI. An attacker who controls a plugin’s upstream repository can defeat commit-pinning verification, so a developer’s “pinned” plugin install silently resolves to malicious code instead — inheriting that developer’s local credentials, SSH keys and any cloud or production access on the machine. The researcher reports finding no evidence of exploitation in the wild. Anthropic patched in Claude Code 2.1.179 and OpenAI in Codex 0.146.0; Microsoft has not patched Copilot, and Google is reported to have retired Gemini CLI without fixing it. Single primary source This account rests on one discloser (AIR Security), corroborated only by trade-press syndication of the same disclosure — the “no exploitation found” claim and the two vendors’ unpatched status are reported, not independently confirmed here.

Why it matters These tools increasingly hold write access to developer machines and cloud credentials; a supply-chain flaw in their plugin systems is a direct new risk category, arriving days after last week’s Cisco, Chrome and record Patch Tuesday cluster.

AIR Security disclosure (primary researcher) · CyberSecurityNews, GBHackers, aicybr.com (secondary syndication of the same disclosure).
