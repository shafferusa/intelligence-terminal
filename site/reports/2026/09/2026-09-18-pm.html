Top Stories

Trump bars CNN, MSNBC and Politico from the White House

Calling their coverage “FICTION and LIES,” Trump said Friday he is banning three news organizations effective immediately — the sharpest move yet in a running conflict between the administration and the press corps that covers it.

In a Friday afternoon Truth Social post, Trump said he is banning CNN, MS NOW (the rebranded MSNBC) and Politico from the White House, calling their reporting “FAKE NEWS” and warning other outlets “would follow.” He singled out MS NOW’s rebrand, saying it reflected “a lack of viewership and credibility,” and separately criticized the roughly $8 million the federal government pays for Politico Pro subscriptions, calling that spending “illegal and ridiculous.”

As of Friday afternoon there was no confirmation the outlets’ White House access had actually been revoked in practice; CNN said its staff remained in their workspace. Bruce D. Brown, president of the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, called the move “flatly unconstitutional” and “textbook viewpoint discrimination.” A federal judge ruled against the administration on a related press-access dispute last year, after the Associated Press sued over being excluded from White House events following its refusal to adopt the administration’s preferred “Gulf of America” terminology.

Why it matters A sitting president publicly announcing the exclusion of named news organizations from the White House, on the stated basis of their coverage, raises First Amendment and press-access questions with a live legal precedent already on the books.
What’s next Watch for whether access badges are actually revoked, and whether any of the three outlets files suit citing the AP precedent.

Trump’s Truth Social post (primary) · Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press statement (primary) · CNBC, CNN, NBC News, Washington Post, Time, NPR, Al Jazeera.

Buffett steps down as Berkshire Hathaway’s chairman after six decades

Warren Buffett, 96, becomes chairman emeritus; his son Howard Buffett, a Berkshire director for 33 years, takes the chair.

Berkshire Hathaway announced Friday that Warren Buffett is stepping down as chairman effective immediately. Buffett had already handed the chief executive role to Greg Abel in 2025; in his accompanying shareholder letter he wrote that “Father Time always wins” and said Abel had “exceeded” his expectations in the CEO seat. Berkshire’s Class B shares fell roughly 1% on the news — a stock up only about 1% year-to-date against the S&P 500’s 11%-plus gain, a lag some coverage attributes in part to rising oil prices and a broader investor preference for higher-growth names rather than to today’s announcement specifically.

Why it matters Closes a 60-year chapter at one of the most closely watched companies in American finance and completes a succession Buffett has been telegraphing for years.

Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter (primary) · CNBC, CNN, NPR, Axios, Washington Post, NBC News.

Fed’s Bowman says the central bank’s own supervisors missed the warning signs before SVB failed

An independent review commissioned by Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman finds staff “knew, or should have known” about Silicon Valley Bank’s vulnerabilities more than a year before its 2023 collapse.

Speaking at a London luncheon Friday afternoon, Bowman unveiled an independent review, conducted by Starling Advisory Group, into the Fed’s supervisory failures ahead of SVB’s collapse. The review found supervisory staff “knew, or should have known” about the bank’s vulnerabilities as early as March 2022 but failed to act “prompt[ly] and decisive[ly],” citing a “long-standing culture of risk aversion” inside the Fed’s supervisory arm. It found that regulatory tailoring adopted during the first Trump administration played no role in the failure, clearing former Vice Chair Randal Quarles of responsibility for it. In a companion speech, Bowman said a revamped bank stress-testing framework — replacing what she called an “opaque and unnecessarily unpredictable” regime — will be finalized “in the coming weeks.”

Why it matters A sitting Fed vice chair publicly faulting her own agency’s supervisors, rather than deregulation, for a bank failure is a notable break from the administration’s deregulatory framing of bank oversight, and a new stress-test regime would directly affect how much capital large banks must hold.

Fed Vice Chair Bowman’s speech texts (primary, via federalreserve.gov) · CNN, American Banker, ABA Banking Journal, Banking Dive.

A second reported Hormuz tanker strike as South Korea declines to send troops for the Iran war

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claims it struck and detained a Togo-flagged tanker; separately, a UKMTO-reported vessel fire near Oman appears to be a distinct, second incident the same day.

UKMTO reported a tanker hit by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz early Friday, causing a fire that was extinguished with the crew reported safe — described in some reporting as a second Hormuz incident within hours of an earlier one. Separately, Iran’s IRGC Navy publicly claimed it struck and detained a Togo-flagged tanker named Trend for an alleged unauthorized passage through the strait; UKMTO logged a related security incident roughly 16 nautical miles from Khasab, Oman, but has not confirmed whether the two reports describe the same vessel. Iran’s claim of authorship and the tanker’s identity rest on Iranian state media alone and are unconfirmed by any other government.

South Korean President Lee Jae Myung said at a Friday press conference that South Korea “will not” send troops for the Iran war or to the Strait of Hormuz, the clearest on-record response yet to Trump’s repeated personal requests for Korean support; Lee left open a narrower role protecting only South Korea’s own merchant shipping. It lands days before Trump is expected to meet Gulf leaders around next week’s UN General Assembly session — a meeting officials have cited as a likely moment of clarity on the “big decision” on further military action Trump described in Thursday’s Axios interview.

What’s next Watch for whether Trump's Gulf-leaders meeting around the UNGA produces any public signal on his stated decision, and whether any government other than Iran confirms today’s tanker claims.

UKMTO statements (primary) · IRGC/Iranian state media (primary, single-party, disputed) · President Lee’s press conference (primary) · Korea Herald, Korea JoongAng Daily, Al Jazeera, Washington Times.

Russia opens a three-day election as fears of a post-vote mobilization wave build

Russia’s State Duma election runs through Sunday; some Ukrainian and European officials expect a new conscription wave once it ends, a claim the Kremlin flatly denies.

Russia’s 2026 parliamentary election began Friday and runs through Sunday, Sept. 20. Bloomberg and other outlets report that Russians — including, per that reporting, some Kremlin and recruitment-center officials — increasingly fear a mass mobilization once the vote concludes; one unnamed European security official cited a figure of up to 300,000 additional conscripts by year-end. Putin has publicly dismissed the mobilization warnings as “complete nonsense,” and Zelensky has said he expects any call-up to begin in late September or early October — a claim from the opposing side of the war, not an independently confirmed fact. Separately, Russia struck Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia with a reported wave of roughly 93 drones that destroyed a shopping complex in a large fire, and hit the grounds of a hospital in Kherson’s Korabelny district with an explosive dropped from a drone; no casualties were reported at the hospital site. Ukraine’s General Staff also says Russia has begun substituting less-accurate modified training missiles for its more precise ballistic missiles at scale — a claim, like Ukraine’s reported daily Russian-casualty figures, that comes from a combatant and could not be independently verified.

Why it matters A confirmed new mobilization wave would be a significant escalation in Russian force generation for the Ukraine war; the report is logging an explicit forecast below on whether one is announced within two weeks of the election’s close.

Bloomberg, Moscow Times, Russia Matters, Ukraine General Staff statements (combatant-sourced) · Mezha.net, Ukrinform.

Voting-rights groups sue over armed federal agents at polling places as early voting begins

A nine-organization coalition led by the NAACP argues the administration’s suggestions that armed ICE officers could appear near polling sites amount to illegal voter intimidation, as a separate NPR report surfaces a DHS document tying a non-English-language voting list to a DOJ prosecution checklist.

A coalition including the NAACP, Mi Familia en Acción, OCA-Asian Pacific American Advocates and four national unions filed a federal suit Friday in the U.S. District Court for D.C., arguing that repeated administration suggestions that armed federal or ICE officers could appear at or near polling sites during the midterms constitute illegal voter intimidation under the 1965 Voting Rights Act. Separately, NPR reports it obtained an internal DHS email, dated last December, in which a border-policy official sent a 78-page list of counties and states required to provide non-English election materials to a DHS undersecretary and the official leading the administration’s noncitizen-voting review, with a DOJ voter-prosecution “criminal checklist” attached; the purpose of pairing the two documents is unclear given heavy redaction. DHS told NPR on the record that it “is not planning operations targeting polling locations.”

Both stories land the same day early voting opened in Virginia, South Dakota and Minnesota — the first states to begin balloting for the Nov. 3 midterms.

Why it matters Two distinct legal and reporting threads converging on the same question — whether federal enforcement activity around the midterms could chill turnout in specific communities — arriving as voting itself begins.

Coalition complaint via AALDEF (primary) · DHS statement to NPR (primary) · NPR (exclusive reporting) · MS NOW.

US growth data cools: industrial output stalls, a first manufacturing decline in seven months

The Conference Board’s Leading Economic Index fell for the first time since March, adding a second soft print to a day that otherwise carried little fresh economic news.

Industrial production was flat in August (0.0%), missing a consensus near +0.2% to +0.3% and down from July’s +0.2% gain; manufacturing output alone fell 0.3%, its first monthly decline after seven straight increases, with durable goods down 0.5% and nondurables flat. Utilities output rose 1.8% on electricity demand, and computer/electronic-products and electrical-equipment utilization both rose — a detail consistent with continued AI-infrastructure capital spending even as the broader factory sector cooled. Capacity utilization held at 76.3%, matching both the July level and consensus, still 3.1 points below its 1972–2025 long-run average. Separately, the Conference Board’s Leading Economic Index fell 0.1% to 99.5 in August, its first decline since March, with four of ten components negative and consumer expectations the largest drag; the Board still projects 1.9% GDP growth for 2026 but trimmed its 2027 outlook to 1.8% from 1.9%.

Why it matters Two independent gauges pointing the same direction — a factory-sector pause and a broader leading indicator turning down for the first time in five months — is a modest but genuine cooling signal, landing two days after the Fed’s hawkish rate hike.

Federal Reserve G.17 industrial production release (primary) · Conference Board release (primary) · FRED, Trading Economics.

Poland narrows talks on a potential permanent US Army base to its west

A day after Trump credited “great progress” toward a base to President Nawrocki, a Polish deputy defense minister says any site will “definitely” be in western Poland.

Polish Deputy Defense Minister Magdalena Sobkowiak-Czarnecka said Friday any US Army base under discussion would “definitely” be located in western Poland — the first concrete detail beyond Trump’s Thursday Truth Social post citing progress toward the arrangement. No site, troop count, or finalized agreement has been announced. The disclosure follows this week’s warning from Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk that Russia may be preparing drone or rocket strikes on Poland or other NATO members, presented as “accidental,” to test the alliance’s Article 5 resolve; Russia’s foreign ministry called the warning “contrived.”

Polish Deputy Defense Ministry statement (primary) · Trump’s Truth Social post (primary) · RFE/RL, Washington Times, TimesLive.

Java Sea ferry search shifts to recovery as divers enter the wreck

Five days after the Virgo Transport 8 capsized, Indonesian navy divers entered the hull for what appears to be the first time and recovered three bodies.

Indonesia’s national search-and-rescue agency began a salvage operation Friday, five days after the ferry capsized with 243 aboard, and divers entered the wreck to recover three bodies — the first confirmed physical entry into the hull since the sinking. That brings the confirmed toll to nine dead against roughly 126 still missing; fast currents, water pressure and the vessel’s design continue to hamper access.

Indonesian search-and-rescue authorities (primary, via wire) · Malay Mail, US News.

Sudan gold-mine collapse kills dozens in RSF-held territory

Two attributed sources give conflicting death tolls for a mine collapse in West Kordofan — a disaster, not a strike, in a region the war has left without independent access.

Disputed toll The Sudanese Doctors Network reported at least 67 dead in a gold-mine collapse at Al-Zaraa, West Kordofan, an area under Rapid Support Forces control; an anonymous RSF source cited by AFP put the toll at least 82. Neither figure could be independently verified given the absence of independent access to the area, itself a consequence of Sudan’s ongoing civil war, now in its fourth year amid famine conditions the World Food Programme says affect 24.6 million people.

Sudanese Doctors Network (primary, single-source) · anonymous RSF source via AFP (primary, single-source, disputed toll).
