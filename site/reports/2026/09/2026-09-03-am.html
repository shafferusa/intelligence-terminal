Top Stories

Iran widens the Gulf war overnight, striking Kuwait and, apparently for the first time, the UAE

Kuwait’s military confirms it intercepted an incoming barrage; the reported strike on a UAE air base is so far sourced mainly to Iran’s own account.

Iran’s military said early Thursday it fired missiles and drones at US-linked facilities in Kuwait and the United Arab Emirates, in what would be the widest single round yet of the six-month confrontation over the Strait of Hormuz. Kuwait’s army said its air defenses engaged incoming projectiles near a military camp, a power station and a desalination plant close to Ahmed al-Jaber air base; Kuwaiti officials have not reported casualties. Qatar’s Foreign Ministry issued a pointed condemnation of the Kuwait strike Wednesday, calling it a violation of Kuwaiti sovereignty and holding Iran “legally responsible” — a notable break from Doha’s usual mediator posture in this standoff.

Unverified The reported strike on the UAE’s Al Minhad air base is so far sourced mainly to Iranian state claims echoed by regional wires; neither the UAE government nor US CENTCOM had confirmed an attack on Emirati soil as of this writing. This report treats it as moderate confidence pending that confirmation.

The attack follows Tuesday’s Iranian strikes on Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait and Iraq, which were themselves retaliation for the weekend’s roughly 100-target US strike inside Iran under Washington’s disclosed “tanker for tanker” policy.

Why it matters Two countries — Kuwait for a second time, and apparently the UAE for the first — have now taken direct fire in a dispute that began over tanker traffic. Oil and gold both jumped overnight even as US equity futures stayed close to flat, a market that has grown used to this pattern of strike-and-shrug.
What’s next Whether Washington or Abu Dhabi responds directly within the next day or two is the swing factor for whether this stays a war of gestures.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard statement, Kuwait’s army statement, Qatar’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs (primary, via wire) · Times of Israel, ABC News, i24NEWS, APA (secondary)

Federal judge blocks Trump’s newest birthright-citizenship order

Judge Deborah Boardman called the order “almost certainly unconstitutional” three months after the Supreme Court struck down its predecessor.

U.S. District Judge Deborah Boardman (D. Md.) issued a preliminary injunction Wednesday blocking the administration’s latest attempt to restrict birthright citizenship — this version targeting children born to alleged members of designated foreign terrorist organizations or employees of certain foreign governments. Boardman, a Biden appointee, wrote the order was “almost certainly unconstitutional” in light of the Supreme Court’s June ruling against the administration’s original Day-One order on the same underlying question.

Why it matters This is the second time in three months a federal court has stopped a version of this policy. The administration has not said whether it will appeal.

CNN, NBC News, WTOP, Washington Times (confirmed-multiple)

Broadcom’s slide was about revenue guidance, not AI chips — last night’s framing needs a correction

The company’s own filing shows Q4 AI-semiconductor guidance was raised to $21.7 billion; the miss was on total company revenue against an even higher bar.

Broadcom’s SEC filing on its third-quarter results, reviewed directly this run, shows AI-semiconductor revenue of $16.7 billion for the quarter just ended (up 221% year over year) — and CEO Hock Tan told investors that figure will “accelerate to $21.7 billion” next quarter, up 236% year over year. That is a raise, not the disappointment this report described in Tuesday’s closing edition. What actually fell short was total fourth-quarter revenue guidance of approximately $34.8 billion (still up 93% year over year) against a roughly $35.03 billion analyst consensus — a narrow miss that appears to be what sent shares down more than 6% after hours, per multiple market reports; the exact settled magnitude was not yet printed as of this writing.

Why it matters This reverses Tuesday night’s framing rather than refining it. See the colophon for the formal correction.

Broadcom Inc. Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1 (primary, SEC EDGAR) · multiple wire reports on the after-hours share move

Uber to cut about 3,300 jobs, its largest round since the pandemic

Roughly 10% of staff and a fifth of management roles go, to help fund more than $10 billion in robotaxi investment.

Uber said Wednesday it will lay off approximately 3,300 employees, about 10% of its workforce and roughly a fifth of management positions, in a restructuring the company frames as funding its push into autonomous-vehicle partnerships rather than raising new capital for it.

CISA adds a critical JFrog Artifactory flaw to its must-patch list

The authentication-bypass bug, under active exploitation since at least Monday, lets attackers mint admin tokens inside software build-and-release pipelines.

CISA added CVE-2026-82329 (CVSS 9.8) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Tuesday, part of a seven-CVE batch that also included ownCloud and Linux Kernel flaws, setting a September 10 federal remediation deadline. watchTowr first reported active exploitation Monday; attackers are using the bug to mint admin tokens and enumerate credentials on unpatched Artifactory servers. No victims have been named.

CISA KEV catalog (primary) · watchTowr research · SC Media

Gaza’s ceasefire framework strains again after a disputed Gaza City raid

Hamas warns the truce “could collapse”; a Gaza security source now disputes Israel’s account of who was actually captured Tuesday.

Hamas warned this week that the ceasefire framework could collapse following Tuesday’s Gaza City raid, in which Israel said it captured the group’s internal-security chief. Disputed A Gaza security source now claims the operation against that “prominent” figure was actually thwarted, and that only a lower-ranking official was taken — a direct contradiction of Israel’s own account that this report has not been able to resolve either way.

Bloomberg, regional wire coverage (disputed on the raid’s outcome)

Pennsylvania’s measles dispute escalates as the CDC’s national count excludes both deaths the state reported

The CDC’s tally shows zero 2026 measles deaths nationally, directly contradicting Pennsylvania’s count of two — and the state’s own coroner disputes the cause.

The Lancaster County coroner’s dispute with Gov. Shapiro’s administration has widened into a three-way disagreement. Coroner Dr. Stephen Diamantoni told the Philadelphia Inquirer an infant’s death the state classified as “measles-associated” was actually caused by a lacerated spleen, not measles, despite the presence of measles antibodies. Separately, the CDC’s own national tally now shows zero confirmed 2026 measles deaths — omitting both fatalities Pennsylvania’s Department of Health has counted. HHS Secretary Kennedy has suggested the deaths may have been “fabricated”; Shapiro says Kennedy is spreading dangerous misinformation. Neither side has produced new documentation resolving the underlying cause-of-death question.

Lancaster County Coroner’s office (primary), Pennsylvania Dept. of Health (primary) · The Hill, Fox News, Medical Daily

BepiColombo begins its final approach to Mercury today

The ESA/JAXA spacecraft separates its transfer module this morning, seven years after launch, with orbital insertion set for November.

ESA and JAXA’s BepiColombo mission enters its Mercury-arrival phase today: the Mercury Transfer Module is scheduled to separate from the orbiter pair around 8:00 AM ET, with the Mercury Planetary Orbiter and Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter settling into their science orbits in November. The spacecraft launched in October 2018 and has used a long series of gravity-assist flybys to reach the solar system’s innermost planet.

ESA, CNN (confirmed-multiple)

Before the Open

Equity futures are close to flat and mixed — S&P and Russell futures slightly lower, Dow futures slightly higher, Nasdaq futures down about a fifth of a percent — while oil and gold both jumped hard overnight (WTI crude up roughly 1.9%, gold up more than 2%) and the dollar softened modestly against a basket of major currencies. The 10-year Treasury yield sits near 4.79%, essentially unchanged from Tuesday’s close, and the VIX ticked up about 1.5% in premarket trading to the mid-15s, still comfortably inside its normal, non-inverted term structure. Futures are not a guaranteed open.

The overnight tape is pricing a widened Gulf conflict as background risk rather than a shock: gold and oil are doing the safe-haven work while equity futures barely move. The most fragile assumption in that pricing is that last night’s Kuwait and UAE strikes produced no US or allied casualties and no disruption to actual tanker traffic through Hormuz — either one would very likely break the pattern of markets shrugging off this conflict that has held for the past several sessions.

Risks & Scenarios

The US or an allied power responds directly to last night’s Kuwait/UAE strikes within 48–72 hours. Probability 30–45%, on the basis of this week’s pattern of same-week (not same-day) retaliation, and the fact that a confirmed UAE strike would be a bigger provocation than Tuesday’s hits on Jordan and Bahrain, which drew no immediate US military response. If it happens: oil and gold extend their overnight gains and equity futures likely give back today’s calm.

Missouri’s Supreme Court rules on the congressional map before Friday’s certification deadline. Probability 50–65%, on the basis of multiple outlets citing court-watchers’ expectation of a “quick ruling” after Tuesday’s arguments and the hard Sept. 8 deadline forcing the court’s hand either way.

Friday’s August jobs report comes in weak, extending the ADP signal. Probability 40–55%, on the basis of two consecutive soft ADP prints (a downwardly revised 44,000 in July, 38,000 in August) — a directional signal only, since ADP and the official nonfarm payrolls figure have diverged meaningfully in prior months.

The administration appeals Judge Boardman’s birthright-citizenship injunction within the week. Probability 55–70%, on the basis of the administration’s consistent pattern of quickly appealing similar injunctions earlier this year.
