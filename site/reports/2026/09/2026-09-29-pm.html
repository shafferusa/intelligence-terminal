Top Stories

Williams says there is “no need for urgency,” and October hike bets fall toward a coin flip, but the 10-year still closes at 5.26%

The New York Fed president, the committee’s permanent voter and usually its centre, pushed the next hike toward late in the year. Short-term yields fell. Long-term yields rose anyway, so the long end is worried about something other than the next Fed meeting.

John Williams spoke at 2 PM in Buffalo. In his prepared remarks, as reported by Bloomberg and Reuters, he said: “If the economy evolves in a manner broadly consistent with my forecast, one further upward adjustment of the federal funds target range may be appropriate late this year to support a timelier return of inflation to target.” He then added: “With the policy action we took at our September meeting, there is no need for urgency, and we have time to gather more information.” The quotes come from the two wire services, which agree word for word.

Governor Michael Barr had spoken earlier. His text on the Board’s website was read directly for this edition, and it was plainly more hawkish. “In my base case, further policy adjustments are likely to be needed to ensure inflation comes down to target in a timely fashion.” He counted “only two months of data consistent with 2 percent core PCE inflation over the past 20 months.” He described the labour market as “in rough balance,” with job growth averaging around 80,000 a month.

The two do not strictly disagree. Both expect another hike. The difference is timing, and in the market timing is what counts. Seeking Alpha reported that the futures-implied probability of an October 28 hike fell to just over 50% after Williams, from about 70% on Monday. We could not confirm the closing CME FedWatch figure. Likely contributor The 2-year yield peaked near 4.96% before Williams spoke and fell back to about 4.88% afterwards, according to a market report. Treasury’s official close was 4.89%, down 3bp on the day.

The long end went the other way. The 10-year par yield closed at 5.26%, up 2bp, and the 20-year at 5.64%. The 30-year closed at 5.59%, after trading as high as about 5.61% intraday, its highest since June 2002 according to MarketScreener and a Bloomberg headline. As a result, the gap between the 2-year and 10-year yields widened to 37bp from 32bp. Oil fell more than 2% on the day. On Monday, the latest FRED data show, the whole rise in the 10-year came through the real yield (2.83% to 2.90%), while the 10-year breakeven was unchanged at 2.34%. That points to a higher term premium: the extra return investors demand for holding long bonds.

Stocks barely registered any of this. The S&P 500 opened at about 7,700 and fell to a session low near 7,654 around 1 PM. It recovered after Williams spoke and closed down 0.17%. The VIX finished at 16.04, almost unchanged.

Why it matters The Fed can ease its tone and the long end can still sell off. A falling front end with a rising long end is the version of higher rates that hurts housing and anyone refinancing long-term debt, and a patient Fed does not relieve it. The 10-year has now closed above 5.25%, one of the two lines this paper named on Monday as a threat to the equity market’s calm. The other, high-yield spreads above 3%, was crossed on Monday.
What’s next August PCE at 8:30 AM Wednesday. The Cleveland Fed’s nowcast puts core PCE at about 0.27% for the month and 3.4% over the year, and a single consensus preview has 0.3%. A print of 0.3% or more would undercut Williams’s patience. Then ISM on Thursday and payrolls on Friday.

Oil falls for a second day as Saudi Red Sea exports recover; Brent holds above $100

The Saudi pipeline that bypasses Hormuz is running again, and the Energy Department is offering another 40 million barrels from the strategic reserve.

CNBC reported Brent down 2.6% at $102.59 and WTI down about 3.5% at $89.38. The November contracts on Yahoo agree to within a few cents. Over two sessions Brent has now fallen from Monday’s intraday high of $108.83. Likely contributor CNBC tied the fall to recovering crude exports from Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea ports, as the East-West pipeline runs at about 3.5 million barrels a day. Saudi Aramco has not published a figure. The Energy Department separately issued a request for proposals for a 40-million-barrel exchange from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve’s Big Hill and Bryan Mound sites, for November–December delivery. It is part of the previously announced 172-million-barrel release.

The United States oil fund (USO) fell 4.4% and the energy sector fund (XLE) fell 0.9%. CNN, citing Kpler, reported that flows through Hormuz last week were about 13.1 million barrels a day, roughly 80% of pre-war levels. The diplomatic track has not moved (see The World).

Why it matters Oil is the main inflation input the Fed has cited all month, and today Brent sits $6 below Monday’s high. The risk runs both ways. Iran’s negotiator warned today that “no infrastructure will remain safe” without security guarantees. A strike on the pipeline would reverse all of this within a day.

Fair Isaac falls 26.5% as Fannie and Freddie move to a pricing grid that includes VantageScore

This morning’s 18% drop deepened through the day. FICO’s hold on mortgage underwriting is the heart of its pricing power, and that is what the market is now marking down.

Shares closed at $617.87, down $223.02, on about 4.9 million shares, roughly twelve times the normal volume. Confirmed catalyst FHFA Director Bill Pulte said late Monday on X that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac will move to a single loan-pricing grid that includes VantageScore. Rocket Mortgage said it will accept VantageScore. Until now, FICO’s classic score has been effectively required on loans the two agencies buy. That is the source of the royalty increases FICO has pushed through in recent years. The stock is now 69% below its 52-week high of $1,998. One report said it was FICO’s worst day since 1989. That comparison is single-sourced.

What’s next The implementation details are not yet public: the timing, and whether lenders may choose either score. They will decide how much of FICO’s mortgage revenue is really at risk.

FHFA Director’s public statement via Yahoo Finance, Motley Fool and MarketScreener · Yahoo Finance (close and volume).

The smart-ring maker reportedly had about four times as many orders as shares, and it still pulled the deal, citing uncertainty in the IPO market.

The United States

Supreme Court lets third-country deportations resume and sets the case for December argument

The one-page emergency order puts a Massachusetts judge’s block on hold, so the administration can again deport people with final removal orders to countries other than their own. The Court will hear the full case in December.

The unsigned order gave no vote count. Justices Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson noted they would have denied the stay. The Court asked the parties to address three questions: whether the lower courts had jurisdiction and could grant nationwide relief, whether the policy violates due process, and whether it violates the Convention Against Torture. This is an interim order, not a decision on the merits. A final ruling would likely come in 2027.

What’s next Deportations under the framework can resume now. The Court’s new term formally opens Monday, October 5, when the full list of cases granted after this week’s long conference is due.

Pentagon says a breach at its personnel data agency exposed records on about 3 million people

Social Security numbers and military records sat exposed for about nine months before the flaw was found in July.

The Defense Manpower Data Center breach affected about 2.76 million living people and 294,000 deceased people. Exposed fields included Social Security numbers and military personnel records. Access ran from October 2025 to July 2026, and the problem was discovered around July 16. The Pentagon says the flaw is patched, has seen no evidence of misuse and has not said who was responsible.

The World

Iran’s Hormuz plan: Washington’s reply is still pending, and Trump says he offered Tehran “NOTHING”

Iran expected a final US answer through Qatar by Tuesday. By publication we found no report that it had been delivered.

Foreign Minister Araghchi said late Monday that Qatari mediators would take ideas back to the US side, and that Iran expected Washington’s answer through Doha by Tuesday. He left New York on Tuesday. Qatar’s spokesman Majed Al-Ansari said mediation is continuing and Qatar is “relaying messages.”

Iran’s proposal would reopen the strait within seven days. In return it asks for the US naval blockade to be lifted, waivers on oil sanctions, about $12 billion in frozen assets and a regional ceasefire that includes Lebanon. Trump denied an Axios report that the US would offer sanctions relief, writing “I offered them NOTHING!” A US official said there is no deal without the nuclear programme. Iranian negotiator Ghalibaf warned that “if our security is not guaranteed, no infrastructure will remain safe.” Separately, the Treasury sanctioned 10 people and entities over weapons procurement for Iran.

Why it matters Oil fell sharply today even though no reply has been confirmed (see Top Stories). Crude prices are now assuming some easing of supply. A public rejection would test that assumption quickly.

AI & Technology

OpenAI’s DevDay: GPT-6.1 Sol at a fifth of Astra’s price, plus always-on agents in ChatGPT

The day after it dropped the Astra upgrade, OpenAI led its developer conference with price, not raw capability.

Sam Altman’s keynote in San Francisco introduced GPT-6.1 Sol. OpenAI says Sol comes close to GPT-6 Astra on agentic coding, computer use and professional work, at about one-fifth of Astra’s token price. API prices are $2 per million input tokens, $0.10 for cached input and $10 for output. The company also launched Dots, always-on agents in ChatGPT for Pro and Business Premium subscribers; an “Ultrafast” speed tier; tools for people and agents to work together; and Codex that runs in the cloud from any device. OpenAI says it announced more than 20 items in all.

Bloomberg and TechCrunch separately reported that OpenAI is seeking at least $30 billion at about $1.4 trillion before the new money, as a bridge while its IPO is delayed. Both describe the talks as early, with no term sheet. OpenAI’s last round, in March, raised $122 billion at $852 billion after the money.

Why it matters A cheaper frontier-class model pushes down the price of AI tokens across the industry. That helps software companies that build on these models.

    Pennsylvania

    State House passes a chatbot-safety bill for minors and a fertility-coverage mandate for cancer patients

    Both bills passed on Monday and now go to the Senate.

    • HB 2006 (Rep. Melissa Shusterman) passed 133–70, with all Democrats and 30 Republicans voting yes. It would require chatbot operators to verify users’ ages and get parental consent for minors. It would bar chatbots from encouraging suicide or violence or exposing children to sexual content. The Senate passed its own chatbot bill in March, so the two versions would have to be reconciled.
    • HB 2649 (Rep. Bridget Kosierowski) passed 172–31. It would require insurers to cover fertility preservation for cancer patients whose treatment may leave them infertile. Twenty-one other states already require this.

    Pennsylvania House Democratic Caucus releases, The Center Square, Local21 News.
