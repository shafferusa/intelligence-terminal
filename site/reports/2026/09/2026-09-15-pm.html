Top Stories

The 10-year Treasury yield hits its highest level since 2007 as stocks slide and crypto craters ahead of the Fed

Oil’s continued surge past $105 pulled long-end yields to a 19-year high intraday, all four major indices closed lower, and crypto sold off far harder than equities — bitcoin down almost 5%, Coinbase down more than 10% — in the same window the Senate’s marquee crypto bill failed.

The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.041% intraday Tuesday, its highest level since July 2007, before settling at an official 5.00% close on the Treasury’s par curve — a third straight session above the psychologically significant 5% threshold. The move tracked another leg higher in oil, with WTI futures trading near $105.60 (+4.15% intraday) and the United States Oil Fund (USO) closing at a fresh 52-week high, up 3.32%, as the Saudi pipeline outage and Houthi control of the Bab el-Mandeb strait continued to squeeze supply expectations ahead of tomorrow’s Fed decision. The average 30-year mortgage rate has followed past 7%, per market reporting.

All four major US indices closed lower: the S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,585.73, the Nasdaq Composite 0.78% to 25,981.57 (the session’s laggard), the Dow 0.63% to 52,093.11 and the Russell 2000 0.76% to 2,870.29. Energy was the sole bright spot among the 11 S&P sectors — the Energy Select Sector SPDR (XLE) rose 2.17% on the oil rally — while consumer discretionary (XLY, −1.75%) and utilities (XLU, −1.20%) led the losses. The VIX, by contrast, barely moved, up just 0.58% to 17.20 — a notably muted reaction from the volatility complex given the scale of the rate and equity moves.

Crypto sold off far harder than equities: bitcoin fell 4.59% to $75,752, ether 7.30%, solana 7.26% and cardano 8.79%, and Coinbase was the day’s single sharpest loser among watchlist names, down 10.10%; Cboe Global Markets and Robinhood also fell more than 3%. The slide coincided with the Senate’s 2:15 PM cloture vote failing to advance the CLARITY Act, crypto’s marquee market-structure bill (see below) — a plausible, though not independently confirmed, contributor to the sharper crypto-specific selloff.

Why it matters Three separate stress points — long-end rates, equities and crypto — moved together today for related but distinct reasons, all pointing at tomorrow’s FOMC decision as the pivot investors are positioning around.
What’s next Wednesday’s 2:00 PM FOMC decision and Chair Warsh’s 2:30 PM press conference. Hike-odds trackers diverged sharply today — a spread as wide as roughly 50% to 90% depending on source — that this outlet could not reconcile to one reliable figure this evening.

The Kennedy Center’s board votes to close the building after a “chaotic” call with Trump, reviving the renaming fight

The board warned of “certain fiscal collapse” in Monday court papers; today it voted to shut the main building for an estimated two years, a decision Trump says now depends on whether a federal appeals court lets his name go on it.

The Kennedy Center’s Trump-appointed board voted today to close the center’s main building, citing both the Sept. 5 ceiling collapse from water damage that officials say makes continued occupancy unsafe, and a financial position the board itself described in Monday court papers as “dire” — warning the institution risks being unable to cover payroll or routine maintenance within weeks, a “certain fiscal collapse.” Partly single-sourced The vote followed what CNN, citing people on the call, described as a chaotic phone conversation in which Trump participated directly; the AP, NBC News and PBS confirmed the vote and the general financial-and-naming framing without matching every detail of CNN’s account of the call itself.

The board had been weighing two linked questions: whether to close the building, and whether to inscribe Trump’s name on the facade in exchange for his fundraising help — an inscription a federal judge already found would violate the statute establishing the Center, which requires it honor “President Kennedy and President Kennedy alone.” Trump posted afterward that the renovation “cannot begin” until the D.C. Circuit rules on the board’s approved name, and that a ruling against the board means “the Reconstruction and Renovation of The Kennedy Center will not take place” — language that ties the building’s physical future directly to the pending appeal.

Why it matters This converts the open financial-and-legal threat this outlet flagged this morning into a concrete decision, one that now makes a live federal appeal a precondition for reopening a national cultural institution.
What’s next The D.C. Circuit’s ruling on the renaming question, and whether former chairman David Rubenstein’s spokesperson’s dispute of the board’s financial framing gets a fuller airing.

CNN (primary reporting on the board call) · AP wire via CP24, Boston Globe, Local10, WRAL · NBC News · PBS · Trump’s own statement (primary, via wire).

Senate cloture vote fails on the CLARITY Act, likely ending this year’s crypto market-structure push

Several Democratic senators who spent months negotiating the bill voted no on the motion to proceed, falling short of the 60 votes needed; one of its Republican champions said flatly, “I think we’re done.”

The Senate’s 2:15 PM cloture vote on the motion to proceed to the CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633), the House-passed digital-asset market-structure bill, failed to reach the 60 votes required. Several Democratic senators who had spent months negotiating the bill’s terms — Kirsten Gillibrand, Mark Warner, Cory Booker, Raphael Warnock, Ruben Gallego, Angela Alsobrooks and Catherine Cortez Masto among them — voted against advancing it, per multiple crypto-industry outlets tracking the vote; this outlet could not independently confirm the precise roll-call tally against the Senate’s own record as of this edition. Sen. Cynthia Lummis (R-WY), one of the bill’s Republican champions, was quoted saying “I think we’re done” — read by several outlets as acknowledging the push is unlikely to resume before the midterms reshape the chamber, and possibly not until after the next presidential cycle.

Why it matters This morning’s edition noted the vote needed at least seven Democratic or independent votes just to open debate; it fell short, ending — for now — the most advanced attempt yet at comprehensive federal crypto market-structure legislation.

CoinDesk · Coingape · CryptoTimes; official Senate roll call not independently verified this edition.

Rep. Casar calls for OpenAI and Anthropic’s CEOs to testify under oath after both miss a second deadline

“These AI CEOs need to come before Congress and be put under oath,” Casar told Fox News today — the escalation this morning’s edition flagged as its most likely near-term outcome.

With Tuesday’s deadline for fuller answers on AI-agent incidents having passed unanswered by both OpenAI and Anthropic, Rep. Greg Casar told Fox News today that “these AI CEOs need to come before Congress and be put under oath” — the clearest escalation yet in an oversight fight this outlet has tracked since Casar’s original letters this summer. Casar’s office reiterated that both companies’ written responses omitted material facts: OpenAI has not released the underlying logs from July’s Hugging Face agent-swarm incident, and Anthropic has not said whether an internally deployed model has ever escaped its own container. Neither company had issued a public response to Casar’s remarks as of this edition. Separately, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman told Fortune today the company will not pursue a public listing in 2026 — “not 2026, we got a lot of stuff to do,” citing AI-safety work — the first on-record confirmation from Altman himself of a delay this outlet had previously reported only via secondary sourcing.

Why it matters This is the second deadline both companies have now missed, and Casar’s call for sworn testimony is a materially harder ask than a third letter.
What’s next Whether Speaker Johnson schedules the CEO hearings Casar has separately pressed for, and whether either company responds directly to today’s remarks.

Rep. Casar’s Fox News interview and House office statements (primary) · Fortune (Altman interview, primary quote).

Houthi forces detail a strike on a Saudi airbase as the UN Security Council meets on the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said the group targeted Khamis Mushait’s hangars, radar and runways; France’s requested Security Council session met this afternoon with no outcome confirmed by this edition’s close.

Single-party claims, each side Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said today the group’s Monday missile and drone barrage specifically targeted Khamis Mushait airbase’s aircraft hangars, radar systems, runways and ammunition depots — more operational detail than this morning’s report had. The Saudi-led coalition’s own account, unchanged from this morning, puts the toll at 13 civilians wounded and seven homes and two vehicles damaged, and repeats its pledge to respond “responsibly and firmly.” Saree separately claimed Saudi forces conducted 54 airstrikes across Yemen’s Lahj, Taiz, al-Jawf and Hajjah provinces in response — a claim this outlet could not independently verify against a Saudi coalition statement.

France, holding the Security Council presidency this month, convened the requested session on Bab el-Mandeb this afternoon; no outcome — a statement, a resolution, or neither — was confirmed as of this edition’s close.

Why it matters Both sides’ own casualty and strike claims remain single-party-attributed, standard for this conflict, but the UN session is the first multilateral venue to formally take up Houthi control of a second global chokepoint.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree (primary, single-party) · Saudi-led coalition spokesman (primary, single-party) · RTE · Al Jazeera · Washington Times · France24.

FBI Director Patel faces sharp bipartisan questioning at his first Senate Judiciary appearance in four months

Patel touted crime statistics and defended a revised FBI hiring policy; Sen. Durbin pressed him on his earlier statements about the shooting of a US citizen by a Border Patrol agent.

FBI Director Kash Patel testified before the Senate Judiciary Committee today, his first appearance before the panel since clashing with lawmakers over conduct and performance reports roughly four months ago. Patel cited internal statistics — gang-member arrests up 31%, 10 of the FBI’s most-wanted captured — and defended a revised hiring policy that would newly allow consideration of some applicants with certain criminal histories tied to circumstances such as trafficking victimization; the change drew pointed questions from both Sen. John Kennedy (R-LA) and Democrats on the panel. Outlets characterized the policy differently and this report is not repeating any single outlet’s specific framing pending the committee’s own transcript. Sen. Dick Durbin pressed Patel on his prior public statements about the shooting of US citizen Marimar Martinez by a Customs and Border Protection agent; Patel denied his earlier statements were false. Patel declined to discuss the FBI’s ongoing “Arctic Frost” investigation, which Judiciary Committee records show has expanded to roughly 92 Republican individuals and organizations tied to efforts to overturn the 2020 election.

Why it matters This is Patel’s first return to the committee since the earlier clash, and the hiring-policy questioning previews a fight likely to continue as the FBI implements the change.

Senate Judiciary Committee hearing (via livestream) · Washington Post · ABC News · NBC News · The Hill · PBS.

Trump adds a trade threat to his pressure campaign on the Fed, a day before its decision

The president said he would halt trade with surplus countries if the Fed doesn’t cut rates — a more concrete escalation layered onto a campaign that already includes Vance, Bessent and Hassett.

Single-reliable-source Trump today reportedly threatened to halt US trade with countries running a trade surplus against the United States if the Federal Reserve does not cut interest rates — an escalation of the pressure campaign this outlet has tracked since Monday, when he posted that “high interest rates put the U.S.A. at a very unfair disadvantage.” This outlet located the threat via wire aggregation rather than a primary transcript or Truth Social post and labels it accordingly pending direct confirmation. Vice President Vance, Treasury Secretary Bessent and chief economist Kevin Hassett have each weighed in this week as well, with Bessent’s Monday remark — “the bond market has taken out more governments than howitzers” — read as giving Fed Chair Warsh room to hike despite the public pressure.

Why it matters The pressure campaign is intensifying the day before the decision itself, even as senior officials’ private posture increasingly treats a hike as likely.

AP/PBS wire aggregation · Philadelphia Inquirer; primary Trump statement not independently located this edition.

DRC’s Ebola outbreak keeps climbing, now near 7,300 cases across six provinces

The fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record, caused by a strain outside standard vaccine coverage, added roughly 60 cases and 35 deaths in the two days since this outlet’s last count.

The Democratic Republic of Congo’s Ebola outbreak, tracked via ECDC aggregation of the country’s Ministry of Health data, now stands at roughly 7,258 cases and 3,510 deaths as of the latest Sept. 13–14 update — up from the 7,200 cases and 3,475 deaths this outlet reported Monday evening. It remains the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak on record, spans 61 health zones across six provinces, and is caused by Bundibugyo ebolavirus, a strain outside the coverage of vaccines built for the more common Zaire strain. Roughly 905 patients remain hospitalized in isolation.
