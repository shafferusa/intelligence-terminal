Top Stories

Iran strikes Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait and Iraq; oil jumps to five-week highs

Before dawn Wednesday, Iran retaliated for the weekend’s roughly 100-target US strike with missiles and drones against four Gulf states — the widest single round of this six-month standoff.

Iran fired ballistic missiles and drones early Wednesday at targets across four US-aligned states. Jordan’s military said 13 missiles entered its airspace and it intercepted 10, with 3 landing in remote areas; Camp Titin came under what Jordanian officials called an intense missile attack. Bahrain said its air defenses intercepted drones aimed at Sheikh Isa Air Base and a desalination plant. Kuwait said drones targeted Ali Al Salem Air Base, hitting ammunition stores and communications infrastructure, with its airport and control tower also reportedly struck. Iraq was also named among the targeted countries. Iran’s Revolutionary Guard claimed the attacks via statement; NPR, CNN, Al Jazeera, Gulf News and the National (UAE), along with the named governments’ own statements, corroborate that the strikes occurred, making this Confirmed-multiple. The retaliation follows CENTCOM’s strike on roughly 100 Iranian targets overnight Aug. 31 into Sept. 1 — including, for the first time, two Iranian government tankers — under a newly disclosed “tanker for tanker” policy, itself a response to the still-unclaimed strike on the Saudi-flagged tanker Sidr that killed two Filipino crew members late Monday.

President Trump told Fox News the US gave Gulf allies advance notice of the weekend strikes and said he is in no hurry to force Iran back to the table — “I like our position now much better…their economy [is] totally collapsing” — while calling Iran’s leadership “crazy…stupid.” Some reporting, sourced more thinly (PBS NewsHour, Yahoo/AP), says Gulf allies were rattled and some officials were unhappy at the degree of US coordination ahead of the strike that triggered today’s response; treat that specific friction claim as developing, moderate confidence. Oil reacted directly: WTI crude closed at $90.70, up 0.53% on the day after Tuesday’s larger jump, while Brent settled near $95.32, its highest level in five to six weeks.

Why it matters This is the widest single round of Iranian retaliation since the standoff began six months ago — four countries in one morning, rather than the one- or two-target responses seen after prior US strikes — and it lands the same week the US has committed itself to a standing “tanker for tanker” retaliation policy that structurally invites another round.
What’s next Whether the US responds again under its new policy, whether any of today’s strikes caused casualties beyond the intercepted totals reported so far, and whether the reported Gulf-ally coordination friction hardens into something more than a single-sourced detail.

Jordanian, Bahraini and Kuwaiti government statements (primary) · Iran’s Revolutionary Guard statement (primary) · CENTCOM (primary, weekend strike) · NPR · CNN · Al Jazeera · Gulf News · The National.

Broadcom beats on revenue and EPS, but AI chip guidance disappoints and shares fall after hours

Fiscal third-quarter results topped estimates on both lines; a Q4 AI semiconductor guide below what analysts wanted, with no raised full-year AI forecast, is what moved the stock.

Broadcom reported fiscal third-quarter revenue of roughly $29.6 billion against a consensus near $29.5–29.9 billion, and adjusted EPS of roughly $3.32 against a consensus near $3.21–3.30 — a clear beat on the headline numbers, and its fourth-quarter revenue guide of $29.4 billion topped the roughly $28.6 billion analysts wanted. The company’s regular-session close was $367.15, down a modest 0.68% into the print. What moved the stock came after the bell: Broadcom guided fourth-quarter AI semiconductor sales to roughly $16 billion, below the roughly $17.2 billion analysts were modeling, and did not raise its full-year AI chip forecast — the detail investors were most focused on given the stock’s run on AI-infrastructure demand. Preliminary Multiple outlets report shares fell sharply in after-hours trading, but the exact size of the move is not yet settled — reports checked this run ranged from roughly 6.5% to as much as 12%, within the roughly 12% move the options market had been pricing in either direction. This report is not printing a single after-hours percentage and will report the confirmed regular-session reaction in tomorrow’s Morning Brief once a full trading session has priced it in.

Why it matters It is the week’s largest scheduled catalyst for this watchlist and an AI-infrastructure bellwether; a beat-but-sell-off reaction on soft forward AI guidance, rather than the headline numbers, echoes the pattern from Broadcom’s prior quarter.

ADP: August private payrolls rise just 38,000, the weakest since January

The print missed consensus by roughly 8,000–10,000 and marks a second straight soft month, complicating the case for a September rate hike two days ahead of Friday’s official jobs report.

ADP’s National Employment Report showed private-sector payrolls rose 38,000 in August, below a consensus clustered near 46,000–48,000 and down from an already-weak, downwardly-revised July gain of 44,000 — the smallest monthly gain since January. Education and health services led with 45,000 new jobs; leisure and hospitality added 16,000 and construction 12,000; goods-producing sectors lost 10,000 jobs overall, with manufacturing down 17,000, and professional and business services fell 16,000. Nearly all of the net gain came from large employers with 500 or more workers (+34,000); small firms added just 3,000. Wage growth held roughly flat — job-stayers up 3.0% year over year, job-changers up 4.4%. Confirmed-primary

Why it matters This is the data point Fed Chair Warsh’s hawkish case has to contend with directly: a weakening labor market, two days before the official August jobs report and roughly two weeks before the Sept. 16 FOMC meeting, cuts against an inflation-focused argument for tightening.

ADP Research Institute (primary) · CNBC · ZeroHedge.

Utah judge rules Tyler Robinson will stand trial, facing a possible death penalty, for Charlie Kirk’s killing

Judge Tony Graf found probable cause on all seven counts Tuesday; Robinson pleaded not guilty and is due back in court in October. This report did not cover the ruling in yesterday’s or this morning’s editions — it should have, and is covering it one news cycle late.

A Utah judge ruled Tuesday that the case against Tyler Robinson, accused of fatally shooting conservative activist Charlie Kirk at a Utah Valley University rally on Sept. 10, 2025, can proceed to trial. Judge Tony Graf found the state met the probable-cause standard on all seven counts, including aggravated murder and victim-targeting enhancements that make Robinson eligible for the death penalty. Robinson’s attorneys entered a plea of not guilty at the hearing; he is due back in court in October. Confirmed-multiple — CNN, NPR, NBC News, Deseret News, Time and KUER all reported the ruling and plea consistently.

Why it matters This is one of the highest-profile capital murder cases in the country, and this outlet’s research pass for Tuesday’s and this morning’s editions did not surface it despite same-day, multi-outlet coverage — a coverage gap, not an inaccurate statement previously published; logged to the corrections ledger and disclosed in this edition’s colophon per this report’s standing practice.

Two critical software flaws under active exploitation, chained for credential theft

VulnCheck reports attackers pairing an unauthenticated Langflow code-execution bug with a Ruby on Rails credentials-exposure flaw; detections have grown roughly sevenfold in three days.

Security firm VulnCheck reports two critical, unauthenticated remote-code-execution-class flaws being chained in live attacks: CVE-2026-0768, a code-validator bypass in the Langflow AI-workflow platform, and CVE-2026-66066, a Ruby on Rails bug that exposes an application’s secret_key_base and credentials via arbitrary file read. VulnCheck says detections rose from roughly 50 on Aug. 30 to 360 by Monday, concentrated from a single France-linked source IP hitting research canaries in Singapore, Israel and the UK. Moderate confidence — the reporting rests on VulnCheck’s own telemetry, corroborated by The Hacker News and SocRadar; no government advisory has yet confirmed scope or named victims.

Why it matters It is another instance of the pattern this report has tracked repeatedly this year — a fast, automated exploitation wave beginning within days of public disclosure, well ahead of most organizations’ patch cycles.

VulnCheck (primary research) · The Hacker News · SocRadar · CyberPress.

Stocks rally broadly despite the Gulf escalation as the VIX tumbles

The S&P, Nasdaq, Dow and Russell 2000 all closed higher and volatility fell nearly 7% — a weak ADP print pulling more attention than a four-country Iranian strike.

The S&P 500 closed up 0.46%, the Nasdaq Composite up 0.45%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 0.56% and the Russell 2000 up 1.13% — small caps and regional banks (KRE +2.23%) led, and the Cboe Volatility Index fell 6.98% to 15.20, with the front-end VIX9D falling even harder (−12.28%). That is a genuinely counterintuitive combination against the morning’s news: a wider, four-country round of the Hormuz standoff coincided with equities rising and volatility falling, not the reverse. This report’s read, detailed in What Moved Markets below, is that markets have now seen several rounds of this exact escalation pattern and treated it as familiar rather than new, while ADP’s weak print gave investors a fresh reason to price a more dovish Fed. Likely contributor, not a single confirmed catalyst

Why it matters A market that no longer reacts to escalation in a six-month-old standoff is itself informative — either the risk is being underpriced, or repeated exposure has genuinely reduced its perceived odds of spreading beyond the current participants.
