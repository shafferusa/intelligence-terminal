Top Stories

CNN, Politico and MS NOW take the White House ban to federal court

The three organizations filed a First Amendment and due-process suit today, a day after the White House pulled CNN from its decades-old pool-camera rotation.

CNN, MS NOW and Politico filed a 27-page complaint today in U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, seeking a temporary restraining order restoring their White House access. The suit follows Trump’s Sept. 18 Truth Social post banning the three outlets, in which he called their coverage “FICTION and LIES” and warned other outlets “would follow.” Reporters from each outlet — CNN’s Betsy Klein, MS NOW’s Akayla Gardner and Politico’s Cheyenne Haslett — had their credentials seized Saturday. Attorney Ted Boutrous, who represented CNN in its 2018 suit over Jim Acosta’s revoked press pass, is on the filing; the case is assigned to Judge Timothy Kelly, a 2017 Trump appointee.

The dispute escalated further today: the White House press pool announced it will not cover pool events involving the president while CNN cannot fulfill its scheduled rotation duties, after the White House pulled CNN from its turn representing the pool — breaking decades of precedent for how that rotation works.

Why it matters The 2018 Acosta case, which the plaintiffs’ own attorney litigated, ended in the administration’s access-revocation being blocked as a due-process violation. A ruling here would set fresh boundaries on how a president can manage White House access for outlets whose coverage he objects to.
What’s next Judge Kelly could rule on the temporary-restraining-order request within days. No hearing date has been set as of this edition.

Complaint filed today (primary, via reporting) · Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press · NPR · CNBC · CNN · NBC News · ABC News.

Houthi forces push for the last high ground in Yemen; a tanker is struck entering Hormuz

Five Yemeni military sources describe fighting for the Kahboub Mountains as Saudi-backed government forces retreat, while an unidentified projectile hit a tanker in the strait.

Houthi forces are pushing to seize the Kahboub Mountains highlands spanning Taiz and Lahij provinces — the last high ground separating their newly seized Red Sea coast from the remaining Saudi-backed government territory in the south — per five Yemeni military sources cited by Reuters. Saudi Arabia has launched what those sources describe as hundreds of strikes on Houthi positions in the highlands, but government forces are reportedly withdrawing rather than holding the line. UN figures put this flare-up’s toll at roughly 700 killed and more than 120,000 displaced.

Separately, the UK Maritime Trade Operations authority reported that an unidentified projectile struck a tanker entering the Strait of Hormuz today, lightly injuring two crew members; the vessel continued under its own power. No party has claimed responsibility. This is distinct from the Yemen highlands fighting and from Sunday’s reported call-off of planned US strikes on the Houthis after Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman’s direct appeal to Trump. Fuller reporting today (via AP wire) says the strikes had been approved and munitions were being loaded onto aircraft by midday Sunday before Trump called it off at the last minute, citing a direct US-Houthi channel; Single-reliable-source cluster Reuters says it could not independently verify the loading detail, and CENTCOM has not commented.

Why it matters A Houthi victory in the highlands would complete their control of Yemen’s Red Sea-facing territory; the unclaimed Hormuz strike adds a second, unresolved category of incident in the strait this outlet has tracked since the war widened.

Reuters (via wire syndication) · UKMTO statement · UN casualty/displacement figures · US News/AP wire.

Zelensky and Trump to meet in New York tomorrow, their first sit-down since the new sanctions law

The meeting follows Trump’s signing of a sweeping Russia sanctions bill and lands the same day Beijing publicly pushed back against it, days ahead of the Trump-Xi summit.

Zelensky announced today that he and Trump will meet on the UN General Assembly sidelines tomorrow, arranged by phone call. “This meeting could change a lot. There is diplomatic momentum,” Zelensky said, thanking Trump for this month’s Witkoff-Kushner visit to Kyiv. The meeting comes two days after Trump signed the “Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026,” which targets Putin, Russian oligarchs, banks, the defense and energy sectors and the shadow tanker fleet, and authorizes tariffs of up to 100% on the largest buyers of Russian oil — a category that includes China (roughly half of Russia’s crude exports) and India (roughly 37%). China’s Commerce Ministry said this week it “reserves the right to take all necessary measures” and opposes the law’s secondary-sanctions authority, a fresh point of friction heading into Wednesday’s Trump-Xi summit.

Overnight drone-barrage figures firmed up since this morning: Ukraine’s military says roughly 1,600 drones were launched nationwide overnight into today, about 450 of them toward Moscow — the Financial Times called it Ukraine’s largest-ever drone attack on the Russian capital — while Russia’s defense ministry claims it downed more than 1,900 drones plus several guided bombs, cruise missiles and rockets. Both figures are each side’s own claim, not independently verified. Casualty counts from Russia’s retaliatory strike on Zaporizhzhia (a shopping mall, a university building and residential high-rises were hit) are still inconsistent across outlets hours later — Ukrainian trackers range from one to six killed and roughly eight to 38 wounded — and this report is not resolving the discrepancy tonight.

Why it matters This would be the first Trump-Zelensky meeting since the sanctions law’s signing, landing in the same week as the Trump-Xi summit and a fresh Chinese objection to the law’s reach.

Zelensky’s own statement (primary) · Ukraine’s and Russia’s defense ministries (primary, each single-party) · China’s Commerce Ministry (primary) · AP wire · Financial Times.

States settle antitrust fight, clearing Paramount’s $111 billion Warner Bros. Discovery deal

A 12-state-led coalition’s settlement extracts five years of film-output and no-layoff commitments; the merger is expected to close “no later than early October.”

California Attorney General Rob Bonta, leading a 12-state coalition, announced a settlement today resolving the multistate lawsuit that had sought to block Paramount Skydance’s roughly $111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. The terms: a five-year, court-enforceable commitment to increase film output, an additional $1.5 billion commitment to domestic film production, a $47.5 million fund for merger-affected workers, a five-year prohibition on writer layoffs at CBS News, $17.5 million to a health fund plus the states’ attorneys’ fees, and separate operation, for now, of Warner Bros. Studios and Paramount Pictures. WBD CEO David Zaslav told staff the merger is expected to close “no later than early October.” Unconfirmed detail Coverage located this evening does not specify whether all 12 states actually signed the settlement or whether some remained holdouts, as earlier reporting had suggested for California and Connecticut — worth resolving in a future edition.

California Department of Justice press release (primary) · NPR · CNN.

Russia’s Duma election ends with United Russia’s largest-ever majority; leaked documents allege vote-rigging tools

With about 95% counted, the ruling party holds roughly 355 of 450 seats — a bigger margin than this morning’s partial count showed — while one outlet says it has verified leaked documents describing an e-voting platform built to alter results.

This morning’s edition reported a partial count from Russia’s Central Election Commission of 57.36% and 258 of 450 seats for United Russia. With roughly 95% of the vote now counted, the party sits at 57.8–57.83% of the party-list vote and a projected 355 seats — the largest majority in Duma history — with officials claiming a record 59% turnout. Meduza, citing unnamed Kremlin insiders, says the result was achieved “through very dirty means” — opinion sourced to anonymous insiders, not an independent finding, and reported here as such. Separately, the investigative outlet iStories says it has verified the authenticity of leaked documents describing a new e-voting platform, “Vybory 2.0,” with tooling capable of altering vote protocols, editing voter rolls and uploading unverified electronic results; Russia’s Central Election Commission has previously called the system “impossible to hack.” Single-outlet verification No second newsroom has independently corroborated the leaked documents’ authenticity or scope.

Russia’s Central Election Commission (primary, government-reported, not independently audited) · iStories · Meduza · Freedom House.

AI-stock rally sends the Nasdaq to a record close; AMD crosses $1 trillion

Meta’s new “Muse” AI-agent launch triggered a chip-demand rally that carried AMD past a $1 trillion market cap, even as one of the market’s steadiest growth stories — Novo Nordisk — fell nearly 8% on its own disappointing outlook.

Markets closed sharply higher: the S&P 500 rose 1.49% to 7,764.70, the Nasdaq Composite rose 2.26% to a record 27,122.09 — its first record close since June — the Dow rose 0.71% to 52,048.83, and the Russell 2000 rose 0.52%. Meta shares rose 11.34% on the “Muse” AI-agent product launch, per multiple wires; AMD rose 9.95% to $615.52 and crossed a $1 trillion market valuation, on chip-demand-rally spillover plus bullish comments at Citi’s TMT conference. Outside reporting cited by wire coverage put Intel up roughly 12% the same session — Intel is not on this outlet’s own watchlist, so that figure is sourced to outside reporting rather than independently recomputed here. The rally was broad across the AI-infrastructure and space names this outlet tracks: Marvell rose 5.38%, and Rocket Lab, Redwire, AST SpaceMobile and Intuitive Machines all rose more than 5%, without a single identified catalyst beyond the general risk-on tone.

The session’s one steep decliner among names this outlet tracks: Novo Nordisk fell 7.96% to $39.80 after its Capital Markets Day disappointed growth-target expectations amid intensifying competition from Eli Lilly — a confirmed catalyst, per multiple wires.

Why it matters Chip-sector demand tied to AI capital spending is dominating market direction this month more than macro data has — a pattern this outlet has flagged since the “pace the frontier” selloff two weeks ago.

UN General Assembly week opens as Trump arrives in New York, meets Mayor Mamdani

Trump’s Monday stop at Gracie Mansion precedes tomorrow’s assembly address, bilaterals with the UK and Japan’s prime ministers, and the Zelensky meeting — all before Wednesday’s Trump-Xi summit.

Trump arrived in New York this afternoon ahead of the UN General Assembly’s 81st high-level week, which opens tomorrow under the presidency of Bangladesh’s Khalilur Rahman. He met New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani at Gracie Mansion just before 4 PM ET — their third in-person meeting, closed to press, with the stated focus “issues affecting New York City and New Yorkers.” Mamdani was slated to hold a news conference afterward; no readout had been published as of this edition’s close, so this report is not characterizing an outcome. Tomorrow’s schedule: Trump addresses the assembly in the morning, with bilaterals reportedly including UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham (their first in-person meeting) and Japan Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi — who is reportedly planning to raise China and Taiwan concerns with Trump just ahead of the Xi summit — plus the newly announced Zelensky meeting.

The summit itself: Xi is reported arriving Wednesday for talks Thursday, his first Washington visit in over a decade, described by multiple outlets as “the summit of the three Ts — Taiwan, technology, trade.” A pending $14 billion Taiwan arms package remains the central friction point; some reporting says Trump may delay its approval until after November’s APEC summit to protect the meeting. Treasury Secretary Bessent met Chinese counterparts in New York today for final preparatory talks; one floated outcome under discussion is reciprocal tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion of “non-strategic” goods.

CNN live coverage · ABC7 New York · The Hill · The National · Semafor.

Java Sea ferry search: three more bodies recovered, toll now 13 dead, 122 missing

Indonesian search-and-rescue authorities recovered three more bodies today, the ninth day of operations since the ferry capsized in the Java Sea with 243 aboard. Confirmed deaths now stand at 13, with 108 rescued and 122 still missing.

Transit advocates push riders to weigh in on PRT’s bus-network redesign before Sept. 30

Pennsylvania

Two unresolved state Supreme Court rulings loom over the legislature’s Sept. 28 return

Pennsylvania’s General Assembly returns to session Sept. 28 with two state Supreme Court rulings still unaddressed. The March 26 decision striking down mandatory life-without-parole for felony murder as unconstitutional has already blown past the court’s own deadline for lawmakers to write a replacement sentencing scheme, leaving judges and prosecutors without guidance in what could become the state’s largest resentencing effort, affecting more than 1,100 people.
