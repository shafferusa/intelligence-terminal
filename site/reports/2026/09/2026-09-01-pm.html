Top Stories

US strikes IRGC targets in Iran; the overnight tanker attacks remain unclaimed

CENTCOM confirmed “limited, precise action” against Revolutionary Guard forces around midday; Iran fired at US-linked bases in Jordan and, by its own account, at the UAE, both intercepted with no casualties.

Around noon ET, US forces struck Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets inside Iran — Iranian state-affiliated media pointed to sites near Bandar Abbas, Konarak, Chabahar, Qeshm and Sirik, though CENTCOM has not officially confirmed target locations. CENTCOM described the action as “limited, precise action” against IRGC forces, rejecting Iran’s framing of it as unprovoked aggression. President Trump called the strikes “large and powerful,” describing them as retaliation for what he called Iran’s failed attempt to lay additional sea mines in the strait. Unverified figures Specific casualty or target-count figures circulating on some outlets — including claims of dozens of targets or IRGC vessels struck — could not be independently corroborated by this report and should be treated as unconfirmed pending an official CENTCOM accounting.

Iran responded within hours: its Revolutionary Guard fired missiles at US-linked bases in Jordan (King Hussein and Al-Azraq air bases), which Jordan’s military said intercepted all eight missiles with no damage or casualties. Iran also said it struck the UAE with a drone; the UAE said only that it “dealt with” an Iranian drone over its territorial waters, again an interception, not a hit — a near-repeat of Monday’s disputed UAE claim. Separately, the two tankers struck overnight near the Strait of Hormuz (the Saudi-flagged VLCC Sidr and the Liberia-flagged Senegal Prosperity, per UKMTO) remain unclaimed and unattributed as of this writing; no government, including Iran’s, has taken responsibility. Oil reacted directly: the CME WTI front-month contract rose to $90.79 a barrel, up roughly 5.9% on the day, with wire reporting putting Brent above $95 intraday — among the sharpest single-day moves of this six-month standoff.

Why it matters This is the second consecutive day of direct US military action against Iran, following Sunday’s strike near Larak Island — a pace of escalation, not a single incident, and the tanker attacks that triggered today’s response remain unattributed to anyone at all.
What’s next Whether any actor claims the tanker strikes in the coming days, and whether Iran attempts a further response beyond the Jordan and UAE incidents, both of which were intercepted without casualties.

CENTCOM statement (primary, via wire) · Jordan’s military statement · UAE Ministry of Defense statement · UKMTO (primary, tanker incidents) · Bloomberg · Washington Post · CNN · Stars and Stripes · UPI.

Go deeper

Two things are worth separating. The US strike itself and Iran’s Jordan/UAE response are each confirmed by multiple independent, on-record government statements (CENTCOM, Jordan, the UAE) and are not in dispute at the level of “did something happen.” What remains genuinely open is scale and attribution: the specific target count and any Iranian casualty figures come only from Iranian state media or unverified secondary aggregation, and the overnight tanker strikes that set off this round have no claimed perpetrator from any side — including Iran, which has claimed responsibility for other actions in this standoff but not these. Treat any specific numbers beyond the confirmed government statements as provisional.

House passes H.R. 6500, 370–48, funding the government through Dec. 11

The Senate-passed continuing resolution clears its two-thirds threshold by a wide margin and heads to the president’s desk, avoiding a shutdown at the Sept. 30 deadline.

The House passed H.R. 6500, the “Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027,” 370–48 Tuesday afternoon — 19 Republicans and 29 Democrats voted no. The bill, which the Senate passed roughly 90–6 in early August, funds the government at current levels through Dec. 11 and now goes to President Trump for signature. Because it was brought up under suspension of the rules, it needed a two-thirds majority rather than a simple one; the lopsided final margin cleared that bar comfortably.

Why it matters It removes the shutdown question from the table well ahead of the Sept. 30 deadline and, per multiple outlets, is among the earliest a continuing resolution has cleared Congress in recent memory — before the Dec. 11 expiration becomes this cycle’s next funding fight.

Congress.gov bill record (primary) · Axios · Roll Call · ABC7.

Israel says a Gaza City raid captured Hamas’s internal-security chief; covering strikes killed at least three

The operation resolves this morning’s disputed “assassination attempt” narrative toward an arrest framing, though the covering strikes’ civilian toll is separately reported and undisputed by Israel.

Israeli officials — including Defense Minister Katz, the IDF and Shin Bet — say the pre-dawn Shin Bet-led raid in Gaza City’s Al-Katiba area captured Muein al-Arabid, described as the head of Hamas’s internal-security apparatus. A firefight broke out during the extraction; Israeli aircraft struck to cover it, and Gaza officials say the covering strikes killed at least three Palestinians, including two children. Israel has not disputed the casualty figure from the covering strikes, only the earlier Hamas-aligned characterization of the operation as an assassination attempt on an internal-security official, which this report treated as unconfirmed this morning. That specific claim was not repeated in today’s reporting.

Why it matters It is the most serious individual flare-up inside the Gaza ceasefire framework in over a month, and the “arrest, not assassination” framing that has emerged does not erase the reported child deaths from the covering strikes, a detail Israel has not addressed directly.

IDF, Shin Bet and Defense Minister Katz statements (primary, via wire) · Gaza health officials · Haaretz · Al Jazeera · Times of Israel.

House to vote tonight on censuring Rep. Chuck Edwards after an Ethics Committee finding

The committee found “persistent unprofessional and inappropriate conduct” toward two female staffers; GOP leadership is not whipping the vote, and Edwards denies the findings.

The House Ethics Committee found Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-NC) engaged in persistent unprofessional and inappropriate conduct toward two female staffers. The House scheduled a floor vote on a censure resolution for this evening, with voting beginning at 5:15 PM ET — after this edition’s cutoff. Speaker Johnson’s office is reportedly not whipping the vote, leaving members a free vote; if it passes, Edwards would be the first member censured by his own party’s majority in decades. Edwards denies the committee’s findings.

What’s next The result was not available at this edition’s cutoff and will be reported in tomorrow’s Morning Brief. A free vote with no leadership whip on a specific, undisputed factual finding makes the outcome genuinely uncertain rather than a formality.

House Ethics Committee finding (primary) · Washington Post · Axios · NBC News.

Anthropic ships Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1

Fable 5.1 is broadly available via the API at unchanged headline pricing; Mythos 5.1 is restricted to a vetted early-access program.

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1, broadly available through its API, and Claude Mythos 5.1, restricted to a vetted early-access program the company calls Project Glasswing. Both models carry a 1-million-token context window, 128,000-token maximum output, mid-conversation effort adjustment and content-provenance tagging, per the company and multiple independent outlets. Fable 5.1’s API pricing is unchanged at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens; the cost of a cached-prompt read drops to a quarter of its prior level.

Why it matters It is the day’s only confirmed frontier-model release from a major lab, and lands the same week Congress is still waiting on Anthropic and OpenAI for individual answers on their separately disclosed security incidents.

Anthropic (primary) · Bloomberg · TechCrunch · MacRumors · 9to5Mac.

Nepal-Tibet flood toll crosses 1,000 as body recovery continues

Nepal’s side of the count has risen from roughly 919 Monday to more than 1,000 combined, with numbers on both sides of the border still being reconciled.

The combined death toll from the Aug. 26 glacier-collapse flood has crossed 1,000, continuing the pattern this report noted Monday: the rise reflects ongoing body recovery — some bodies found well downstream in India — rather than a reconciliation dispute between Nepali agencies. Nepal’s disaster authorities report several thousand recovered and thousands still missing; China’s Tibet-side count remains far smaller (single digits to low double-digit deaths, several hundred missing) and continues to sit well below Nepal’s per-capita toll, a gap that has not narrowed. Figures still moving Precise combined totals varied by several dozen across outlets checked today; treat the “crossed 1,000” framing as directionally solid and the exact figure as provisional pending a single reconciled count from Nepali authorities.

Microsoft 365 outage resolves after more than a day

A misconfigured core-authentication networking change knocked out Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint and related services beginning Monday; Microsoft reported recovery trending positive by this afternoon.

Microsoft confirmed a multi-service 365 outage that began around 8 AM ET Monday, traced to a misconfigured core-authentication networking change. Exchange Online mail flow and search were restored by roughly noon ET today; Teams, SharePoint, Copilot, Defender XDR and the admin center reported continued improvement through the afternoon. The incident ran more than 24 hours across a major enterprise cloud platform — a long duration for a single Microsoft cloud incident, even one Microsoft itself has not called unprecedented.

Why it matters A day-plus outage across mail, chat and file-sharing services touches a large share of US enterprise and government users simultaneously; Microsoft has not yet published a root-cause post-mortem beyond the initial networking-misconfiguration explanation.

Microsoft (primary, service-health updates) · TechCrunch · Computerworld.

Critical JFrog Artifactory flaw under active exploitation; CISA adds PaperCut zero-days to its catalog

Researchers say attackers are minting admin tokens on unpatched Artifactory servers; the PaperCut additions and the China-linked “Fire Ant” Cisco research reported Sunday carry no new developments today.

Security firm WatchTowr reports active exploitation of CVE-2026-82329, a critical (CVSS 9.8) authentication-bypass flaw in JFrog Artifactory patched Aug. 28, with attackers minting administrator tokens and enumerating credentials and access topology on unpatched servers. Moderate-to-strong confidence The exploitation reporting traces to WatchTowr’s own telemetry, corroborated by multiple outlets citing that research; no government advisory has yet confirmed scope or named victims. Separately, Monday’s CISA additions of two PaperCut zero-days to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog (federal remediation deadline Sept. 14) and Sygnia’s China-linked “Fire Ant” research against Cisco network gear carried no new developments today.

Why it matters Artifactory sits inside software build-and-release pipelines at a large number of enterprises; an admin-token-minting bypass there is a supply-chain-adjacent risk, not just a single-server compromise.

WatchTowr research · The Hacker News · SecurityWeek · GBHackers · CISA KEV catalog (primary, PaperCut).

Stocks fall broadly as Gulf risk escalates and yields climb

Energy, utilities and health care held up; chips, space names and alternative-asset managers led the declines as the VIX spiked 9.5%.

The S&P 500 closed down 0.71%, the Nasdaq Composite down 1.03%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 0.79% and the Russell 2000 down 1.23% — a broader decline than Monday’s, against the backdrop of the day’s direct US-Iran military exchange and oil’s sharpest jump of this standoff. The Cboe Volatility Index rose 9.5% to 16.34, still a moderate absolute level but the largest one-day VIX move this report has recorded in several weeks; the front-end VIX9D rose even more (+16.1%), consistent with a short-horizon geopolitical shock rather than a structural repricing. Full detail sits in What Moved Markets and Winners & Losers, below.

Why it matters Two consecutive days of direct US-Iran military action are now visibly pricing into equities, oil and volatility together, on top of an already-hawkish Fed repricing from Chair Warsh’s Jackson Hole and G20 remarks.

The Economy

July JOLTS job openings came in at 7.271 million, a touch below the roughly 7.30–7.36 million consensus — a narrow miss, not a blowout downside surprise — but June’s reading was revised down sharply to 7.182 million, a roughly 177,000 downward revision, the largest in this data series since 2025. Hires and separations were little changed; one detail drawing analyst attention was a 188,000 drop in professional and business-services hires in July, read by some as a leading indicator ahead of Friday’s August jobs report.

The ISM Manufacturing PMI came in at 54.6% for August, below the roughly 55.2% consensus and down from July’s 55.6% — a modest deceleration, though still the eighth straight month of manufacturing expansion. The New Orders Index fell to 53.7% from 56.7%, still expansionary but a clearer slowdown signal than the headline number alone.

Neither release was decisive enough to move September rate-hike odds sharply in either direction; CME-implied odds sat in the mid-to-high 60s percent today, roughly where they stood before the data, having climbed off Fed Chair Warsh’s hawkish Jackson Hole and G20 remarks. The Treasury par-yield curve moved higher across the board: the 3-month bill rose 1 basis point to 3.92%, the 2-year note rose 5 basis points to 4.39%, the 10-year note rose 4 basis points to 4.79% — its highest level in this report’s tracking window — and the 30-year rose 2 basis points to 5.27%. The 2s10s spread narrowed slightly to roughly 40 basis points from 41; the 3m10y spread widened to roughly 87 basis points from 84. Today’s move looks multi-causal: a continuing hawkish Fed repricing, oil’s jump feeding directly into inflation-expectations concern, and a broader global government-bond selloff running alongside the geopolitical shock, not attributable to any single cause. Science & Space

NASA’s Jessica Meir and ESA’s Sophie Adenot completed the station’s 284th spacewalk at 3:29 PM ET, 6 hours 49 minutes after starting — in line with the planned roughly 6.5 hours and with no reported anomalies. They installed a new navigation retroreflector on Harmony’s forward port, replaced a truss-mounted camera, swabbed the station’s exterior for microorganisms and began prep work for a future spacewalk to replace the Alpha Magnetic Spectrometer’s radiator; it was the sixth all-female ISS spacewalk on record. Tonight’s Falcon 9 Starlink launch from Vandenberg remains on schedule for roughly 3:46 AM ET Wednesday, and Rocket Lab’s Electron “Owl Around the World” mission from Mahia, New Zealand — carrying a Synspective SAR satellite, and already slipped twice on site weather — is targeting roughly 7:00 AM ET Wednesday.
