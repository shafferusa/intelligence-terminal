Top Stories

Roberts halts the ballroom injunction hours before it would have stopped construction

An administrative stay from the Chief Justice alone — not a ruling on who controls federal property funding — but it lets work resume immediately while the full Court decides.

Chief Justice John Roberts signed an order Friday staying the D.C. Circuit’s injunction against the White House East Wing ballroom project, which was set to take effect at midnight absent Supreme Court action. The order holds "pending further order of the undersigned or of the Court" — standard language for an administrative stay, which freezes the status quo while the justices decide whether to grant the fuller emergency application, not a decision on the merits. Construction on the roughly $400 million project can resume immediately under the stay.

The underlying dispute — whether the executive branch can redirect federal property funds for the project without the congressional appropriation the challengers say is required — is unresolved. Docket 26A203 remains before the full Court, with no timeline for a further order.

Why it matters This is the first concrete Supreme Court action of the week on either of the two live emergency dockets (the mail-ballot application remains fully briefed with no order). A stay from one justice is a much lower bar than a merits ruling, and it can be reversed by later Court action.
What’s next Whether the full Court converts the administrative stay into a longer-lived order, denies the government’s application outright, or simply lets construction proceed indefinitely under Roberts’ holding action.

Supreme Court docket 26A203 (primary) · CBS News · CNN · Fox News · Deadline.

Canada’s tariff deadline arrives at midnight with a deal “close” but still unsigned

Autos reportedly cut to 15%, steel and aluminum halved — but nothing is on paper as the 50% tariff pause runs out.

Canadian and US negotiators met again in Washington Friday afternoon with the paused 50% Section 338 tariffs due back at 12:01 AM ET Saturday. Reported terms under discussion include cutting the US auto tariff from 25% to 15% and halving steel and aluminum tariffs to 25%, with Canada’s dairy supply-management system protected. Ottawa removed many of its own retaliatory tariffs on US goods Friday even without a signed reciprocal text, though Canada’s 25% tariffs on US autos, steel and aluminum remain in place for now.

This is the second time this specific deadline has been approached without a signed agreement; Trump paused the tariffs 90 minutes before an earlier deadline this month.

Why it matters A snapback would hit a major trading partner with a 50% tariff on short notice for the second time in weeks — this resolves the open forecast logged Thursday on whether a deal or extension arrives before Friday’s close.

CNBC · CP24/BNN Bloomberg · Globe and Mail · US News.

Bessent formalizes “economic D-Day” against Iran as gold and oil futures push higher

Treasury calls it the “toughest sanctions in history”; the president says the goal is reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

Treasury Secretary Bessent said Friday the US will impose what he called the toughest sanctions in its history on Iran, working with allies to isolate the regime economically alongside the ongoing naval presence in the Gulf. Trump described it as unprecedented economic warfare intended to force an end to Iran’s nuclear program. No specific new sanctions list has been published yet — Bessent is set to detail the full package Monday, a date set earlier this week alongside Thursday’s Treasury sanctions on Iranian currency-exchange and shell-company networks.

Gold futures ran further on the day, up over 3% intraday, while crude oil’s move was more mixed — WTI futures actually eased slightly even as some commodity desks cited a Hormuz risk premium. Market narrative Multiple outlets link the gold move to both the Iran escalation and a separate Treasury announcement doubling the size of its long-bond buybacks; neither is a single confirmed catalyst on its own.

Why it matters This is the clearest public statement yet tying the sanctions campaign to the Hormuz standoff, and it lands the same week Iran’s foreign minister called a shipping arrangement with Oman as intermediary "very close."

US News · The Hill · Boston Globe · OPB · Bloomberg (oil/gold pricing).

Russia’s “double-tap” drone strike on a Kryvyi Rih shopping center kills six

Zelensky says a second drone wave hit after rescuers arrived; roughly 91 wounded, including children.

A Russian drone strike hit a shopping center in Kryvyi Rih Friday, killing six people and wounding roughly 91, six of them children, according to Ukrainian officials. Zelensky described the attack as a "double tap" — a second wave of drones striking after emergency responders had arrived at the scene of the first. This is separate from Wednesday night’s barrage on Kyiv, whose toll climbed to 17 dead earlier this week.

No new diplomatic movement was reported alongside the strike. Zelensky’s disclosure this week that Ukraine holds roughly 1% of the US Patriot interceptor stock it once had access to remains the operative explanation offered for why recent barrages have gone largely unintercepted.

AP/Click on Detroit · EA WorldView.

Turkey issues an arrest warrant for Netanyahu as the Idlib-area strike target comes into focus

Israel says it hit Abu al-Duhur airbase, which it says was being retrofitted for Turkish forces; Ankara denies any troop presence there.

Reporting on Israel’s strike on a Syrian airbase near Idlib now identifies the target as Abu al-Duhur, roughly 70 kilometers from the Turkish border, which Israel says was being prepared to host Turkish forces. Turkey denies any troop presence at the site and has called the strike reckless; a Turkish arrest warrant for Netanyahu has surfaced in coverage this week, alongside resurfaced remarks from Netanyahu that Israel will not tolerate Turkish entrenchment toward Aleppo. No new strikes were reported since Thursday.

Haaretz · Washington Times · Middle East Eye.

DRC’s Ebola outbreak is “spreading faster and wider” than ever, UN warns, as response funding runs low

Case count rose 82 in two days; the UN’s senior Ebola coordinator calls the growth exponential.

The Democratic Republic of Congo’s Ebola outbreak, already a declared WHO Public Health Emergency of International Concern, has worsened: the latest figures put confirmed cases at 5,290 and deaths at 2,516, up from 5,208 and 2,476 just two days earlier. UN Senior Ebola Coordinator Julien Harneis said the outbreak is spreading faster and wider than at any prior point and warned that response funding may run out within weeks. Ituri province remains hardest hit at 4,447 cases and 1,984 deaths, with North Kivu at 663 cases and 452 deaths.

Why it matters This is a genuine acceleration, not a data-lag artifact — and it arrives alongside an explicit funding warning from the UN’s own coordinator, which is new information beyond the PHEIC-extension confirmation reported this morning.

WHO Disease Outbreak News (primary) · UN News · Al Jazeera.

Walmart’s selloff draws a wave of price-target cuts as Deere’s divergent quarter underlines a tariff-refund pattern

Seven brokerages trimmed targets Friday, mostly while keeping bullish ratings — a valuation reset, not a thesis change.

A wave of analyst price-target cuts hit Walmart Friday following Wednesday’s 9.15% plunge: JPMorgan ($137→$125), Wells Fargo ($140→$120), BMO ($145→$126), Wolfe ($133→$115), Baird ($140→$120), RBC ($137→$131) and Guggenheim ($135→$130) all cited the comp-sales deceleration to 2.6% (versus roughly 3.5% expected) as the driver, while most kept Overweight, Outperform or Buy ratings — reading as a valuation reset rather than a change of thesis. Walmart shares finished essentially flat Friday.

The broader pattern this earnings season, first visible in Wednesday’s Walmart-versus-Deere contrast, continues: several retailers’ headline beats are being flattered by tariff-refund pass-throughs that don’t reflect durable demand, and the market is pricing the number underneath the headline.

Benzinga · Investing.com · Barchart · MarketBeat.

Wall Street closes the week higher after a flash PMI hits its fastest pace in four years

Composite output jumped to 56.0 in August, led by services; manufacturing actually slipped to a five-month low.

S&P Global’s flash composite PMI for August came in at 56.0, up from 54.5 in July and the highest reading since April 2022. Services drove the gain, surging to 56.8, a 20-month high, while manufacturing slipped to 53.2, a five-month low — a divergence worth noting rather than treating the headline as uniform strength. Hiring rose at its strongest pace since January 2025. Likely contributor Equities extended gains through the session on the release, alongside Bessent’s Thursday remarks that Treasury has doubled the size of its long-bond buyback operations to $4 billion-plus; neither is confirmed as the sole driver of the day’s move.

S&P Global flash PMI (primary release) · Bloomberg · Benzinga · Kitco.

Indonesia’s Flores earthquake toll jumps to 83 as displacement figures emerge

State media puts the toll well above the ~68–70 range that had circulated for days, with roughly 100,000 displaced.

Single source, official Indonesia’s BNPB disaster agency, via Xinhua, now puts the earthquake death toll at 83, along with 328 seriously injured, 639 with minor injuries, roughly 100,000 displaced and nearly 45,000 homes damaged. Other trackers (PBS, CBS) still show the older near-70 figure as of their last update, so treat the toll as climbing and not yet fully reconciled across outlets rather than settled at 83.

No courtroom activity found for Friday; Zuckerberg remains a listed future witness with no date set.
