Top Stories

Nvidia beats big, guides to $108B and assumes zero China data-center revenue

Revenue of $96.2 billion topped consensus by roughly $4 billion and Data Center sales rose 117% — but the quarter that mattered most was the outlook, which now bakes in nothing at all from China.

Nvidia’s own filed results (SEC 8-K, exhibit 99.1, filed 4:21 PM ET) show second-quarter fiscal-2027 revenue of $96.221 billion, up 18% from the prior quarter and 106% from a year ago, against a consensus of roughly $92–93 billion. Data Center revenue was $89.0 billion, up 117% year over year and now 92% of total sales. Non-GAAP diluted EPS was $2.22 (GAAP $2.46), both ahead of Street estimates near $2.08–2.13. Gross margin held at 75.0%. CEO Jensen Huang: “AI has reached its inflection point. It’s doing useful work. Its tokens are productive and profitable. Now, compute is revenue.”

The guidance is the story. Nvidia expects third-quarter revenue of $108.0 billion, plus or minus 2%, roughly $4 billion above the prior Street consensus — and says explicitly it is “not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China” in that outlook, a franker statement than the company has previously made about the market it has been fighting export restrictions to keep. Gross margin is guided to 74.0% for the new quarter. The company returned about $26.0 billion to shareholders in buybacks and dividends during the quarter and has roughly $99.0 billion left under its repurchase authorization.

Going into the print, Nvidia shares closed the regular session down 1.59% to $209.66 — part of the market’s pre-earnings caution rather than any reaction to results, which were not public until after the 4:00 PM close. After-hours trading was volatile and incomplete at file time; this report does not attempt to characterize it. Nvidia has fallen the day after four straight prior earnings reports despite beating each time — a pattern several previews flagged this week as the key overhang on an otherwise clean beat-and-raise.

Why it matters A beat of this size, paired with a guide that explicitly zeroes out China, either resolves or reframes the memory- and AI-infrastructure-financing anxiety that has hung over chip names all week — depending on whether the market reads “zero China” as conservatism already priced in or a real, newly disclosed hole in the growth story.
What’s next The earnings call (around 5:00 PM ET) and Thursday’s open will show the market’s actual verdict; a forecast on the magnitude of that move is logged below.

Iran-Oman Hormuz framework hardens to a military-vessel ban; Washington quietly objects

Deputy Foreign Minister Gharibabadi detailed a temporary, Iranian-controlled routing scheme with a 30-to-60-day clock to negotiate something permanent — and the Trump administration has told Oman it doesn’t like part of it.

Iran’s deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi laid out specifics of the framework Oman’s foreign minister discussed in Tehran this week: no military vessels of any nation would transit the strait under the arrangement; inbound Gulf-bound traffic would route entirely through Iranian territorial waters, with outbound traffic split between Iranian and Omani waters; and the whole scheme is explicitly temporary, with Iran and Oman required to negotiate a permanent arrangement within 30 to 60 days. Gharibabadi repeated that Iran considers the strait fully open only once the US meets commitments under June’s now-lapsed interim deal.

Separately, multiple outlets report the Trump administration has told Oman it opposes part of the emerging arrangement — specifically joint Iranian-Omani management of the outbound route. No US official has confirmed this on the record as of file time, and it has not been denied by Tehran or Muscat either.

Oil, which sold off sharply on Monday’s sanctions rollout and again Tuesday on the first word of this framework, was little changed to modestly higher today (USO +0.95%) even as the details hardened — a sign the market had already priced most of the de-escalation trajectory in, not that today’s specifics changed the calculus much further.

Why it matters A framework this specific — with a hard deadline and an explicit military-vessel exclusion — is a real diplomatic proposal, not a talking point, and confirmed US-Oman friction over its terms shows Washington is not simply endorsing whatever Tehran and Muscat agree to.

Iranian state television via Al Jazeera, Fortune, CNBC (Confirmed-multiple) · US-Oman friction reporting via CNBC and wire pickups (Single-reliable-source on the specific objection, not yet confirmed on the record).

Talwani vacates the mail-ballot injunction; 25 states sue on the merits as the rule takes effect

The rule this morning’s edition called “unenforceable while her injunction stands” is no longer unenforceable — the injunction is gone, and a new coalition lawsuit has replaced it.

Judge Indira Talwani (D. Mass.) vacated her own nationwide preliminary injunction against the mail-ballot executive order today, writing she was compelled to by the Supreme Court’s Monday decision lifting a companion injunction in a related case. With the injunction gone, USPS’s final rule was formally published in the Federal Register this morning and is now in legal effect nationwide — a real change from this morning’s framing, in which the rule was set to publish but remain unenforceable.

Democratic attorneys general from 23 states and D.C., joined by Pennsylvania’s governor, filed a new lawsuit in Boston federal court today challenging the now-final rule directly on the merits — a different posture from earlier suits that challenged it pre-emptively. New York Attorney General Letitia James called it the federal government “attempting to meddle” in election administration.

Why it matters The fight has shifted from “can they publish the rule” to “is the published rule lawful,” with the litigation posture now mirroring the rule’s actual legal status for the first time since this dispute began in late July.
What’s next No hearing date has been reported for the new Boston suit; this report will follow it as it develops.

Axios, Washington Post, CT Mirror, Democracy Docket (Confirmed-multiple).

Catastrophic flash flood at the Nepal-Tibet border; death toll in open dispute

Casualty count disputed An ice-and-rock avalanche into a Himalayan river triggered flash floods through four Nepali districts and into Tibet; outlets are reporting death tolls ranging from single digits to over 150.

A large ice-and-rock avalanche collapsed into the Lhende Khola, a tributary of the Bhote Koshi/Trishuli river system near the Nepal-Tibet border, early today — registering seismically like a magnitude-5.2 event and triggering flash floods through Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading and Gorkha districts north of Kathmandu, and across the border into Tibet. Roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure were damaged.

Unverified/developing The death toll is genuinely unsettled across outlets at file time: figures reported range from “at least 8 killed, hundreds missing” in the earliest wire accounts up to “at least 95 to 98 dead in Nepal plus 3 in China” and one headline citing “at least 160 killed.” Nepal’s tourism ministry says roughly 403 tourists, including Americans, are unaccounted for. This report is not printing a single figure because none has stabilized; Al Jazeera, CNN, NPR and Japan Times all carry different numbers as of file time, each attributed to local or provincial officials whose own counts are still moving.

What’s next Search-and-rescue access to the affected districts will determine how quickly the toll stabilizes; this report will carry a reconciled figure once one outlet's count is not immediately superseded by another's.

Al Jazeera, CNN, NPR, Japan Times, Fox Weather — corroborate the event; the casualty count itself remains Unverified/Preliminary across all of them.

Islamabad hospital nursery fire kills 14 newborns

An apparent air-conditioning compressor explosion ignited a fire in a hospital’s neonatal ward; Pakistan’s prime minister has ordered an investigation.

A fire broke out around 7:00 AM local time today in the neonatal nursery on the third floor of the Mother and Child Centre at the Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences in Islamabad, apparently triggered by an air-conditioning compressor explosion. Of roughly 15 newborns present, 14 died; one was rescued. Multiple reports describe locked ward doors and a delayed rescue response, not yet independently confirmed. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has ordered a formal investigation.

Why it matters A single-incident tragedy of this scale at a major public hospital, with a government investigation already ordered, is likely to become a story about hospital fire-safety standards as much as about the fire itself.

Al Jazeera, CNN, CBS News, ABC (Australia) — Confirmed-multiple on the death toll and cause.

Trump declares a national emergency over foreign-sourced grid equipment

A new executive order revives a first-term authority to block bulk-power-system equipment tied to a foreign adversary — aimed above all at Chinese-made transformers and grid software.

President Trump signed an executive order today declaring a national emergency under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act and the National Emergencies Act over foreign-sourced bulk-power-system electric equipment — transformers, grid software and other digital components — citing risk from equipment sourced from countries such as China. It bars federal agencies and US persons from acquiring, transferring or installing such equipment where a foreign government or national has an interest that poses “unacceptable risk” to national security. The order revives and updates a similar 2020 Trump-administration order that the Biden administration revoked in 2021.

Why it matters This is a new use of emergency economic powers specifically targeting the electric grid’s supply chain — it will affect utility procurement practices and adds to a run of executive actions this year framing Chinese-made hardware as a national-security question, following the Apple/CXMT memory-chip dispute covered in Business below.

Department of Energy (energy.gov), White House presidential-actions page (Confirmed-multiple, primary) · Utility Dive, wire coverage.

Taiwan indictment: Nvidia and Supermicro now on the record; scheme detailed at roughly $2.5B

Both companies have now issued statements on Monday’s indictment of nine people over illegal AI-server exports to China, and the scale of the scheme is clearer — 74 servers reached China through three separate routes.

Taiwanese prosecutors’ Monday indictment of nine people — including an Nvidia Taiwan employee and two former Supermicro Taiwan employees — over illegally exporting export-controlled AI servers to China is now detailed at an estimated scheme value of roughly $2.5 billion: 74 servers reportedly reached China via three routes (50 through Indonesia, 16 direct, 8 via Japan and Hong Kong), using dummy packaging, false labeling and a pass-through company; a separate attempt involving 56 more servers failed and those units remain in Taiwan. Nvidia says it will work with Taiwanese authorities to help resolve the allegations; Supermicro says its own cooperation with investigators led to the arrests and that it is not a target.

Taiwan prosecutors via wire coverage (Confirmed-multiple) · Nvidia and Supermicro statements (primary, company).

The Economy

Real GDP grew at an unrevised 1.5% annualized rate in the second quarter, the Bureau of Economic Analysis’s second estimate confirmed this morning, down from 2.1% in the first quarter; an upward revision to consumer spending was offset by a matching upward revision to imports, netting to no change from the advance print. Real gross domestic income rose a faster 2.2%, and corporate profits jumped $400.9 billion in the quarter — more than five times the first quarter’s $74.4 billion increase, a gap between the profits and GDP measures worth watching but not yet explained by anything this report has found.

July’s personal income and outlays report showed income up $115.1 billion (0.4%), disposable income up $125.9 billion (0.5%) and consumption up $36.3 billion (0.2%), with the saving rate at 3.0% — all confirmed directly against BEA’s own release. The PCE price index, the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, held at 3.7% year over year headline and 3.3% core, both unchanged from June but a touch above the roughly 3.6% consensus for headline — secondary-sourced (CNBC, FXStreet), not independently confirmed against BEA’s own price-index table this run. Sticky, not accelerating, is the fairest read: inflation isn’t getting worse, but it isn’t getting better either, one day before Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s first Jackson Hole keynote.

Durable goods orders rose 1.1% in July’s advance estimate to $339.3 billion, driven by a 2.3% jump in transportation equipment; ex-transportation orders rose a more modest 0.4%. Today’s 5-year Treasury note auction ($70B) cleared at a 4.393% high yield with a 2.37 bid-to-cover ratio — confirmed directly against TreasuryDirect’s own results and essentially in line with last month’s 5-year auction (4.408%, 2.28 cover), unremarkable demand despite the hotter-than-expected inflation print earlier in the day.

County Council bill would bar data centers from county-owned land

A bill introduced Tuesday by a majority-sized bloc of county council members (Grzybek, Hallam, Madonna-Emmerling, Rose, Geiger Shulman, Catena and Council President Naccarati-Chapkis) would bar data-center construction and supporting infrastructure — including cryptocurrency-mining facilities, generators and cooling towers — on county-owned property, potentially affecting nearly 30,000 acres.
