Section 5What Changed in the World

The week's dominant shift was macro, not geopolitical: a labor market that had been merely "cooling" all summer delivered an outright contraction (-23,000 payrolls), reopening a rate-cut discussion the Fed's July 3-way hawkish dissent had seemed to close off. That single data point did more to move the S&P 500 to a fresh record than any of the week's individual corporate catalysts.

On Iran, the picture genuinely bifurcated: the diplomatic track improved concretely (the Oman Hormuz-corridor agreement), while the maritime-harassment and cyberattack tracks continued largely unabated (a further tanker attack, a wider water-utility cyberattack footprint reaching an actual operational safety impact in Georgia). Neither a full-de-escalation nor a full-escalation framing captures the week accurately.

Corporate America's AI-capex story continued splitting along familiar lines — infrastructure/demand beneficiaries (Palantir, the space-sector earnings cluster, AVGO, NVDA) outperformed AI-capex payers absorbing near-term cost (SpaceX's own capex disclosure, AMD, Google amid its leadership transition) — the same pattern this report tracked through the prior two weeks' mega-cap earnings.

Section 6 · weeklyUS Politics & Government

Todd Blanche's Attorney General nomination dominated the week's domestic-politics coverage (see Top Story 3), resolving 50-49 in an overnight Saturday vote after clearing committee 12-10 on Tuesday. The path ran through two additional secured GOP votes (Curtis, Husted) and Sen. Cassidy's Friday commitment offsetting Sens. Collins's and Murkowski's no votes.

The Government Accountability Office released a review (covering Jan 2025-Jul 2026) finding DOGE's claimed $110B in federal savings unreliable: 2,503 of 13,476 "terminated" contracts were not actually cancelled ($27.4B of claimed savings affected), the GAO could not verify the method behind 96% of claimed grant savings, and 108 of 264 "terminated" leases were already being eliminated before DOGE's involvement. DOGE did not respond to the GAO's requests for comment. This is reported here as a nonpartisan audit finding on a signature initiative's headline figure, without characterizing the initiative itself.

The referral is not a mandate — DOJ will independently decide whether to act on it.

The FY2027 NDAA remains blocked in the Senate with no procedural movement since the July 14 cloture failure; published floor schedules point to a continuing-resolution/funding cloture vote and Medicaid hearings as Monday's focus, not NDAA, ahead of the chamber's Aug 10 recess. Section 25 · learning · no advance on weekendsPhysics Weekly Recap

Physics 11/71 (current) · this week's arc: positions 6–10

This week's five topics built the vector-and-motion foundation that everything in kinematics rests on: vectors (quantities with both size and direction), coordinate systems (the frame you measure those vectors against), and then the three motion quantities in sequence — position, velocity, and acceleration, each one the rate of change of the one before it. The through-line is that motion in more than one dimension is just independent motion along each coordinate axis, tracked with vector arithmetic rather than a single number.

The week closed at "graphs of motion" (position 11, current), which is the payoff for the whole sequence: position-time, velocity-time, and acceleration-time graphs are three views of the same underlying story, and the slope of each one is the quantity above it (velocity is the slope of position vs. time; acceleration is the slope of velocity vs. time). No advance this weekend, per the never-advance-on-weekends rule; Monday's edition continues from position 11.

Section 26 · learning · no advance on weekendsSpaceflight Weekly Recap

Spaceflight 11/75 (current) · this week's arc: positions 6–10

This week's curriculum arc built directly toward — and then derived — the single most important equation in rocketry. It opened with exhaust velocity (how fast propellant leaves the nozzle, the direct measure of engine efficiency) and arrived at the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation itself: the relationship between a rocket's velocity change, its exhaust velocity, and the ratio of its starting mass to its ending mass. The remaining three topics unpack that mass ratio into its practical pieces — mass fraction, propellant fraction, and payload fraction — the accounting that tells an engineer how much of a rocket's total mass can actually be useful cargo once structure and propellant are subtracted out.

The week closed at "staging" (position 11, current), the direct engineering answer to the payload-fraction problem: dropping spent structure mid-flight so the rocket equation gets to restart with a smaller, lighter vehicle. No advance this weekend; Monday's edition continues from position 11.

Section 27 · learning · no advance on weekendsQuant/ML Weekly Recap

Quant/ML 11/118 (current) · this week's arc: positions 6–10

This week moved from describing a single return series to modeling its structure over time. It opened with covariance and correlation (how two series move together) and the autocorrelation function (how a single series correlates with its own past), then the partial autocorrelation function (isolating the correlation at each lag after removing shorter-lag effects) and the formal conditions for weak stationarity — the assumption, often violated by real financial data, that a series' mean, variance, and lag-structure don't drift over time. The arc closed by introducing the autoregressive model AR(p), the general form that expresses today's value as a weighted sum of its own recent past plus noise.

The week's current position (11, AR(1)) is the simplest special case of that general model — today's value as a scaled version of yesterday's value alone. This week's own market action offers a live illustration: SpaceX's four consecutive large single-session moves (Section 15.2) show a return series where the current session's move is poorly explained by the prior session's move alone — a real-world case where an AR(1) model would badly underfit, since each move was driven by a distinct fresh catalyst (earnings, lockup, contract news) rather than momentum from the prior session. No advance this weekend; Monday's edition continues from position 11.
