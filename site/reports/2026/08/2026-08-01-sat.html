1. The US-Iran war widened sharply, then turned toward imminent new strikes

Initial event
The week opened with a fragile pause holding into its fourth/fifth day (this system corrected an off-by-one day-count error mid-week — see Section 29).
Development
Tuesday night, Iran fired ballistic missiles at a US base in Jordan (all intercepted); the US and Saudi forces struck back in Iraq. Wednesday night into Thursday, the US struck dozens of IRGC targets across Iran in a two-hour wave; Iran retaliated against Jordan again, Kuwait (one worker killed), and — newly confirmed this weekend — Egypt, via a drone strike Cairo itself acknowledged. Friday, Iran claimed a drone strike destroyed or damaged six F-35s and killed personnel at Kuwait’s Ahmad al-Jaber base; CENTCOM explicitly denied the claim, stating no US aircraft were destroyed or damaged at either the Jordan or Kuwait bases.
Final status (as of this weekend)
Not resolved — accelerating. Kuwait intercepted a fresh wave of Iranian drones early Saturday. Multiple outlets (CBS, ABC, Axios, Fortune/WSJ, PBS) report the US and Israel are preparing strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure — power plants, refineries, bridges — “as soon as this weekend,” discussed at a Friday Camp David Cabinet meeting where President Trump said “we’ll be hitting them very hard. At some point, they’re going to say, ‘We just can’t take it anymore.’” No final order had been given as of this report’s cutoff; officials cautioned strikes could still be called off, and there was reported internal discussion of concluding any strikes before Monday’s market open.
Why it mattered
This is now a multi-country conflict (Iran, Jordan, Kuwait, Egypt, plus US and Israeli involvement) with direct spillover into oil prices (WTI +7% on Wednesday alone), mega-cap energy earnings (Exxon/Chevron windfall profits), and Treasury-market risk premia.

4. A magnitude-7.1 earthquake struck Kumamoto, Japan — the death toll kept climbing all week

Initial event
The quake struck overnight Monday-into-Tuesday, initially reported as at least 1 dead and 100+ injured.
Development
The toll climbed daily and inconsistently across outlets all week — 13 vs. 18 disputed Wednesday, 25–34 disputed Thursday, converging near 34 by Friday morning across most outlets (CGTN alone citing 35). TSMC, Sony, and Tokyo Electron all disclosed same-day facility impacts (evacuations; Tokyo Electron suspended two factories through Wednesday), deepening an already-jittery AI-infrastructure stock selloff. 5. Trump’s Board of Peace announced a Hamas disarmament roadmap for Gaza — both sides are publicly skeptical

Initial event
Announced Thursday July 30 (mediated by Egypt, Qatar, and Turkey via the Board of Peace) — this system missed the story in Friday morning’s edition and disclosed the gap explicitly rather than silently backfilling it (see Section 29).
Development
The full 15-point roadmap surfaced this weekend: it transfers civilian and security governance from Hamas to a new National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG), backed by a not-yet-deployed International Stabilization Force (ISF). The IDF and Hamas are to cease military operations immediately while an implementation mechanism is prepared within 14 days (roughly Aug 13); police-held weapons transfer first, then heavier weapons are decommissioned, all certified by an International Verification Committee. Each stage is explicitly tied to a corresponding stage of Israeli withdrawal.
Final status
Announced but not accepted by either side’s full public posture: Israeli officials are reported “deeply skeptical” Hamas will disarm and have not confirmed acceptance of the roadmap; a senior Hamas official’s comments suggest the disarmament-before-withdrawal sequencing remains contested. Separately, a monitoring group (FDD) reports the IDF logged 32 Gaza ceasefire violations between July 10–31.
Why it mattered
The first concrete, published implementation mechanism for ending Hamas’s armed presence in Gaza since the broader ceasefire framework was established.
What was misunderstood
Initial coverage framed this as an “agreement” before either side had actually accepted its terms — a distinction this report is holding onto rather than adopting the more definitive framing some outlets used.
Unresolved
Whether the 14-day implementation-mechanism deadline (~Aug 13) is met; this system has an open forecast on it (2026-07-31-pm-2, 45-55% it is not finalized on schedule).
Keep on watchlist?

6. The DRC Ebola outbreak became the second-largest on record

Initial event
Confirmed May 15 in Ituri Province, caused by Bundibugyo virus disease — a strain with no approved vaccine or treatment.
Development
Case/death counts accelerated through the week: roughly 3,200 cases/1,405 deaths (Jul 26) → 3,442/1,521 (Jul 30) → 3,553/1,558 (this system’s own Jul 31 gather). The outbreak surpassed the 2018–2020 DRC outbreak’s total case count in just over two months, versus that outbreak’s nearly two years to reach the same scale — now explicitly described by outlets as the fastest-spreading Ebola outbreak ever documented, reaching 1,000 deaths in under 10 weeks versus roughly eight months for the 2013–2016 West African outbreak.
Final status
Still accelerating as of this weekend, with no sign of deceleration in the latest data.
Why it mattered
Nearly 90% of cases concentrated in a conflict-affected region with weak government control and infrastructure, complicating containment; WHO says global spread risk remains low since Ebola does not travel through the air.
What was misunderstood
This story received comparatively little attention against the Iran and Gaza news cycles this week despite its historic severity — flagged in Section 23 (Undercovered Stories).
Unresolved
Whether containment measures gain traction; vaccine/treatment development timeline for the Bundibugyo strain.

Section 5What Changed in the World

The week’s dominant shift was from de-escalation to escalation on the Iran front: a fragile pause that began the week gave way to direct strikes inside Iran, retaliation against three countries, and by the weekend, reporting of imminent US/Israel strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure. Trump’s approval on handling the war fell further into record-low territory across multiple pollsters (AP-NORC 33%, Quinnipiac 32%, Reuters/Ipsos 36%), with even Republican support for continued military action now under half (48%).

Domestically, the macro data shifted from “uncertain” to “genuinely stagflation-shaped”: a GDP miss paired with a hot wage-cost print, delivered two days after a Fed that just told markets it isn’t cutting soon. Corporate America split cleanly along AI-capex-return lines — cloud/AI infrastructure beneficiaries (Microsoft, Amazon) surged while AI-capex payers with softer guidance (Meta, Apple, Arm, Qualcomm) fell, a pattern that has now held for multiple consecutive earnings cycles.

On diplomacy, the week produced the first concrete Gaza disarmament mechanism since the broader ceasefire framework, even as neither side has embraced it — a genuine, if fragile, positive development set against an otherwise escalatory week.

A federal appeals court had blocked key parts of the order for 23 mostly Democratic-led states and D.C.; the challengers’ response, along with the federal government’s, is due Monday, August 3 by 4:00 PM ET — a procedural, not merits, deadline. Separately, a First Circuit panel reversed district-court TPS orders for South Sudan and Ethiopia, citing the Supreme Court’s Mullin v. Doe ruling.

Section 26 · learning recapSpaceflight Weekly Recap
