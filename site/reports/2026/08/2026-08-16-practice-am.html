Top Stories

US-Iran ceasefire lapses at the Hormuz deadline; Iran publicly rebuffs Trump’s claim to the Strait

The 60-day Islamabad truce expires with no extension agreed, a third tanker struck in five days, and Iran’s deputy foreign minister answering a presidential boast with one line: it cannot be seized “by tweet.”

The US-Iran ceasefire signed in Islamabad on June 17 runs out today. A senior Iranian source told Reuters over the weekend that no talks have taken place on extending it; Tehran’s position is that Washington must first return to the interim deal and set an implementation timeline before any extension is possible. A senior White House official on Sunday called the situation simply “static.” No party has announced what, if anything, happens at the deadline itself — this report found no confirmation of an automatic snapback to hostilities, only that the truce’s stated term has run out unresolved.

The backdrop hardened over the weekend. President Trump told a rally Friday that after Iran is “very badly defeated,” he would “pretty soon” declare the Strait of Hormuz “a territory of the United States.” Iran’s deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, answered on X Saturday: “The Strait of Hormuz cannot be seized by Tweet, nor by aircraft carrier, nor by issuing an order, nor by an election speech. … This strait will only be closed and opened under Iran’s command.” A third ADNOC tanker was struck in the strait Friday evening with no reported injuries, following two Thursday strikes the UAE has attributed to the IRGC; UKMTO separately reported an unidentified projectile hitting a bulk carrier. A headline surfaced Saturday referencing a further vessel incident, but this report could not reach a fetchable primary account of it and is not reporting a fourth strike as confirmed.

Why it matters Roughly a fifth of global oil supply transits the strait. Markets have so far treated five days of escalating rhetoric and repeated tanker strikes as background risk rather than a supply threat — WTI is little changed since Thursday. That gap between the rhetoric and the price is this week’s central test.
What’s next Whether Monday brings any statement marking the deadline’s actual lapse, and whether a fourth tanker incident gets confirmed. Related, and separately reported: Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon Saturday killed at least 11 people in the deadliest exchange since that ceasefire began in June — Bloomberg linked the timing explicitly to the expiring Iran truce, though the Lebanon and Iran tracks are formally separate conflicts.

Reuters (via The National, Middle East Monitor) · Gharibabadi’s own statement (primary, via CNBC, Fox News, NBC News, The Hill) · UAE Foreign Ministry · UKMTO · Bloomberg, Times of Israel (Lebanon strikes).

Go deeper

Also new this weekend: Iran’s military wrote to the ICRC claiming Qatar is holding three Iranian pilots missing since March; Qatar’s foreign ministry spokesperson denied it, saying Qatari air defenses shot down two Iranian jets that breached Qatari airspace without responding to warnings, and that the remains of one pilot were already repatriated to Shiraz in late July. It is a distinct Iran-Qatar friction point, not part of the US track, but it adds to a regional picture of Iran contesting multiple fronts at once as the ceasefire clock runs out.

Kushner meets Hamas’s leadership directly in Cairo; sits down with Netanyahu today

The first reported direct Kushner-Hamas meeting, plus a Sisi sit-down, precede today’s Israel stop — the most concrete US push on Gaza disarmament since Netanyahu rejected the roadmap’s sequencing on Aug. 9.

Jared Kushner, Tony Blair and Board of Peace envoy Nikolay Mladenov held a rare direct meeting with Hamas’s leadership in Egypt on Sunday, aimed at securing the group’s commitment to disarm under the 15-point US-backed roadmap. Separately, Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi met Kushner in El-Alamein the same day to discuss ceasefire implementation. Multiple outlets report the three envoys are set to meet Prime Minister Netanyahu today. No outlet has reported what, if anything, Hamas agreed to — this is a process story, not yet a resolution.

IDF operations continued in Gaza over the weekend despite the ceasefire: strikes near Khan Younis and Nuseirat killed at least one person and wounded several others Sunday, including, per Palestinian reporting not yet corroborated by a wire service this report checked, a 14-year-old killed by gunfire while reportedly gathering firewood. The IDF said it struck militants it identified as Islamic Jihad and Hamas operatives in the same areas.

Why it matters Netanyahu continues to reject linking a phased IDF withdrawal to phased Hamas disarmament, insisting on full demilitarization first; Hamas says it won’t disarm until strikes stop and aid increases. Today’s meeting is the first attempt at closing that gap with all three US envoys and Netanyahu in the same room since the Aug. 9 rejection.

Times of Israel · Al Jazeera · AP (via KSAT) · Jerusalem Post · Middle East Monitor (Sisi meeting; Gaza strikes, single-sourced on the casualty specifics).

Senate confirms Todd Blanche as attorney general 50-49 in a predawn vote; voting-bill standoff ends without a bill

Two fights that had kept the Senate past its scheduled recess both broke early Saturday morning — one with a confirmation, the other with a punt to September.

The Senate confirmed Todd Blanche as attorney general 50-49 after 4 a.m. Saturday; only Sens. Collins (R-ME) and Murkowski (R-AK) crossed over against him, and every Democrat voted no. Blanche appeared on Meet the Press Sunday, discussing election-integrity policy and saying DOJ’s proposed $1.8 billion “Anti-Weaponization Fund” is not moving forward amid legal challenges.

In the same overnight session, the standoff over the SAVE America Act — the proof-of-citizenship voting bill President Trump had demanded the Senate stay in session to pass — ended without a vote. Republicans told the White House late Friday they would instead try to attach voting-bill elements to a broader budget package, one that would also fund the Iran-related military operation and replenish Pentagon accounts, when the Senate returns Sept. 14.

What’s next Whether the folded-in voting provisions survive the reconciliation process, and whether the September budget fight becomes the next flashpoint over election administration ahead of the midterms.

Washington Post · NPR · NBC News · Meet the Press transcript · PBS, ABC News wire (SAVE Act standoff).

Mail-ballot executive order: Supreme Court still hasn’t ruled; Justice Jackson gives states a week to respond

Judge Talwani’s nationwide injunction stays in force while the emergency stay application sits with the full Court — and a separate $100 bond comes due at the First Circuit Tuesday.

No Supreme Court ruling has issued on the administration’s request to stay Judge Talwani’s nationwide injunction against the March 31 mail-ballot executive order. The Court has not granted an immediate administrative stay; Justice Jackson gave the challenging states a full week to respond to the application, which is still running. Twelve states have joined DOJ in requesting the stay; 23 states are supporting the injunction. Election-law analysts continue to say the order likely could not be implemented in time for the November midterms even if the Court eventually allows it, given the logistics involved.

SCOTUSblog · Election Law Blog · Votebeat.

Ukraine launches its second major drone barrage in three days; Russia strikes Kyiv and Kryvyi Rih

Roughly 600 drones targeted the Moscow region overnight; Russia answered with cruise and ballistic missiles on Kyiv, killing one person in Kryvyi Rih.

Ukraine sent roughly 600 drones at the Moscow region overnight Saturday into Sunday, wounding three people; Russian authorities claimed 201 intercepted over the Moscow region specifically, a separate, later count from the roughly 822 Russia claimed intercepted nationwide after Friday night’s larger barrage. Ukraine also struck a missile-fuel plant in Russia’s Rostov region and sparked a fire at a warehouse near Moscow, per Ukrainian and Russian reporting.

Russia retaliated with Oniks anti-ship missiles, ballistic missiles and more than 100 drones against Kyiv and other cities, hitting industrial sites including a Kremenchuk oil refinery and an ArcelorMittal steel plant; a strike on Kryvyi Rih killed one woman and wounded six. A separate, single-sourced Al Jazeera headline reported a NATO jet downing a drone over Romanian airspace; this report could not verify that claim against a NATO or Romanian government statement and is not treating it as confirmed.

What’s next Whether the Romania drone-incursion claim is confirmed by NATO or Bucharest, which would mark a materially different kind of story — alliance territory, not just Ukrainian or Russian soil.

France24 · Al Jazeera · Ukrainian and Russian government claims, labeled as such and not independently verified.

A five-year-old hardware-wallet flaw has quietly drained roughly $116 million in bitcoin since late July

A weak random-number bug in Coldcard firmware built in March 2021 means any wallet seed generated since then may be compromised — and the running loss total keeps climbing.

An attacker has been draining bitcoin since around July 30 by exploiting a five-year-old firmware flaw in Coinkite’s Coldcard hardware wallets that produced weak, predictable seed generation — in some cases as low as 40 bits of effective key strength instead of 128. Blockchain-intelligence firm TRM Labs put the running total near 1,816 BTC, roughly $116 million, as of its most recent count; earlier tallies from other outlets ranged from $70 million to over $130 million as the incident developed across multiple waves, so treat the exact dollar figure as moving rather than settled. Coinkite has patched the flaw; any user who generated a seed on the affected firmware between 2021 and the patch should treat that wallet as compromised.

TRM Labs (primary research) · Bloomberg · TechCrunch.

Actively exploited VMware vCenter flaw still absent from the federal KEV catalog, and this report could not check directly

361 victims across 47 countries and counting — but a direct check of CISA’s own catalog was blocked again this run.

Unresolved sourcing gap CVE-2026-59310, a critical (CVSS 9.8) directory-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter, remains under active, wide-scale exploitation first observed Aug. 3 — five days after Broadcom’s disclosure. Security researchers now count 361 or more victim IP addresses across 47 countries, with attackers installing reverse-SSH tools for persistent access; there is no workaround, only the patch. Whether the CVE has been added to CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog could not be checked this run — a direct fetch of cisa.gov was blocked by this environment, the same recurring issue prior editions have logged. This report is not asserting a KEV status either way until that check succeeds.

Rapid7 · SecurityWeek · BleepingComputer, TheHackerNews (secondary, multiple, consistent on exploitation scope).

Colombia earthquake toll revised down to 289 as search operations wind down

A rare downward revision, after cross-checking, following five straight upward revisions since the Aug. 10 quake.

The confirmed death toll from Colombia’s Aug. 10 earthquake stands at 289 as of the latest reporting, revised down from the 294 this report carried Friday, after authorities cross-checked and deduplicated hospital and morgue records. 143 people remain missing. Search-and-rescue operations are winding down in the hardest-hit areas of Chocó department and Cali, with the response shifting toward debris clearance; 185,016 people are recorded as affected across 450 municipalities in 15 departments.

Rio Times · US News · CNN.

DRC Ebola outbreak reaches a sixth province ahead of Tuesday’s WHO emergency review

4,665 cases and 2,184 deaths as of the last available count — already the fastest-growing outbreak on record, now spreading geographically as well.

The World Health Organization’s most recent situation report, covering data through Aug. 12, put the outbreak at 4,665 confirmed cases and 2,184 deaths, up 99 cases and 56 deaths from the prior count; no weekend update was published, which is typical for WHO’s reporting schedule. Africa CDC confirmed the outbreak has now spread to a sixth province, Bas-Uele. The IHR Emergency Committee holds its second review of the outbreak’s public-health-emergency status Tuesday, its first since the declaration in May. No vaccine or specific treatment exists for this Bundibugyo-species outbreak, though candidates are in testing.

World Health Organization (primary) · Africa CDC · ECDC.

Politics & Government

A fuller picture emerged this week of how President Trump was moved between aircraft in Turkey on July 8 after intelligence, relayed by Israel, warned of a possible Iranian assassination attempt during his Ankara-to-England leg. Reporting since Aug. 10 describes Trump boarding an alternate military aircraft after being concealed in a catering truck on the tarmac, while Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent flew aboard the publicly visible Air Force One as a decoy; Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth flew with Trump on the switched plane. A follow-up Washington Post report said the CIA held only “low confidence” in the threat before the switch was made, raising the question of whether the operation was warranted. Trump has since confirmed the episode publicly.

Speculation over Karoline Leavitt’s replacement as White House press secretary continued into the weekend with no announcement; names circulating in press and betting-market activity — Scott Jennings, Anna Kelly, Alina Habba and Katie Miller among them — remain unconfirmed reporting, not a White House decision.

Broadcom fell nearly 6% Friday after BofA downgraded its bond rating from overweight to marketweight, flagging that its AI chip-financing platform — the XPV vehicle built with Apollo and Blackstone — could carry roughly $370 billion of senior debt by 2029 at full 20-gigawatt scale, including some $150 billion of new issuance in 2027 alone; under an extreme full-default scenario, BofA modeled potential losses near $42 billion. Credit spreads on the platform had already widened 20–30 basis points since June, meaning bond markets partly priced the risk ahead of Friday’s equity move. Technology & AI

OpenAI released GPT-5.6-Cyber on Aug. 10, its first model built specifically for offensive-security work, available only through a restricted program the company calls Daybreak Red. OpenAI rates it “High,” not “Critical,” on cybersecurity capability under its own Preparedness Framework, but acknowledges it “meaningfully elevates attacker capability if misused” — the company cites a 95% completion rate on advanced cybersecurity evaluation tasks versus 2% for its standard model under normal restrictions. Hardware security keys become mandatory for all Daybreak accounts starting Sept. 1. It is a genuine dual-use release: the same capability that helps defenders find and patch flaws like the VMware vCenter bug above is explicitly the capability OpenAI is restricting access to.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

County reform commission recommends mandatory reassessment every four years

Allegheny County’s Government Review Commission voted unanimously Aug. 12 to recommend that the county adopt regularly scheduled countywide property reassessments every four years; current values still date to 2012. Commission chair Rohit Acharya said the goal is to “depoliticize this process,” making reassessment “a routine, professional, data-driven public function” rather than a periodic political fight. It is a recommendation, not yet law — county council or the executive would need to act on it next.

Government Review Commission (primary) · WESA · PublicSource.

PRT’s Red Line closed between Overbrook and South Hills Junction through Aug. 31

Pittsburgh Regional Transit has closed the Red Line between Overbrook and South Hills Junction for safety-critical track replacement — rail dating to 1982 — through the end of the month. South Hills commuters using the T should expect the shuttle routing for the rest of August.

Pennsylvania

State grapples with fallout as automatic life sentences for felony murder end without a legislative fix

The Pennsylvania Supreme Court’s March ruling in Commonwealth v. Lee — a Pittsburgh case — struck down mandatory life-without-parole for second-degree (felony) murder as unconstitutional and gave the legislature 120 days to write a replacement. That deadline passed July 24 without a new law, and the state’s high court has since rejected prosecutors’ request for another 120-day stay. The practical result, laid out in reporting as recently as today: roughly 1,100 people currently serving life for second-degree murder now have an opening to challenge their sentences, with no legislative framework yet governing how resentencing should work.

Pennsylvania Supreme Court ruling (primary) · Philadelphia Inquirer · Pennsylvania Capital-Star.
