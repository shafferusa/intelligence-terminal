Top Stories

Iran says it is going “fully offensive” in the Strait of Hormuz, with a deadline of weeks

A senior Iranian official tells Reuters Tehran is prepared to strike the US naval presence if the terms of last month’s memorandum aren’t honored — the first public deadline either side has attached to the standoff.

A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran is shifting to a “fully offensive” posture in the Strait of Hormuz, warning of a “timely and precise” strike intended to break the US naval presence in the strait if Washington does not implement the terms of the memorandum of understanding that lapsed Monday — including Iran’s claimed right, under what the official called “point five,” to manage shipping traffic through the strait, which the US rejects. The official put the window at “a few weeks.” The warning follows Monday’s first fatality of the tanker-strike campaign, a projectile that killed a bulk carrier’s chief engineer.

The picture is not uniformly hawkish. Iran’s foreign ministry spokesman separately said Tehran and Oman had reached an understanding on managing strait shipping routes, brokered through Qatar — a diplomatic-track claim that sits uneasily next to the offensive-posture warning, and this report is not able to reconcile the two. Trump, for a second time, threatened to bomb Oman over its mediation role, a threat Oman’s former US ambassador called “puzzling.” The USS George Washington is transiting toward the region to relieve the USS Abraham Lincoln, whose crew has passed 240 days deployed without a port call. No new strike was reported overnight.

Why it matters This is the first time either side has attached a public deadline to the Hormuz standoff. A strike on a US vessel, or a renewed mining campaign, would be the sharpest escalation since the campaign began.
What’s next Watch for any US response to the deadline itself, and for whether the Oman-mediated shipping understanding produces a concrete de-escalation step in the next few days. Oil and VIX are the fastest-moving tells.

Reuters (primary quote) · CNN, Al Jazeera, NPR (corroborating) · Iran foreign ministry statement (Oman claim, single-sourced).

Trump pauses Canada’s 50% tariffs ninety minutes before they were due to hit

A three-day reprieve on ~$20 billion of Canadian goods, announced by social-media post just before the Section 338 tariffs’ midnight deadline, with Carney citing “substantial progress” but no finished deal.

Trump paused the 50% Section 338 tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods — dairy, alcohol, motor vehicles and the broader Annex II list — less than two hours before they were due to take effect at midnight Wednesday, posting that a deal was “pending.” Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed the two governments had made “substantial progress” in a Tuesday call but said “important work” remains. The pause runs roughly three days, through the end of the week; no USMCA carve-out exists in the underlying proclamation if it lapses without a deal.

What’s next The new deadline, effectively around Friday, lands squarely on top of this week’s retail-earnings block. Lowe’s and Target report today under the tariff still hanging over their supply chains.

NBC News, Axios, CP24/CTV (Carney statement) — Confirmed-multiple.

Kushner and Netanyahu edge toward a Gaza disarmament framework; an Israeli strike on a fishing harbor kills six

A US general would oversee an initial Hamas weapons handover under the emerging plan — but Israel won’t commit to withdrawing until disarmament is complete, and strikes continue in parallel with the talks.

After a marathon Monday meeting in Jerusalem that also included envoy Steve Witkoff and Tony Blair, Kushner and Netanyahu agreed in principle that a US general should oversee an initial handover of Hamas weapons, and that the two sides would form joint working groups on demilitarization and humanitarian basics like water. Israel has not committed to the full US plan, and the core dispute — whether the IDF withdraws before or only after disarmament — is unresolved; Netanyahu’s side wants disarmament first. Kushner also held direct talks with Hamas negotiator Khalil al-Hayya in Egypt before the Jerusalem meeting.

Separately, an Israeli strike on a fishing harbor and seaside cafe in Gaza City killed six people, with a child reported among the dead by Palestinian health officials. The IDF says it targeted a meeting of Hamas Nukhba-force commanders, including one company commander and three platoon commanders; Gaza health authorities have not independently confirmed the identities or affiliations of those killed.

Why it matters The sequencing dispute — disarm first or withdraw in parallel — is the same one that has stalled every version of this framework since it surfaced. Strikes continuing alongside the talks underline how far from a ceasefire the two sides remain.

Bloomberg, NBC News, Axios, Times of Israel (framework) · IDF statement, Gaza health ministry (strike, casualty identities unconfirmed by Gaza side) — Confirmed-multiple.

Ukraine flies one of its largest drone barrages of the war; Russia kills 10 in a Kharkiv missile strike; Moscow threatens the UK

Roughly 800 drones, a deadly retaliatory strike on a Kharkiv-region town, and now a Russian threat of “consequences” against Britain over UK-made drones used against Russian refineries.

Ukraine launched one of its largest drone attacks of the war overnight — roughly 800 drones by Russia’s own count, the second-largest since January 2025 — striking targets around Moscow, Crimea and the Black Sea and Azov Sea coasts. Russia retaliated with a missile strike, reportedly two small cruise missiles, on a busy intersection in Pechenihy in the Kharkiv region, killing 10 and wounding 17 to 18 people and damaging a post office, shops and homes. Zelensky said Ukraine “will definitely respond,” though no strike specifically tied to that promised response has been separately confirmed.

In a new thread, Russia’s embassy in London threatened the UK with “consequences” over Ukraine’s use of British-made drones — from two UK companies — in six months of strikes on Russian oil refineries, including recent hits near Volgograd and Yaroslavl. The UK Defence Ministry and Manchester Mayor Andy Burnham said Britain remains “100%” committed to supporting Ukraine.

Why it matters Both sides are running some of their largest strike volumes of the war in quick succession, and Moscow explicitly widening its rhetoric to a NATO member is a new escalatory register, not just a Ukraine-Russia exchange.

Interfax-Ukraine, Zelensky statement, Al Jazeera, Bloomberg, Kyiv Independent — Confirmed-multiple.

Two Supreme Court emergency dockets remain unresolved as the ballroom case’s Friday deadline nears

Neither the mail-ballot executive-order case nor the $400 million East Wing ballroom dispute has drawn a ruling, and the Solicitor General has explicitly asked the Court to act before Friday.

The White House’s emergency application to let construction resume on the roughly $400 million East Wing ballroom project during appeal remains before the Court after the National Trust for Historic Preservation filed its response Monday, arguing construction would become “irreversible” if allowed to continue. The Solicitor General has asked the Court to rule before Friday, August 21, when the D.C. Circuit’s injunction otherwise takes effect. Separately, the Court has not acted on the mail-ballot executive-order case either, where Judge Talwani’s injunction now bars USPS nationwide from any further implementation and the Justice Department says it is “reviewing all options.”

What’s next Friday is a hard deadline on the ballroom case; a ruling could come on the shadow docket with no advance notice.

SCOTUSblog, CBS News, PBS NewsHour, ABC News — Confirmed-multiple.

China’s LandSpace lands an orbital rocket booster for the first time

Zhuque-3’s second flight put its first stage down on legs at a Chinese recovery site, making the company the third in the world to land an orbital-class booster — and quietly retiring this report’s own working estimate that the flight was still weeks away.

LandSpace’s Zhuque-3 launched at 7:35 PM ET Tuesday from the Dongfeng Commercial Space Innovation Pilot Zone, deployed the Honghu-03 satellite to its planned orbit, and landed its first stage on legs at a designated recovery site — China’s first successful land-based landing of an orbital-class booster, and only the third such landing by any company after SpaceX and Blue Origin. Fixes made after the vehicle’s first flight — fewer landing-burn engines, added abort logic, improved thermal protection — appear to have worked. Independent trackers had been carrying the flight as provisional for late August; it flew well ahead of that estimate, a correction to this report’s own prior tracking.

Separately, the Chang’e 7 lunar probe and its Long March 5 booster were rolled out to the Wenchang launch pad Wednesday, and China’s space agency says the roughly Aug. 24 launch window remains on track — the clearest evidence yet that the Aug. 10 Long March 7A failure, for which no root cause has still been published, is not grounding the wider fleet.

Why it matters Reusable-booster landing has been a SpaceX and Blue Origin monopoly; a successful Chinese land landing is a real capability milestone, and a reputational counterweight to this month’s Long March failure.

Xinhua, Space.com, Bloomberg (landing) · CGTN, China Manned Space Agency (Chang’e 7 rollout) — Confirmed-multiple.

The VMware flaw finally lands on CISA’s exploited-vulnerabilities list, three weeks after exploitation began

CVE-2026-59310, actively exploited against 361-plus victims in 47 countries, was added to the federal KEV catalog Tuesday — ending a gap this report has flagged for a week and a half.

Partly confirmed CISA added the Broadcom VMware vCenter path-traversal flaw (CVSS 9.8) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 18, with a federal-agency remediation deadline of August 21 — per CISA’s own alert metadata and one security-industry aggregator. This report could not independently corroborate the listing through a second security-press outlet before publication, so treat the KEV addition itself as confirmed-via-primary-adjacent-source rather than fully cross-confirmed; the underlying exploitation (361+ victims, suspected China-nexus actor deploying a Babuk-derived ransomware strain via reverse-SSH backdoor) has been independently confirmed for weeks. Broadcom patched the flaw July 29; there is no workaround short of patching.

CISA KEV alert (Aug 18) · Rapid7, BleepingComputer, TheHackerNews — Single-reliable-source on the KEV-listing detail specifically.

Federal judge permanently blocks the FBI’s move to the Reagan Building, reinstates Greenbelt

A Maryland court finds the FBI and GSA exceeded their statutory authority in redirecting the bureau’s new headquarters away from its congressionally designated site.

US District Judge Theodore Chuang issued a permanent injunction Monday blocking the FBI and General Services Administration from relocating FBI headquarters from its designated site in Greenbelt, Maryland to the Reagan Building in Washington, ruling the agencies exceeded their statutory authority and that redirecting congressionally appropriated funds toward the D.C. site was “arbitrary and capricious.” Greenbelt is reinstated as the selected site.

CBS News, CNN, Government Executive, WTOP — Confirmed-multiple.

Lowe’s beats narrowly on a soft quarter; Target’s print still unconfirmed at press time

A 0.2% comp-sales gain at Lowe’s and an adjusted-EPS beat set a modest bar; Target, TJX and Estée Lauder round out the morning’s retail block against the backdrop of last week’s soft consumer data.

Lowe’s reported fiscal second-quarter net sales of $24.0 billion, up about 1.6% year over year, with comparable sales up just 0.2% — a fifth straight positive quarter, per CEO Marvin Ellison, but a soft one, with Pro, online and home-services demand offsetting continued DIY weakness. Adjusted EPS of $4.40 beat the roughly $4.22 Street consensus. Unconfirmed at press time Target was scheduled to report before this morning’s open; the only figures available at press time — an adjusted EPS of $2.05 against a $2.09 estimate, and comparable sales down 3.2% at stores against a 4.3% gain in digital — come from a single wire-syndicated source and had not been corroborated by a second independent outlet or Target’s own SEC filing as of this writing. Treat those Target figures as provisional. TJX and Estée Lauder also report before the open; Walmart closes the week’s retail block tomorrow.

Why it matters This is the first hard read on consumer demand since last Friday’s retail-sales and sentiment misses, and it lands the same morning Canada’s tariff deadline was pushed to Friday — both bear directly on these companies’ cost and demand pictures this quarter.

PR Newswire (Lowe’s release), 24/7 Wall St, Yahoo Finance — Confirmed-multiple (Lowe’s); Zacks-sourced wire coverage — Single-reliable-source (Target, pending confirmation).

WHO reviews the DRC Ebola outbreak without a public outcome as the toll keeps climbing

The Emergency Committee’s second scheduled review of the six-month-old public-health emergency met Tuesday; the director-general calls it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record and still not under control.

WHO’s International Health Regulations Emergency Committee held its second periodic review of the Democratic Republic of Congo’s Ebola outbreak Tuesday — a scheduled reassessment of the Public Health Emergency of International Concern first declared in May, not a fresh declaration. Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus told the committee the outbreak, now roughly 4,945 confirmed cases and 2,325 deaths across six provinces and 55 health zones, is the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record and still spreading outside known contact-tracing chains. No formal outcome statement or updated recommendations from the committee had been published as of this writing.

Trump-endorsed candidates won contested Republican primaries in Florida and Wyoming; Alaska’s top-four blanket primary sends an incumbent Republican and a Democrat-aligned former House member through to the ranked-choice general.

Mike Dunleavy term-limited, remained too close to call as of this writing.

Pittsburgh · Allegheny County

Judge orders Allegheny County to conduct its first property reassessment since 2012

Common Pleas Judge Kenneth Valasek ruled the county must have “materially and substantially commenced” a countywide reassessment of its 585,000-plus properties by July 1, 2027, completed by 2032, with regular reassessments required going forward. The ruling, in Laun v. Allegheny County, ends more than a decade of stalemate on an issue that has produced significant tax-base inequities across the county’s municipalities and school districts, South Hills communities included; reassessments must be revenue-neutral under state law.

Allegheny County government bulletin (primary) · TribLive, Axios Pittsburgh, WESA, PublicSource.

PRT opens final comment period on its Bus Line Refresh network overhaul

Pittsburgh Regional Transit’s proposed redesign adds 14 routes, eliminates 20 and modifies 56 of the current network, aiming to raise the share of routes at 30-minute-or-better frequency from 27 to 43 and to boost weekend service. Public comment runs through September 30, with hearings before the board’s final vote.

PRT Engage PRT site (primary) · Pittsburgh Post-Gazette, WESA.

Pennsylvania

Shapiro makes his data-center power rules binding, among the strictest in the country

Gov. Josh Shapiro signed an executive order converting his previously voluntary “Governor’s Responsible Infrastructure Development” principles into binding requirements: AI data-center developers must supply and pay for their own power, are barred from the state’s fast-track permitting process, cannot use NDAs to shield terms, and the Public Utility Commission is directed to ensure data centers — not residential or business ratepayers — bear grid-upgrade costs. The move follows the GOP-controlled state Senate declining to act on the House-passed version of the same rules.
