Top Stories

Merck and Moderna’s personalized cancer vaccine clears its first Phase 3 trial — and Moderna’s stock nearly triples

The Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial is the first positive late-stage readout for an individualized, mRNA-based cancer therapy anywhere; it sent the S&P 500 health care sector to a record.

Merck and Moderna announced Wednesday that intismeran autogene — a personalized mRNA vaccine designed around each patient’s own tumor mutations — met the primary endpoint of recurrence-free survival, plus the key secondary endpoint of distant metastasis-free survival, when combined with Merck’s Keytruda in patients with high-risk, surgically resected stage IIB-IV melanoma. The trial randomized 1,137 patients 2:1 to the combination versus Keytruda alone. The companies’ own joint release calls it the first positive Phase 3 result for an individualized neoantigen therapy, and for an mRNA-based cancer therapy specifically — a genuinely new category of medicine clearing its first large controlled trial, not an incremental update to an existing one.

The market reaction was the sharpest of the year on this outlet’s watchlist: Moderna’s stock (not normally tracked here, checked specifically today) closed near $174, up roughly 177% from Tuesday’s $62.96. Merck rose 12.60% to $152.20, a fresh 52-week high. The rally spread well beyond the two companies — BioNTech (not a Merck/Moderna partner, but another mRNA-platform maker) rose about 22%, Novavax about 11% — and the S&P 500’s health care sector (XLV) gained 3.51% to close at a record, the best-performing of the 11 sector SPDRs by a wide margin. Vertex, Regeneron, Thermo Fisher and Danaher all rose 3.8-6.0% alongside it, more consistent with a sector-wide re-rating than pure single-stock enthusiasm.

Why it matters Personalized neoantigen vaccines have been a research thesis for over a decade without a positive Phase 3 to point to. A win here is a proof of concept for the whole mRNA-oncology approach, not just this one melanoma indication — which is almost certainly why the rally reached companies with no direct stake in this specific trial.
What’s next Regulatory filings based on this data, and whether Merck/Moderna or competitors extend the same platform to other cancer types where neoantigen vaccines have been tested with less success so far.

Merck.com and Moderna (news.modernatx.com) joint press release (primary) · this run’s own Yahoo Finance data for the stock moves.

UAE suspends all trade with Iran after saying it shot down two missiles fired from Iranian territory; Tehran denies firing anything

The UAE Foreign Ministry halted all trade, exchanges and financial transactions with Iran “until further notice” — a partner that supplies more than 30% of Iran’s imports — while Iran’s foreign ministry calls the missile claim baseless.

The United Arab Emirates said Wednesday its air defenses detected and intercepted two ballistic missiles launched from Iranian territory, one falling inside UAE territorial waters and one outside, with no reported casualties or damage. The UAE Foreign Ministry responded by suspending all trade, exchanges and financial transactions with Iran “until further notice,” calling the incident part of a regional escalation undermining peace and security. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei flatly denied Iran launched any missiles, calling the claim baseless and suggesting a false-flag framing — this outlet treats the missile launch itself as disputed between the two governments, with no independent forensic confirmation of origin found. The economic step is not in dispute: the UAE supplies more than 30% of Iran’s imports, and Iran’s economy is already under strain (the IMF has forecast roughly 70% inflation and a 5.4% contraction this year).

The suspension lands on top of an already-live Hormuz standoff: a projectile killed a tanker’s chief engineer Monday, and a senior Iranian official told Reuters (still single-source, no on-record Iranian confirmation) that Tehran is shifting to a “fully offensive” posture with a self-set deadline of “a few weeks.” Separately today, President Trump said the US Navy’s Hormuz posture has been “extremely effective,” that the strait is “open and operating,” and that the US has “total control” there — a status-quo restatement, not a new de-escalation signal.

Why it matters Whichever side is telling the truth about the missiles, a full UAE-Iran trade halt is a real economic escalation between two Gulf states, arriving while the underlying US-Iran standoff is already unresolved.

UAE Foreign and Defence Ministry statements (primary, via wire) · Washington Post, Fortune, Al Jazeera, Xinhua · Iranian foreign ministry (denial).

Markets snap a three-day losing streak as the Treasury doubles bond buybacks and a split Fed weighs a hike it didn’t take

The 20-year auction came in soft but well short of the record some feared after Treasury increased its long-bond buyback size; July’s FOMC minutes show three regional presidents wanted to raise rates, not cut them.

The S&P 500 rose 0.21% to 7,707.98, the Nasdaq 0.16% to 26,331.09, the Dow 0.22% to 53,463.05 and the Russell 2000 0.50% to 3,032.94, ending a three-session losing streak. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.65% from Tuesday’s 4.71%, and TLT rose 1.67% — a likely contributor to the equity gain, not a confirmed single cause. Treasury increased the size of its liquidity-support buyback operations for long-dated coupons, from roughly $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation, reported by multiple outlets to have pulled 20-year yields down in the run-up to today’s auction. That auction (20-Year Bond, $16 billion, 1:00 PM ET) priced at a 5.204% high yield — the second-highest on record for the tenor, but below the roughly 5.27% level the when-issued market had been signaling this morning, and short of October 2023’s 5.245% record. Demand was soft on its own terms: bid-to-cover fell to 2.53 from 2.64 in July and 2.75 in June.

The Federal Reserve released minutes from its July 28-29 meeting at 2:00 PM ET, confirming the 9-3 vote to hold the federal funds rate at 3.50-3.75%. The three dissents — Cleveland’s Beth Hammack, Minneapolis’s Neel Kashkari and Dallas’s Lorie Logan — each wanted a 25-basis-point hike, an unusual hawkish dissent rather than the more familiar dovish kind. The minutes describe “many participants” as assessing that further tightening would likely be necessary if inflation did not decline, with “several” explicitly favoring a hike at the meeting itself. Separately, Chair Kevin Warsh raised the idea of cutting the FOMC’s meeting count from eight per year to six, to let more data accumulate between decisions; no vote was taken and the 2026 calendar is unchanged.

Why it matters A committee with three sitting presidents publicly on record wanting to hike, released the same afternoon Treasury had to lean on its own buyback tool to keep a long-bond auction from setting a record, is not a market pricing in confident disinflation.

Correction: Target’s real quarter was a strong beat, not the miss this morning’s edition described

This morning’s report cited a single wire-syndicated figure that turned out to be last year’s EPS attached to this year’s quarter. Target’s actual, SEC-filed results show comparable sales up 3.8% and EPS more than double what was published.

Target’s own second-quarter release, filed with the SEC this morning, shows net sales up 5.3% to $26.5 billion, comparable sales up 3.8% (traffic up 3.6%), store comps up 2.7% and digital comps up 8.7% — broad-based growth across channels, demographics and all six core merchandising categories. GAAP and adjusted EPS were $4.11, more than double the $2.05 figure this morning’s edition reported as this quarter’s result. That $2.05 figure is real, but it is last year’s second-quarter EPS, which Target’s own release cites for comparison — the prior single-source wire report this outlet relied on this morning appears to have attached the wrong year’s number to the current quarter, and it went to print unverified against a primary filing. This quarter’s result included a $994 million pretax tariff-refund benefit ($1.65 of the $4.11); excluding it, adjusted EPS still rose roughly 20% year over year. Target raised full-year guidance to roughly 5% sales growth and EPS of $9.90-$10.90. The stock rose 4.28% to $159.00, and Gordon Haskett, Wolfe Research, Jefferies and Wells Fargo all raised price targets or ratings.

Why it matters This is a same-day correction to this outlet’s own reporting, not a company restatement — the error originated in a secondary wire source this morning’s edition treated as sufficient before a primary SEC filing was available. Logged to the corrections ledger.

Target Corporation SEC 8-K/press release (primary, verified directly this run) · CNBC, Bloomberg, StockTitan.

Marvell wins an expanded custom-chip deal with Google, jumps 10%; Broadcom falls on the read that it’s lost exclusivity

A newly filed SEC disclosure shows Marvell issued Google a warrant for nearly 59 million shares tied to a custom-silicon partnership spanning Google’s TPU ecosystem — territory investors had treated as Broadcom’s alone.

Marvell disclosed in an SEC 8-K filed today that it expanded its commercial relationship with Google on July 29 to develop custom semiconductor products — AI inference accelerators, storage controllers, network interface controllers, memory-interface controllers and near-memory compute — attached to Google’s TPU ecosystem. On August 18, Marvell issued Google a warrant to purchase up to 58,970,907 shares at $206.58 each; roughly 1.36 million shares vest quarterly over the first year, with the remainder vesting on Google’s discretionary purchases through fiscal 2033. Marvell’s stock rose 9.85% to $237.27. Broadcom fell 4.61% to $362.48, on top of pressure already building from the VMware exploitation story below and a BofA credit downgrade last week — the read from CNBC and other outlets is that the market had treated Google as more exclusively Broadcom’s TPU partner, and this filing undercuts that.

A Meta whistleblower testifies the company took a “don’t ask, don’t tell” approach to underage users — as Zuckerberg testifies the same day, in a different trial

Two separate Meta trials are underway in California, and outlets are conflating them: Zuckerberg testified Wednesday in a Los Angeles state-court case, not the federal 29-state trial in Oakland.

In the federal trial in Oakland (29 states led by California, Colorado, Kentucky and New Jersey, before Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers), day two’s testimony came from Arturo Béjar, a former Meta engineer turned whistleblower, who said the company took a “don’t ask, don’t tell” approach to users under 13 and consistently prioritized engagement and ad revenue over safety despite internal warnings. Meta’s cross-examination of Béjar began today and is ongoing; Mark Zuckerberg remains on the states’ witness list for roughly three hours of testimony at an unspecified later date in that case.

Separately, in a Los Angeles state-court trial consolidating roughly 1,000 personal-injury suits (JCCP 5255, before Judge Carolyn Kuhl), Zuckerberg himself testified Wednesday — on teen Instagram use, his past congressional testimony, and Instagram beauty filters. His entourage was threatened with contempt for wearing recording-capable Meta AI glasses inside the no-recording courtroom. This report keeps the two cases distinct because several outlets have not.

Gaza harbor-strike toll rises to seven as the disarmament sequencing dispute hardens rather than resolves

A seventh victim died of injuries from Tuesday’s strike; a day-old analysis finds Israel has reduced the pace of strikes without moving off its no-withdrawal-before-disarmament position.

Gaza health officials say the death toll from Tuesday’s Israeli strike on a fishing-harbor cafe in Gaza City rose to seven today after Musab Muhammad Madi died of his injuries, with 14 wounded. The IDF says the strike targeted a meeting of Hamas Nukhba-force commanders, including an alleged participant in the October 7 attack — a claim Gaza health officials have not independently confirmed. On the broader disarmament track, an analysis published today (Foundation for Defense of Democracies) describes Israel as having reduced the pace of strikes since the Kushner-Netanyahu framework meeting while still rejecting an initial IDF withdrawal, and cites IDF figures counting 16 ceasefire violations between July 31 and August 18. Netanyahu continues to insist Israeli forces stay until Hamas is fully disarmed — a hardening of the position this outlet has tracked since August 9, not a resolution of it.

Al Jazeera, Al-Monitor, The National, IMEMC (toll) · Foundation for Defense of Democracies (analysis) · IDF statement (target claim, unconfirmed by Gaza officials).

The VMware flaw exploited in 47 countries is now confirmed well beyond one report, and Broadcom absorbs a second hit today

Independent security researchers at half a dozen outlets now corroborate the scope of CVE-2026-59310’s exploitation, with a federal patch deadline two days away and no workaround available.

Multiple independent security-research outlets — The Hacker News, SecurityWeek, Dark Reading, Rapid7 and others — now separately confirm the scope of active exploitation of VMware vCenter’s CVE-2026-59310: 361 compromised IPs across 47 countries, concentrated in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran and France, with attackers deploying a reverse-SSH tool for persistence since first contact around August 3, five days after Broadcom’s initial disclosure. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Monday with a federal remediation deadline of Friday, August 21; Broadcom says no workaround exists, only patching. No attribution to a specific threat actor is confirmed by any outlet. Broadcom’s stock fell 4.61% today — see the Marvell story above for what this report believes is the larger driver, though the VMware overhang and last week’s credit downgrade are compounding factors this report cannot fully separate.

Rapid7, The Hacker News, SecurityWeek, Dark Reading · CISA KEV catalog (primary, via alert stream).

WHO keeps the DRC Ebola outbreak a declared global emergency as confirmed cases pass 5,000

Following Tuesday’s Emergency Committee review, the World Health Organization says the outbreak remains a public-health emergency of international concern, now the deadliest and fastest-spreading in the DRC’s recorded history.

The World Health Organization confirmed today that its International Health Regulations Emergency Committee, meeting Tuesday for its second review, recommended the DRC Ebola outbreak remain a Public Health Emergency of International Concern — no de-escalation. Updated figures cited by Director-General Tedros put the outbreak at 5,021 confirmed cases and 2,378 deaths across 55 health zones in six provinces, a case-fatality rate near 47%, confirmed as the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record and the fastest-spreading.
