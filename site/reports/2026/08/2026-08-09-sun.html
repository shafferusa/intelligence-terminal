Friday's session itself was unusually broad and unusually violent in single names: SpaceX (SPCX) added roughly 23% across Thursday-Friday post-earnings/post-lockup (a short squeeze, not a lockup-driven selloff), Airbnb (ABNB) jumped 17.4% on a Q2 beat-and-raise, and a cluster of small-cap defense/space names (RDW +14.9%, FLY +11.0%, PLTR +10.3%, LUNR +9.8%, RKLB +9.5%) rallied together.

Iran calls a Hormuz deal “close” but rejects resuming direct US talks; a fourth Gulf tanker incident emerges

Iranian FM statement Sat Aug 8 · Reported Sun Aug 9 · Last checked 09:20 ET

What happened
Iranian FM Abbas Araghchi said Saturday that an Oman-brokered arrangement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is “close” but is not sufficient by itself — reopening is also conditioned on the US “making amends” for alleged violations of the underlying ceasefire memorandum. Iran's proposed transit arrangement would reportedly ban American and Israeli vessels from the strait entirely and fine violators up to 20% of cargo value, with Iran itself controlling navigation and security. Bloomberg separately reported Sunday that Iran has rejected resuming direct US talks “for now,” pending US compliance with the June memorandum of understanding.
What is confirmed
Araghchi's public statement and its terms (Bloomberg, Japan Times, CNN, NPR, PBS — Confirmed-multiple). No independent confirmation of a signed deal, a breakdown, or any new US/Israeli strikes since Friday.
What remains uncertain
Whether Iran's posture is a negotiating tactic ahead of a near-term deal or a genuine stalemate; no scheduled diplomatic event this week is confirmed to resolve it.
Context
This is a direct continuation of the disputed Aug 1–2 “cancellation” of planned US/Israeli strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure, which Iran denied requesting at the time. Separately, a fourth maritime incident in the region emerged this run: the Liberia-flagged bulk carrier Minoan Pioneer was struck by an unidentified projectile near Oman's Al Khasab on Aug 3, causing a blackout and fire, with one crew member still reported missing — a distinct incident from the two LNG-tanker strikes (Gaslog Salem, Gaslog Shanghai) tracked in last week's edition.

No specific implementation timeline reported yet; watch for joint military exercises or procurement announcements.

Other geopolitical threads

Russia-Ukraine: The late-July Poland missile-crash incident appears to have de-escalated without a formal NATO Article 4 consultation invoked, per the last available statement from NATO's SACEUR. The war itself continued at high intensity this weekend: Russia struck Ukraine overnight Aug 8 with roughly 151 strike UAVs, six Iskander-M missiles and S-400-launched munitions across ten regions including Kyiv, Kharkiv and Zaporizhzhia; Ukraine's Defense Intelligence confirmed a strike on Russia's Ilsky oil refinery in Krasnodar the same night, continuing a 40-day deep-strike campaign against Russian refining capacity. Caution: some casualty figures circulating for Russian losses trace to Russian state-aligned aggregator sites reproducing Russian MoD-style claims — this report treats those as unverified and does not repeat specific tallies.

Ethiopia-Tigray fighting: Ethiopian National Defense Force units clashed with TPLF-linked forces near Shererina, on the Sudan border, Aug 1–2, involving artillery and drones; no official casualty count has been released, and hundreds of civilians reportedly crossed into Sudan. Confirmed-multiple on the fact of fighting; casualty figures remain unavailable. No update on containment was surfaced this run.

Iran-linked water-utility cyberattacks: now confirmed to have touched at least 12 states (up from 7 in last week's edition), with impacts including loss of monitoring/control and, in isolated cases, pressure loss — no confirmed water-quality or contamination incident anywhere. Attribution remains contested: security researchers (citing TTP similarity to the CyberAv3ngers group) suspect Iran, but the FBI/EPA's Jul 30 joint advisory did not definitively attribute the campaign, and President Trump said Friday he does not “think there was an Iranian cyberattack” — a direct, on-the-record contradiction of the security-researcher consensus that this report flags rather than resolves. Confirmed-multiple on scope; Unverified on attribution.

DRC Ebola outbreak: now confirmed as the largest Ebola outbreak in DRC's history, with case counts having crossed 4,000 (most recent figures cited around 4,053 cases / 1,850 deaths, versus 3,626/1,589 two weeks ago), a roughly 44% case-fatality rate for this Bundibugyo-strain outbreak, and still concentrated across the same five provinces (no confirmed sixth-province spread). Children make up about a quarter of cases but a third of deaths. WHO is urging a trial of the Ervebo vaccine despite it not being strain-matched, and a new treatment center opened this week “at the heart of the outbreak.” Confirmed-multiple (Al Jazeera, UN News, WHO, ECDC, CDC).

Kumamoto, Japan earthquake recovery: the death toll rose from 36 to 39 as of Saturday, including at least one confirmed heatstroke-related disaster death among evacuees; roughly 69,400 households remain without running water with no firm restoration timetable, compounded by a record 40.3°C heatwave on Aug 3. The government has designated the event a “specific emergency disaster” and Prime Minister Takaiichi pledged roughly ¥20 billion (~$182M) in recovery funding; the active rescue phase is described as effectively over as Japan transitions to reconstruction. Confirmed-multiple (Japan Times, Japan Digital Agency, Fukuoka Now).

Section 20 · learning · week previewPhysics Week Preview

Physics 11/71 · Graphs of motion (current) → previewing Newton's laws, Forces, Free-body diagrams, Friction

This week's arc turns from describing motion graphically to explaining why motion changes at all. Graphs of motion (position, velocity and acceleration plotted against time) are the visual toolkit for reading a system's behavior at a glance; Newton's laws then supply the causal machinery underneath those graphs — inertia, force equals mass times acceleration, and action-reaction pairs. Forces and free-body diagrams turn that abstract law into a concrete accounting method for every push and pull acting on an object, and friction — the resistive force between surfaces — is the first specific force examined in detail, setting up the same equation-of-motion habits this week's spaceflight track (staging, thrust-to-weight) and quant track (autoregression) each apply in their own domains.

Section 21 · learning · week previewSpaceflight Week Preview

Spaceflight 11/75 · Staging (current) → previewing Thrust-to-weight ratio, Atmospheric pressure, Drag, Max Q

Staging — dropping spent rocket mass along the way to orbit — is why every orbital launch vehicle is built as a stack rather than a single tank. This week's sequence moves from that structural idea into the forces a vehicle actually fights during ascent: thrust-to-weight ratio determines whether a rocket can even lift off (it must exceed 1) and how hard it accelerates once flying; atmospheric pressure and drag describe the resistance the vehicle pushes through, which thins with altitude; and Max Q, the point of maximum aerodynamic stress, is where those two effects combine into the single most structurally demanding moment of a normal ascent — directly relevant to reading Monday's Zhuque-3 and Starlink launch attempts in Section 13.

Section 22 · learning · week previewQuant/ML Week Preview

Quant/ML 11/118 · Autoregressive Model AR(1) (current) → previewing Moving Average Model MA(q), MA(1), ARMA(p,q), CAPM Alpha Formula

AR(1) forecasts a series' next value from a weighted share of its own immediately preceding value — the simplest possible use of last week's autocorrelation idea. This week's sequence generalizes that in two directions: the moving-average family (MA(q), then the single-lag case MA(1)) instead models a series as a weighted sum of past random shocks rather than past levels, and ARMA(p,q) combines both approaches into one model. The week closes with a sharp turn from time-series forecasting to asset pricing: the CAPM alpha formula, which measures a portfolio's excess return above what its market exposure (beta) alone would predict — the standard yardstick this report implicitly reaches for whenever it separates a stock's own-news move from a broad-market one, as in this week's SpaceX and Palantir stories (Section 16).
