Top Stories

Hormuz strike kills a crew member as the US-Iran ceasefire deadline lapses; Trump threatens Oman

A bulk carrier’s chief engineer is dead after an unidentified projectile hit the vessel overnight — the first fatality in a month of Hormuz tanker strikes — while Trump threatens a third country over the stalled reopening talks.

UKMTO reported early Tuesday that a vessel — identified by maritime-security sources as the Greek-owned bulk carrier Minoan Dignity — was struck by an unidentified projectile while transiting outbound through the Strait of Hormuz, with the ship’s Company Security Officer reporting the incident at 1:35 AM UTC. The strike caused an engine-room fire and killed the vessel’s chief engineer — the first confirmed fatality in the string of Hormuz incidents this report has tracked since early August; prior strikes on ADNOC-linked vessels caused no reported injuries. The Omani Coast Guard is assisting the remaining crew; no attribution for the projectile has been reported, and no environmental impact has been described. Vessel crossings remain in the single digits per day, down from more than 130 before the war.

The strike lands hours after the 60-day Islamabad ceasefire’s deadline passed with no extension. Trump said Monday he is “not in a hurry” to extend it and called Iranian negotiators “good poker players, but they’re dying” — and, asked about active Iran-Oman talks on reopening the Strait, said he would “bomb” Oman if it “gets in the way.” Iran confirmed it is in active talks with Oman on a reopening arrangement; Tehran’s deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, has separately reiterated that the Strait “will remain Iranian” and cannot be seized “by tweet, nor by aircraft carrier.” One Pakistani wire report claimed the US and Iran had agreed to extend the ceasefire; that conflicts with Trump’s on-record refusal and is not adopted here.

Why it matters This is the first Hormuz incident with a confirmed death, and it lands the same day Washington is publicly threatening a mediator rather than just Iran. Oil and equities have so far priced five-plus days of tanker strikes as background risk; a fatality changes the character of that bet.
What’s next Whether Iran, the US or a third party claims or denies responsibility for the projectile, and whether the Oman-mediated reopening talks continue despite Trump’s threat.

DRC’s Ebola outbreak becomes the deadliest the country has ever recorded; WHO reconvenes today

The confirmed toll has passed the 2018-2020 eastern DRC epidemic’s death count, with WHO’s second emergency review of the outbreak’s global-health-emergency status scheduled for today — no outcome published yet.

WHO and Africa CDC figures as of August 16 put the outbreak at 4,945 confirmed cases and 2,325 deaths, up from 4,727 and 2,214 on August 15 — 101 new infections in the preceding 24 hours alone. The death toll now exceeds the 2,299 recorded in the 2018-2020 eastern DRC epidemic, making this the deadliest Ebola outbreak in the country’s history, with a case-fatality rate near 46%. At least 155 healthcare workers have been infected and 45 have died. The World Health Organization’s International Health Regulations Emergency Committee was scheduled to hold its second review of the outbreak today — its first since the May 17 declaration of a public-health emergency of international concern — but no outcome or statement had been published as of this writing.

What’s next Whether the committee renews, narrows or expands its recommendations; this report will carry the outcome once WHO publishes it.

World Health Organization, Africa CDC (primary) · CIDRAP.

Home Depot beats on both lines under interim leadership, reaffirms guidance

The first quarter reported since CEO Ted Decker’s medical leave began shows adjusted EPS of $4.92 against a roughly $4.71-4.73 consensus and sales up 5.7%, with full-year guidance held.

Home Depot reported fiscal Q2 results before Tuesday’s open: sales of $47.9 billion, up 5.7% year over year, against a consensus clustered around $47.2-47.5 billion, with total comparable sales up 1.7%. Adjusted diluted EPS came in at $4.92 versus $4.68 a year earlier and a roughly $4.71-4.73 consensus — a clear beat on both lines. The company reaffirmed full-year guidance (comparable sales flat to up 2%, total sales growth of roughly 2.5-4.5%), citing IEEPA tariff refunds partly offsetting fuel, energy and input-cost pressure. This is the first quarter reported under the interim “Office of the CEO” — Ann-Marie Campbell overseeing stores and operations, CFO Richard McPhail handling finance and the Pro business as interim principal executive — since Decker began medical leave August 12. McPhail said results “exceeded our expectations” with “broad based demand across the business.” Neither the release nor coverage reviewed for this edition addressed Decker’s prognosis or a return timeline. The print lands one day before new 50% Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods take effect; the stock’s reaction was untested as of this writing, before the open.

Home Depot investor relations, SEC EDGAR 8-K (primary).

Kushner and Netanyahu’s Gaza disarmament framework hardens rather than resolves

The two sides agreed disarmament should begin with a US-general-supervised weapons handover, but Netanyahu is publicly doubling down on no Israeli pullback before full disarmament — the opposite of a compromise on the sequencing dispute this report has tracked since August 9.

Netanyahu and Kushner met roughly four hours Monday; the two sides agreed Hamas disarmament should start with a weapons handover supervised by a US general, with “no pullbacks whatsoever” by Israel before full disarmament of Hamas’s light and heavy weapons, per Netanyahu’s public position afterward. Kushner separately pressed Netanyahu to test Hamas’s disarmament commitment with small, verifiable steps rather than waiting for a comprehensive handover; Netanyahu, heading into an October Israeli election, remains skeptical. Netanyahu also pressed Kushner on his direct Cairo meeting with Hamas’s Khalil al-Hayya. No public Hamas response to the general-supervised framework has surfaced. The core dispute this report has followed since Netanyahu rejected the roadmap’s sequencing on August 9 — whether Israeli withdrawal or Hamas disarmament comes first — is not resolved by Monday’s meeting; if anything, Netanyahu’s public framing has hardened.

NBC News, Axios, Times of Israel, Al Jazeera, CBS News.

Two Supreme Court emergency dockets hit their deadlines today; no ruling on either yet

A $100 bond in the mail-ballot case and the preservationists’ response in the East Wing ballroom case were both due today, with an Aug. 21 enforcement date approaching in the ballroom matter — neither has produced a public ruling as of this writing.

Judge Indira Talwani’s preliminary injunction blocking the administration’s mail-ballot executive order required plaintiff organizations to post a nominal $100 bond by today; DOJ’s emergency stay application at the Supreme Court remains pending, with 23 states supporting the injunction and 12 backing DOJ. Separately, the government’s emergency application to let the roughly $400 million White House East Wing ballroom project resume construction during appeal set the National Trust for Historic Preservation’s response deadline for today, ahead of an August 21 date when a lower-court order otherwise takes effect; the government’s filing cites a July 8 intelligence warning of a possible missile threat to Air Force One as part of its national-security urgency argument. Every court that has reviewed the ballroom case so far has ruled the construction unlawful without congressional approval. No outlet reviewed for this edition confirms a Supreme Court ruling or a filed National Trust response as of this writing on either matter; both sit on the emergency “shadow docket,” which can move without advance notice.

Mangione’s Manhattan murder trial postponed over a double-jeopardy motion

Judge Gregory Carro canceled the September 8 trial date and gave the Manhattan DA until October 9 to respond to the defense’s argument that Mangione’s federal guilty plea bars a second state prosecution for the same conduct.

Judge Gregory Carro on Monday postponed Luigi Mangione’s Manhattan state murder trial, which had been set to begin September 8, giving the district attorney’s office until October 9 to respond to the defense’s double-jeopardy motion, with a hearing set for December 10 — roughly a week before Mangione’s December 18 federal sentencing on the stalking charges he pleaded guilty to on August 14. The defense argues the federal conduct and the state murder charge are “coterminous” and that a second prosecution is barred; the well-established “separate sovereigns” exception to double jeopardy — letting state and federal governments each prosecute the same conduct — gives the defense a steep legal hurdle. The Manhattan DA’s office says it remains “prepared to litigate the defense motions” and intends to keep pursuing the state case.

CNN, PBS NewsHour, NBC News, Fox News.

Ukraine flies its largest Moscow-area drone attack in two years; Russia claims 620-plus intercepted

Roughly 800 drones targeted the Moscow region overnight, wounding three including a child, as both sides continue trading large-scale strikes with no sign of de-escalation.

Ukraine launched roughly 800 drones overnight into Tuesday, with Russia reporting 620-plus aimed at the Moscow region — Moscow’s mayor said air defenses downed 180 drones between Monday evening and 5 AM Tuesday, and Russian state media called it the largest Moscow-area drone attack in two years. Three people were wounded, including a 10-year-old girl, per the Moscow region’s governor; a Wildberries warehouse previously damaged in an earlier strike was reportedly hit again. Ukraine’s air force said it downed or suppressed 111 of 147 Russian drones fired at Ukraine over the same period, indicating the exchange continues in both directions.

Al Jazeera, Kyiv Independent, NPR, ABC News, Euronews · TASS (Russian figures, labeled as such).

A widely exploited VMware flaw still isn’t on the federal KEV list — while a newer one got added within days

CVE-2026-59310 has hit 361-plus victims across 47 countries and is now tied, with moderate confidence, to a China-nexus actor and Babuk-derived ransomware — yet a separate AI-framework flaw disclosed far more recently was added to CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog within days.

The VMware vCenter flaw this report has tracked since early August remains under active, wide-scale exploitation — 361-plus victims across 47 countries, concentrated in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran and France — and still is not listed in CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog; direct cisa.gov fetches were blocked again this run, a recurring environment limitation, but the absence is corroborated by security-industry tracking (OpenCVE, SecurityWeek). German incident-response firm QUIRSO assessed with “moderate confidence” that the campaign is run by a Chinese-speaking actor, citing UTC+8 timing patterns and Chinese-language tooling; a separate report describes a suspected China-nexus actor deploying Babuk-derived ransomware via the same flaw, which may be an overlapping or distinct campaign. By contrast, CISA added a different flaw — CVE-2025-62593, a code-injection vulnerability in the Ray AI/ML framework exploited by the “ShadowRay 2.0” cryptomining botnet — to its KEV catalog on August 17, with a federal remediation deadline of August 20. This report could not confirm a documented link between the VMware exploitation and Broadcom’s recent bond-rating downgrade, which analysts instead tie to leverage in Broadcom’s AI-financing platform with Blackstone and Apollo.

TheHackerNews, DarkReading, CISA KEV alert (Ray) · OpenCVE, SecurityWeek.

Canada’s 50% tariffs take effect tomorrow; talks reportedly at an impasse

Prime Minister Carney says he expects to speak with Trump before the deadline, but Canadian officials described the two sides as at an impasse as of Monday on the roughly $20 billion in affected goods.

Section 338 tariffs of 50% on a range of Canadian goods — dairy, alcoholic beverages, motor vehicles and other items worth roughly $20 billion a year, with no USMCA exemption — take effect Wednesday absent a deal. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney called the talks “very delicate and intense” and said he expects to speak with Trump before the deadline; Canadian officials described the two governments as at an impasse as of Monday, with talks reportedly covering “all strategic sectors” including autos. Carney said Canada “will not accept a bad deal.” No resolution had been reported as of this writing. This lands directly against the retail-earnings week Home Depot opened this morning: Lowe’s and Target report Wednesday, Walmart Thursday.

Washington Post, CBC, Bloomberg.
