Top Stories

A federal judge says Washington defied her on mail ballots — then lets the rule take effect anyway

Judge Talwani found USPS “feigned compliance” with her injunction, but declined to block today’s Federal Register publication because the agency itself says it cannot enforce the rule while her order stands.

Monday, the Supreme Court granted the administration and 12 Republican-led states an emergency stay of a separate, broader Talwani injunction — the one blocking Trump’s mail-voting executive order in 23 states — in a 6-3, unsigned shadow-docket order. Justice Jackson dissented sharply, and the majority added a caveat: its action “does not mean that any measure taken by the Government to implement the Order will necessarily be lawful.” Talwani’s separate Aug. 11 injunction, which blocks USPS from implementing new mail-ballot envelope and barcode procedures, was not disturbed by Monday’s order and remains in effect.

Tuesday, Talwani ruled on the narrower fight: she found USPS defied her explicit directive by finalizing the mail-ballot rule and sending it to the Federal Register with an immediate effective date. But because USPS itself has acknowledged it cannot implement the rule while her injunction stands, she declined to order additional relief blocking today’s scheduled publication. Net effect: the rule publishes today, but USPS remains barred from actually enforcing it for the 2026 midterms under her still-standing order.

Why it matters Two federal orders now point in different directions on the same rule, with the practical effect resting on USPS’s own compliance rather than a clean court resolution — an unusual, ambiguous state for an election-administration rule to be in this close to the midterms.
What’s next Both the government and the plaintiff states retain appeal paths on their respective losses. Watch for a fresh emergency filing from either side, or for Talwani to further clarify enforcement once the rule is formally in the Federal Register.

Darline Graham wins the runoff to keep her late brother’s Senate seat in Republican hands

Trump’s endorsement and a six-figure, last-day ad buy from his political operation carried Graham past Rep. Ralph Norman by five points.

Sen. Darline Graham defeated Rep. Ralph Norman 52.5%–47.5% (193,396 to 175,327 votes) in Tuesday’s special GOP primary runoff to permanently fill the South Carolina Senate seat she has held on an interim basis since her brother Lindsey Graham’s death. Trump endorsed and campaigned for Graham, and MAGA Inc. spent $827,000 on her behalf in the runoff’s final day.

Graham advances to the Nov. 3 special general election against Democratic nominee Dr. Annie Andrews.

What’s next The Nov. 3 general in a solidly Republican state; the more closely watched question through the fall will be whether Trump’s endorsement record — already showing ten primary losses this cycle in separate races — keeps its footing in races where he is actively engaged, as this one was.

Iran and Oman float a Hormuz framework — while Tehran flatly disputes Trump’s mine-clearing claim

Iran’s deputy foreign minister dismissed the mine-clearing claim outright: “The Strait of Hormuz has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian.”

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi met his Omani counterpart in Tehran Tuesday, and the two countries floated a framework for a temporary shipping corridor through the strait, including joint mine-clearing and closing the existing UN-authorized southern route, which Iran opposes. Iran’s Deputy FM Kazem Gharibabadi called the talks “constructive,” though no deal is finalized.

Separately, Trump posted Tuesday that “all mines have been removed and/or detonated” from the strait’s international waters, crediting the U.S. Navy, and warned Iran against laying more. Gharibabadi rejected the framing directly and asserted Iranian sovereignty over the strait. Disputed No independent U.S. Navy confirmation of the mine-clearing claim has surfaced; treat it as the administration’s own account, contested by the other party.

Why it matters This is the first time either side has engaged directly with a specific operational claim in this standoff, rather than trading general rhetoric — a sign the diplomatic track (the corridor talks) and the confrontational track (the mine dispute) are now running in parallel rather than in sequence.

Al Jazeera · US News · CNN · The Hill · Jerusalem Post.

Carney matches Trump’s tariffs dollar-for-dollar as the trade war with Canada hardens further

Ottawa is doubling retaliatory tariffs to 50% on US steel, aluminum, dairy and furniture, backed by a C$25 billion domestic support package.

Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed Tuesday that Canada will double its retaliatory tariffs to 50% on US steel, aluminum, dairy, furniture, clothing and electronics, effective Sept. 8 — matching Trump’s own threatened doubling of Canada’s auto and parts tariffs for Jan. 1, 2027. Ottawa is pairing the move with roughly C$25 billion in support for Canadian businesses and workers hurt by the trade war. Carney separately said he will “never let Trump own Canada.” There is no sign either government has reopened formal talks since Carney described them as “collapsed” Monday.

What’s next Sept. 8 is now the hard date on both sides’ retaliation. A forecast on whether talks resume before then is logged in this edition’s ledger.

Bloomberg · Washington Post · Prime Minister of Canada (official).

State Department confirms it is preparing to revoke visas for up to 200,000 people who later sought asylum

A named spokesman put the department on the record for the first time on what would be the largest mass visa revocation in US history.

State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott confirmed the department is preparing to revoke B1/B2 (business and tourist) visas issued between 2016 and 2026 to people who later filed for asylum, coordinating with DHS; Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau also commented publicly. Officials describe a gradual rollout, not a single action, and say revocation would not automatically trigger deportation — affected people would instead be shifted to a different immigration category while their asylum cases proceed. No formal regulation or Federal Register notice has been published.

Why it matters This is a verification-level upgrade, not new substance: what was a single-sourced AP report Monday is now confirmed on the record by five outlets citing the same named officials, though it remains a plan being prepared rather than an executed action.

CNN · NPR · PBS · Al Jazeera · ABC News.

DRC’s Ebola outbreak keeps outrunning its funding, UN says, as it becomes the country’s deadliest ever

The UN’s own Ebola coordinator warned response funding “will run out” within weeks, even as the outbreak becomes the largest ever recorded in the DRC.

The outbreak now stands at 5,514 confirmed cases and 2,642 deaths as of Aug. 23, the largest ever documented in the Democratic Republic of Congo and the fastest-growing on record, concentrated in Ituri, North Kivu and Haut Uele provinces. UN Senior Ebola Coordinator Julien Harneis said directly: “We’re only covered for the next weeks, and very soon funding will run out.” At least 158 health workers have been infected and 45 have died.

Why it matters A named UN official putting a specific timeline on a funding shortfall, in an outbreak already the country’s worst on record, raises the stakes from a case-count update to an active resourcing crisis.

UN News · WHO Disease Outbreak News.

Two Ethiopian peacekeepers killed in an unclaimed South Sudan ambush

UNMISS has demanded a full investigation; no group has claimed responsibility.

Armed assailants ambushed a UN patrol near Padjut village, Jonglei State, Monday, killing two peacekeepers — now identified as Ethiopian nationals — and injuring seven others, including South Sudanese police and civilian UN staff. UNMISS chief Anita Kiki Gbeho and the UN Secretary-General have condemned the attack. No group has claimed it, and no attacker has been identified.

Russia strikes four Ukrainian cities overnight, killing one in Kharkiv

Kyiv, Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia and Dnipro were all hit simultaneously; the Kryvyi Rih shopping-center toll from last week holds at 16 dead.

A missile strike in Kharkiv’s Osnovianskyi district killed one person, per Mayor Ihor Terekhov, in an overnight barrage that also hit Kyiv — where debris from an intercepted drone or missile damaged a 16-story apartment building — along with Zaporizhzhia and Dnipro. The Aug. 21 Kryvyi Rih shopping-center strike, which killed 16, remains the deadliest single incident of the past two weeks and its toll is unchanged.

Nvidia reports tonight into a market already nervous about chips — and a memory fight the White House has joined

Consensus sits near $93.6 billion in revenue and $2.13 in EPS; Commerce Secretary Lutnick has now weighed in against Apple sourcing Chinese memory.

Nvidia reports its fiscal second-quarter results after today’s close, with a call at 5:00 PM ET. Consensus per Finnhub sits at roughly $93.6 billion in revenue and $2.13 in adjusted EPS, up sharply year over year; one widely cited pattern from market previews notes Nvidia has fallen the session after 6 of its last 8 earnings reports, including the last 4 in a row — a historical pattern, not a prediction, but a sign expectations are already stretched.

The report lands inside a wider chip and memory backdrop: Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told the Wall Street Journal the administration “does not approve” of Apple sourcing Chinese-made DRAM from CXMT to address the ongoing memory shortage, a report that had already pressured Micron, Western Digital and SanDisk shares in recent sessions. Separately, Broadcom’s reported AI-infrastructure financing talks — tied in part to Anthropic’s data-center buildout — have grown from roughly $60 billion earlier this month to as much as $100 billion in the latest reporting.

What’s next A forecast on the magnitude of tomorrow’s post-earnings move is logged in this edition’s ledger; Marvell reports Thursday, a direct read-through on the Google TPU partnership.

China’s Chang’e 7 delay hardens toward 2027 as the launch window closes

Multiple outlets now describe the slip as stretching to “at least 2027,” though China’s space agency itself has not confirmed a new date.

Following last weekend’s scrub for Typhoon Narra, Scientific American, SpaceNews and Science/AAAS all now describe Chang’e 7’s delay as likely running into 2027, since the lunar-south-pole Sun-Earth-Moon launch geometry window needed for this mission has closed for the year. Unconfirmed China’s Manned Space Agency has not itself published a replacement date — the 2027 figure is press analysis, not an official commitment, and a tracking database used by the launch industry lists the mission’s status only as “to be determined.”
