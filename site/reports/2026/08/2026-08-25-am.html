Top Stories

Supreme Court lifts injunction on Trump’s mail-ballot order, 6-3 — but on narrow, procedural grounds

The ruling clears the way for the order to take effect in California and 22 other states this year; the judge who blocked it has ordered new briefing due today and could reissue a narrower order within days.

The Supreme Court ruled 6-3 along ideological lines Monday evening, announced at 6:11 PM ET, staying U.S. District Judge Indira Talwani’s June injunction against key provisions of Trump’s March 31 executive order, “Ensuring Citizenship Verification and Integrity in Federal Elections.” The order directs creation of a federal list of eligible voters and instructs the U.S. Postal Service to deliver ballots only to people on it; Talwani’s injunction had blocked it in California and 22 other states plus D.C. that challenged it. Justices Sotomayor and Kagan dissented. The majority’s stated rationale is narrow: that Talwani acted before federal agencies had even finalized how they would implement the order, not a ruling that the order itself is lawful.

A separate, nationwide injunction still blocks the Postal Service piece specifically and is untouched by Monday’s ruling. Talwani has ordered new briefing due today and, per her own stated timeline, could issue a fresh, narrower injunction within days — which would likely return quickly to the Supreme Court on a second emergency application. That step has not happened as of this morning and is forward-looking, not confirmed.

Why it matters This resolves one of two Supreme Court emergency dockets this report has tracked as unresolved for more than two weeks, directly touching how ballots reach voters in 23 states ahead of November’s midterms.
What’s next Talwani’s ruling on the new briefing, expected in the coming days, is the immediate marker to watch.

SCOTUS order and Talwani’s docket (primary) · Washington Post, Bloomberg, NBC News, CNN, CNBC, Axios, NPR, CBS News, The Hill, SCOTUSblog (multiple).

Go deeper

The 6-3 ideological split and the majority’s choice to rule on implementation timing rather than the order’s substance both matter for what comes next: it leaves the underlying legal question — whether the executive branch can condition ballot delivery on a federally compiled eligibility list — open for a later merits case, while letting the order proceed in practice for this election cycle. Talwani’s options on remand are narrow: she can find a way to reissue a version of her injunction that survives the majority’s stated objection (agencies now have finalized implementation plans, closing the gap the Court cited), or she can decline to reissue and let the order stand pending further litigation. Either choice sets up a fast-moving sequence over the next 1-2 weeks that will matter more for 2026 ballot administration than Monday’s ruling did on its own.

South Carolina votes today on whether Trump-endorsed Darline Graham survives a Ralph Norman challenge

Public polling has favored Graham throughout the race, but Norman’s campaign claims a 52-40 internal lead, and reporting describes Graham’s own internal numbers softening in the race’s final days.

Polls close at 7:00 PM ET today in South Carolina’s Republican Senate primary runoff, the last step before a Nov. 3 special general election against Democrat Annie Andrews for the seat of the late Sen. Lindsey Graham. Darline Graham, appointed to the seat and endorsed by Trump, led the Aug. 11 first-round primary 33-24% over Rep. Norman’s campaign nonetheless claims internal polling showing him ahead 52-40, and multiple outlets have reported Graham’s own internal numbers softening in the campaign’s closing days — a genuine conflict between public and internal polling signals that will not resolve until tonight’s count. Trump-endorsed candidates have lost 10 primaries this cycle, one short of his 2022 record of 11.

Iran holds off any kinetic response to Monday’s sanctions as Oman and Pakistan press a diplomatic track

No Gulf state has joined the US campaign and Iran has taken no action against Gulf shipping since Monday’s “Operation Economic Outcast” rollout, even as Iran’s military spokesman claims Hormuz remains under Iranian control.

A day after Treasury Secretary Bessent detailed the full sanctions package targeting Iranian oil brokers, shadow-fleet vessels and shipping, gold and crypto networks across the UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore and Switzerland — with a further action against an unnamed major financial institution reportedly expected by week’s end — Iran has not carried out the all-Gulf oil blockade its security council secretary Mohsen Rezaei threatened over the weekend. IRGC spokesman Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebi instead called the US pressure a “tacit admission” of military failure and claimed, without independent verification, that Iranian forces have pushed back hostile naval activity and that the Strait of Hormuz remains under Iranian control. Iran’s parliamentary national-security committee separately advanced — not yet passed — a bill imposing Hormuz transit fees of up to roughly $2 million on ships from “permitted” countries.

On the diplomatic side, Oman’s foreign minister is visiting Tehran today to discuss Hormuz and maritime safety with his Iranian counterpart, following an Aug. 21 phone call, and Pakistan’s interior minister and army chief met Iranian leadership in Tehran reporting “significant progress” toward de-escalation and restoring an earlier Pakistan-mediated interim framework. Both meetings are independently confirmed; Iran’s military claims of pushed-back naval forces are not.

Why it matters The active diplomatic track running in parallel with the sanctions rollout is a meaningful data point against the near-term risk of the all-Gulf blockade Iran threatened over the weekend — without it, Monday’s escalation in rhetoric would read more ominously.

Al Jazeera live coverage, CNN, Mehr News/IRNA/Press TV (Iranian state media, labeled as such), Business Recorder (multiple).

Fresh overnight barrage hits four Ukrainian cities as the Kryvyi Rih toll holds at 16

Russia launched a mixed missile-and-drone attack on Kyiv, Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia and Dnipro overnight; Ukraine says it intercepted roughly 83% of the drones.

Russia launched an Iskander-M ballistic missile, a Kh-31P guided air-launched missile, cruise-type Banderol munitions and roughly 150 Shahed and decoy drones at Ukraine starting around 6:00 PM ET Monday, according to Ukraine’s Air Force, which says it intercepted 124 of the drones (about 83%). Strikes and debris were reported in at least four cities: fires and building damage from drone debris in Kyiv’s Holosiivskyi district, and damage in Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia and Dnipro, where residential buildings were hit and fires broke out. No casualty figures for this specific overnight strike had been confirmed as of this morning — only property damage so far. Separately, the death toll from last week’s Kryvyi Rih double-tap shopping-mall strike remains settled at 16 dead and roughly 130-140 wounded, unchanged since the weekend.

Carney holds the line on Canada tariffs as Trump’s doubled-tariff threat sits unanswered

No new action from either government overnight; Canada’s Sept. 8 retaliatory tariffs still stand against Trump’s threat to double US auto, parts and steel tariffs on Canada to 50% starting Jan. 1, 2027.

Prime Minister Mark Carney, speaking in Quebec, reiterated Monday that Canada “could not accept what the U.S. had offered, nor could we give what they asked,” and said Canada would continue to match US tariffs “dollar for dollar.” That leaves Canada’s already-announced Sept. 8 retaliatory tariffs on US steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, and electronics on track, with no new escalation from either side since Trump’s Monday threat to double US tariffs on Canadian autos, parts and steel to 50% effective Jan. 1, 2027 — the fourth escalation in this dispute in five days. Auto-sector shares (Ford, Stellantis, GM) remain the clearest market read on the standoff.

Chip and space stocks sell off hard into Wednesday’s Nvidia earnings; a memory-sector selloff adds a second front

Nvidia fell for a seventh straight session and memory names dropped sharply on a report that Apple is testing Chinese-made DRAM, even as no company-specific news explains Monday’s parallel selloff in small-cap space stocks.

Nvidia fell 2.91% Monday, its seventh consecutive down session, with the broader semiconductor complex weak alongside it (SMH -2.43%, AMD -3.49%, Broadcom -2.63%, Taiwan Semiconductor -2.11%) ahead of Wednesday’s after-close earnings, where consensus clusters near $93-95 billion in revenue. A second, distinct pressure hit memory names specifically: Micron fell 5.83%, with SanDisk and Western Digital both down sharply, after multiple outlets reported Apple is testing DRAM from Chinese rival ChangXin Memory Technologies and lobbying to keep CXMT off US trade-restriction lists — CXMT is reportedly weighing a second Beijing fab that could more than double its monthly wafer output. That report traces to one underlying account reproduced across several outlets rather than independent confirmation from Apple or CXMT, and should be read as reported, not confirmed.

Space stocks fell even harder with no identified company-specific catalyst: AST SpaceMobile -9.18%, Intuitive Machines -9.06%, BlackSky -7.78%, Voyager -7.55%, Rocket Lab -5.91%, SpaceX (SPCX) -1.44%. Defense names were broadly weaker too (AeroVironment -7.5%, Kratos -7.17%, BWX Technologies -4.63%, Palantir -2.25%), while financials and consumer staples held up or gained (JPMorgan +1.37%, Schwab +1.20%, Walmart +2.69%, Target +2.69%, Costco +2.50%) — a rotation out of high-beta growth and into value and defensive names that reads as pre-earnings de-risking rather than a reaction to any single piece of news.

Why it matters A market already rotating defensively two days before the week’s largest catalyst raises the stakes on Wednesday’s print — both for what Nvidia says about demand and for whether Monday’s selloff in adjacent high-beta names (space, defense) was really about Nvidia at all, or a separate positioning story.

State Department reportedly preparing revocation of up to 200,000 visitor visas tied to later asylum claims

Officials describe what would be the largest single mass visa revocation in US history; the action is still in planning, and the figure rests on a single wire report reproduced across outlets.

Single-reliable-source Unnamed officials tell the Associated Press that the State Department, coordinating with the Department of Homeland Security, is moving to revoke B1/B2 short-term visitor visas issued between 2016 and 2026 to holders who later filed for asylum — potentially affecting roughly 200,000 people, which the report describes as the largest single mass visa revocation in US history. The action is described as still in planning rather than executed; no formal State Department announcement has been found. Because this traces to one wire report, reproduced by multiple outlets but not independently corroborated, and describes a still-prospective large-scale immigration action, it is labeled single-source pending a second independent confirmation or a formal government announcement.

Associated Press (single primary wire source, reproduced by Spectrum News, PBS, ABC News, US News).

Roman Space Telescope encapsulated for a Sunday launch; astronauts complete a spacewalk today

NASA and SpaceX finished sealing Roman inside its Falcon Heavy fairing Monday, on track for liftoff Sunday, Aug. 30 — not Saturday, a date this report has previously misstated (see Corrections).

NASA and SpaceX completed encapsulation of the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope inside its Falcon Heavy payload fairing Monday, following last week’s completed Flight Readiness Review. Launch remains no-earlier-than Sunday, Aug. 30 at 7:26 AM ET from Kennedy Space Center’s LC-39A, with a backup opportunity Monday, Aug. 31 at 7:22 AM ET — still roughly eight months ahead of the mission’s original committed readiness date. Separately, NASA astronaut Anil Menon and ESA astronaut Sophie Adenot (the first French woman to conduct a spacewalk) began a roughly 6.5-hour spacewalk this morning to finish replacing a space-to-ground antenna on the ISS truss and, time permitting, swap a docking-navigation reflector on the Harmony module — Menon’s third spacewalk this month and the station’s 283rd overall.

Judge orders Allegheny County’s first full property reassessment since 2012; county says it will comply

Pennsylvania

Some districts shift charter students from yellow buses to PRT transit cards after a state ruling

Following a January state Supreme Court ruling that districts may end charter-student bus service as long as they provide a free alternative, districts including Woodland Hills, near Pittsburgh, are moving charter-enrolled students onto public-transit cards instead of yellow buses, citing driver shortages and cost. Charter advocates say transit is less reliable for children. A statewide policy shift with direct effect on families in any district with charter-enrolled students.

Cross-asset

Stocks and bonds moved together Monday — equities rotated defensively even as long-end yields fell 4bp, rather than bonds and stocks pricing different stories.
