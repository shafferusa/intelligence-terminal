Today’s News

Kushner meets Hamas’s leader in Cairo; a Netanyahu sit-down is next

Hamas says it will disarm — but only once Netanyahu commits to the same roadmap publicly, which is exactly what Monday’s meeting is supposed to test.

Jared Kushner, Tony Blair and Board of Peace director Nickolay Mladenov met Hamas’s leadership in Egypt on Sunday, a rare direct sit-down described by people briefed on it as “very productive.” The 90-minute session focused on getting Hamas to commit to practical, sequenced steps under the stalled 15-point roadmap. Hamas officials reaffirmed the group’s willingness to disarm and demilitarize Gaza — but told Kushner they will only begin implementing it once Prime Minister Netanyahu publicly commits to the plan’s terms himself. Kushner is scheduled to meet Netanyahu on Monday, with Blair and Mladenov.

The trip is the first concrete high-level US follow-through since Netanyahu’s Aug. 9 rejection of the document, and it lands with regional pressure building: Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Egypt, Jordan, Qatar and Turkey issued a joint statement this weekend blaming Israel for thwarting the roadmap. Separately, Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon killed 11 people Saturday — the deadliest toll since June’s truce — which Lebanon’s president explicitly linked to the US-backed Gaza talks, and Hezbollah has warned of an “appropriate response.” In a separate radio interview this weekend, Netanyahu also referred to the United Kingdom as “the Islamic republic of Britain,” a remark that drew swift criticism and adds friction to a week already carrying real diplomatic weight.

Why it matters This is the closest the roadmap has come to a real test since Netanyahu’s rejection: Hamas has now put a specific, public condition on the table rather than a general objection, and Monday’s meeting is where that condition either gets met or doesn’t.
What’s next Monday’s Kushner-Netanyahu meeting. Whether Netanyahu offers anything resembling the public commitment Hamas is asking for will likely determine whether this week’s checkpoint becomes next week’s breakthrough or another missed deadline.

ABC News, NPR, Axios, Boston.com (wire) · Times of Israel live coverage · regional-statement coverage via Al Jazeera-linked wires.

Go deeper

The roadmap has now missed two informal deadlines — the original 14-day implementation window that lapsed Aug. 13, and the softer expectation that some negotiating reset would follow within days. What changed this weekend is procedural, not substantive: for the first time, a specific, sequenced condition (Hamas disarms once Netanyahu publicly commits) is on the table rather than a standoff between two general positions. That is progress in form even before it is progress in substance, and it is why this report treats Monday’s meeting as more consequential than the prior week’s passed checkpoints.

Ukraine flies its largest drone attack of the war; Russia hits back across the country

Hundreds of drones toward Moscow, missiles and drones back on Kyiv — a weekend escalation, not a lull, right as both sides head into a week with no ceasefire talks scheduled.

Ukraine launched hundreds of drones at Russia overnight Saturday into Sunday, one of the largest such attacks of the war. Russia’s defense ministry said it destroyed 822 Ukrainian drones, with roughly 600 tracked heading toward Moscow; Russia’s Rostov region took the heaviest hit, with more than 150 drones damaging homes and a railway station and sparking a forest fire, killing five people, while an 83-year-old man died when a drone struck a home in the Moscow region.

Russia retaliated across Ukraine, killing at least seven civilians and injuring 51. Ukraine’s air force said Russia struck Kyiv, Kremenchuk (Poltava Oblast) and Kryvyi Rih (Dnipropetrovsk Oblast) with Onyx supersonic anti-ship missiles, ballistic missiles and 106 long-range attack drones; Ukrainian air defenses intercepted three of the ballistic/cruise missiles and 85 of the 106 drones. Fires broke out across Kyiv, injuring six. Separately, a Ukrainian drone was reportedly downed over Romanian airspace by a NATO jet — a claim this report has not independently confirmed against a primary NATO or Romanian government source and is flagging rather than asserting.

Why it matters This is a scale escalation on both sides at once, not a one-off strike-and-response: Ukraine’s drone count and Russia’s retaliatory footprint (three cities, multiple missile types) both mark this as one of the conflict’s bigger weekends, arriving in the same week Zelensky signed fresh sanctions on Russian missile-program figures.

Kyiv Independent, Al Jazeera, CNN, NPR, Washington Times · Russian Ministry of Defense claims (labeled as such) · Ukraine Air Force statement.

Trump presses his Hormuz “territory” claim; Iran rebuffs it as a new vessel is struck

Iran’s government says there is “nothing like a ceasefire” in place at all, a harder line than the strait dispute itself.

President Trump reiterated over the weekend that he intends to declare the Strait of Hormuz US “territory,” a claim he first floated Friday at a campaign rally in Garden City, New York, and separately said the US has “total control” over the strait and might “keep it.” Iran’s government rejected the claim at multiple levels: Foreign Minister Araghchi’s office said the two countries do “not have anything like a ceasefire” and that no negotiations are underway, while Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said flatly that “the Strait of Hormuz has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian; this strait will only be closed and opened under Iran’s command.”

The rhetorical escalation lands days after Friday evening’s twin ADNOC vessel strikes and Iran’s seizure of a Marshall Islands-flagged tanker, which broke a ten-day quiet stretch in the maritime-harassment campaign. The UK Maritime Trade Operations Centre reported a further bulk carrier struck by an unidentified projectile in the strait over the weekend, a fifth-plus incident this report is tracking without yet being able to attribute. Strait shipping traffic remains near three-month lows.

Why it matters Neither side is using the language of de-escalation. Iran’s explicit denial that any ceasefire exists is a harder position than a dispute over strait sovereignty alone, and it removes the ambiguity this report has previously used to describe the state of hostilities as a fragile pause.
What’s next Watch for a US or Israeli response to the new vessel incidents, and for whether Trump’s territorial rhetoric hardens into a concrete policy step this week.

CNBC, Al Jazeera, CBS News, Fox News live coverage · Notus · UKMTO advisory.

Magnitude-7.7 earthquake kills 53 in eastern Indonesia; tsunami warning lifted

A shallow quake off Flores Island, more than 230 aftershocks, and a second, unrelated 6.9 quake off Sumatra that caused no damage because of its extreme depth.

A magnitude-7.7 earthquake struck off Indonesia’s Flores Island early Saturday local time, roughly 68 kilometers north-northwest of Ende in East Nusa Tenggara province, at a shallow depth of about 10 kilometers. Indonesia’s National Disaster Management Agency put the confirmed toll at 53 dead as of Sunday, with more than 1,300 homes damaged — two-thirds of them severely — along with health facilities, schools and government buildings. More than 230 aftershocks followed through the day, the strongest a magnitude 6.2. A separate magnitude-6.9 quake struck off Sumatra later Saturday, thousands of kilometers away; its extreme depth (about 172.5 kilometers) meant no casualties or damage were reported.

Indonesia’s meteorology and geophysics agency issued a tsunami warning for affected coastlines, urging residents away from beaches and rivers toward higher ground; the warning was lifted later Saturday.

Why it matters Indonesia sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire and has absorbed a run of serious seismic events this cycle; a shallow M7.7 near a populated coastline carries genuine tsunami risk even when, as here, the wave threat did not materialize.

Al Jazeera, NBC News, NPR, ABC News, Bloomberg · BNPB (Indonesia’s disaster agency, primary).

Colombia’s earthquake toll revised down for the first time, to 289

After a week of only-ever-rising figures, cross-checking pulled the confirmed count back from 294 — a reconciliation, not new deaths.

The confirmed death toll from the Aug. 10 magnitude-7.4 earthquake near San José del Palmar, Chocó department, stands at 289 as of Sunday, down from 294 after authorities cross-checked casualty records — the first downward revision this report has tracked in a week where the figure climbed almost daily (132 → 224 → 265 → 273 → 294). Roughly 143 people remain missing, and search-and-rescue operations are described as entering their final phase. The quake affected an estimated 185,000 people across 450 municipalities in 15 departments.

Why it matters A downward revision after a week of centralizing casualty reporting through the presidency is a sign the count is stabilizing, not evidence the disaster was smaller than believed — the affected-population and displacement figures are unchanged.

Rio Times, CNN, Time, Wikipedia tracker · Colombian disaster-management authority (UNGRD), via the presidency’s centralized reporting.

Bitcoin barely moved; Bitcoin ETFs just logged their worst week of outflows in six weeks, while Solana’s funds are doing the opposite.

Bitcoin traded near $63,100 over the weekend, up roughly 0.05% on the day — a tight range reflecting subdued weekend volume, renewed spot Bitcoin-ETF withdrawals and lingering uncertainty over US crypto regulation. Ether held near $1,880 on similarly muted volume. The total crypto market capitalization slipped 0.62% on the week to about $2.24 trillion as of Friday, with regulatory delay and growth-slowdown fears cited as the drag. Solana’s own market cap stands near $43.5 billion.

Why it matters Crypto is the only market with a pulse this weekend, and its own signal is quiet risk-aversion — institutional Bitcoin-ETF money heading for the exits even as prices barely move — a backdrop equities will open into Monday alongside the week’s bigger catalysts.

What Changed in the World

Three checkpoints came and went this week without resolving, and all three now read as more urgent than they did seven days ago, not less. Gaza’s roadmap moved from a flat standoff to a specific, testable condition — Hamas will disarm once Netanyahu commits publicly — which is a real if fragile step forward. The Hormuz maritime campaign moved from a quiet stretch that looked like de-escalation to two fresh incidents and the hardest rhetorical exchange of the cycle, with Trump now talking about the strait as US territory and Iran denying a ceasefire exists at all. And the US economy’s soft-landing narrative, which had strengthened on Wednesday’s cool CPI, cracked on Friday’s retail-sales and sentiment misses — the first data this cycle to suggest the consumer, not just inflation, is the thing to watch.

Underneath the geopolitics, the market’s own structure kept revealing the same pattern: single-name and single-sector volatility (SpaceX, the alt-asset managers, space-sector earnings, mega-cap tech’s AI-infrastructure split) dominated over index-level moves. The S&P 500 finished the week essentially flat. That is not a quiet week underneath the surface — it is several large, offsetting swings that happened to net out close to zero.

Politics & Government

The mail-ballot executive order’s legal fight escalated on two fronts this week: a federal judge blocked the US Postal Service from implementing it, the second concrete court setback, and the Justice Department appealed Judge Talwani’s nationwide injunction to the 1st Circuit while urging the Supreme Court to act, with a $100 bond due at the circuit court Aug. 18. No Supreme Court ruling had landed as of this report’s Sunday-evening cutoff. A second, legally distinct emergency-docket matter is now also before the justices: the administration is asking the Supreme Court to let construction resume on the roughly $400 million White House East Wing ballroom project, after a divided D.C. appeals panel found the White House lacked authority to build it without congressional approval. The panel’s own injunction takes effect Friday, Aug. 21, so a Supreme Court decision on the ballroom is realistically due sometime this coming week.

Luigi Mangione pleaded guilty Friday to federal stalking charges tied to the December 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson, admitting to the shooting; sentencing is set for Dec. 18. His defense has since moved to dismiss the separate Manhattan state murder case on double-jeopardy grounds. New York’s double-jeopardy statute is narrower than the federal constitutional doctrine, but the federal government and New York state are traditionally treated as “separate sovereigns” that can each prosecute the same conduct — the Manhattan DA’s office intends to fight the motion, and it will fall to the state trial judge to decide whether New York’s own statute reaches this case.
