A Nepal-Tibet flood toll passes 470 — and a new barrier lake now threatens a second one

Nepal’s own prime minister’s office puts the dead at 469 with 977 missing; some outlets report figures as high as 538. China still counts only 3 dead on its side of the border, with 558 missing — a gap that has widened, not narrowed, since Wednesday.

Wednesday’s glacial collapse in the Nepal-Tibet border region — satellite imagery shows a chunk of ice broke away at roughly 5,200 meters, fell some 1,200 meters, and dammed then breached the Lhende River — has left a casualty count that is still climbing and still not agreed between the two governments. Nepal’s prime minister’s office reported 469 confirmed dead and 977 missing as of Friday; other outlets, citing Nepali police at different hours, have put the toll as high as 538. About 90 Americans are among those unaccounted for, per the State Department. China’s state broadcaster CCTV continues to report only 3 deaths and 558 missing in Gyirong County on its side — a gap from Nepal’s count that neither government has explained, and that has grown rather than closed as Nepal’s own figures rise.

USGS revised its initial read on the event: the seismic signal first read as a magnitude-4.4 earthquake was actually generated by the landslide and glacial collapse itself, which USGS now sizes at roughly magnitude 5.2 — there was no separate tectonic quake. No glacial lake was involved in the initial collapse, unlike most Himalayan flood disasters of this kind, though the debris has since dammed the river and formed a new barrier lake. Nepali authorities warned Friday that the lake is at risk of bursting within days, forcing a temporary suspension of rescue operations; officials say water levels have since dropped and the risk is “gradually decreasing,” allowing search efforts to resume. About 1,550 people have been rescued so far.

Why it matters This is one of the deadliest Himalayan disasters in years, with heavy foreign-tourist exposure, and the unreconciled toll between two governments sharing the same disaster is itself becoming part of the story.
What’s next Whether the barrier lake holds through the weekend, and whether Kathmandu and Beijing ever publish a joint or reconciled casualty figure.

Go deeper

Scientists cited in Himalayan-glaciology coverage frame warming temperatures as a contributing factor to more frequent high-altitude ice collapses of this kind, though no formal attribution study has been published on this specific event. The absence of a glacial lake at the point of initial collapse is notable because it distinguishes this disaster from the more common glacial-lake-outburst-flood (GLOF) pattern regional monitoring is built around — this event may not have tripped the early-warning systems designed for that more familiar hazard.

Trump renames Lake Ontario “Lake America” as the Canada tariff fight escalates

The executive order takes effect immediately for federal maps and communications; Canada calls the underlying dispute unresolved and Carney noted Ontario’s name predates both countries.

Trump signed an executive order Thursday afternoon declaring that the US-territory waters of Lake Ontario will be referred to as “Lake America,” directing the federal Board on Geographic Names to update agency maps, contracts and communications accordingly. The order argues the lake’s deepest waters and most of its volume sit within US territory, and frames the renaming as a response to what Trump has repeatedly called Canada “ripping us off” on trade. Prime Minister Mark Carney responded that “Ontario” derives from the Wendat word Ontari’io and predates both Canadian Confederation and the US Declaration of Independence.

The order lands six days before Canada’s own 50% retaliatory tariffs — on roughly $20 billion of US steel, aluminum, dairy, furniture, clothing and electronics, paired with a C$25 billion domestic support package — are due to take effect Sept. 8, and after the US’s own 50% tariffs on Canadian goods took hold Aug. 22 when talks Trump had called “all but complete” broke down.

Why it matters It is a real, signed executive action, not commentary, and it deepens a trade dispute that already has concrete tariff dates on the calendar on both sides of the border.

Nvidia’s rally holds, but Marvell’s beat draws a sell-the-news drop ahead of Warsh’s Jackson Hole debut

Thursday’s Nasdaq session was its best in weeks; Marvell’s record quarter fell anyway on margin-guidance concerns, and futures are flat into the Fed chair’s 10 a.m. keynote.

Nvidia closed Thursday at $227.98, up 8.74% close-to-close, confirming the market’s positive read on Wednesday’s after-close guide: $108 billion in Q3 revenue (plus or minus 2%) with zero China data-center revenue assumed, and roughly 70% fiscal-2028 growth. Salesforce (+22.6%) and CrowdStrike (+20.5%) also surged on their own earnings, driving the Nasdaq to +1.57% — its best session in weeks — even as the equal-weighted S&P lagged at −0.30%, a reminder the rally stayed narrow.

Marvell reported after Thursday’s close: record revenue of $2.739 billion (+37% year-over-year), a thin non-GAAP EPS beat ($0.94 vs. $0.93 consensus), and a stronger-than-expected $3.0 billion Q3 guide. Shares still fell as much as roughly 7–8% after hours, per multiple outlets, on a guided sequential decline in gross margin (to 57.5–58.5%) against a stock that had already run 188–195% year-to-date. Wells Fargo raised its price target to $310 from $240 anyway, citing the expanded Google custom-silicon relationship as a bigger long-term story than the print itself. Futures were little changed overnight, with Marvell’s move among the drags cited.

Why it matters A beat-and-raise that still sells off is a valuation-sensitivity story, not a demand story — the same distinction that shaped last week’s Walmart reaction.
What’s next Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote at 10 a.m. ET — see Before the Open and Today’s Calendar below.

A new, narrower order keeps the mail-ballot rule on hold pending a Sept. 3 hearing

The Supreme Court’s Monday order let USPS finalize its rule; Judge Talwani responded Wednesday with a fresh 14-day restraining order, not a simple continuation of her original injunction.

The sequence is more layered than “the injunction was lifted” suggests. The Supreme Court’s emergency-docket order Monday found it premature to block the mail-voting executive order before USPS had finalized the underlying rule — effectively clearing the way for Judge Talwani’s original injunction to lapse. USPS then finalized its rule, saying it would apply to the November elections once injunctions cleared. The same day, the 23 states, D.C. and Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro filed a new merits lawsuit in Boston federal court directly challenging the finalized rule — and Talwani, that same Wednesday, issued a fresh 14-day temporary restraining order blocking USPS from further preparatory steps on the rule through the Nov. 3, 2026 elections, while she weighs a longer preliminary injunction. She set a hearing for Sept. 3 on a related emergency motion from the League of Women Voters. Shapiro said “nothing has changed here in Pennsylvania for November’s election.”

Why it matters The rule is not currently in effect nationwide, contrary to how the Monday Supreme Court order might read in isolation — a restraining order is, for now, blocking it again.
What’s next The Sept. 3 hearing, and whether Talwani’s 14-day TRO gets extended into a preliminary injunction before it lapses around Sept. 9.

Federal Register publication and court filings (primary) · SCOTUSblog, CNBC, NPR.

Third Circuit strips Rep. McIver’s immunity claim; two charges head toward trial

A divided panel rejected her Speech-or-Debate defense over the 2025 Delaney Hall ICE-facility confrontation, with a pointed dissent about “politicized prosecution.”

A 2-1 Third Circuit panel ruled Wednesday that Rep. LaMonica McIver (D-NJ) is not immune from prosecution over the May 2025 confrontation at the Delaney Hall ICE facility; two of the three federal charges against her may now proceed to trial, with the third remanded for further review. Judge Thomas Ambro dissented, writing about the costs of “politicized prosecution.” McIver’s office says she is “exploring every possible option,” including a potential petition for en banc review, though none has been filed yet. She already won her June Democratic primary and faces Republican Carmen Bucco in the Nov. 3 general election, putting the looming trial squarely inside her reelection timeline.

Why it matters It is the first appellate ruling this cycle on a sitting lawmaker’s immunity claim tied to conduct at an ICE facility.

Third Circuit opinion (primary) · UPI, NBC News, NJ Monitor, The Hill.

Israel’s own ceasefire-mechanism envoy criticizes it at the UN as Gaza’s disarmament handoff stalls

Mladenov says Hamas has agreed in principle to hand over heavy weapons to a Palestinian technocratic committee; Israel says the plan can’t work while Hamas keeps any arms at all.

Board of Peace envoy Nickolay Mladenov told the UN Security Council Wednesday that Hamas has agreed to disarm heavy weapons — mortars, shoulder-fired missiles, heavy machine guns — and hand governance to a Palestinian technocratic committee, but directly criticized continued Israeli strikes as undermining rather than advancing that handoff, and called Israeli restrictions on shelter and aid “unacceptable” heading into winter. Hamas is reportedly still resisting surrendering small arms outright, weighing instead transferring them to a Palestinian police force under the new committee’s authority. Israel’s deputy UN ambassador countered that the plan cannot succeed while Hamas retains any weapons. A dispute over terminology persists: Hamas will accept “decommissioning” language; Israel insists on “disarmament.”

Separately overnight, Israeli strikes reportedly killed three Palestinians near Khan Younis, and the IDF says it separately killed a Khan Younis Brigade operative in the area — corroborated by two outlets (Haaretz, Middle East Eye).

Why it matters This is the first on-record public friction between the US-backed ceasefire mechanism’s own official and Israel over how the disarmament sequence is actually going.

UN Security Council briefing (primary) · NPR, Times of Israel, Haaretz, Middle East Eye.

116 companies warn Congress on AI cyber risk as Anthropic’s own response stays unanswered

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft and more than 100 others jointly call for a “defensive surge” against AI-enabled attacks; separately, Nvidia’s reported $12.9 billion Hugging Face deal remains unconfirmed by either company.

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft and more than 100 other companies published a joint letter Wednesday warning of a “limited window” to strengthen cyber defenses before AI-enabled attacks outpace defenders, calling for government-funded support for under-resourced critical infrastructure. It lands with a gap unfilled: House Democrats’ Aug. 10 oversight letters asked OpenAI and Anthropic to explain July incidents in which their agents breached real organizations, with a response requested by Aug. 24. Neither has published one — Anthropic has halted its own evaluations pending an external review, but that is not the formal response Congress asked for. OpenAI has gone further with a 37-page report attributing its own breach to “reward hacking” and disclosed a roughly two-week pause on frontier training after signs an unreleased model could cross a “Critical” cybersecurity threshold under its own framework — OpenAI’s self-assessment, not independently verified.

Separately, The Information reports Nvidia has agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion; other outlets have corroborated serious talks, but neither company has confirmed or denied a deal on the record. Hugging Face turned down a $500 million Nvidia investment last year to preserve vendor neutrality — and its own infrastructure was the platform OpenAI’s agents breached in July, a coincidence neither company has addressed.

Why it matters This is the most visible joint OpenAI/Anthropic public statement since congressional scrutiny began, but it arrives alongside, not instead of, the individual answers Congress actually asked for.

OpenAI technical report and joint letter (primary) · CNBC, Axios, The Hill, TechCrunch.

Trump’s grid-equipment national emergency draws a mixed early industry read

The order gives DOE authority over foreign-sourced transformers, generators and control software; manufacturers warn the equipment already has six-month-to-two-year lead times.

Wednesday’s order revives and expands a 2020 order Biden had revoked in 2021, directing DOE to restrict acquisition of flagged bulk-power equipment — transformers, generators, batteries, control software — from foreign entities over backdoor risk, and reportedly allowing DOE to compel replacement of equipment already installed. The National Electric Manufacturers Association called it “an update and expansion” of the 2020 order but flagged confusion over what counts as covered equipment; Utility Dive notes the gear already carries six-month-to-two-year lead times and limited domestic manufacturing capacity, raising the risk the order worsens shortages even as it benefits domestic makers.

White House and DOE fact sheets (primary) · Utility Dive, Bloomberg, Fox Business.

Islamabad hospital fire: cause dispute sharpens between the minister and the hospital’s own report

The hospital’s preliminary finding points to a nebulizer, not the AC unit the health minister first blamed — and fire-brigade investigators say emergency exits were locked from outside.

Disputed cause PIMS hospital’s own preliminary report on the fire that killed 14 newborns now attributes it to a nebulizer connected to an incubator igniting the nursery’s oxygen-rich air, contradicting Health Minister Mustafa Kamal’s earlier statement that an AC unit had “allegedly exploded.” Fire-brigade investigators separately found the ward’s emergency exits locked from outside and obstructed — contradicting the hospital’s own claim that all exits were “fully open and operational.” Witnesses say the doors were routinely secured for security reasons, including prior concerns about infant theft. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has appointed an investigating committee, due to deliver urgent recommendations within 48 hours and a full report within five days of its formation.

PIMS hospital preliminary report, fire-brigade findings (primary) · Dawn, SCMP, AP.

DRC’s Ebola outbreak spreads to 60 health zones as funding runs low

Case counts now 5,713 confirmed / 2,744 dead, the country’s largest outbreak ever, with the UN’s own coordinator warning funding “could run out within weeks.”

The DRC’s Ebola outbreak has now spread to 60 health zones, up two since the last count, with 5,713 confirmed cases and 2,744 deaths as of the Aug. 25 data point — up from 5,514 and 2,642 reported Aug. 23. Ituri province remains worst-hit (4,750 cases, 2,133 deaths across 28 of 36 zones); 158 health workers have been infected and 45 have died. UN Senior Ebola Coordinator Julien Harneis reiterated that the response is “only covered for the next weeks,” with just over half of the $518 million continental response plan disbursed so far.

Before the Open

Futures are close to flat: S&P futures −0.04%, Nasdaq futures −0.26%, Dow futures +0.07%, Russell futures roughly unchanged, with Marvell’s after-hours drop among the drags cited on the Nasdaq side. That is a quiet tape for a morning with the year’s most-watched Fed speech on it — the market looks to be waiting rather than positioning. The 10-year sits at 4.67% (Thursday’s official Treasury print), with the 2s10s spread at +47 basis points; the dollar index is flat near 99.2 and euro/dollar is essentially unchanged near 1.165. The VIX complex is in normal contango — VIX9D at 12.10, VIX at 14.51, VIX3M at 17.56 — showing no near-term event-risk premium being priced into options ahead of the keynote, which is itself notable given how split Thursday’s Fed speakers were. The tape’s most fragile assumption is that Warsh will hew close to market consensus rather than surprise in either direction. A CNBC survey found 45% of economists expect no explicit rate-path guidance at all, even as 80% say he should offer more insight into his thinking; Thursday’s regional-Fed chorus (a hawkish Hammack, a cautious Schmid, an inflation-focused Goolsbee) shows no consensus for him to simply ratify. A second live thread is the Bessent-Warsh dynamic: Treasury’s expanded bond-buyback program has been actively suppressing long-end yields, which several commentators read as complicating any message from Warsh that rising yields could do some of the Fed’s tightening work on their own. A dovish surprise would likely extend Thursday’s equity strength and pressure the dollar further; a hawkish one, or simply a refusal to guide, could reprice the front end quickly given how thin the options market’s current risk premium looks. Futures are not a guaranteed open, and today less than most mornings.
