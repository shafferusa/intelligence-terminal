Top Stories

Chip and AI-networking suppliers rally as L3Harris’s CEO exit drags defense lower

Marvell and Micron led a chip-sector rally on a bullish supply-chain note while L3Harris’s abrupt CEO departure pulled the broader defense group down — two distinct, confirmed catalysts, not a single market mood.

Major indices closed modestly lower Monday: the S&P 500 fell 0.52% to 7,745.06, the Dow 0.51% to 53,459.78, and the Nasdaq 0.32% to 26,644.91, while the Russell 2000 held up best, off just 0.35% to 3,057.54 (this run’s own Yahoo Finance close-series data). The modest headline moves sat on top of sharp single-name and sector dispersion.

Marvell Technology rose 5.54% to $234.33 and Micron gained 4.13% to $1,011.75 after Mizuho published a research note Sunday citing supply-chain checks pointing to a strong ramp of Nvidia’s next-generation VR200 AI platform — a setup the firm framed as positive for near-package and co-packaged optical connectivity broadly, and specifically for Marvell, Credo, Nvidia, Broadcom and Lumentum into 2027. Credo and Ciena, not on this report’s watchlist, were reported up similarly elsewhere. Broadcom itself barely moved (−0.14%) and Nvidia was flat (−0.07%), suggesting the market drew a narrow line between the optical/networking layer and the broader AI-chip complex rather than buying AI exposure indiscriminately.

L3Harris Technologies fell 4.61% to $278.38 after CEO Christopher Kubasik’s immediate departure; the company said the exit did not involve any issue with its financial reporting and reaffirmed 2026 revenue guidance, but investors sold the stock on the leadership uncertainty regardless. The broader defense group softened alongside it — Northrop Grumman −2.67%, Lockheed Martin −2.45%, Boeing −2.47%, General Dynamics −1.17%, and AeroVironment the sector’s worst decliner at −6.06% — without a confirmed company-specific reason found for the wider group beyond L3Harris’s own news, a week after a Pentagon production-acceleration framework had lifted many of the same names.

KKR (−4.54%) and Apollo (−4.42%) extended the round-trip pattern this report has tracked since Nvidia’s $500 billion AI-financing platform story broke Aug. 10 — up, down, up, down across the last several sessions — again with no company-specific catalyst confirmed via this run’s own EDGAR check of recent filings.

Why it matters The market is drawing sharper lines within the AI trade than the broad-brush “chips up, spenders down” narrative suggests — a single analyst note can move a specific supply-chain niche without moving Nvidia or Broadcom at all, and a single CEO’s exit can move an entire defense sub-sector's sentiment on no company-specific news of its own.
What’s next Home Depot reports before Tuesday’s open, followed by Lowe’s and Target Wednesday and Walmart Thursday — the week’s next test of whether today’s dispersion reflects genuine fundamentals or thin-summer positioning.

Kushner and Netanyahu agree to Gaza disarmament working groups, sequencing still unresolved

Three hours in Jerusalem produced agreement on how Hamas’s disarmament should be overseen — not on whether Israeli troops withdraw before or after it happens.

Jared Kushner met Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for roughly three hours in Jerusalem Monday, a day after Kushner’s own direct meeting with Hamas political leader Khalil al-Hayya in Cairo. Tony Blair and Nickolay Mladenov, the Board of Peace’s high representative for Gaza, joined the Jerusalem session. The two sides agreed to establish two working groups — one on Hamas’s disarmament and “demilitarization,” to be overseen by an American general, and one on Gaza’s public-health needs — and agreed the weapons handover should be “prompt and completed before any reconstruction happens in Gaza.”

What the meeting did not resolve is the sequencing dispute this report has tracked since Netanyahu’s Aug. 9 rejection of the Board of Peace’s original 15-point plan: Kushner reportedly assured Netanyahu that the Israeli military will not be compelled to withdraw from Gaza until Hamas actually disarms, leaving Hamas’s own precondition — an Israeli withdrawal first — unaddressed. One outlet describes the session as having “failed to break the deadlock” on that specific point even as the working-group mechanism itself represents forward motion.

What’s next The two working groups’ first output, and whether Hamas publicly accepts the disarm-before-withdraw framing Kushner conveyed to Netanyahu.

Iran’s 60-day Hormuz ceasefire expires; Tehran claims full control of the strait

The memorandum of understanding that lifted the US naval blockade in exchange for 60 days of free passage lapsed at the deadline with no extension, and oil is responding.

The ceasefire memorandum President Trump and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian signed June 17 — under which the US lifted its naval blockade and Iran committed to 60 days of free commercial passage through the Strait of Hormuz — expired Monday with no public extension agreed by either government, as this morning’s edition reported. Iran’s government went further over the weekend, with Tehran claiming full control over the strait; shipping-data trackers describe oil flow through Hormuz at a three-month low.

Single-sourced claim Iran’s “full control” claim has not been independently confirmed by a Western government statement found this run. Oil and gold both moved: West Texas Intermediate crude rose 2.91% and gold gained 1.00% on the SPDR Gold Shares ETF (this run’s own data) — a plausible, not confirmed, link to the ceasefire’s expiry given the timing (Likely contributor, not Confirmed catalyst; no single trading-desk attribution was found). No new US or Israeli military action, and no new confirmed strike on shipping, was found this run beyond the standing, previously reported pattern.

Fox News Digital · Britannica’s “2026 Iran war” tracker · Congress.gov CRS product on Hormuz security · CNN. Confirmed-multiple on the ceasefire’s expiry; Single-reliable-source-cluster on Iran’s control claim.

Russia says it downed a record 1,478 drones overnight as Ukraine’s barrage campaign continues

Moscow struck Odesa port infrastructure while claiming its largest single-night intercept total of the war — a figure this report can attribute only to Russian officials.

Russia’s Defense Ministry said it shot down 1,478 Ukrainian drones over the past 24 hours, described by Russian state media as a record, while separately striking port infrastructure in Ukraine’s Odesa region overnight. Single-sourced figure The 1,478 intercept count comes from Russian officials only and is not independently verified. The exchange follows Ukraine’s own record weekend barrage, which hit a Wildberries logistics hub near Podolsk and killed at least six — part of a campaign this report has tracked that specifically targets Russia’s retail and logistics infrastructure, not only military sites.

Intuitive Machines gains 7.2% on a $600 million-plus satellite contract

An unnamed customer authorized a multi-satellite communications program — the company’s second disclosure this month in roughly that dollar range, and this report cannot confirm whether the two are the same deal.

Home Depot reports tomorrow in its first call since the CEO’s medical leave began

Consensus clusters around $4.71–4.73 a share; the market’s real interest is in Ann-Marie Campbell and Richard McPhail’s interim leadership, not the headline print.

Home Depot reports fiscal second-quarter results before Tuesday’s 8:00 AM ET open. Consensus estimates cluster around $4.71–4.73 a share on roughly $47.5 billion in revenue, against $4.68 a year earlier. The print comes six days after CEO Ted Decker’s Aug. 12 announcement of a temporary medical leave, with day-to-day operations split between Ann-Marie Campbell (US stores and operations) and CFO Richard McPhail (finance and the Pro subsidiaries) in the interim. Analysts say comparable sales, large-ticket and Pro demand, margins, and any commentary on tariff costs will matter more than the EPS print itself. Home Depot closed today at $337.88, off 0.29%, after finishing last week down 4.71%.

What’s next A forecast logged Aug. 12 (Home Depot does not fall a further 3%+ over five sessions from that close) resolves around tomorrow’s session; Lowe’s and Target follow Wednesday, Walmart Thursday, and 50% Section 338 tariffs on Canada take effect Wednesday absent a deal.

Two unrelated Supreme Court emergency matters share Tuesday’s deadline

A mail-ballot injunction bond and the White House ballroom project’s response window both come due at once — coincidence of calendar, not of subject.

The Justice Department’s $100 bond in its appeal of the nationwide mail-ballot injunction is due at the 1st Circuit Tuesday, after that court ruled 2-1 against the department’s stay request over the weekend; the department’s own emergency application to the Supreme Court remains pending. Separately, Chief Justice Roberts set Tuesday as the response deadline for preservationist plaintiffs opposing the White House’s request to resume its roughly $400 million East Wing ballroom project during appeal — a filing that separately disclosed a previously unreported July 8 intelligence warning, relayed by Israel, of a possible missile threat to Air Force One near Turkey. No ruling in either matter had been issued as of this report’s research.
