Today’s News

FBI seizes Eric Swalwell’s devices at SFO, then searches his D.C. home, as the investigation widens

Federal agents intercepted the former congressman Saturday evening and searched his Washington home Sunday — the sharpest escalation yet in a probe that already includes at least four accusers.

Federal agents intercepted former Rep. Eric Swalwell (D-Calif.) at San Francisco International Airport on Saturday evening and seized his electronic devices, including an iPhone and a MacBook Pro, under a search warrant. The following day, the FBI searched Swalwell’s Washington, D.C. home. Both actions are part of a widening federal investigation into sexual-assault and sexual-misconduct allegations against Swalwell, who resigned his California House seat in April after the San Francisco Chronicle reported a former aide’s allegation that he twice assaulted her while she was too intoxicated to consent, and that he sent her an unsolicited explicit image. At least four accusers have reportedly been contacted by the FBI; Swalwell also faces separate inquiries from the Manhattan District Attorney’s office and the Los Angeles Sheriff’s Department.

Swalwell has not been arrested or charged and denies the allegations through his attorney, who has called the probe political retaliation. This is an investigation, not a charge — no indictment has been sought as of Sunday.

What’s next Whether the seized devices produce a charging decision, and whether any of the other three inquiries (Manhattan DA, LA Sheriff) moves first.

NBC News · CNN · Washington Post · NPR · The Hill.

Federal judge strikes down the administration’s 75-country visa suspension

A district judge found Secretary of State Rubio exceeded his statutory authority by suspending visa processing by nationality rather than individualized assessment.

U.S. District Judge Jeannette Vargas (S.D.N.Y.) ruled Friday that the administration’s suspension of immigrant-visa processing for nationals of 75 countries — including Afghanistan, Iran, Russia and Somalia — was “contrary to law and in excess of” Secretary of State Marco Rubio’s statutory authority, finding it improperly discriminated by nationality rather than individualized assessment. The administration has not indicated whether it will appeal.

Washington Post · CNN · NPR · The Hill · JURIST.

Kryvyi Rih toll settles at 16 dead as fresh strikes hit Ukraine and Russia overnight

Friday’s disputed range has converged toward the higher end; both sides struck again into Sunday morning.

The death toll from Friday’s Russian strike on a Kryvyi Rih shopping mall has settled at 16 killed and roughly 140 injured, with about 50 victims still hospitalized and nine people, including two children, still listed missing as of Saturday — the converged figure after Saturday morning’s wider 6-to-16-plus range, still sourced to Ukrainian officials and not independently adjudicated. Russian strikes killed six more people across Ukraine Saturday, and early Sunday a ballistic-missile alert triggered a warehouse fire in Kyiv’s Darnytskyi district while a Russian drone killed one and wounded four in Zaporizhzhia. Unverified Russian Telegram channels reported a Ukrainian strike on an Ozon logistics center in Orenburg, Russia overnight; Western outlets have not corroborated it. A Russian drone separately hit a passenger train in Odesa Oblast, per Ukrainian Railways’ own CEO. Putin has warned of “far more destructive” strikes to come; no summit with Zelensky is scheduled.

UNN · CNN · ABC News · Manila Times wire reporting.

Bitcoin’s record weekly gain survives a flash crash, but the rally isn’t clean

A ~22% weekly gain round-tripped through the sharpest crypto flash crash since October 2025 early Saturday morning.

Bitcoin reached an intraday high near $79,500 early Saturday before a flash crash just after 04:00 UTC took it down to roughly $76,500 within six minutes — described across crypto-market outlets as the sharpest such crash since October 2025, with liquidation estimates in the $1.7–1.8 billion range over 24 hours (a much larger $108 billion figure circulating on crypto-trade blogs is not corroborated by mainstream financial press and is treated here with caution). As of this report, Bitcoin trades near $77,300, still up roughly 20–22% for the week — its best week since March 2024 — but the round trip through Saturday’s crash means the gain has not held cleanly. Thinner weekend liquidity is cited as a contributing factor. Market narrative Trump’s renewed push for the CLARITY Act and Treasury’s debt-buyback announcement are both cited as likely contributors to the week’s rally; no outlet has drawn a confirmed single causal line.

CoinDesk · Bloomberg · CryptoRank · Coinpedia.

Chang’e 7’s launch window slips one day, to Monday evening

A correction to this report’s own prior tracking: the mission has not launched, and the window now opens roughly 24 hours later than last estimated.

China’s Chang’e 7 lunar south-pole mission, which this report had tracked toward a roughly 8:00 PM ET Sunday window, has not launched as of this writing. Launch Library 2’s own schedule now specifies a net time of 8:30 PM ET Monday, August 24 — a one-day slip from the prior estimate, still within the daily-repeating window CASC has described through August 31. The Long March 5 will carry an orbiter, lander, hopping probe and rover toward the illuminated rim of Shackleton crater, hunting for water ice and scouting a future lunar research-base site; any landing attempt would come months later, near year-end.

Top Themes This Week

Two dated catalysts do most of the work. With Congress in recess, no FOMC meeting scheduled, and only Low-to-Medium-importance domestic data on the calendar until Wednesday, this week’s genuine uncertainty concentrates almost entirely into Monday’s Iran sanctions rollout and Wednesday’s Nvidia earnings — a narrower, more binary setup than most weeks this report has covered.

Rhetoric on Iran is already ahead of the actual sanctions text. Iran’s Foreign Ministry has pre-emptively rejected Monday’s package as unlawful, and Supreme National Security Council secretary Rezaei has threatened to halt all Gulf oil traffic if any neighboring state joins the campaign — both statements landed before Bessent has said a word about what the package actually contains.

The AI-infrastructure trade gets its clearest single-name test of the summer. Nvidia’s print, with consensus clustering near $93–95 billion in revenue, lands the same week as Marvell’s own report (a direct read-through on the Google TPU deal and Broadcom-exclusivity dispute this report has tracked all month) and one day before the PCE inflation print — three separate tests of the same AI-capex-versus-inflation tension inside 48 hours.

Jackson Hole lands at an awkward moment for the Fed’s new chair. Kevin Warsh’s first keynote as Fed Chair falls one day after the PCE print and two and a half weeks before the September 16 FOMC decision — the clearest opportunity yet to read his own framework rather than his predecessor’s.

The weekend’s biggest story has nothing to do with the week ahead’s calendar. The widening FBI investigation into Eric Swalwell is a genuinely new escalation (a home search, seized devices) that arrived with no advance scheduling and could produce further developments on any day this week without warning.

Politics & Government Outlook

Both chambers of Congress remain in their August recess all week — the House holds no floor votes (next expected Aug 31) and the Senate meets only in pro forma session, so any national political news this week is more likely to come from the courts, agencies or campaign trail than the Hill. Two live Supreme Court emergency-docket matters could produce an order on any day without advance notice: the fully briefed mail-ballot executive-order case, where the administration continues pressing for a prompt ruling ahead of midterm election-administration deadlines, and the East Wing ballroom case, where Chief Justice Roberts’s Friday administrative stay could be extended, modified, or referred to the full Court at any time. Tuesday’s South Carolina special-Senate primary runoff (see calendar above) is the week’s one confirmed dated election event, and a live test of Trump’s endorsement strength after ten primary losses this cycle. The Missouri Supreme Court could act at any point on the appeal of Friday’s ruling upholding the state’s GOP-drawn congressional map, though no hearing date is confirmed. No confirmation-hearing date has been set for FDA nominee Heidi Overton, and DOJ has given no public timetable on the Fauci contempt referral — both could move without warning. Separately, Senate Appropriations leaders have reportedly struck a bipartisan framework to fund the government past the Sept 30 fiscal year-end into mid-December, which may shape recess-period messaging even though no floor vote is scheduled this week.

The World Ahead

Middle East / Iran — the week’s dominant story. Monday’s sanctions detail (see calendar) is expected to include sweeping secondary sanctions pressuring banks, firms and governments — explicit pressure on China, which buys roughly 80% of Iran’s seaborne oil exports — framed by Bessent as forcing US allies to choose sides. Watch for whether China responds or continues purchases regardless, whether Iran follows through on its threat to halt all Gulf oil traffic if a neighboring state joins the campaign, and whether the long-discussed Iran-Oman shipping corridor (reported “in final drafting” since early August) is finalized. Strait traffic remains constrained: the US Department of Energy puts current throughput at 8–9 million barrels a day via “dark transit” workarounds, and a Liberia-flagged tanker held near Qeshm Island remained stationary as of Friday, the first confirmed Iranian seizure since June 22.

Gaza. No fixed deadline this week, but three threads to track: the EU’s still-“preparing” sanctions package over Israel’s E1 settlement tender (submissions due Oct 19, one week before Israel’s Oct 27 election), the unresolved Hamas disarmament-sequencing dispute, and possible further IDF criminal-probe announcements from the roughly 145 wartime incidents still under review after last week’s first two.

Russia-Ukraine. No summit is scheduled; Zelensky’s standing offer to meet Putin remains unanswered. Expect continued reciprocal strikes given Putin’s warning of “far more destructive” hits to come, with elevated risk of a further mass-casualty incident following Kryvyi Rih.

Israel-Syria-Turkey. US envoy Tom Barrack has said Israel’s strike on Syria’s Abu al-Duhur airbase carried “a real risk of rapid escalation” involving Turkey and gave the US no advance warning — a break in normal deconfliction. Washington is reportedly in quiet talks to relaunch an Israel-Syria deconfliction cell, possibly including Turkey; watch for whether that materializes, and for any Interpol action on Turkey’s Netanyahu arrest-warrant request.

Africa. The DRC Ebola outbreak’s funding-shortfall clock is the key trigger to watch — UN officials say existing coverage runs out “within weeks,” so a fresh case-count update or formal shortfall announcement is plausible. Case counts stood at 5,290 confirmed / 2,516 dead as of Aug 20 data, with no update found over the weekend. Given the pattern of roughly one Somali piracy incident every two to four weeks since April, a further Gulf of Aden hijacking is a reasonable risk, alongside continued uncertainty over the Sibu 1 tanker seized Aug 20.

Elsewhere. No major scheduled events were identified this week for China/Taiwan beyond ongoing gray-zone pressure activity. North Korea could test further short-range missiles if the US-South Korea drill cycle that has driven its recent launches continues. Venezuela’s US-backed transition talks remain open-ended with a promised timeline this month for judicial reform.
