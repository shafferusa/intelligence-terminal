Top Stories

Iran fires on a US base in Jordan and, by its own account, at the UAE; oil jumps

Jordan says it intercepted eight missiles with no casualties; the UAE confirms downing one Iranian drone but flatly denies Iran’s claim that it struck an air base — a dispute that is itself part of the story.

The exchange that began over the weekend continued into this afternoon. After US forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island late Saturday or early Sunday — CENTCOM says intelligence indicated Iran’s Revolutionary Guard was preparing to lay sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz — Iran launched what it called “Punishment of the Aggressor” early Monday: a combined missile-and-drone attack on Jordan’s King Hussein and Al-Azraq air bases. Jordan’s military says it intercepted all eight missiles fired; no casualties were reported. Iranian state media separately claims two named IRGC personnel were killed in the original US strike and two more wounded, a figure inconsistent across Iranian outlets and confirmed by no independent source.

Disputed Iran’s military also says it struck the UAE’s Al Minhad Air Base near Dubai with a drone. The UAE’s Ministry of Defense confirms intercepting one Iranian drone over its territorial waters but explicitly denies any base was hit, calling the claim of a missile strike “false”; the UAE’s foreign ministry condemned the drone itself as a “dangerous escalation” and holds Iran “fully responsible.” CENTCOM has separately rejected Iran’s characterization of the weekend strike as an unprovoked “act of aggression,” calling it “limited, precise action against IRGC minelaying forces posing an imminent threat” to shipping. Separately, President Trump posted an AI-generated video over the weekend claiming Kharg Island — which handles roughly 90% of Iran’s oil exports — was “being blown to smithereens.” Unverified No Pentagon statement corroborates a strike there, no independent reporting shows any damage, and Iran’s national oil company chief dismissed the claim Monday as “laughable,” saying conditions at the island are calm; this report treats the video as an unverified, likely fabricated claim, not a confirmed military event.

Oil reacted directly: Brent crude rose to roughly $90.69 a barrel, up about 2.9% on the day and briefly topping $90 intraday, while this report’s own watchlist proxy for US crude (the USO fund) closed up 3.08%. Multiple outlets citing Reuters- and Bloomberg-sourced reporting note 6–8 million barrels a day are still flowing through Hormuz despite the standoff, with no confirmed physical closure of the strait.

Why it matters This is the first direct exchange of fire between the US and Iran in a month, and the first time Iran has struck — or claimed to strike — a third country’s territory (the UAE) in this round of the standoff, alongside a confirmed strike on a US ally (Jordan).
What’s next Whether the UAE lets the drone incident drop as an isolated event or treats it as an escalation requiring its own response, and whether Iran attempts any further retaliation this week.

CENTCOM (primary, via wire) · Jordan’s military statement · UAE Ministry of Defense and Foreign Ministry statements · Al Jazeera · Fox News · Jerusalem Post · Khaleej Times · RFE/RL · The National · OilPrice.com.

Go deeper

Two separate verification tracks are worth keeping apart. The Jordan strike-and-interception is confirmed by multiple independent sources on both the US and Jordanian sides and is not in dispute. The UAE strike is disputed at the most basic factual level — Iran says it hit a base, the UAE says its territory was not struck and only a drone was intercepted over water — and this report is not resolving that dispute, only reporting both accounts. The IRGC casualty figures from the original US strike come exclusively from Iranian state media and have not been corroborated by CENTCOM or any independent source; treat them as a claim, not a confirmed toll. The Kharg Island video is the outlier: it originated from the president’s own social media account, is AI-generated by its own description in reporting, and has no supporting evidence of any kind from any government or independent outlet — the most responsible read is that no strike on Kharg Island has occurred.

Russia carries out its threatened “massive” strike on Ukraine’s energy grid

A large drone-and-missile barrage hit three regions overnight, framed by Moscow as retaliation for Ukraine’s own strikes on Russian oil and gas facilities.

Russia’s defense ministry confirmed Monday it proceeded with the large-scale energy-infrastructure strike it had threatened last week, describing it as retaliation for Ukraine’s continuing drone campaign against Russian refineries and gas facilities. A barrage of drones and missiles beginning around midnight struck energy infrastructure in Zaporizhzhia, Rivne and Lviv regions; regional officials report at least three additional deaths from this barrage, separate from the toll from last week’s strike on the village of Myla near Kyiv, which holds steady at 38 dead and four missing. On the diplomatic track, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov added a pointed line to Sunday’s rejection of a Trump-Putin-Zelensky summit, saying “actually working toward a settlement is exactly what the Kyiv side does not want” — a direct blame-shift that Kyiv has not yet answered. Single-source, unverified Separately, a deputy chief of staff to President Zelensky says Ukrainian intelligence indicates Putin has ordered the military to prepare scenarios for a new offensive in northern Ukraine; this is an Ukrainian-sourced intelligence claim with no independent corroboration.

Why it matters It is Moscow following through on an explicit threat rather than continuing at the prior pace of strikes, and it lands the same week both sides are publicly blaming each other for the collapse of a floated peace-summit idea.

Russian Ministry of Defense statement · regional Ukrainian officials (social media) · NPR · CNBC · Euronews · Meduza · Time.

CDC leaves Pennsylvania’s two measles deaths out of its national count

The federal agency says it is still reviewing whether measles actually caused the deaths Gov. Shapiro announced Saturday — turning a war of words with HHS Secretary Kennedy into a concrete data dispute.

The CDC’s national measles tally, updated Sunday, omits the two Pennsylvania deaths Gov. Josh Shapiro announced Saturday after what he described as a contentious call with HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. The agency’s stated reason: it is still reviewing “additional information regarding the circumstances and causes of death” and says available information “does not establish whether measles caused or contributed to the deaths.” That statement lands on top of, not instead of, the dispute already running through this outlet’s coverage since Saturday: Lancaster County Coroner Dr. Stephen Diamantoni says one of the two deaths, an Aug. 14 infant in Christiana Borough, was caused by a ruptured spleen and attributes zero measles deaths in his county, directly contradicting the state’s count.

Pennsylvania’s health secretary, Dr. Debra Bogen, says she “thoroughly reviewed” the count and that it was properly reported to the CDC’s own measles response team; the state Department of Health disputes any suggestion it withheld data, saying it remains “in touch with” CDC program staff and shares data regularly. Kennedy himself, in a weekend social-media post, suggested the deaths “may even have been altogether fabricated by one of the Governor’s hopeful staffers” — a claim with no supporting evidence that this report is reporting as a statement, not a fact. The state’s own case count stands at roughly 460 across 31 counties, and its Health Department has run more than 4,100 MMR vaccinations at 91 pop-up clinics since April, with about 40 more planned, independent of the political dispute over the death count.

Why it matters A federal health agency publicly declining to count a state-reported outbreak death is a genuine, escalating institutional rift — not just rhetoric — with credibility implications for public-health messaging heading into respiratory-illness season.
What’s next Whether the CDC updates its count once its review concludes, and whether Pennsylvania’s Health Department responds formally to Kennedy’s fabrication claim beyond standing by its numbers.

CDC national measles surveillance update · Pennsylvania Dept. of Health (primary) · Philadelphia Inquirer · PhillyVoice · NBC10 Philadelphia · CNN · LNP/LancasterOnline.

House returns; the government-funding bill’s floor vote firms up for this week

H.R. 6500, the Senate-passed continuing resolution funding the government through Dec. 11, needs a two-thirds majority under the suspension procedure it’s being brought up on.

The House returned from its August recess today for a one-week session built around H.R. 6500, the “Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027” — the Senate-amended version that passed 90-6 on Aug. 8 and funds the government through Dec. 11. The Majority Leader’s official schedule and a CBO publication both confirm the bill number and that it will be taken up under suspension of the rules, which requires a two-thirds majority rather than a simple one; no exact vote hour is set, with expectations pointing to sometime Tuesday through Thursday. Today’s own floor business was pushed to a 6:30 PM ET vote series, after this report’s research window. Also on this week’s agenda: a Republican resolution “condemning socialism” and a possible vote on a Senate-passed Russia-sanctions bill.

Why it matters A two-thirds threshold is a meaningfully higher bar than most floor votes clear, so a bill that has already passed the Senate by a wide margin is not automatically a sure thing in the House this week.

Majority Leader weekly schedule (primary) · Congressional Budget Office publication 62588 (primary) · congress.gov · PBS NewsHour · Daily Signal.

Trump threatens NBC’s Kristen Welker with FCC action; the commission’s lone Democrat calls it “dangerous”

FCC Chair Brendan Carr, a Trump appointee, has not responded to the threat; Commissioner Anna Gomez says the agency “has no authority to punish journalists this administration doesn’t like.”

President Trump posted Sunday that NBC’s Kristen Welker should be “reported to the FCC for rebuke or punishment,” accusing her of saying his primary endorsements had “mixed results” — a characterization Welker did not make on-air Sunday; the closest matching comments appear to have come in separate, local Washington-area promotional appearances, and the president’s own team has touted endorsement win rates near 100% in Senate and House primaries. FCC Chair Brendan Carr did not respond to press inquiries over the weekend. The commission’s sole Democratic commissioner, Anna Gomez, did respond publicly, saying the FCC “has no authority to punish journalists this administration doesn’t like” and calling the threat “dangerous” to democratic norms.

Why it matters It is a live test of how a Republican-appointed FCC chair responds to a presidential call to use the agency against a specific journalist, with the response so far being silence from the chair and public pushback only from the commission’s minority party member.

CNN · The Hill · Variety · Forbes · Fortune · Editor & Publisher.

CISA adds two PaperCut zero-days to its exploited-vulnerabilities catalog as researchers detail a new campaign against Cisco gear

The PaperCut flaws allow unauthenticated remote code execution when chained; separately, a private-sector report describes a China-linked group turning Cisco routers into covert surveillance platforms.

CISA added two actively exploited PaperCut NG/MF print-management vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog Monday: CVE-2026-81578 (an improper access-control flaw letting unauthenticated requests reach admin functions) and CVE-2026-82078 (unsafe dynamic class loading in database-connection utilities, rated 9.4 of 10 on the CVSS scale). Chained together, the two allow pre-authentication remote code execution as the PaperCut server process. PaperCut disclosed the flaws Aug. 27 and shipped an emergency patch the next day; the federal civilian remediation deadline is Sept. 14.

Separately, cybersecurity firm Sygnia published new findings Aug. 30 that the China-nexus actor it tracks as “Fire Ant” — previously reported compromising VMware ESXi hypervisors — has expanded to hijacking Cisco IOS XR routers, TACACS+ authentication servers and Linux management hosts, turning routers into covert traffic-collection and credential-harvesting platforms while suppressing logging. Moderate confidence The China-nexus attribution is Sygnia’s own private-sector assessment; no US government agency has confirmed it, and no victims have been named.

Why it matters Two distinct, primary-sourced cyber developments in one day — a federal catalog addition with a hard compliance deadline, and new evidence a known state-linked actor is broadening from hypervisors into core network infrastructure.

CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog (primary) · Sygnia (primary research) · BleepingComputer · SecurityAffairs · eSentire advisory.

ICE deported more than 100 people to eight African countries, none their own

Single-reliable-source CBS News, citing internal government documents, reports three flights over ten days carried Afghans, Cubans, Nicaraguans, Iranians and others to nations they have no connection to.

CBS News reports, based on internal government documents it says it obtained, that ICE deported more than 100 people over a ten-day span via three flights to eight African countries — Burundi, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Equatorial Guinea, Eswatini, Liberia, Rwanda and Sierra Leone — none of them the deportees’ home countries. Those removed include nationals of Afghanistan (among them Afghans who worked alongside the US military), Cuba, Nicaragua, Iran, Nepal, Turkey and Venezuela. Other outlets have picked up the reporting without independently confirming it, so this report treats it as single-reliable-source pending corroboration.

Why it matters “Third-country” removals to nations with no connection to the deportee are a distinct and more legally contested practice than deportation to a person’s home country, and the inclusion of Afghan wartime allies raises the stakes of an already-scrutinized policy.

CBS News (primary reporting, citing internal documents).

Nepal-Tibet flood toll passes 900; Nepal says the jump reflects recovery, not a data dispute

The combined toll rose from roughly 750 Sunday to 919 Monday — a jump this report can now attribute to bodies still surfacing downstream, not to the toll-reconciliation dispute it had been tracking.

The combined Nepal-Tibet death toll from the Aug. 26 glacier-collapse flood now stands at 919 (903 in Nepal, 16 in Tibet), with roughly 4,700 still missing. Unlike the toll disputes this report tracked last week between Nepal’s own police and prime minister’s office, this jump appears attributable to ongoing body recovery — some bodies have been found up to 240 kilometers downstream in India, and morgues are reportedly running low on storage and identification capacity — rather than to a reconciliation between competing counts. Roughly 933 missing hydropower workers in Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts remain part of the search.

ABC News/AP · Al Jazeera · NBC News · CNN · TASS (citing Chinese state media).
