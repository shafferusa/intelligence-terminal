Top Stories

Iran says it is shifting to a “fully offensive” posture in the Strait, with a deadline of weeks

Hours after this morning’s first fatal tanker strike, a senior Iranian official warns of a “timely and precise” attack to break the US naval presence in the Strait of Hormuz if talks don’t produce results within “a few weeks.”

A senior Iranian official told Reuters today that Tehran is moving to a “fully offensive” military posture in the Strait of Hormuz, warning of a “timely and precise” strike intended to break the US naval presence there if diplomacy fails to produce results within “a few weeks” — Iran’s clearest public deadline yet since the 60-day ceasefire lapsed Monday. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf separately reiterated that the Strait stays closed until the US lifts what Iran calls a naval blockade and unfreezes Iranian assets. No attribution has been claimed or confirmed for this morning’s fatal strike on the bulk carrier Minoan Dignity; UKMTO’s threat assessment remains at its highest tier, describing attacks as “increasing.” A separate report of a Liberia-flagged tanker loitering unexpectedly near Qeshm Island today comes from a single maritime-intelligence tracker and has not been independently corroborated — this report treats it as unverified, not as a new incident.

Why it matters This is Iran’s most explicit public statement yet that it is abandoning a defensive posture, paired with a concrete (if vague) timeline. Markets moved on it today — oil held a small gain, VIX jumped over 4%, equities sold off — but nothing here has been tested against an actual second fatality or a US response.
What’s next Whether the Oman-mediated reopening talks continue despite Trump’s Monday threat to “bomb” Oman, and whether any US or allied response follows.

Reuters (via Arabian Business, Khaama Press) · Washington Times (Ghalibaf) · UKMTO (primary, threat level).

Tech and space names lead a broad afternoon selloff while defense stocks rally

The S&P 500 fell 0.69% and the Nasdaq 1.33% as semiconductor, AI-infrastructure and space-sector names sold off hard, while Lockheed, Northrop and RTX all rallied more than 1.5% — a split this report cannot fully separate from the day’s Hormuz headlines.

Tuesday’s session closed broadly lower: the S&P 500 fell 0.69% to 7,691.76, the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.33% to 26,289.71, the Russell 2000 fell 1.30%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average held up comparatively well at −0.22%. The damage concentrated in semiconductors and AI infrastructure — the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell 4.09%, Broadcom fell 3.17%, Nvidia fell 2.34%, Meta fell 4.45% — and in the space sector, where AST SpaceMobile fell 5.72%, Intuitive Machines fell 5.25%, Rocket Lab fell 3.56% and SpaceX fell 1.98%. Defense stocks moved the opposite direction: Northrop Grumman +3.32%, Lockheed Martin +2.26%, RTX +1.74%. This report cannot confirm a single catalyst tying the two moves together; the day’s AI/semiconductor and space-sector weakness coincided with both a broad risk-off tone following Iran’s escalatory statement and continued digestion of last week’s AI-financing and space-earnings news, while the defense rally is consistent with (but not confirmed as caused by) a market pricing in more Hormuz-related demand for missile defense. VIX rose 4.28% to 15.84, still a low absolute level. Notably, gold fell 1.71% and copper fell 2.20% even as equities sold off and VIX rose — a cross-asset pattern that doesn’t fit a clean flight-to-safety story, and this report does not force one.

Home Depot’s stock climbs despite the selloff around it, as analysts raise targets

The stock reclaimed its 50-day moving average and drew price-target hikes from Stifel, RBC and Jefferies, a rare green mark on an otherwise red session.

This morning’s beat — adjusted EPS $4.92 against a roughly $4.71-4.73 consensus, sales up 5.7% — held up against the tape: Home Depot's stock rose today even as the broader market sold off, reclaiming its 50-day moving average (roughly $338) and briefly testing resistance near $341. Sell-side reaction was mostly favorable: Stifel raised its target from $320 to $340, RBC from $340 to $343, and Jefferies from $360 to $398; Bernstein trimmed slightly, from $346 to $344. Commentary framed the quarter as Home Depot making the best of a still-frozen housing market, reported entirely under the interim “Office of the CEO” structure put in place after CEO Ted Decker’s Aug. 12 medical leave began. No prognosis or return timeline for Decker has been addressed by the company as of this writing.

Israeli strike kills six in Gaza, a day after the Kushner-Netanyahu meeting

The IDF says it targeted a meeting of elite Hamas Nukhba-force commanders near a Gaza City fishing harbor; Hamas has not confirmed any of its own casualties, as it typically does not.

An Israeli strike on a small cafe inside a fishing harbor in western Gaza City killed at least six Palestinians and wounded 14 others Tuesday, per Gaza health officials; the Israeli military said the strike targeted a meeting of Hamas’s elite Nukhba-force commanders who it said were planning attacks on Israeli forces in the enclave. Hamas did not confirm whether any of its own commanders or fighters were killed, consistent with its general practice. The strike lands one day after Netanyahu and Kushner met roughly four hours on a Gaza disarmament framework that hardened rather than resolved — the two sides agreed disarmament should begin with a US-general-supervised weapons handover, but Netanyahu is publicly insisting on no Israeli pullback before full disarmament, the opposite of a compromise on the sequencing dispute this report has tracked since Aug. 9. No Hamas response to either the framework or today’s strike has surfaced as of this writing.

Reuters (via GV Wire, TimesLIVE, Internazionale) · Times of Israel, Al-Monitor, Israel National News.

Trump and Carney speak by phone as Canada’s tariff deadline arrives with no deal

The call produced no announced agreement; 50% Section 338 tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods take effect just after midnight, landing directly against tomorrow’s Lowe’s and Target earnings.

President Trump and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney spoke by phone today, confirmed by multiple outlets, but neither government announced an agreement or an extension. Carney had called the talks “very delicate and intense” Monday; Canadian trade officials met USTR Jamieson Greer and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick in Washington this week covering “all strategic sectors,” including autos. Absent a deal, 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods — dairy, alcoholic beverages, motor vehicles and other categories, with no USMCA exemption — take effect just after midnight Wednesday. The timing lands directly against the second day of this week’s big-box retail earnings block: Lowe’s and Target report before Wednesday’s open, Walmart Thursday.

Two Supreme Court emergency dockets remain unresolved after today’s deadlines

The National Trust filed its court-ordered response in the East Wing ballroom case arguing construction will become “irreversible”; DOJ says it “disagrees” with an expanded mail-ballot injunction. Neither matter has produced a ruling.

In the East Wing ballroom case, the National Trust for Historic Preservation filed its court-ordered response today, arguing the roughly $400 million construction project will become irreversible if allowed to continue during the government’s appeal; no ruling from the Supreme Court has been issued as of this writing, with an Aug. 21 enforcement date still pending if the Court does not act. In the mail-ballot case, Judge Indira Talwani’s injunction against the administration’s executive order was expanded this week to bar USPS from any further implementation nationwide; DOJ said it “disagrees” and is “reviewing all options,” but no Supreme Court ruling on its pending emergency stay application has surfaced. Both remain on the Court’s emergency “shadow docket,” which can move without advance notice.

SCOTUSblog, NPR, Votebeat · AP (via Yahoo, ClickOnDetroit).

WHO’s Ebola emergency committee meets on the DRC outbreak; no decision published

The Director-General’s opening remarks cited roughly 5,000 infections and more than 2,300 deaths across six provinces, but the committee’s recommendation on the outbreak’s emergency status was not released as of this writing.

The World Health Organization’s International Health Regulations Emergency Committee convened today for its second review of the DRC Ebola outbreak, the first since the May 17 declaration of a public-health emergency of international concern. WHO published the Director-General’s opening remarks, citing roughly 5,000 confirmed infections and more than 2,300 deaths across six provinces and 55 health zones — making it, as this morning’s edition reported, the deadliest Ebola outbreak in the DRC’s recorded history. No final committee statement or decision on whether to renew, narrow or expand the emergency declaration had been published as of this writing; this report will carry the outcome once WHO releases it.

World Health Organization (primary).

Russia strikes Kharkiv region, killing 10, after Ukraine’s largest Moscow-area drone attack in two years

Zelensky says Ukraine “will definitely respond” after a Russian missile strike on Pechenihy killed 10, hours after roughly 637-800 Ukrainian drones (accounts vary) targeted the Moscow region overnight.

Russia struck the town of Pechenihy in Ukraine’s Kharkiv region today, killing 10, according to President Zelensky, who said Ukraine “will definitely respond.” The strike followed overnight reporting on Ukraine’s largest drone attack on the Moscow region in roughly two years — outlets vary on the exact count, from about 637 to nearly 800 drones, with Russia claiming 620-plus intercepted and Moscow’s mayor citing 180 shot down over the capital region specifically; a Wildberries warehouse previously damaged in an earlier strike was reportedly hit again, and three people, including a child, were wounded. This report treats the precise drone count as unsettled across sources rather than adopting a single figure. Ukraine’s air force separately said it intercepted or suppressed 111 of 147 Russian drones fired at Ukraine over the same period — the exchange continues in both directions with no sign of de-escalation.

Washington Post, CBS News, AP (via Yahoo) · Kyiv Independent, Moscow Times, TASS (Russian figures, labeled as such).

Stripe finalizes its $7 billion-plus acquisition of AI-routing startup OpenRouter

The price is more than five times the $1.3 billion valuation OpenRouter set in a Series B round just three months ago, as the payments giant moves to own the routing layer between developers and AI models.

Stripe has finalized its acquisition of OpenRouter, an AI-model marketplace that helps developers route requests among 400-plus models, for more than $7 billion — a roughly 5.4x markup on the $1.3 billion valuation OpenRouter set in a Series B round in May. The deal had been reported as in-progress since mid-July; neither company has disclosed detailed integration plans. The acquisition puts a large, concrete dollar figure behind Stripe’s bet that the layer routing developers to AI models is becoming as commercially important as the payment rails underneath them.

Bloomberg, TechCrunch, Fortune, Forkast.

CISA added a second Ray AI-framework vulnerability to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog today, even as CVE-2026-59310 — hitting 361-plus victims in 47 countries — remains absent.

CISA added a new Ray-project vulnerability (CVE-2026-33824) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog today, with a federal patch deadline of Aug. 21 — the second Ray AI-framework flaw added to the list in two days, after CVE-2025-62593 was added Aug. 17 over the “ShadowRay 2.0” cryptomining campaign. Woodland Hills cuts charter students off yellow buses, shifts 140-plus students to PRT cards

Woodland Hills School District told more than 140 students at LIFE Male STEAM Academy, a charter school, that it will provide Pittsburgh Regional Transit cards instead of yellow-bus service this school year — the district’s only offered option, two weeks before classes start. The move follows a January 2026 Pennsylvania Supreme Court ruling holding that districts may end charter-student busing so long as they provide a free transportation alternative; state law otherwise requires districts to bus most charter students within their boundaries. Republican state lawmakers are pushing legislation to reverse the ruling’s effect, and the charter school says it may return to court. The pattern is playing out at multiple Pennsylvania districts, not Woodland Hills alone.

Spotlight PA, Washington Post, WITF, Altoona Mirror.

Pennsylvania

Gov. Shapiro signs executive order imposing binding limits on AI data-center development statewide

Gov. Josh Shapiro signed Executive Order 2026-05 today, converting the previously voluntary “GRID” (Governor’s Responsible Infrastructure Development) standards into binding requirements for AI data centers, which Shapiro’s office called the nation’s strictest such guardrails. Developers must bring and pay for their own power, with a “significant portion” from clean sources; local communities gain effective veto power, since state approval now requires local approval first; data centers — not residential or business ratepayers — must be the first cut off in a grid emergency; and projects lose access to the state’s fast-track permitting process, with nondisclosure agreements barred. The order follows the GOP-controlled state Senate’s decision not to act on GRID legislation the House had passed, and is relevant statewide to utility customers, including in South Fayette and Bridgeville, given this month’s Pennsylvania American Water rate increase.

Commonwealth of Pennsylvania governor’s newsroom (primary) · Philadelphia Inquirer, Local21News, NRDC.
