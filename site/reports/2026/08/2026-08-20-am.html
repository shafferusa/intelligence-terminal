Top Stories

Russia’s heaviest strike on Kyiv in months damages a children’s hospital; toll disputed 5–14+

A combined missile-and-drone barrage hit more than a dozen sites across the Ukrainian capital overnight; a separate drone strike on a Zhytomyr police headquarters killed at least two more.

Russia launched a large combined ballistic-missile-and-drone attack on Kyiv overnight into Thursday, damaging a children’s hospital in the Solomianskyi district — windows blown out, cars burning on the grounds — along with a school and residential buildings across at least twelve locations. President Zelensky said Russia “combined various types of projectiles, cruise missiles and drones to inflict as much damage as possible on civilian infrastructure.” Disputed toll Casualty counts diverge sharply across outlets and remain unsettled: the Kyiv Independent and AP cited five dead and 23 injured; CNN and ABC News cited Kyiv’s mayor and Zelensky putting the figure at least 12 dead; one wire count put it at least 14 dead across the wider Kyiv region. A separate Russian drone strike hit a police headquarters in Zhytomyr, killing two and injuring roughly 40.

Ukraine appears to have continued its own long-range campaign in response: drones reportedly struck an oil refinery in Russia’s Tatarstan region and an oil terminal in Krasnodar Krai overnight, part of an ongoing effort against Russian oil infrastructure. This follows Monday’s roughly 800-drone Ukrainian barrage on the Moscow area and a Russian strike that killed ten in the Kharkiv region — both sides are running some of their largest strike volumes of the war, not de-escalating.

Why it matters A hospital hit directly, even if not deliberately targeted, raises the war’s visible civilian toll at a moment when Russia’s London embassy is separately threatening the UK over its drone supplies to Ukraine — two fronts of the same escalatory pattern.

CNN, Kyiv Independent, ABC News, AP — confirmed-multiple on the attack itself; casualty toll disputed and still developing, presented as a range rather than a single number.

Israel strikes a Syrian airbase near Idlib; Turkey accuses it of “inventing pretexts,” a NATO-adjacent rift opens

Israel cited an alleged Turkish plan to base forces at Abu al-Duhur; Turkey and Syria both deny it, and Axios reports the strikes have put Netanyahu at odds with Washington.

Israeli warplanes struck the Abu al-Duhur military airbase in Syria’s Idlib province in eight airstrikes this week, with Prime Minister Netanyahu saying Syria had ignored repeated warnings over an alleged Turkish plan to establish a military presence there that Israel says would threaten its security. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz then directly warned Turkish President Erdogan against “dragging Turkey into dangerous adventures in Syria.” Turkey’s foreign ministry called the strikes a violation of Syria’s sovereignty and demanded a firmer international response; a Turkish official denied any base presence was planned and accused Israel of “inventing pretexts.” Syria’s government separately said no Turkish buildup was planned.

The US regional envoy reportedly called the strikes an “unnecessary escalation,” and Axios reports the episode has put Netanyahu at odds with Trump — Turkey is a NATO member and a close US partner on Syria policy, and this system has not previously tracked friction at this level between Israel and a NATO ally over Syria.

What’s next No further strikes or statements reported since Wednesday; watch for a Turkish or Israeli move that either de-escalates or hardens the dispute, and for any US mediation.

NPR, Axios, Times of Israel, Arab News, Turkish Minute, Japan Times — confirmed-multiple.

Trump threatens Iran with “Economic D-Day” as Hormuz “fully offensive” deadline warning holds

No reported strikes overnight, but both the rhetorical and economic escalation continue: a new US sanctions threat, a renewed Oman-bombing threat, and the UAE’s trade suspension with Iran still in effect.

Trump posted Wednesday what he called the “most crushing economic operation” against Iran — an “Economic D-Day” threatening severe consequences for any country whose financial institutions, businesses, airports or government aid Iran, extending the existing sanctions campaign layered atop the US Navy’s blockade posture in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran’s foreign ministry rejected it as a “diversion” from US debt problems. This follows Monday’s warning from a senior Iranian official to Reuters that Tehran is shifting to a “fully offensive” posture with a self-declared deadline of “a few weeks” to see the lapsed US-Iran memorandum honored or the naval presence broken by force — the first public deadline either side has attached to the standoff. No reporting found of any strike or kinetic move overnight tied to that warning; a US official said this week “Iran’s IRGC doesn’t control the strait. We do.”

Trump also renewed a threat to bomb Oman if it “gets in the way” of the Strait talks it is brokering; Sen. Tim Kaine says he will introduce a resolution barring military action against Oman. The UAE’s trade suspension with Iran — imposed Tuesday after Abu Dhabi said it intercepted two missiles fired from Iranian territory, which Iran denies launching — remains in effect with no de-escalation reported since. USS George Washington continues transiting to relieve USS Abraham Lincoln, on station roughly 240–260 consecutive days, the longest of any carrier in the modern era.

Biggest risk Iran’s self-declared deadline is unspecific enough that any point in the next few weeks could see it tested; oil and the VIX remain this report’s fastest tells if the standoff moves from rhetoric to action.

Reuters, Al Jazeera, CNBC, The Hill, The National — confirmed-multiple on the threats and posture; no strike confirmed overnight.

Meta whistleblower and a former executive testify Zuckerberg’s safety denials were false

In the $1.4 trillion Oakland federal trial brought by 29 states, Arturo Bejar said he interacted with Zuckerberg “dozens of times” and called his public denials untrue; a separate ex-executive said profit came before child safety.

Whistleblower Arturo Bejar, who spent eight years on Meta product safety, testified Wednesday that he interacted with Mark Zuckerberg “dozens of times” and that Zuckerberg’s public statements denying Meta prioritized profit over child safety were not true. A separate former Meta executive testified the same day that the company put profit ahead of child safety, describing a deliberate strategy to “hook” users. The Oakland federal trial — brought by a 29-state coalition alleging harm to young users, with damages that could run into the trillions — is expected to run six to seven weeks; Zuckerberg and Adam Mosseri are on the states’ witness list but no date is set for either to testify.

This is a distinct case from the earlier Los Angeles state-court trial (JCCP 5255), where Zuckerberg already testified and which concluded in March with a $6 million jury verdict split 70/30 between Meta and Google — the two should not be conflated, and this report has kept them separate since they first appeared.

NPR, Press Democrat, Detroit News — confirmed-multiple.

Marvell’s expanded Google chip deal deepens Broadcom’s credit and exclusivity troubles

MRVL rose 9.85% Wednesday on an SEC-disclosed TPU-ecosystem partnership with Google; AVGO fell 4.61% on the read that its assumed exclusivity has ended, compounding VMware-exploit fallout and credit-downgrade pressure.

Marvell disclosed in an SEC 8-K that it expanded its partnership with Google on July 29 across the TPU ecosystem — AI inference accelerators plus storage, network and memory-interface controllers — with Google receiving a warrant for up to 58,970,907 Marvell shares at a $206.58 strike, vesting through fiscal 2033. Marvell closed +9.85% at $237.27. Broadcom fell 4.61% to $362.48 on the market’s read that Google is no longer its exclusive TPU partner, though Broadcom’s own core TPU development agreement with Google reportedly still runs through 2031 — Marvell looks additive to the supply chain rather than a wholesale replacement, per follow-up analyst commentary. The move compounds pressure already on Broadcom from the VMware vCenter exploit (the company it owns) and continued scrutiny of the roughly $35 billion “AI XPV” financing platform it built with Apollo and Blackstone, cited in an earlier bond-rating downgrade.

Why it matters This reframes Google’s TPU supply chain as genuinely multi-vendor rather than Broadcom-exclusive — a structural read on AVGO that does not depend on any single quarter’s results.

SEC 8-K (primary, this run’s own EDGAR fetch) · CNBC · The Register · this run’s own market data.

Trump-endorsed candidates’ primary losses reach ten this cycle as Rep. Cory Mills is ousted

Mills lost Florida’s 7th District primary amid an active ethics probe and a restraining order; Trump says he told Mills to “get out” but never publicly rescinded his endorsement before the loss.

In Florida’s primary, Trump-endorsed Rep. Cory Mills lost to Ryan Elijah roughly 46% to 34% amid an active House Ethics probe, a restraining order, and allegations from an ex-girlfriend including threats to release explicit images of her, plus campaign-finance concerns. SCOTUS ballroom case fully briefed; ruling could land any time before Friday’s injunction deadline

The applicants’ reply brief was filed Wednesday, completing briefing on the emergency docket; the D.C. Circuit injunction against East Wing ballroom construction otherwise takes effect Friday.

A direct check of the Supreme Court’s own docket (No. 26A203) shows briefing is now complete: the National Trust for Historic Preservation filed its opposition Tuesday, five amicus briefs followed the same day, and the applicants (the administration) filed their reply Wednesday. No order has been issued as of this morning. The underlying dispute: a D.C. Circuit panel upheld a district-court injunction against continued ~$400 million East Wing ballroom construction, finding the administration lacks authority to demolish and rebuild without Congress; that injunction takes effect Friday absent Supreme Court action. The administration argues the project is “beyond the point of no return” and has cited a recent reported missile threat to Air Force One as urgency justification in its filings. Separately, the Court’s mail-ballot executive-order emergency docket also remains fully briefed with no ruling, despite the Solicitor General telling justices it is “critical that the Court act promptly.”

Merck and Moderna’s melanoma vaccine draws independent praise as a “landmark”; mRNA sector rallies further

The trial’s principal investigator called Tuesday’s Phase 3 result a paradigm-shifting moment; BioNTech rose in sympathy despite having no data of its own to report.

A day after Merck and Moderna announced their personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine (intismeran autogene, paired with Keytruda) met its endpoints in the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial, independent expert reaction has arrived: Prof. Georgina Long, the trial’s principal investigator at the Melanoma Institute Australia, called it “a landmark moment for adjuvant melanoma treatment” with the potential to “establish a new treatment paradigm.” Merck’s oncology R&D head Marjorie Green called it “a meaningful milestone.” The broader mRNA and biotech sector kept rallying: BioNTech, which has no comparable data readout of its own, rose sharply in apparent sympathy, treated by investors as validation of the individualized-neoantigen approach generally. A judge orders the county’s first countywide property reassessment since 2012

Common Pleas Judge Kenneth Valasek ruled Allegheny County must begin a reassessment of its 585,000-plus properties by 2027 and complete it by 2032, with regular reassessments required every five years after that.
