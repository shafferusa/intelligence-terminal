Top Stories

US strikes Iranian launchers near Hormuz; Iran fires missiles at US-linked bases in Jordan

The first direct US-Iran military exchange in a month, and the sharpest escalation yet of the standoff over the strait.

US Central Command said forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island, near the Strait of Hormuz, late Saturday into Sunday, after intelligence indicated Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) personnel were preparing to launch sea mines into the strait. CENTCOM spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins confirmed the strike; it is the first publicly acknowledged US strike on Iranian forces since late July, a month in which the administration had signaled a shift toward economic rather than military pressure.

The IRGC vowed retaliation, saying it had suffered casualties in the US strike — a claim carried only by Iranian state broadcasters (IRIB/Press TV) and not independently verified. Early Monday, Iran fired ballistic missiles at two bases hosting US personnel in Jordan, King Hussein Air Base and Al-Azraq Air Base; Jordan’s military said it intercepted eight missiles that entered its airspace, with no casualties or damage reported. The strike and interception are reported by CNN, Gulf News, RFE/RL, ABC Australia, the Jerusalem Post and Time, consistently.

Markets moved first on oil: WTI crude was up roughly 3–4% in early trading, by far the largest move across futures, commodities and currencies this morning, while equity-index futures were little changed. That split — a sharp oil reaction against flat equity futures — suggests traders are treating this as an energy-supply risk rather than a broader risk-off event, for now.

Why it matters This is the most direct US-Iran military contact since the standoff over the strait began in February, and it comes atop six months of tanker seizures, shadow-fleet piracy and dueling claims over who actually controls Hormuz traffic. A second Iranian strike beyond Jordan would be the clearest signal this is escalating rather than a one-off exchange.
What’s next Watch for any further IRGC action against US forces, allies or shipping in the region, and for whether the White House frames Monday’s exchange as closed or ongoing. Oil (CL=F, USO) and Gulf shipping insurance rates are the most direct market channels.

Nepal-Tibet flood toll jumps past 900 dead as Nepal’s own tally keeps shifting

The combined death toll has nearly doubled from Sunday’s figure inside 24 hours, and Nepal’s disaster authority and police still haven’t converged on one number.

As of Monday, Nepal’s own count stands at 903 dead with roughly 4,247 still missing; combined with Tibet’s 16 dead and 546 missing, the cross-border total is 919 dead and about 4,793 missing. That is a sharp jump from the 734–750-plus figure this outlet reported Sunday. NBC News, CNN, the Korea Times, UPI and TASS (citing Chinese state television) all report figures in the 900–919 range as of Monday, but it remains unclear whether the jump reflects new casualties, a reconciliation of Nepal’s own disaster-authority and police counts, or both — the specific reconciliation this outlet has been tracking since last week has not been confirmed either way.

Lancaster County coroner disputes Pennsylvania’s measles-death count; CDC says Harrisburg has shut it out

The state calls an infant’s death “measles-associated”; the county coroner’s own autopsy blames a ruptured spleen and says his office attributes zero deaths in the county to measles.

Lancaster County Coroner Dr. Stephen Diamantoni told LNP/LancasterOnline that an infant who died Aug. 14 at a Christiana Borough birthing center died of a lacerated, ruptured spleen and massive blood loss; the infant tested positive for measles, but the forensic pathologist found no evidence the spleen was inflamed or infected by the virus. Diamantoni said his office currently attributes zero deaths in the county to measles — a direct challenge to Gov. Shapiro’s Aug. 29 announcement of the state’s first two measles-related deaths in roughly 25 years. Pennsylvania’s Health Department, per Politico reporting Aug. 30, has since clarified that its “measles-associated” label means measles was detected in the deceased, not that it was necessarily the immediate cause of death.

Separately, the CDC posted publicly that it “appreciate[s] Secretary Kennedy’s efforts to clarify conflicting information from Pennsylvania officials” and said “the Governor’s Office has not provided CDC with information relevant to the outbreak and has declined our offers of assistance” — the agency’s own account, corroborated by ABC News and The Hill. CMS Administrator Dr. Mehmet Oz said publicly, “We don’t know if the children died from measles or had another condition.” This outlet has not independently verified either side’s characterization of what the other has or hasn’t shared. Pennsylvania’s statewide case count reached 460 confirmed across 31 counties as of Aug. 29, up from the low-to-mid 300s a few days earlier.

Why it matters This is now a three-way dispute — a named county coroner, the state health department and federal officials — over how to characterize the same set of facts, in the middle of the country’s first measles deaths in a generation. The underlying case count is not in dispute; the cause-of-death framing is.

Anthropic warns infostealer malware is hijacking Claude accounts to burn through paid usage

Six known credential-stealing malware families are lifting already-logged-in browser sessions to bypass passwords and two-factor authentication entirely.

Anthropic disclosed that six infostealer malware families — Vidar, LummaC2, StealC, RedLine and Acreed on Windows, Atomic Stealer (AMOS) on macOS — are stealing authenticated browser session cookies from infected machines and using them to hijack Claude logins, bypassing passwords and two-factor authentication because the session is already signed in. The company said it is proactively signing out affected users, removing saved payment methods and refunding unauthorized charges, and stated plainly that it has “no reason to believe this malware is related to Claude, installed through Claude, or related to anything you did with Claude” — the campaigns are general-purpose credential theft, not a Claude-specific compromise. The disclosure is corroborated by BleepingComputer, Security Affairs, The Cyber Express and other outlets, all citing Anthropic’s own advisory.

Why it matters It is a rare instance of an AI lab issuing a security advisory about attacks on its users rather than on its models, and it marks paid AI-subscription accounts as a monetizable target for existing credential-theft infrastructure — the same malware families that have long targeted banking and crypto-exchange logins.

Anthropic (primary disclosure) · BleepingComputer · Security Affairs · The Cyber Express.

September rate-hike odds climb toward 60% as Warsh and Bessent pull in different directions

CME-implied odds of a hike have nearly doubled since Wednesday even as Treasury keeps intervening to hold long yields down.

CME FedWatch-implied odds of a 25-basis-point September hike stood near 60% as of the weekend, up from about 56% Friday and roughly 35% before Fed Chair Warsh’s Thursday Jackson Hole keynote. Deutsche Bank called the speech hawkish and “more specific” than expected; not everyone agrees the repricing is right — Miller Tabak’s Matthew Maley argued there is “no empirical basis for a rate hike” given soft labor data, and one analysis put the true CME-implied probability closer to 58% against social-media framing suggesting figures near 90%. The 30-year Treasury yield touched roughly 5.3%, a near two-decade high, prompting Treasury Secretary Bessent to intervene by doubling the department’s buyback capacity — a structurally awkward pairing, since Warsh is arguing for less Fed communication and more market-driven price discovery at the same time Treasury is actively managing long-end yields down.

Roman Space Telescope “flying on its own” after a clean launch; Turkey signs the Artemis Accords today

No anomalies reported since Sunday’s 7:26 a.m. ET liftoff, as the telescope begins its months-long transit and commissioning journey to L2.

NASA’s mission blog confirmed the Solar Array Sun Shield fully deployed roughly 83 minutes after Sunday’s Falcon Heavy launch from LC-39A, Goddard mission control acquired telemetry within about seven minutes of liftoff, and the Falcon Heavy side boosters landed and were recovered. NASA has since concluded live launch coverage and posted a “Roman Space Telescope Flying on Its Own” update; no anomalies have been reported by NASA, SpaceNews, NPR or Space.com. NASA now describes the trip to the Sun-Earth L2 point as a roughly three-month journey including commissioning, not just a one-month transit — a fuller framing of the same timeline rather than a change to it; first images remain expected around January 2027.

Separately, NASA said Turkey will sign the Artemis Accords at NASA headquarters at 2:30 p.m. ET today, a scheduled but not-yet-occurred event as of this writing.

NASA mission blog (primary) · SpaceNews · NPR · Space.com.
