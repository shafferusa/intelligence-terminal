Today’s News

CIA director's secret Moscow trip surfaces a Trump-Putin-Zelensky summit proposal, as Russia vows "massive" strikes and Kyiv's toll from Friday's attack climbs to 38

Putin has reportedly rejected the idea so far. No summit date exists. The diplomacy landed the same weekend Russia kept striking.

CIA Director John Ratcliffe made a covert visit to Moscow this past week and met Russian intelligence chiefs Sergei Naryshkin (SVR) and Alexander Bortnikov (FSB), floating a trilateral Trump-Putin-Zelensky summit aimed at ending the war, according to Axios's original reporting corroborated by the New York Times, which reported Ratcliffe gave Bortnikov "a bleak assessment" of Russia's war position and urged a deal before conditions worsen for Moscow. US officials briefed Zelensky on the trip Friday; Zelensky has since voiced support for the idea to reporters, per Kyiv Post and Ukrainska Pravda. President Trump characterized Ratcliffe's trip publicly as "standard business." Single source Putin's reported rejection of the summit comes from unnamed sources cited by US outlets; the Kremlin has not addressed it directly on the record, so treat that specific detail as unconfirmed rather than settled.

The proposal surfaced as the war's tempo stayed high. Zelensky said Sunday the death toll from Friday night's drone-and-missile strike on Myla, near Kyiv, rose to 38 with four still missing and roughly 20 injured, including two children — up from the 37 dead reported in yesterday's edition. Russia's defense ministry separately said this weekend it is preparing "massive" strikes on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, a stated intention rather than an executed attack as of this writing. Overnight bombardment continued at a heavy pace: more than 20 drone and artillery hits struck four districts of Dnipropetrovsk region, and a Sunday-morning strike hit warehouse facilities in Zaporizhzhia, sparking a fire.

Why it matters This is the first reported concrete high-level diplomatic overture on ending the war in weeks, arriving the same weekend the death toll from the war's deadliest recent strike kept climbing and Russia signaled it intends to escalate against Ukraine's energy grid heading into autumn.
What’s next No summit date exists. Putin's actual posture — not the version reported by unnamed sources — is the open question this report will keep chasing on the record.

Nepal-Tibet flood toll climbs past 750, still unreconciled between Nepal's own agencies

Nepal's own disaster authority now counts 734 dead, up from 626 on Saturday. China's Tibet-side count nearly tripled to 16. Neither side has explained why.

Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority put the domestic death toll at 734 by Sunday, up sharply from Nepal Police's 626 reported Saturday, with nearly 2,500 people still missing in Nepal alone. Combined across the border, several outlets now cite a regional figure of at least 750 dead and more than 3,000 missing. China's Tibet-side count rose to 16 dead with 546 missing, up from the roughly 5-7 dead reported over the prior two days — still a far lower per-capita toll than Nepal's side, and still not reconciled against it. More than 8,100 people have been rescued in Nepal per the country's Red Cross, which estimates over 90,000 people affected overall. On the physical hazard: Chinese state media describe the landslide-dammed lake that overflowed Friday as "currently stable" with a "relatively low" chance of a further breach, though it remains rescue crews' top operational concern with more rain possible.

What’s next This report's own forecast logged Friday on a joint or reconciled Nepal-China toll by Sept 4 (15–25%, still open) looks less likely by the day — the gap between Nepal's own police and disaster-authority numbers is widening domestically, before any cross-border reconciliation is even attempted.

NBC News · ABC News · CNN · Al Jazeera · China Central Television.

NASA's Roman Space Telescope launches successfully on a Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center

On time, on the primary attempt, no anomalies reported at any stage — a clean flight for a mission running roughly eight months ahead of its original schedule.

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope lifted off at 7:26 AM ET Sunday from Launch Complex 39A aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy, whose 27 Merlin engines produced more than five million pounds of thrust. The spacecraft separated cleanly from the second stage roughly 31 minutes after liftoff and, within the following half hour, deployed its solar-array sun shield — six panels, four of them swinging outward — which provides both power and thermal protection for the observatory's instruments. Roman is now transiting toward the Sun-Earth L2 point, roughly a million miles out, a journey expected to take about a month; NASA plans weeks of commissioning followed by months of science-readiness checkout, with first images expected around January 2027. The $3.9 billion infrared flagship is designed to survey billions of galaxies, hunt exoplanets and study dark energy and black holes.

Why it matters This resolves the open forecast this report logged Friday on whether the mission would launch successfully in its primary or backup window — it did, on the first attempt, with no anomalies reported. Formal grading follows in next Saturday's scorecard.

NASA (primary) · NPR · Space.com.

Gaza sees fresh Sunday strikes as the ceasefire's own overseer's collapse warning stands unanswered

Four Palestinians died in two separate strikes Sunday. Israel and Gaza's health authorities dispute who the strikes hit; neither disputes that they happened.

An Israeli strike near a bakery in Deir al-Balah, central Gaza, killed two Palestinians on Sunday, including a three-year-old child, according to Gaza medics; the Israeli military said it had targeted a Hamas fighter. A separate strike in Khan Younis killed two more people the Israeli military identified as Nukhba operatives. Board of Peace director-general Nickolay Mladenov's Friday warning that the ceasefire mechanism risks collapsing over the disarmament-sequencing dispute this report has tracked for weeks stands unanswered — no new statement from Mladenov, Israel or Hamas has surfaced since. Disputed As with prior Gaza strikes this report has covered, the fact of the strikes is confirmed by multiple outlets; who exactly was targeted and killed is contested between Gaza's health authorities and the Israeli military, and this report presents both characterizations rather than adjudicating between them.

Bitcoin gives back part of last week's rally as Warsh's hawkish tone ripples into the weekend

The only market that traded this weekend moved the same direction equities did Friday — down, on the same rate-path repricing.

Bitcoin traded around $78,630 as of this morning per CoinGecko, down from the roughly $80,000 high it touched last Thursday on the Nvidia-driven risk-on tape, though up modestly (+1.2%) over the trailing 24 hours as of this report's own data pull. US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw $201.8 million in net outflows Friday, ending a nine-day inflow streak. Total crypto market capitalization sits at roughly $2.64 trillion, with Bitcoin dominance at 59.6%.

Why it matters Crypto is the only market that traded over the weekend, and its round trip — down sharply Saturday, partially recovering by Sunday morning — is a live read on how durable Friday's rate-path repricing actually is heading into a week that will test it further with real data.

Turkey issues a fresh, separate arrest warrant for Netanyahu over the May flotilla interception

This is a new warrant on a new allegation, not a repeat of the Gaza-tied one already on record.

Turkey's Justice Ministry is seeking Interpol red notices for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and one other Israeli official over Israel's May interception of an aid flotilla, alleging genocide, as part of a broader 35-person case. This is distinct from an earlier Turkish arrest warrant tied directly to the Gaza campaign, and marks an escalation rather than a continuation of what this report described last week as a cooling dispute between the two governments. Israel does not recognize Turkish jurisdiction over its officials, and Interpol red notices against sitting heads of government or ministers are rarely acted upon by member states; the practical effect is likely to remain diplomatic and symbolic rather than operational.

Times of Israel · Haaretz · Ynet.

Politics & Government Outlook

Judge Talwani's Sept 3 hearing is the week's clearest legal checkpoint: her Aug 27 order issued a fresh 14-day TRO blocking USPS from enforcing new ballot-design and rejection rules through the November elections, finding the record "lacks any evidence of fraudulent mail-in voting" and that USPS's stated interest is "dwarfed by the overwhelming risk of pervasive disenfranchisement," in her own words. Thursday's hearing decides whether that becomes a standing preliminary injunction. Some legal commentators expect this litigation eventually returns to the Supreme Court regardless of Thursday's outcome — that is analyst opinion, not a scheduled fact, and this report labels it as such. The World Ahead

Beyond the Ratcliffe summit proposal above, no Ukraine peace talks are scheduled this week; Ukraine's military-intelligence chief Budanov has voiced hope that negotiations "could resume in September" with US involvement, but that is aspirational language, not a calendared event, and Putin has reportedly said he has no plans to negotiate until Russian forces take the rest of Donbas. Expect the war's tempo — and the risk of another mass-casualty strike, given Ukraine's still-unresolved Patriot interceptor shortage — to be the more reliable signal than diplomacy this week.

Gaza's ceasefire mechanism has no scheduled talks this week either; expect continuing attrition (strikes, disagreement over Hamas's disarmament sequencing) rather than a diplomatic breakthrough or a formal collapse. The Iran-Oman shipping-corridor framework announced Aug 26 remains conditional: Iran says a full reopening awaits US compliance with a now-lapsed June interim deal, and "technical talks" toward a longer-term arrangement have no date. Qatar's mediation continues as a third parallel track alongside Oman and the US, following its prime minister's first high-level visit to Tehran of the war last Thursday. The dueling Iranian and American claims over who controls the Strait of Hormuz remain unverified on both sides, with no new vessel incident found this weekend.

North Korea's "Freedom Edge" trilateral drill runs Sept 7–11, just after this week, but pre-drill rhetoric is likely to continue: Kim Yo Jong has reiterated Pyongyang's "no interest" in reduced drills and separately condemned a reported $125 million Sidewinder missile sale to South Korea, warning of a "serious, immediate and powerful response." North Korea fired roughly ten short-range ballistic missiles on Aug 20, its largest single-day launch in months and a plausible — not confirmed — pattern for a pre-drill signal this week.

The DRC's Ebola outbreak stood at 5,794 confirmed cases and 2,786 deaths as of Aug 27 per the World Health Organization, still the deadliest in the country's history. The one new development is a positive one: DRC's health ministry launched an ERVEBO vaccination campaign for frontline workers Aug 27 across three provinces, the first concrete response scale-up since the funding-shortfall warning this report tracked the prior week. Separately, the WHO declared a related but distinct outbreak in Uganda over on Aug 26 after 42 days with no new case — a contrast point, not a resolution of the DRC's own outbreak.

The Economy Ahead

Friday's August jobs report is the week's central event, and the previews disagree with each other by an unusual margin worth naming rather than smoothing over: one roundup puts consensus near 110,000 jobs added with unemployment holding at 4.2%; a more recent preview puts it closer to 58,000 with unemployment at 4.1%. Individual named estimates span the same range — Interactive Brokers' José Torres at 140,000 (above the pack), LPL Financial's Jeff Roach at 50,000 (below it), Capital Economics at 90,000, citing immigration curbs constraining labor supply. This report is presenting a range, roughly 50,000 to 140,000, rather than picking a single number that several credentialed forecasters themselves don't agree on.

September rate-hike odds after Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole debut are similarly source-dependent: a CME-derived measure puts the odds near 60%, up from roughly 35% before the speech, while Kalshi's own prediction market moved to 47%. Three named economists hold three different views on what happens next — Natixis's Christopher Hodge says the market was underpriced before and is "now appropriately priced"; Navy Federal's Heather Long expects no September move but one by October or December; Morgan Stanley's Michael Gapen argues decelerating inflation keeps the Fed on the sideline in September. The Fed's blackout period for the Sept 16 meeting begins Saturday, so officials remain free to speak Monday through Thursday this week, though no specific speaker events were found scheduled.

Credit and volatility both eased into the weekend rather than confirming Friday's equity wobble: the ICE BofA high-yield spread tightened to 2.63% (from 2.75% the prior week) and the VIX actually fell to 14.43, in normal contango against VIX9D (11.22) and VIX3M (17.48) — no inversion, no stress signal. The Treasury curve steepened at the front this week (2-year yield +10bp to 4.34%) while compressing at the long end (30-year -5bp to 5.22%), flattening 2s10s by 11bp net to 39bp.
