1. A genuinely mixed inflation week: CPI cools, PPI's core quadruples, retail sales and sentiment both miss

Initial event
Wednesday's July CPI printed cooler than feared: headline +0.1% m/m / +3.4% y/y (matching consensus, down from June's 3.5%), core +0.2% m/m / +2.5% y/y, also matching consensus. Markets read it as unambiguously dovish — VIX fell 5.4% same session, the sharpest single-session move this report tracked all week.
Development
Thursday's July PPI complicated the picture: headline flat (0.0% m/m, +4.7% y/y) but core quadrupled its prior pace to +0.4% m/m (+4.7% y/y), driven by services (portfolio management +6.5%) and construction, even as goods and energy fell. Equities extended Wednesday's rally anyway (S&P +0.65%, Nasdaq +0.81%), a genuine data/price-action disconnect this report does not force a narrative around. Jobless claims kept climbing (209,000, a fourth straight weekly rise from 189,000 three weeks earlier). Friday's July retail sales then missed outright: −0.6% m/m headline (sharpest since May 2025), control group −0.4% vs. +0.3% consensus, and August's preliminary University of Michigan sentiment fell to 51.0 vs. 54.5 consensus, with 1-year inflation expectations rising to 4.3%.
Final status
The week closes with the Fed's hawkish-vs-dovish tension sharpened rather than resolved: Cleveland Fed's Beth Hammack said she wants to “act now” after the hot core PPI, while Friday's demand-side data argues the opposite. No FOMC meeting until Sept 15–16; no Fed official has yet commented publicly on either print.
Why it mattered
This is the single data thread every other market move this week sits on top of — the CPI relief rally, the PPI-driven yield dip, and Friday's soft-landing-turned-soft-demand read on retail sales/sentiment all flow from the same underlying question the Fed has to resolve next month.
What was misunderstood
The market's same-session read of each release proved a poor guide to the next one — Wednesday's CPI relief did not anticipate Thursday's hot core PPI, and neither anticipated Friday's demand miss. Three consecutive releases each surprised the prevailing narrative from the day before.
Unresolved
Whether September brings a hold, cut, or (per Warsh's private hawkish signaling reported earlier this month) even a hike; whether the retail-sales/sentiment miss is the start of a genuine demand slowdown or a one-month air pocket.
Keep on watchlist?
Yes — central through Jackson Hole (Aug 27) and the Sept FOMC.

2. Colombia's earthquake toll climbs from 132 to 273 across five distinct official figures in four days

Initial event
The Aug 10 magnitude-7.4 quake near San José del Palmar, Chocó, killed an initial ~20, rising to 132 confirmed by Tuesday morning.
Development
The toll then moved through a genuinely unusual sequence of disagreeing government sources: 132 (Tue am) → 224 (Tue pm, Colombian Association of Capital Cities) → a stalled UNGRD count still citing 181 against media tallies above 250 (Wed) → President de la Espriella's own figure of 265, 84 above UNGRD's simultaneous 181 (Thu am) → a final reconciliation to 273 dead / 3,824 injured / 377 missing once the president ordered all casualty communications centralized through his office (Thu pm). A sixth-day figure of 281 (Fri) and an uncorroborated 285 from a single Colombian outlet (Sat, not yet matched by wire services) extend the climb further.
Final status
273 dead is the last figure this report can source to a named, on-record government press conference (UNGRD Director David Santiago Tamayo, Aug 13); 281 is the most recent wire-corroborated figure (AP-derived, Aug 14). More than 12,500 homes destroyed, 74,000+ damaged; a domestic controversy has emerged over the Petro government's initial reluctance to accept foreign search-and-rescue assistance.
Why it mattered
Beyond the humanitarian scale, the toll's own reconciliation arc became a story about crisis-communications governance — two arms of the same government citing figures 84 apart, resolved only by an explicit presidential order to centralize reporting.
What was misunderstood
Early in the week it looked like a standard under-count-then-catch-up pattern; the presidential-vs-UNGRD gap revealed it was actually two parallel, uncoordinated counting processes.
Unresolved
Whether 273/281 is close to final, or whether the pattern of continued upward revision (five moves in four days) still has room to run; the Sat 285 figure is not yet independently confirmed.
Keep on watchlist?
Yes, at reduced cadence — toll confirmation, not escalation.

Sources: UNGRD press conference (primary) · Colombian presidency statements (primary) · ABC News, Bloomberg, BBC, ColombiaOne, The City Paper Bogotá (secondary, partly single-sourced on the latest 285 figure) · USGS (quake parameters, primary).

Section 5What Changed in the World

Two deadline-shaped storylines that last Sunday's outlook flagged as “trending toward missed deadlines” both did exactly that, and both then produced concrete follow-through rather than simply fading: Gaza's checkpoint passed unresolved but drew Kushner's personal intervention, and the quiet Hormuz stretch ended in new incidents rather than a durable de-escalation. A third deadline story — Colombia's earthquake toll — resolved faster than its own four-day pattern suggested once the government centralized its reporting, a reminder that governance fixes can outrun even an established data-quality problem.

On the humanitarian side, the DRC Ebola outbreak's crossing of 2,000 deaths and Colombia's climbing toll both represent genuine escalations in ongoing crises rather than new events — this report's own coverage intensity for both lagged their scale, addressed in Section 23. The Chamoli, India tunnel-flood landslide (Aug 13–14, at least 6–7 confirmed dead, trapped/missing figures fluctuating wildly across outlets) was a genuinely new disaster this week with figures still unstable as of Friday's cutoff.

In markets, the week's defining shift was the breakdown of any single dominant narrative: three macro-data surprises in three consecutive sessions (CPI, PPI, retail sales/sentiment) and three separate single-name earnings shocks (YSS, LUNR, the alt-asset-manager round trip) each moved markets independently, with the S&P 500's modest +0.36% weekly return masking substantially more churn underneath. A domestic story with limited market relevance but genuine national significance — Luigi Mangione's Friday guilty plea to federal stalking charges in the killing of UnitedHealthcare's CEO, with sentencing set for Dec. 18 — closed out the week.
