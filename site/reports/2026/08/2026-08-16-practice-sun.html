Today’s News

Kushner meets Hamas’s leadership in Cairo; a Netanyahu sit-down is next

Hamas says it will disarm — but only once Netanyahu commits to the same roadmap publicly, which is exactly what Monday’s meeting is supposed to test.

Jared Kushner, Tony Blair and Board of Peace envoy Nickolay Mladenov met Hamas’s leadership in Cairo on Sunday, in the presence of Qatari, Egyptian and Turkish officials — a rare direct sit-down people briefed on it described as “very productive.” The roughly 90-minute session focused on getting Hamas to commit to practical, sequenced steps under the stalled 15-point disarmament roadmap. Hamas reaffirmed its willingness to disarm and demilitarize Gaza, but told Kushner it will only begin implementing the plan once Prime Minister Netanyahu publicly commits to its terms himself. Kushner and Mladenov then landed in Israel and are scheduled to meet Netanyahu on Monday, with Blair.

It is the first concrete high-level US follow-through since Netanyahu’s Aug. 9 rejection of the roadmap, and it lands with regional pressure building: Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Egypt, Jordan, Qatar and Turkey issued a joint statement over the weekend blaming Israel for thwarting the plan. Separately, Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon killed 11–15 people Saturday — the deadliest toll since June’s truce, and one Lebanon’s president explicitly linked to the US-backed Gaza talks.

Why it matters This is the closest the roadmap has come to a real test since Netanyahu’s rejection: Hamas has put a specific, public condition on the table rather than a general objection, and Monday’s meeting is where that condition either gets met or doesn’t.
What’s next Monday’s Kushner-Netanyahu meeting. Whether Netanyahu offers anything resembling the public commitment Hamas is asking for should be the first thing checked in tomorrow morning’s brief — and it is the trigger for the risk this report is tracking in Risks Entering the Week, below.

Washington Post, NPR, KPBS, Boston.com, GPB, NHPR · Times of Israel and Haaretz live coverage · regional joint-statement coverage via Al Jazeera.

Go deeper

The roadmap has now missed two informal deadlines — the original 14-day implementation window that lapsed Aug. 13, and the softer expectation that some negotiating reset would follow within days. What changed this weekend is procedural, not substantive: for the first time, a specific, sequenced condition (Hamas disarms once Netanyahu publicly commits) is on the table rather than a standoff between two general positions. That is progress in form before it is progress in substance — and it is the reason this week’s outlook below treats Monday’s meeting, not the Cairo session itself, as the real checkpoint.

Iran rebuffs Trump’s Hormuz “territory” claim after a 16th tanker strike

“The Strait cannot be seized by tweet, nor by aircraft carrier” — Iran’s deputy foreign minister, after the third ADNOC-linked vessel hit in 48 hours.

Iran struck an ADNOC tanker transiting the Strait of Hormuz Saturday — the third ADNOC-linked vessel attack in 48 hours, and by Al Jazeera’s count the 16th such strike since the campaign began in the spring. President Trump, at a weekend rally, repeated that he intends to declare the Strait US “territory” and said the US has “total control” and might “keep it.” Iran rejected the claim at multiple levels: Foreign Minister Araghchi’s office said the two countries do “not have anything like a ceasefire” and that no negotiations are underway, while Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi wrote on X that the Strait “cannot be seized by tweet, nor by aircraft carrier… has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian,” and told Trump to accept he had “suffered strategic and heavy defeats.”

Shipping traffic through the Strait remains near three-month lows — Windward Daily Intelligence data cited by CNN put it at roughly 17% of the pre-conflict average this past week. The Southern Omani detour route, used since the main channel became too dangerous, has itself absorbed 16 of 18 UKMTO-logged projectile strikes since early July. The US naval blockade barring vessels from Iranian ports remains in effect; USS George Washington continues transiting toward the Gulf to relieve USS Abraham Lincoln, deployed 250-plus days, with no confirmed arrival date.

Why it matters Iran’s explicit denial that any ceasefire exists is a harder position than a dispute over strait sovereignty alone, and neither side is using de-escalation language. This is the sharpest rhetorical exchange this report has tracked in the Hormuz thread since strikes paused briefly in late July.
What’s next Watch for a fourth incident or any US policy step matching Trump’s territorial language — Treasury Secretary Bessent has previewed further “economic isolation” measures this coming week. This is Risk 1 in Risks Entering the Week, below, with a forecast attached.

CNN, Al Jazeera, gCaptain, Jerusalem Post, CNBC, The National, NPR live coverage · UKMTO advisories.

Ukraine flies its largest drone attack of the war; Russia hits back across the country

Hundreds of drones toward Moscow, missiles and drones back on Kyiv — a weekend escalation, not a lull, heading into a week with no ceasefire talks scheduled.

Ukraine launched hundreds of drones at Russia overnight Saturday into Sunday, one of the largest such attacks of the war; Russia’s defense ministry said it destroyed 822 of them, with Moscow Mayor Sobyanin reporting roughly 600 tracked toward the capital. Russia’s Rostov region took the heaviest hit — more than 150 drones damaged homes and a railway station and sparked a forest fire, killing five people, while an 83-year-old man died when a drone struck a home in the Moscow region.

Russia retaliated across Ukraine, killing at least seven civilians and injuring 51. Ukraine’s air force said Russia struck Kyiv, Kremenchuk and Kryvyi Rih with Onyx supersonic anti-ship missiles, ballistic missiles and 106 long-range attack drones; air defenses intercepted three of the missiles and 85 of the drones. Fires broke out across Kyiv, injuring six. A separate claim that a Ukrainian drone was downed over Romanian airspace by a NATO jet remains unconfirmed against a primary NATO or Romanian government source and is flagged rather than asserted here.

Why it matters This is a scale escalation on both sides at once — Ukraine’s drone count and Russia’s three-city retaliatory footprint both mark one of the conflict’s larger weekends, with no diplomatic track scheduled to address it this week.

Kyiv Independent, CNN, NPR, Al Jazeera · Russian Ministry of Defense and Moscow Mayor Sobyanin statements (labeled) · Ukraine Air Force statement.

Indonesia’s earthquake toll holds at 53; Colombia’s is revised down for the first time

Two unrelated disasters, two different trajectories: Indonesia’s count has stabilized after a shallow M7.7; Colombia’s pulled back from 294 to 289 on a reconciliation, not new survivors.

Indonesia’s National Disaster Management Agency (BNPB) held the confirmed toll from Saturday’s magnitude-7.7 quake off Flores Island at 53 dead, with more than 1,300 homes damaged and a tsunami warning issued and later lifted. A separate, unrelated magnitude-6.9 quake off Sumatra caused no casualties given its extreme depth. In Colombia, the confirmed death toll from the Aug. 10 magnitude-7.4 quake near San José del Palmar stands at 289 as of Sunday, down from 294 after authorities cross-checked casualty records — the first downward revision in a week where the figure climbed almost daily. Roughly 143 people remain missing there as search operations enter their final phase.

Why it matters A downward revision after a week of centralizing casualty reporting is a sign Colombia’s count is stabilizing, not evidence the disaster was smaller than believed; Indonesia’s toll bears watching given the country’s exposure to further Ring-of-Fire aftershocks (230-plus have already followed).

What Monday inherits: mixed Asian futures, a quiet crypto tape

Nikkei and Hang Seng futures point modestly higher than Friday’s close; ASX futures point lower — a genuinely mixed handoff into a week that needs Tuesday’s Home Depot print to set its real direction.

With US cash equities shut since Friday, the first live read on how markets are digesting the weekend comes from Asia-Pacific futures ahead of Monday’s open. Japan’s Nikkei 225 futures traded near 68,900 against Friday’s 68,713.8 close; Hong Kong’s Hang Seng futures traded near 25,247 against a 25,116.85 close — both modestly above their prior settle. Australia’s ASX 200 futures pointed the other way, near 9,015 against a 9,115.2 close. Taken together, that is a genuinely mixed setup rather than a clear risk-on or risk-off signal heading into Monday, consistent with a weekend that sharpened rhetoric (Hormuz, Gaza) without a confirmed new shock.

Crypto, the only market that traded through the whole weekend, stayed quiet: bitcoin held near $62,900 as of Sunday evening ET, little-changed from Saturday’s levels, with ether near $1,880. Both are still carrying the tone from Friday’s report — subdued volume, renewed spot Bitcoin-ETF outflows earlier in the week, and no fresh catalyst over the weekend.

Why it matters Neither the futures picture nor crypto is pricing anything dramatic from the weekend’s geopolitical headlines — which either means markets see them as contained, or that Tuesday’s retail earnings and Thursday’s SpaceX unlock are simply bigger, more immediate catalysts than a Sunday-night news cycle. Politics & Government Outlook

Two separate Supreme Court matters could land this week. The mail-ballot executive order case has been fully briefed for weeks with no ruling; DOJ’s $100 bond at the 1st Circuit is due Wednesday, and its supplemental brief continues urging the Court to “act promptly.” A second, legally distinct emergency application — filed Friday — asks the Court to let construction resume on the roughly $400 million White House East Wing ballroom project after a divided D.C. Circuit panel ordered a halt, siding with the National Trust for Historic Preservation; underground work may continue, only the aboveground build is paused. Both are on the emergency “shadow docket,” which can move without advance notice, so either is a genuine candidate for the week’s first real political surprise.

Fed Governor Lisa Cook’s removal fight is unchanged: her attorneys’ letter calling the mortgage-fraud allegations “baseless” remains her most recent formal response, with her 21-day response window running to roughly Aug. 26. Luigi Mangione’s defense has moved to dismiss the separate Manhattan state murder case on double-jeopardy grounds after his Aug. 14 federal guilty plea; Manhattan DA Alvin Bragg’s office says it will fight the motion, and it falls to the state trial judge to decide whether New York’s narrower double-jeopardy statute reaches this case. Congress is largely dark this week — the Senate holds only a pro forma session Monday and the House does not return until Aug. 31 — so no floor action on the stalled NDAA is expected.

The World Ahead

Beyond the Gaza and Hormuz threads covered in Today’s News, the DRC’s Ebola outbreak — already the largest ever recorded in the country and a declared public-health emergency — stood at 4,665 confirmed cases and 2,184 deaths as of the most recent WHO figures, spread now confirmed to a sixth province, with roughly 60–70% of deaths occurring in the community outside formal care. WHO’s IHR Emergency Committee reconvenes Tuesday, Aug. 18 to reassess the outbreak’s PHEIC status — a genuine decision point after three months of continuous case growth, and this week’s most consequential global-health date. Uganda’s linked outbreak was declared over July 28, a positive counterpoint.

Ethiopia’s Tigray region showed a tentative de-escalation this past week, per a single TPLF-affiliated source not independently corroborated by the federal government; Amnesty International separately warned the underlying human-rights risk to residents persists regardless of the immediate military trajectory. No new statement from either side surfaced over the weekend. The Saudi-Turkiye-Pakistan mutual-defense pact, signed two Fridays ago, still shows no formal invocation request after its inconclusive first test (neither Turkiye nor Pakistan responded to the Aug. 9 Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia) — unchanged status heading into the week.

Scenarios

Base scenario (most likely, roughly 40–50%). No fourth Hormuz incident occurs; retail earnings are mixed rather than uniformly weak; the SpaceX unlock passes with a notable but not extreme single-session move; the Kushner-Netanyahu meeting produces engagement without a breakthrough. Equities trade in a range near current levels, with single-name volatility exceeding index-level volatility — consistent with the past several weeks’ pattern. Confirmers: a Home Depot print in line with consensus; SpaceX moving 3–5% around the unlock. Invalidators: any of the shock triggers below.

Bull scenario (roughly 15–20%). Retail earnings broadly beat and reassure on the consumer, Hormuz stays quiet without a fourth incident, the SpaceX unlock passes calmly — echoing the Aug. 6 lockup’s muted reaction — and Netanyahu offers something close to the public commitment Hamas wants. Equities extend toward fresh records, yields ease modestly, oil stays contained. This requires several favorable confirmations to align at once, which is why it carries the lower probability band.

Bear scenario (roughly 20–25%). At least two big-box retailers miss or guide cautiously, confirming a genuine slowdown; the SpaceX unlock triggers mechanical selling pressure; a fourth Hormuz incident occurs without escalating further; the Kushner-Netanyahu meeting ends without any statement. Equities give back a meaningful share of recent gains, discretionary names underperform, yields stay elevated on stagflation-shaped data.

Shock scenario (roughly 8–12%). A Hormuz incident produces confirmed casualties or a direct US/Iran exchange; or the SpaceX unlock triggers a disorderly double-digit decline; or the Gaza track’s failure coincides with a fresh regional escalation, echoing this weekend’s Lebanon strikes. Oil spikes, broad risk-off, a safe-haven bid, and the VIX moves back above its recent range. This requires a confirmed, severe escalation beyond anything observed as of this report’s cutoff — an analytic judgment, not a statistical model.

What would change this outlook: a confirmed fourth Hormuz incident with casualties or any concrete US policy step matching Trump’s territorial rhetoric would immediately move the base case toward bear or shock. A statement from Monday’s Kushner-Netanyahu meeting, in either direction, should be the first thing checked in tomorrow morning’s brief. Two or more retail misses this week would validate Friday’s data as a genuine trend heading into September.
