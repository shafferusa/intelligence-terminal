Top Stories

US-Iran ceasefire lapses at the Hormuz deadline; Iran says Washington must lift its blockade first

The 60-day Islamabad truce runs out with no extension agreed, a third ADNOC tanker struck in 48 hours, and Iran’s deputy foreign minister answering Trump’s claim to the Strait with one line: it cannot be seized “by tweet.”

The US-Iran ceasefire signed in Islamabad on June 17 formally expired over the weekend with no extension reached. Iran’s Foreign Ministry said Monday that Washington must lift what it calls its blockade of the Strait of Hormuz before Tehran will fully reopen it; Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said separately that Iran has “not made a decision to restart negotiations,” and a senior White House official described talks as “static.” Shipping traffic through the Strait remains near 17% of its pre-conflict average, per UKMTO’s most recent weekly count of 151 transits.

The backdrop hardened through the weekend before the deadline. President Trump told a rally Friday he would “pretty soon” declare the Strait “a territory of the United States” once Iran is defeated; a White House official later told the Wall Street Journal Trump was “joking.” Iran’s deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, answered on X: the Strait “cannot be seized by Tweet, nor by aircraft carrier, nor by issuing an order, nor by an election speech.” A third ADNOC-linked vessel was struck Friday evening, the UAE Foreign Ministry accusing Iran’s IRGC of “piracy” and using the Strait as “a tool of economic coercion.” USS George Washington remains in transit — last tracked passing the Strait of Malacca — to relieve USS Abraham Lincoln, which has now logged more than 250 consecutive days at sea.

Why it matters Roughly a fifth of global oil supply transits the Strait. Oil and equities have so far priced five days of escalating rhetoric and repeated tanker strikes as background risk, not a supply threat — WTI is up only modestly since Thursday. That gap between rhetoric and price is this week’s central test.
What’s next Whether any government issues a statement explicitly marking the deadline’s lapse, and whether a fourth tanker incident is confirmed. This report found no evidence of an automatic snapback to open hostilities at the deadline itself — only that the truce’s stated term has run out unresolved.

Kushner meets Hamas’s leadership directly in Cairo; sits down with Netanyahu today

The first reported direct Kushner-Hamas meeting precedes today’s Israel stop — the most concrete US push on Gaza disarmament since Netanyahu rejected the roadmap’s sequencing on Aug. 9 — while Israeli strikes killed at least 11 in southern Lebanon over the same weekend.

Jared Kushner, Tony Blair and Board of Peace envoy Nickolay Mladenov met Hamas leader Khalil al-Hayya directly in El-Alamein, Egypt on Sunday for roughly two hours, pressing implementation of the 15-point disarmament roadmap. Kushner then flew to Israel; as of this morning he and the envoys are meeting or about to meet Netanyahu, with no outcome or readout yet available. Netanyahu publicly rejected the roadmap’s sequencing on Aug. 9, insisting on Hamas disarmament before any further Israeli withdrawal; Hamas has said the reverse.

Separately, Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon Saturday killed at least 11 people — seven in the village of Ansar, including three children, and four more in nearby Deir Al Zahrani — the deadliest exchange since the June truce there. The IDF said the strikes answered a Hezbollah attack that wounded three soldiers and that it had killed a senior Hezbollah commander a day earlier; Hezbollah has threatened retaliation. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun called the strikes a “clear message” aimed at the Gaza negotiations and condemned “repeated violations” of the framework agreement.

What’s next Whether today’s Netanyahu meeting produces any joint statement or confirmed next step; this report is not reporting an outcome that has not yet happened.

Axios, Al Jazeera, NPR, Haaretz (live), CNN, US News (Kushner-Hamas-Netanyahu) · The National, Times of Israel, France24, Euronews, Bloomberg (Lebanon strikes).

Ukraine launches its largest drone attack of the war; Russia retaliates across four regions

Hundreds of drones targeted Russia overnight Saturday into Sunday; Russia’s claim of 822 intercepted is unverified outside Moscow’s own account, while Ukrainian casualties are corroborated across multiple Western outlets.

Ukraine launched one of its largest drone barrages of the war overnight Aug. 15-16, striking a Wildberries warehouse near Podolsk and killing at least six people in Russia, per Ukrainian and Russian reporting. Russia’s defense ministry claims 822 drones were intercepted nationwide — a figure this report could not independently verify and is treating as Russian-official-sourced only. Russia retaliated with a reported 1,550-plus drones, roughly 1,560 aerial bombs and 62 missiles across the week, hitting 13 regions: a ballistic-missile strike on Kryvyi Rih’s ArcelorMittal steel plant killed two and wounded 14, partially halting the plant; Sumy lost one; Kyiv reported six injured including at a book market; Zaporizhzhia lost two. These casualty figures trace to Zelensky and Ukrainian officials, reported by multiple independent Western outlets rather than independently confirmed on the ground — standard sourcing for this conflict, and consistent across CNN, NPR, PBS and CBS.

CNN, CBS News, NPR, PBS NewsHour, France24, Kyiv Post, Ukrainska Pravda · Russian MoD claims labeled as such, not independently verified.

DRC Ebola outbreak reaches a sixth province after a fatal imported case; WHO reconvenes Tuesday

Confirmed cases have climbed to 4,727 and deaths to 2,214 — already the largest Ebola outbreak DRC has ever recorded — as the World Health Organization prepares its second emergency review of the outbreak’s global-health-emergency status.

Africa CDC and WHO regional figures as of Aug. 15 put the outbreak at 4,727 confirmed cases and 2,214 deaths, up from 4,665 and 2,184 as of Aug. 13. The outbreak reached a sixth province, Bas-Uele, after an imported case from Haut-Uele was identified in the provincial capital, Buta; that patient has since died. Fifty-five health zones across six provinces are now affected, with Ituri remaining the epicenter. No pre-meeting statement or updated risk assessment from WHO had been published as of this morning ahead of Tuesday’s IHR Emergency Committee meeting, its second review since the outbreak was declared a public-health emergency of international concern in May.

World Health Organization (primary), Africa CDC · Xinhua, ECDC.

Magnitude-7.7 earthquake strikes Indonesia’s Flores Island; death toll climbing, one much higher figure unverified

Most dated reporting puts the confirmed toll near 47-53 as of Sunday, with thousands evacuated; a single outlet’s figure of 268 dead is a significant outlier this report is not treating as confirmed.

A magnitude-7.7 earthquake struck Saturday morning local time near Ende, on Indonesia’s Flores Island in East Nusa Tenggara province. Most multiply-sourced reporting through Sunday puts the confirmed death toll at 47 to 53, with roughly 5,000 people evacuated, dozens injured and hundreds of aftershocks recorded; officials say the toll is expected to climb further as search operations continue. One outlet reported a substantially higher figure of 268 dead and 151 missing — this report could not corroborate that number against the multiply-sourced range and is flagging it explicitly as disputed rather than averaging it in.

Al Jazeera, CBS News, CNBC, NBC News (Confirmed-multiple, 47-53 range) · one outlier outlet report of 268, labeled Disputed and not adopted.

Record flooding kills at least six across central Indiana; White River breaks a 113-year-old crest record

More than 11 inches of rain in two days pushed the White River past its 1913 record at Anderson and Noblesville, prompting evacuations and dozens of rescues in Indianapolis.

Historic flooding across central Indiana that began Aug. 11 has killed at least six people as of Aug. 16. The White River crested just shy of 25 feet at Anderson and Noblesville, breaking the 1913 record of 23.6 feet by more than a foot after some areas received over 11 inches of rain in two days. Indianapolis flooding peaked early Sunday; the city reopened floodgates and opened emergency shelters, with roughly 100 people and dozens of pets rescued by city firefighters.

National Weather Service (primary), CBS News, NPR, US News.

Two Supreme Court emergency dockets remain unresolved: the mail-ballot order and the White House ballroom project

A 1st Circuit panel rejected DOJ’s request to stay Judge Talwani’s mail-ballot injunction; separately, the government’s ballroom filing newly confirmed a July assassination-threat evacuation of Air Force One as part of its urgency argument.

The 1st Circuit ruled 2-1 over the weekend against DOJ’s request to stay Judge Talwani’s nationwide injunction blocking the administration’s mail-ballot executive order, finding neither DOJ nor the twelve supporting states had shown the order was lawful. DOJ’s emergency stay application remains pending at the Supreme Court, re-upped Sunday per Democracy Docket; a $100 bond Talwani required of plaintiff organizations is due Tuesday. No Supreme Court ruling has issued on either this matter or the administration’s separate Aug. 14 request to let the roughly $400 million White House East Wing ballroom project resume construction pending appeal, after a D.C. Circuit panel halted it. Chief Justice Roberts set the preservationist plaintiffs’ response deadline for tomorrow, Tuesday. The government’s ballroom filing newly disclosed that a July 8 intelligence warning, relayed by Israel, of a possible missile attack on Air Force One near Turkey prompted a secret switch to an alternate aircraft — cited as part of the administration’s national-security urgency argument.

What’s next Both matters sit on the emergency “shadow docket,” which can move without advance notice; Tuesday brings both the mail-ballot bond deadline and the ballroom response deadline.

Democracy Docket, Votebeat, Boston Globe, WBUR (mail-ballot) · PBS NewsHour, ABC News, NBC News, CBS News, The Hill (ballroom filing, primary DOJ filing cited).

Retail-earnings week opens against tariff crosscurrents: Home Depot Tuesday, a Canada tariff deadline and billions in refunds

Home Depot, Lowe’s, Target and Walmart report in sequence this week, the first hard test of Friday’s retail-sales miss, while a 50% Canada tariff deadline and fresh refunds to major retailers complicate the read.

Home Depot reports Q2 results Tuesday before the open — confirmed via its own investor-relations release, correcting this report’s earlier working date of Wednesday — consensus EPS roughly $4.71-4.88, its first results since CEO Ted Decker began temporary medical leave Aug. 12. Lowe’s and Target follow Wednesday, Walmart closes the block Thursday; all four land directly against Friday’s retail-sales and consumer-sentiment miss. Separately, the administration has refunded roughly $100 billion in tariffs to date following the Supreme Court ruling narrowing the tariff regime — reported individual refunds include $2.2 billion to Apple and $1.3 billion to Ford, with Walmart potentially eligible for up to $10.2 billion and Target up to $2.2 billion, a detail directly relevant to this week’s earnings reads.

A busy weekend for cybersecurity: a French tax-agency breach, an escalated macOS flaw, and an ongoing $100M-plus bitcoin theft

France’s DGFiP confirms 678,000 taxpayers exposed, Apple’s Screen Sharing flaw is raised to critical severity, and the VMware vCenter exploit already tracked by this report remains unresolved on the federal KEV catalog.

France’s tax authority, DGFiP, confirmed Aug. 16 that a hacker using the name “Zerobytes” stole records on 678,438 taxpayers — names, dates of birth, addresses, land-registry and tax-bracket data — in a late-June intrusion via compromised internal credentials; the secure taxpayer portal itself was not accessed, and notifications to affected residents begin today. Separately, CISA raised the severity of a macOS Screen Sharing flaw (CVE-2026-65400) from 7.1 to a critical 9.8 on Aug. 14 after Dutch NCSC and CISA both confirmed active exploitation to install cryptominers on exposed Macs; Apple patched it Aug. 6 in Tahoe 26.6.1, Sequoia 15.7.9 and Sonoma 14.8.9. The VMware vCenter flaw (CVE-2026-59310) this report has tracked since early August remains under active, wide-scale exploitation — 361-plus victims across 47 countries — and still could not be confirmed one way or the other against the federal KEV catalog; direct cisa.gov fetches were blocked again this run, a recurring environment issue. Also ongoing: the Coldcard hardware-wallet exploit first reported July 30, tied to a 2021 firmware flaw that weakened seed-phrase randomness; running loss estimates from different trackers span roughly $89 million to over $130 million as the incident has unfolded in waves, with TRM Labs’ most recent count near $112-116 million. Coinkite has patched the flaw and is urging affected users to move funds.

Security Affairs, The Record, Bitcoin.com News (France DGFiP) · The Hacker News, Tom’s Hardware, BleepingComputer (macOS flaw) · Rapid7, SecurityWeek, TheHackerNews (VMware) · Bloomberg, Fortune, CoinDesk, TechCrunch, TRM Labs (Coldcard).

Rocket Lab’s MDA-built Globalstar satellites reach orbit as SpaceX runs a record double launch; China’s Long March 7A probe stays open

Two Falcon 9s flew 38 minutes apart Saturday night, while China has still not said what caused the Aug. 10 Long March 7A failure and this report’s own tracking of the Zhuque-3 reflight date needed correcting.

SpaceX launched two Falcon 9 missions from Florida and California 38 minutes apart Saturday night — one carrying eight satellite platforms Rocket Lab built for MDA Space under a $143 million, 17-satellite Globalstar replenishment deal, the other a Space Force payload — a cadence outlets described as a company record. Rocket Lab confirmed contact with all eight satellites and nominal performance Aug. 16. Separately, China’s CASC has issued no formal statement on what caused the Aug. 10 Long March 7A explosion roughly 83-85 seconds after liftoff; independent trackers now put LandSpace’s Zhuque-3 Flight 2 — postponed after the failure — at Aug. 31, not the Aug. 18 date this report had been carrying, though LandSpace itself has not confirmed the new date. China’s Chang’e 7 lunar mission on a Long March 5 still targets roughly Aug. 24; the shared YF-100 booster engine with Long March 7A remains an unconfirmed, analyst-only grounding-risk question with no CASC decision either way.

GlobeNewswire, Spaceflight Now, Rocket Lab (primary) · SpaceNews, SpaceLaunchSchedule, NextSpaceflight (Zhuque-3 delay, provisional new date).
